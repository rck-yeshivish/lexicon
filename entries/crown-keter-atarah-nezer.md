# Crown: keter / atarah / nezer

## Bottom line

*Keter* is the **royal crown**: all three appearances in Tanach are in the phrase
*keser malchus* (Esther 1:11, 2:17, 6:8), and by the Malbim it sits around the
head without rising above it, a coronet. *Atarah* is the general **crown** or
**wreath**, of any wearer, and it encircles the head and rises above it
(Malbim). *Nezer*
is the crown of **consecration and separation**, worn by the nazir and the Kohen
Gadol, which is why the word marks a man off as set apart. Some hold *keter* is the general word and the other two are particular
kinds, but most read all three as distinct kinds.

## Distinctions

### keter (כתר)
The Malbim: physically, a crown that encircles the head but does not rise above
its height, a coronet; thematically, specifically the crown of royalty. Every
occurrence in Tanach is in the construct *keser malchus*, the royal crown, and
all three are in Megillas Esther (1:11, 2:17, 6:8).

Rabbi Klein heard from Rabbi Zev Leff: *keser* is kingship, *atarah* mere
adornment. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. זר): there are three
crowns of different forms. The *keser* is the sign of rule, worn by kings; the
*atarah* the sign of greatness, worn by ministers and nobles; the *nezer* the
sign of honor, worn by sages and *perushim*, from *nezirus*, set apart from the
crowd, and it is the *tzitz* (Shemos 39:30; Vayikra 21:12; Bamidbar 6:7). Kings
used all three, each as the hour required, since they hold all three qualities
(Shmuel II 1:10; Melachim II 11:12); and perhaps Yosef, *nezir echav* (Devarim
33:16), was crowned in law over his brothers.

### atarah (עטרה)

- The Malbim: a crown that goes all the way around the head and rises above it,
  and which need not belong to a king; *atarah* can name any sort of crown.
- Others hold that the mark of an *atarah* is that it is covered on top, while
  the others need not be.

Because *keter* and *atarah* both name what surrounds the head, Tanach uses them
for surrounding of any kind, much as English "circlet" carries the circle in it.

### nezer (נזר)
Related to *zer*, the golden band that ran around several of the kelim of the
Mishkan, a word that appears ten times in Shemos. But *nezer* carries more than
ornament. It is the root of *nazir*: the Torah calls the man who has forsworn wine,
cutting his hair, and tumah especially holy "because the *nezer*
of his God is upon his head" (Bamidbar 6:7), which shows itself physically in his
long hair and spiritually in his separating himself from the common folk. The same
word explains the stringencies of the Kohen Gadol, "for the *nezer*, the anointing
oil of his God, is upon him" (Vayikra 21:12), and he too is set apart from the
nation in dress and in avodah. So *nezer* is the crown that marks off and
separates, showing a man's standing above the rest. The Malbim adds that
physically the *nezer* encircles only half the head, a tiara.

### klil (כליל) and tag (תג)
*Klil* is the Aramaic for crown, and likewise means what encompasses something.
Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg derives *kallah*, a bride, from it: as a crown
wholly surrounds the head, a *kallah* is wholly surrounded by her ornaments and
jewelry. *Tag* or *taga* is a fifth word for crown, the one used in rabbinic
idiom, and is the word for the *tagin*, the crownlets drawn atop certain letters
in a sefer Torah. Linguists claim *tag* to be a Persian
loanword later taken into Aramaic, and Rabbi Klein adds an aside of his own: given that a
crown marks its wearer out as special, one can easily see how the Aramaic *tag*
became the English "tag," which also serves as a marker of sorts.

## Rendering guidance

- *Keser malchus* in Esther: "the royal crown." Do not drop *malchus*; the Hebrew
  never uses *keter* without it.
- *Atarah*: "crown," or "wreath" where the context is festive or honorific rather
  than royal (a chasan's *atarah*, a crown of praise).
- *Nezer* on the nazir or the Kohen Gadol: "crown" works only if the separation
  is glossed. Better "the crown of consecration," or render the sense outright,
  "the consecration of his God is upon his head," and note the crown image.
- *Tagin*: "crownlets" or "crowns" of the letters, never "tags."
- Trap: English "crown" will take *keter*, *atarah*, and *nezer* together, and the
  three differ in shape and in who wears them. Where a passage lists two of them,
  or darshens the difference, use "royal crown," "crown," and "diadem" and say
  which authority you followed, since the Malbim's shapes are one reading among
  others.
