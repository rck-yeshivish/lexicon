# Balsam: panag / tzari (nataf, ketaf) / bosem / afarsemon

## Bottom line

*Tzari* is the balsam resin of Tanach and of the *ketores*: render it **balm**
or **balsam**, and keep it distinct from the incense's own biblical name for the
same ingredient, *nataf*, literally **the dripping one**. *Bosem*/*besamim* is
the wide word, **fragrance, spices**, never balsam specifically in Tanach.
*Afarsemon* is the Rabbinic word for **balsam oil**. *Panag* is a *hapax
legomenon* at Yechezkel 27:17; *Yossiphon* and Rashi read it as balsam, but
Radak objects that Yechezkel lists *panag* and *tzari* separately, so leave it
untranslated or gloss it. The trap: "balsam" will swallow *tzari*, *panag*,
*bosem* and *afarsemon* at once, flattening a whole disagreement.

## Distinctions

### panag (פנג)
One occurrence, Yechezkel 27:17, among the goods Tyre bought from Eretz Yisrael:
wheat of *Minis*, *panag*, honey, oil and *tzari*. *Yossiphon* (I ch. 38), which
calls Yericho *Ir HaReiach* for its balsam groves, says outright that balsam oil
is *panag* oil; Rashi cites this (to Yechezkel 27:17, Melachim II 20:13,
Yeshayahu 39:2, *Berachos* 43a), as do Rabbi Yosef Kara, Rabbi Eliezer of
Beaugency and Radak. Against it, Radak himself notes that if *tzari* is balsam
oil then *panag* is probably not, since Yechezkel lists them separately; Rabbi
Yosef Teomim-Frankel (*Iggros Pri Megadim* letter 2) takes that as support for
reading *tzari* as something else (Rashi's "theriaca"). Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi
Ashkenazi and Rabbi Shmuel David Luzzatto tie *panag* to *pinuk*, indulgence,
and the Zohar (*Bereishis* 47b, 235b, 245b-246b) likewise links it to
*tafnukim*; *Shoresh Yesha* connects it to *oneg*. The commentators also offer
cassia, wax (*donag*), honeycomb, grass, rice, millet, a baked good, a kind of
wheat, and a place name.

### tzari (צרי), nataf (נטף), ketaf (קטף)
*Tzari* (also *tzori*, *tzri*) appears six times in Tanach: Bereishis 37:25 and
43:11, Yirmiyahu 8:22, 46:11 and 51:8, Yechezkel 27:17. A scented medicinal
preparation; it heads the *ketores* list at *Kerisus* 6a and *Yerushalmi Yoma*
4:5 and so entered the liturgy. Rambam (*Hilchos Klei HaMikdash* 2:4) renders it
in Arabic *al-balasan*, balsam. Its name in the incense passage is *nataf*
(Shemos 30:34), "the one that drips," which suits balsam, whose so-called oil is
sap dripping from the tree; Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, appended to the *ketores*
teaching, says *tzari* is what drips (*notef*) from the *ketaf* tree, and
Rambam's Mishnah commentary at *Sheviis* 7:6 glosses *ketaf* as the balsam tree.
Targum Onkelos renders both *tzari* and *nataf* as *ketaf*; Rabbi Saadia Gaon
and Rabbi Avraham Maimuni render *nataf* as *al-balasan*.

Whether Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel explains the earlier teaching or argues with
it: Rashi (to Shemos 30:34 and *Kerisus* 6a) reads him as explaining; Ramban
(there) reads Rambam as taking him to disagree, which would make *tzari* a piece
of the tree itself rather than its oil (see *Mahari Kurkus* and *Kesef Mishneh*).
*Bereishis Rabbah* 91:11 identifies the *tzari* Yaakov sent to Mitzrayim as
balsam.

### bosem / besamim (בושם)
The general word, some thirty occurrences, for fragrant goods: perfumes, spices,
sweet-smelling things, not balsam in particular. It stands behind the names
Basmas (Bereishis 26:34, 36:3; Melachim I 4:15) and Yivsam (Divrei HaYamim I
7:2). Ramban (to Shemos 25:6 and 30:34) denies a root ב-ש-ם and reads *bosem* as
a compound of *bo* and *sam/samim*, "an elixir in it." Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
traces it to the two-letter ס-ם, putting a thing in its place, since such
potions are kept in designated places. From it comes *besumei* on Purim
(*Megillah* 7b), read by most as intoxication, and by Rabbi Moshe ben Yekutiel
of Rome (*Sefer HaTadir*) and Rabbi Yosef of Saragossa as scented wine.

### afarsemon (אפרסמון) and opopalsamun
*Afarsemon* is the standard Rabbinic word for balsam oil. It is absent from the
Mishnah but common in Gemara: kings not anointed with the *shemen hamishchah*
were anointed with *afarsemon* (*Horayos* 11b-12a, *Kerisus* 5b, *Yerushalmi
Shekalim* 6:1 and *Sotah* 8:3); its berachah (*Berachos* 43a); its flammability
(*Shabbos* 26a); the Daughters of Tzion using it to entice (*Shabbos* 62b, *Yoma*
9b); its medical use (*Shabbos* 140a, *Avodah Zarah* 30a); the thirteen rivers
of it shown to Rabbi Elazar ben Pedas (*Taanis* 25a). Rashi (to Amos 6:6) calls
it the foremost oil, so much so that an unspecified "oil" is sometimes read as
*afarsemon* (*Bava Basra* 80b and *Rosh HaShanah* 23a on Yeshayahu 41:19; Rashi
to Mishlei 27:9). Rabbi Yosef Teomim-Frankel roots the word in *pirsum*, since
balsam's scent made it famous; Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Mussaf HeAruch*)
derived Greek *balsamon* from *baal shemen*. In Modern Hebrew *afarsemon* means
persimmon, a neologism unrelated to the Rabbinic word. *Opopalsamun* is a third
Rabbinic term, in *Bereishis Rabbah* 39:1 (Avraham as sealed *opopalsamun*,
scentless until moved) and 27:3 (used to catch a thief by smell).

## Rendering guidance

- צרי in Chumash and Neviim: "balm" (Bereishis 37:25, 43:11; "is there no balm
  in Gilad," Yirmiyahu 8:22). In the *ketores* list: "balsam," or *tzari* with a
  gloss, since Rashi's theriaca and Rambam's *al-balasan* differ.
- נטף: "the dripping resin," or "*nataf*, the sap that drips." Do not render it
  "balsam"; the word names the manner, and the Targum's *ketaf* names the tree.
- בושם/בשמים: "fragrance," "spices," "perfumes." Never "balsam" in a pasuk.
- אפרסמון: "balsam oil." Never "persimmon."
- Trap: "balsam" absorbs all four. Yechezkel 27:17 has *panag* and *tzari* in
  one list, which is Radak's objection; rendering both "balsam" silently answers
  a question the meforshim left open. Transliterate *panag*.
- Where *bosem* and *afarsemon* stand together, keep "spices" against "balsam
  oil": the *shemen hamishchah* recipe uses *besamim* and did not call for
  balsam, while its informal counterpart did.
