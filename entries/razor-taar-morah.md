# Razor: taar / morah

## Bottom line

*Taar* is the plain word for a **razor**; the Torah uses it for the *nazir*
(Bamidbar 6:5). *Morah* is the word the Neviim use in the same formula (Shoftim
13:5, 16:17; Shmuel I 1:11), and it is contested: Radak renders it **razor**
throughout, while Targum and Rashi render it **scissors** by Shimshon and
**the fear of men** by Shmuel. No source distinguishes the two words where both
mean a razor, and no source in the column draws one. So render both
"razor" unless the passage is Shmuel I 1:11, where the reading itself is the
question.

## Distinctions

### taar (תער)
Bamidbar 6:5, "a *taar* shall not pass over his head." Rav Hirsch (to Bamidbar
6:5) relates it to ע-ר-ה, laying bare or exposing. That also explains *mitaar*,
a sword's scabbard: as the razor takes off the hair and lays the skin bare, so
the sword appears suddenly when it is drawn out.

### morah (מורה)
The word in the Neviim for the same prohibition. Three etymologies are offered.
Rashi (to Shoftim 13:5) ties it to י-ר-ה, "shoots" or "throws away," since the
razor throws the hair off. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim takes the root as מ-ר,
transferring or switching, the root of *temurah* (transferring *kedushah* from
one animal to another) and *mumar* (one who leaves Torah for another religion):
shaving away hair clears the way for new hair to replace it. And *Vayikra
Rabbah* 10:5 hears "fear" in it: why is the *taar* called a *morah*? Because
hair fears (*morah*) only the razor, which shaves it with a shaving of
destruction, as it says "do not destroy the corner of your beard" (Vayikra
19:27).

### The dispute at Shmuel I 1:11
Targum (to Shoftim 13:5 and 16:17) renders *morah* as scissors by Shimshon, and
Targum (to Shmuel I 1:11) renders it "fear of men" by Shmuel; Rashi follows the
same split in each place. Radak renders *morah* "razor" everywhere, and explains
that Targum and Rashi are following Rabbi Yossi, who holds (*Nazir* 66a) that
Shimshon was a *nazir* but Shmuel was not; on that view Chana's vow cannot be
about a razor, so *morah* there must mean the fear of other men. Radak himself
holds like Rabbi Nehorai, that Shmuel was a *nazir*, and so keeps "razor."

## Rendering guidance

- תער: "razor," always.
- מורה: "razor" by default, following Radak.
- Trap: at Shmuel I 1:11 the choice of English decides the *machlokes*. "No
  razor shall come upon his head" commits to Rabbi Nehorai and Radak; "no fear
  of man shall come upon him" commits to Rabbi Yossi, Targum and Rashi. Do not
  choose silently; where the passage bears on whether Shmuel was a *nazir*,
  give one reading in the body and the other in a note.
- Trap: English "razor" absorbs *taar* and *morah* both, which is usually
  harmless, but not where *Vayikra Rabbah* 10:5 is being darshened. There the
  derashah runs on the sound of *morah* as fear, and the English must hold both:
  "the razor, the *morah*, of which the hair alone is afraid."
- Do not manufacture a distinction between the two words in a footnote. The
  article looked and found none, and could not account for why the Torah says
  *taar* and the Neviim say *morah*.
