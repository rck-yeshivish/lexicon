# Salvation: yeshuah / teshuah / hatzalah / purkan

## Bottom line

*Yeshuah* and *teshuah* are the grand words: **salvation**, total release from
oppression, and specifically salvation that answers a cry (*shav'ah*) for help.
*Hatzalah* is the plain one: **rescue**, being pulled out of danger (root sense
"separate, remove"); the Rokeach calls it partial or temporary next to
*yeshuah*. *Purkan* (Aramaic, the Targum's *pidyon*) is **redemption**, being
bought back. The one thing not to get wrong: *hatzalah* is not "salvation" in
the full sense; render it "rescue" or "saving," and reserve "salvation" for
*yeshuah*/*teshuah*.

## Distinctions

### yeshuah (ישועה) and teshuah (תשועה)
Both are tied to crying out: they name the aspect of salvation that is Hashem's
answer to prayer and effort. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 14:13): *shav'ah*, crying
out, is the yearning for a *teshuah*, a cry that is a prayer for salvation and
not a mere complaint. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the biliteral ש-ע underlies
*yeshuah*, *teshuah*, and *shav'ah*, and its core is enjoyment and pleasure (so
too *sha'ashua*, *mishan*, *sha'ah*); the column offers an alternative core of
"smoothness," the smooth passage from prayer to salvation.

Rav Hirsch separates the two. *Yeshuah*, root י-ש-ע, is kin to י-ש-ה and to
the biliteral י-ש, "exists": it is the first-order existence Hashem grants, the
salvation from a danger that threatened one's very being. *Teshuah*, root
ש-ו-ע, is kin to ש-ב-ע (seven) and ש-פ-ע (influx): it concerns what is
peripheral to existence, one's property and economic freedom, so it often names
salvation through military victory over outside conquerors.

Rabbi Yechiel Michel Heilpern and Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok: *yeshuah*
and *teshuah* are salvation from a grave threat, murder or a crime of a sexual
nature (Devarim 22:27), which is why the Midrash (Shemos Rabbah 1:32) infers
from *vayoshi'an* (Shemos 2:17) that the shepherds meant harm to Yisro's
daughters. Peirush HaRokeach: *yeshuah*/*teshuah* is total release from
subjugation. Malbim: *yeshuah* is salvation from the dire situation itself,
without the further protection *hatzalah* implies.

The Gemara's *Yeshua HaBen* (Bava Kamma 80a) is the *pidyon haben* meal (Sefer
HaAruch, Rashi, Tosafos there), because the Targumim render both *yeshuah* and
*pidyon* with words of *purkan*.

### hatzalah (הצלה)
Peirush HaRokeach: only a partial or temporary respite from oppression, not a
total release. Malbim (to Yirmiyahu 15:20): *hatzalah* is the element of
salvation that releases a person from mortal danger; and, against *yeshuah*,
it implies being saved from the situation plus being protected from future
troubles. Heilpern and Rabbi Chanoch Zundel: *hatzalah* is saving from any
threat, not only the gravest. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: from the biliteral צ-ל,
"separation for a constructive purpose" (*tzel*, *tzli*, *atzal*): extricating a
person from peril. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 3:22, Devarim 32:2): root נ-צ-ל, kin
to נ-ש-ל (remove) and נ-ז-ל (flow away): saving by removing someone from the
hazard. Radak connects the "saving" and "removing" senses of נ-צ-ל; Ibn Janach
lists them as two unrelated meanings of one root.

### purkan (פורקן)
The Targum's standard Aramaic for *pidyon*: redemption, Hashem buying us back
after having, as it were, sold us to our oppressors. Chazal used it in *Al
HaNissim* because the root פ-ר-ק already appears in Tanach for salvation (Eichah
5:8, Tehillim 136:24); in Hebrew it also means to slip something off (*porek*),
as the Chashmonaim slipped off the Greek yoke. Rabbi Yitzchak Eliyahu Landau of
Dubno: in *Al HaNissim* it is deliverance from financial ruin.

## Rendering guidance

- ישועה: "salvation"; in a pasuk, "deliverance" also serves. תשועה: "salvation"
  or "deliverance"; where Rav Hirsch's reading fits (a war, a freedom
  regained), "victory" is defensible with a gloss.
- הצלה: "rescue," "saving," "being saved"; מציל: "rescuer," "one who saves."
  Not "salvation."
- פורקן in a tefillah: "redemption"; leave it beside *teshuah* as two words,
  not one.
- Trap: English "salvation" (and "save") absorbs *yeshuah*, *teshuah*, and
  *hatzalah* alike. In *Al HaNissim* (פורקן ... תשועה) or a derashah on why a
  pasuk says *vayoshi'a* rather than *vayatzel*, keep each Hebrew word its own
  English: purkan "redemption," teshuah "salvation," hatzalah "rescue."
- When the three appear together, order by weight: rescue (*hatzalah*) is the
  least, salvation (*yeshuah*/*teshuah*) the whole, redemption (*purkan*) the
  buying-back.
