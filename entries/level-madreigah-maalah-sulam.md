# Level: madreigah / maalah / sulam

## Bottom line

*Maalah* (plural *maalos*) is a step *up*, an ascent; in the abstract a
**step up, an advantage, a virtue**: physically **step, stair**. *Madreigah*
is a graded step or terrace; in later Hebrew a **degree, a level** (of
achievement), which is the word a mussar text means. *Sulam* is a **ladder**,
though Chazal define it by *maalos* and *madreigos* alike, so in Rabbinic
usage it may be a stair. The one thing not to get wrong: in Tanach *maalos*
is the ordinary word (some fifty times; *madreigos* only twice), and Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, the one authority who separates them, makes
*madreigos* a terraced way cut up a mountainside and *maalos* a free-standing
stair in a house or courtyard; that is why the altar prohibition says
*maalos* (Shemos 20:23).

## Distinctions

### maalah (מעלה)
Root ע-ל-ה, near-unanimously: *al*, *maalah* (above), *oleh*, *aliyah*. The
word names stairs by their upward use, as English "elevator" and "escalator"
do, because going up is the feat; a stair goes down too, but the word looks
up. Tanach: the six *maalos* of Shlomo's throne (Melachim I 10:19), which
Rashi glosses as *madreigos* and Targum Yonasan as *dargin*; the fifteen
*Shir HaMaalos* (Tehillim 120-135), matched by the Mishnah (Sukkah 5:4) to
the fifteen steps on which the Leviim sang between the courtyards; the three
*maalos* of the stone before the Menorah (Tamid 3:9). Rambam (Hilchos Beis
HaBechirah 1:17) uses *madreigos* when stating that no stairs may be built to
the altar and the Torah's own *maalos* when fixing the lashes for ascending
by them. Talmudic *maalyuta*, "advantage, superb," is a step up in the
abstract, the root of the abstract sense of *maalah*.

### madreigah (מדרגה)
Root ד-ר-ג (Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Ibn Parchon, Radak), a root with
no other Biblical derivative; twice in Tanach (Yechezkel 38:20, Shir HaShirim
2:14) and twice in the Mishnah (Kilayim 6:2, Sheviis 3:8). Rav Hirsch (to
Bereishis 6:9, Tehillim 25:4): conceptually akin to *derech*, a path that
carries a person to his destination, the roots ד-ר-ג and ד-ר-ך being kin
through *gimmel* and *kaf*; Rabbi Yitzchak of Zeldin (Shoresh Yesha): both are
where the foot treads. Wertheimer (Beiur Shemos HaNirdafim): in Biblical
Hebrew *madreigos* is specifically the terraced rampart built into a
mountainside so a steep slope can be climbed (Rashi to Yechezkel 38:20 and
Shir HaShirim 2:14). From the staggered, graded shape the word widened to any
scale: degrees of heat or of a circle, and any level of attainment, the
spiritual *madreigah*. Aramaic *darga* is the same word (Yevamos 63a, "go
down a *darga* to take a wife," Rashi: not a woman of higher station than
oneself); Modern Hebrew *darga* is a military rank.

### sulam (סולם)
Root ס-ל-ם for the four lexicographers; a *hapax* in Tanach (Yaakov's dream,
Bereishis 28:12), common in the Mishnah (Beitzah 1:3, Bava Basra 2:5, 3:6,
Makkos 2:1). Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 28:12, Bamidbar 20:19, Tehillim 84:6)
derives it from ס-ל-ל, a paved way (*mesilah*), the final *mem* being added,
which puts *sulam* on the same footing as *madreigah* (a path up); Pappenheim
reduces it to the biliteral ס-ל. Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads the *samech* as a
stand-in for the *ayin* before it in the alphabet, so *sulam* is ע-ל, the
root of *maalos*. Rashi defines *sulam* with *maalos* (to Bava Metzia 25a,
Sukkah 53b) and with *madreigos* (to Beitzah 36a), so the three do belong to
one cluster.

## Rendering guidance

- *Madreigah* in the abstract (a person's *madreigah*, *madreigos* of
  avodah): "level"; "degree" where a scale is explicit. Physical *madreigos*:
  "steps," or "terraces" on a hillside (Wertheimer).
- *Maalah* in the abstract: "virtue," "advantage," "a step up"; in Tanach
  physical *maalos* are "steps" or "stairs" (the altar, the throne). *Shir
  HaMaalos*: "Song of Ascents."
- *Sulam*: "ladder" for Yaakov's dream and in the Mishnah unless the Gemara's
  context is plainly a fixed stair; then "stairway" is defensible from Rashi.
- When *maalos* and *madreigos* stand together (Rambam, Hilchos Beis
  HaBechirah 1:17; Rashi glossing one by the other), the second is a gloss,
  not a second object; render the Torah's own word and let the gloss be
  silent or bracketed.
- Trap: "step" absorbs *maalah* and *madreigah*, and "level" absorbs
  *madreigah* and abstract *maalah*. Where a text climbs (a ladder of
  *madreigos*, a *maalah* above a *maalah*), keep one English word per Hebrew
  word, and remember that only *maalah* carries the upward sense in its root.
