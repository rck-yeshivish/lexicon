# Level: madreigah / maalah / sulam

## Bottom line

*Maalah* (plural *maalos*) is a step *up*, an ascent; in the abstract a
**step up, an advantage, a virtue**: physically **step, stair**. *Madreigah*
is a graded step or terrace; in later Hebrew a **degree, a level** (of
achievement, the spiritual *madreigah* among them). *Sulam* is a **ladder**,
though Rashi defines it by *maalos* and *madreigos* alike. In Tanach *maalos*
is the ordinary word (some fifty times; *madreigos* only twice), and Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, who alone here separates them by sense, makes
*madreigos* a terraced way cut up a mountainside and *maalos* a free-standing
stair in a house or courtyard; that is why the altar prohibition says *maalos*
(Shemos 20:23).

## Distinctions

### maalah (מעלה)
Root ע-ל-ה, near-unanimously: *al*, *maalah* (above), *oleh*, *aliyah*. The
word names stairs by their upward use, as English "elevator" and "escalator"
do, because going up is the feat; a stair goes down too, but the word looks
up. Tanach: the six *maalos* of Shlomo's throne (Melachim I 10:19), which
Rashi glosses as *madreigos* and Targum Yonasan as *dargin*; the fifteen
*Shir HaMaalos* (Tehillim 120-135), matched by the Mishnah (Sukkah 5:4) to
the fifteen steps on which the Leviim sang between the courtyards; the three
*maalos* of the stone before the Menorah (Tamid 3:9), on which the Kohen stood
to set it up. In the parallel to Shlomo's throne, pseudo-Rashi (to Divrei
HaYamim II 9:18) likewise explains the *maalos* as *madreigos*, while Targum Rav
Yosef there uses *masok*, an inflection of the Aramaic verb to ascend. From that
pattern Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein offers a suggestion of his own, hedged: the
difference might be that *maalos* is Hebrew while *madreigos* comes from another
language, Aramaic or Akkadian. Rambam (Hilchos Beis HaBechirah 1:17) uses
*madreigos* when stating that no stairs may be built to the altar and the
Torah's own *maalos* when fixing the lashes for ascending by them. Talmudic
*maalyuta*, "advantage, superb," is a step up in the abstract.

### madreigah (מדרגה)
Root ד-ר-ג (Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Ibn Parchon, Radak), a root with no
other Biblical derivative; twice in Tanach (Yechezkel 38:20, Shir HaShirim 2:14)
and twice in the Mishnah (Kilayim 6:2, Sheviis 3:8), each pair carrying one
plural and one singular.

- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 6:9, Tehillim 25:4): conceptually akin to *derech*, a
  path that carries a person to his destination, which Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein
  takes as implying that ד-ר-ג and ד-ר-ך are kin, possibly through the
  interchange of gimmel and kaf.
- Rabbi Yitzchak of Zeldin (Shoresh Yesha) makes the same connection more
  prosaically, both words naming a place the foot treads.
- The German philologist Wilhelm Gesenius likewise held ד-ר-ג related to ד-ר-ך,
  and connected the Hebrew root to Arabic *daraja*, walking by foot, and a stair
  or step; later scholars, among them Rabbi Dr. Ernest Klein and Avraham
  Even-Shoshan, similarly connect these to the Akkadian cognates
  *durgu*/*daragu*, path.

Wertheimer (Beiur Shemos HaNirdafim, a work written to show that no two Hebrew
words are truly synonymous): the two look synonymous on the surface, both naming
what people use to go up and down, but in Biblical Hebrew *madreigos* is
specifically a walkway built to climb a mountain, where the incline is too steep
to be traversed and earth may be filled in around it to make a terraced rampart
(Rashi to Yechezkel 38:20 and Shir HaShirim 2:14), while *maalos* is a
free-standing stairwell in a house or courtyard, not built into a mountainside,
which is why the Torah forbids *maalos* to the altar and not *madreigos*.

From the staggered, graded shape the word widened to any scale: degrees of heat
or of a circle, and any level of attainment, the spiritual *madreigah*. Aramaic
*darga* is the same word (Yevamos 63a, "go down a *darga* to take a wife,"
Rashi: not a woman of a more prestigious class than oneself, lest she not be
willing to accept him); Modern Hebrew *darga* is a military rank. There is also
a cantillation mark called *darga*, of which Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein says he is
unsure how it bears on the discussion; and he notes that *darga* shares its
consonants with English *grade* and *degree*, probably by pure coincidence.

### sulam (סולם)
What, if anything, really separates a ladder from a staircase is an open
question: perhaps that a ladder is moveable and a staircase built in, or that a
ladder can stand nearer to vertical. Root ס-ל-ם for the four lexicographers,
though again no other Biblical word comes from it; a *hapax* in Tanach (Yaakov's dream, Bereishis
28:12), common in the Mishnah (Beitzah 1:3, Bava Basra 2:5, 3:6, Makkos 2:1,
Zavim 3:1, 4:3). Scholars have proposed various etymologies for the root.

- Dr. Chaim Tawil (*An Akkadian Lexical Companion for Biblical Hebrew*) cites
  Landsberger that ס-ל-ם is by metathesis a cognate of Akkadian *simmiltu*,
  stairway, the word used of the staircases up the Babylonian ziggurats; on that
  reading the mem is part of the root and *sulam* means much what *maalos* and
  *madreigos* mean.
- The more conservative approach takes *sulam* from Hebrew ס-ל-ל, paved, whence
  *mesilah*, with the final mem paragogic and so no part of the root, which puts
  *sulam* on the same footing as *madreigah*, a path up; so Rav Hirsch (to
  Bereishis 28:12, Bamidbar 20:19, Tehillim 84:6), Gesenius, Kohut, and the
  *Enhanced Brown-Driver-Briggs*.
- Rabbi Dr. Ernest Klein implies that ס-ל-ל means paving a path by lifting the
  stones in the way and throwing them aside, so that *sulam* comes from the
  "lifting" sense, a means of lifting a person higher; Pappenheim takes a
  similar approach and reduces the root further to the biliteral ס-ל.
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus proposes that where the essential root is ס-ל, the samech
  stands in for the ayin that precedes it in the alef-beis, so the root of
  *sulam* is ע-ל, the very root of *maalos*.

Rashi defines *sulam* with *maalos* (to Bava Metzia 25a, Sukkah 53b) and with
*madreigos* (to Beitzah 36a), which Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein brings as proof
that the three belong to one cluster.

## Rendering guidance

- *Madreigah* in the abstract (a person's *madreigah*, *madreigos* of
  avodah): "level"; "degree" where a scale is explicit. Physical *madreigos*:
  "steps," or "terraces" on a hillside (Wertheimer).
- *Maalah* in the abstract: "virtue," "advantage," "a step up"; in Tanach
  physical *maalos* are "steps" or "stairs" (the altar, the throne). *Shir
  HaMaalos*: "Song of Ascents."
- *Sulam*: "ladder" for Yaakov's dream and in the Mishnah unless the Gemara's
  context is plainly a fixed stair; then "stairway" is defensible from Rashi.
- When *maalos* and *madreigos* stand together (Rambam, Hilchos Beis
  HaBechirah 1:17; Rashi glossing one by the other), the second is a gloss,
  not a second object; render the Torah's own word and let the gloss be
  silent or bracketed.
- Trap: "step" absorbs *maalah* and *madreigah*, and "level" absorbs
  *madreigah* and abstract *maalah*. Where a text climbs (a ladder of
  *madreigos*, a *maalah* above a *maalah*), keep one English word per Hebrew
  word, and remember that only *maalah* carries the upward sense in its root.
