# Beginning: reishis / techilah / kedem (rishon, rosh)

## Bottom line

*Reishis* is the **beginning** that is also the **foremost**: a starting point
that carries importance in its own right. How absolute that starting point is
depends on whom you follow: for Malbim it stands outside a counted sequence
altogether, while the Vilna Gaon has *reishis* mean the first of a series without
denying that something preceded the series. *Rishon* is **first** in a counted
series, one of several. *Techilah* is the **onset**, marking (on Malbim's
account) that a thing did not exist before, without tying itself to what follows.
*Kedem* is **before**, and in space **east**. The one thing not to get wrong: *reishis* is valued, not merely
ordinal. Rendering it flatly "the first" throws away the importance on which
Rashi's opening comment and the Netziv's *terumah* reading both depend.

## Distinctions

### reishis (ראשית) and rishon (ראשון)
The classical lexicographers take ר-א-ש as the root, giving *rosh*, *rishon* and
*reishis* together. *Rosh* is the head anatomically and the head of a nation,
tribe or unit; it also marks the opening of an expected series, *Rosh Chodesh*
(Bamidbar 10:10, 28:11) and *Rosh HaShanah* (Yechezkel 40:1). Menachem Ibn Saruk
(*Machberes Menachem*): *rosh* can also mean a count or tally, especially of
people (Shemos 30:12; Bamidbar 31:49; Tehillim 139:17; Iyov 22:12). Rav Hirsch
(to Shemos 30:12) reads that differently: *rosh* there is not the individuals
counted but the final total, since as the head unites the body's parts, the
number gathers all the units under one head. *Rosh* also means poison (Devarim
29:17, 32:32-33; Iyov 20:16; Eichah 3:19, and more); Rav Hirsch (to Devarim
29:17) has it drawn from both the plant and the animal kingdom, some read it as
snake venom from the snake's *rosh* (Targum and Rashi to Yirmiyahu 8:14), and
others take it as a poisonous plant, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim explaining that the
plant in question has a round top that looks like a head.

Rabbi Klein offers his own account, doubly hedged, of how these senses hang
together: the anatomical head is likely the central sense, the head controls the
body and so lends its name to anything in a controlling position, such positions
usually go to prominent people and so *rosh* comes to mean important, and since
the head normally emerges first at birth *rosh* also comes to mean first. The two
then converge, since what is important tends to be listed or done first.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim treats the middle aleph as extraneous and takes the root
as the biliteral reish-shin, pointing to *rishon* spelled with a yud (Iyov 8:8)
and *reishis* spelled with neither aleph nor yud (Devarim 11:12), and to Aramaic
*reish* (thirteen times in the Aramaic of Daniel; *reish galusa*, *reish
mesivta*, Reish Lakish), where Biblical Aramaic still spells it with an aleph
like the Hebrew *rosh* and later Aramaic replaces that aleph with a yud. Under
that biliteral he gathers *yerushah* (the heir
becomes the head of the estate), *tirosh* (new wine, foremost in quality and at
the start of its existence), *rishayon*/*reshus* (authority), *reshes* (a net,
by which a hunter gains mastery over an animal), *rosheh* (weak, easily
subjugated), and *rash* (destitute, so browbeaten that anything rules him).

*Reishis* carries importance, not only order. The Netziv (*Meromei Sadeh* to
Chullin 136b) uses this to resolve the Tosafists' difficulty. *Terumah* is called
*reishis* (Bamidbar 15:20-21; Devarim 18:4) and the Gemara derives that one may
not consecrate his whole crop, since a remnant must stay ordinary; yet *reishis
hagez* (Devarim 18:4) carries no such requirement. The Netziv: in *terumah*,
*reishis* means importance, so the tithe is taken from the choicest produce of
the field, and importance is relative, so something must be left unimportant
beside it; in the shearing, *reishis* means only first in succession, so the
mitzvah falls on the first batch of wool with no bar to giving all of it. Rabbi
Klein adds, as his own point, that saying a thing comes first does not require
that something come after it, and finds it in "I am *rishon* and I am last, and
besides Me there is no god" (Yeshayahu 44:6): being first does not posit a
second. Rashi (to Bereishis 1:1) reads *b'reishis* for
that valued sense, expounding it of the Torah and of Klal Yisrael, each called
*reishis* elsewhere (Mishlei 8:22; Yirmiyahu 2:3, where Klal Yisrael has
*terumah*-like status and whoever "eats" of them bears the guilt). Targum
Yerushalmi renders *b'reishis* as created with wisdom. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
adds that *rishon* can be spatial as well as temporal: Yaakov set the maidservants
and their children *rishonah*, in front, and Leah and Rachel behind (Bereishis
33:2).

### techilah (תחילה)
Thirteen appearances in Tanach counting its inflections, by Even-Shoshan's
concordance, the opening act of a sequence: "the *techilas* of Hashem's speaking
to Hoshea" (Hoshea 1:2). In the very comment to Bereishis 1:1 that asks why the
Torah opens with creation rather than with the first mitzvos, Rashi's own verb
for beginning the Torah is *lehaschil*, a cognate. Root: ח-ל-ל triliterally (Ibn Janach,
Radak) or the biliteral ח-ל (Menachem Ibn Saruk). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, on the
biliteral, reads that root as circularity and encirclement, so *techilah* is the
moment a process enters its natural cycle and begins to run. The halachic
*lechatchilah* belongs here: lamed plus kaf plus *techilah*, the ideal way to
perform a mitzvah from the outset, before any *bedieved* circumstance arises.

### reishis against techilah
Both render "the beginning," and one pair of pesukim states the same idea with
each: "the *reishis* of wisdom is fear of Hashem" (Tehillim 111:10) against "the
*techilas* of wisdom is fear of Hashem" (Mishlei 9:10).

- Maharal (*Nesivos Olam*, *Nesiv Yiras Hashem* ch. 1): Shlomo HaMelech's
  *techilah* makes *yiras Hashem* and wisdom inseparable, one continuum, as the
  Mishnah has it, no *yirah* without wisdom and no wisdom without *yirah* (Avos
  3:17); this serves his agenda in Mishlei of elevating wisdom. David HaMelech's
  *reishis* instead marks *yiras Hashem* as the important element and the goal of
  wisdom, without which wisdom has no worth.
- Rabbi Zev Hoberman, on the Vilna Gaon to Mishlei 9:10: *reishis* is strictly
  what is chronologically first, while *techilah* can name what comes later in
  time but was the intended goal from the outset. David HaMelech, who worked all
  his life for wisdom, taught that one must pursue *yirah* first; Shlomo
  HaMelech, given wisdom as a gift, taught not that *yirah* must precede it in
  time but that it must be the aim from the start.
- Malbim (*Sefer HaCarmel*): *rishon* is the opening position in a countable
  sequence, defined against the items after it; *reishis* is an absolute or primal
  beginning, not part of a sequence, important in itself and encompassing all
  that follows, which is why creation is *b'reishis*; *techilah* negates a prior
  time without implying any tie to what follows. Malbim adds that creation is
  called *reishis* rather than *techilah* because it marks the origin of time
  itself; Rabbi Klein reports this and then adds that he is not quite sure how it
  adds up.
- The Vilna Gaon (to Yeshayahu 1:26), on "I will restore your judges as at the
  *rishonah* and your counselors as at the *techilah*": a *reishis*-word for the
  judges because David and Shlomo were not the first judges, only the foremost of
  a series; a *techilah*-word for the counselors because Moshe and Aharon were the
  absolute first the nation ever had. The Midrash as we have it (*Yalkut Shimoni*
  to Yeshayahu 391) reads the two the other way round, judges as Moshe and Aharon
  and counselors as David and Shlomo.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's *Yerios Shlomo* opens with thirteen pages on this
distinction; anyone settling a hard case should go there.

### kedem (קדם)
Targum Onkelos renders *b'reishis* as *b'kadmin*. *Kodem* is "before" in time,
with the family *mukdam* (early), *kadmon* and *kadmoniyos* (ancient,
antiquities), *hakdamah* (introduction), *k'dam* (the English prefix "pre-"),
*kidomes* (prefix), *takdim* (precedent, an occurrence the like of which has
happened before), *mikdamah* (advance payment), and in Modern Hebrew *kidum*
(advancement, progress). The term, Rabbi Klein writes, denotes the future
becoming the present. Spatially, *kedem* and *kadimah* are east, the direction the
sun rises from and therefore what comes first. The Aramaic *kaman*, *l'kaman*
and *kamma* (as in Bava Kamma) come from the same root with the dalet dropped,
the loss marked by the *dagesh* in the mem; Rabbi Shaul Goldman notes that this
dropping seemingly happened in the post-Tannaitic period, since it is absent from
Biblical Aramaic, Targum Onkelos and Targum Yonasan and present in Amoraic works
(*Pesikta*, Yerushalmi, Bavli). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim suggests that ק-ד-ם
derives from the biliteral kuf-dalet, reading *kedem* with *kodkod*, head or
skull; Rabbi Klein observes that this parallels *rosh* meaning both head and
beginning.

## Rendering guidance

- ראשית: "the beginning," but where the source is leaning on its worth, "the
  foremost" or "the first and finest." In *reishis chochmah* keep "the beginning
  of wisdom" and carry the importance in a note.
- ראשון: "first." תחילה: "at the outset," "at first," "the onset." לכתחילה stays
  *lechatchilah*.
- קדם temporal: "before," "of old," "antiquity." Spatial: "east."
- Trap: English "first" and "beginning" between them absorb *reishis*, *rishon*
  and *techilah*. Where two of them stand in one pasuk (Yeshayahu 1:26,
  *rishonah* and *techilah* in consecutive clauses), you must split them, or the
  Vilna Gaon's whole reading disappears: "as at first" for the one and "as at the
  very outset" for the other.
- Second trap: ראש is head, chief, tally, poison, and a personal name (a son of
  Binyamin, Bereishis 46:21). Check which before reaching for "head."
- Where *reishis* is a tithe term (*terumah*, *reishis hagez*, *challah*), it is
  the portion itself, "the first of," not "the beginning."
