# Public: b'rabim / b'tzibbur / parhessya / pirsum / b'pumbi

## Bottom line

*B'rabim* is **in front of many**, the plain word for a public setting.
*Tzibbur* is the **community** as a body that can own and appoint, so *b'tzibbur*
is "with the congregation," not merely "in public." *Parhessya* is **openly**,
in the eyes of the community, and its foil is *b'tzina*, in private. *Pirsum*
is the act, **publicizing**, and *b'pumbi* is a public **spectacle**, an affair
made deliberately conspicuous. What must not be flattened is the *parhessya* /
*tzina* pair: wherever a sugya sets them against each other, the point is who
sees, not how many.

## Distinctions

### rabim (רבים)
The Mishnah's word for the public in the plainest sense. One who shames another
*b'rabim* forfeits his portion in the World to Come (Avos 3:11). Elsewhere in
the Mishnah *rabim* is what belongs to the public at large, standing against
*yachid*, the individual owner (Peah 2:1, Kilayim 4:7, Bechoros 1:1, Eruvin 5:6,
Megillah 3:1, Parah 12:4), most familiarly in *reshus harabim*. In Tanach the
word appears some two hundred times in its plain sense of "many," and the step
to "public" is short: "I will thank Hashem greatly with my mouth, and in the
midst of the *rabim* I will praise Him" (Tehillim 109:30). The Mishnah's *yadua
l'rabim*, a vow known to the masses (Gittin 4:7), is the same usage.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Chesheik Shlomo*) traces *rabim* to the biliteral
ב-ר, which he defines as quantitative increase: *rav*/*harbeh* (many), *arbeh*
(locusts, which swarm in multitudes), *arubah* (a skylight, letting in much
light), *rahav* (haughtiness, more self-regard than is fitting), and *ribo* (a
myriad). Rabbi Aharon Marcus adds *riv* (quarrel), from which the Waters of
Merivah take their name, as the multiplying of conflicting claims.

### tzibbur (ציבור)
A mass of individuals treated as one body. In the Mishnah the *tzibbur* is the
public as a legal entity that owns property and appoints its servants: the
korbanos of the *tzibbur*, paid for by Klal Yisrael as a whole (Pesachim 6:5,
7:4, Shekalim 4:1, Yoma 2:7, Sukkah 5:7, Zevachim 5:5, Menachos 2:2, Temurah
1:6, Kerisus 1:6), and the *shliach tzibbur* who leads the tefillos and
discharges the many of their obligation (Berachos 5:5, Rosh Hashanah 4:9).

The word itself is not in Tanach, but its root is: צ-ב-ר occurs seven times
(Bereishis 41:35, 41:49, Shemos 8:10, Chavakuk 1:10, Zecharyah 9:3, Tehillim
39:7, Iyov 27:16), always of gathering or heaping up a collection. A *tzibbur*
is an amassing of people.

### parhessya (פרהסיא) and pirsum (פרסום)
Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer Tishbi*): the two mean the same thing, but
*parhessya* is the noun, publicity, while *pirsum* names the act of publicizing.
Neither appears in Tanach or in the Mishnah; both are common in the Bavli, while
*parhessya* appears in only one teaching in the Yerushalmi (Peah 1:1, Sanhedrin
10:1). *Parhessya* is a loanword from the Greek *parrhesia*, open or frank
speech.

What the rabbinic word stresses is not free speech but visibility to the
community, and it works for good deeds and bad alike. The Gemara weighs a sin
committed in private against one committed *b'farhessya* (Sanhedrin 74a-75a,
Avodah Zarah 27b), the public sin being graver because it sets an example for
others, which is why one must give up his life rather than transgress publicly.
The converse: because Yehudah sanctified Hashem's name *b'farhessya*, admitting
his fault in the episode with Tamar, his name carries all four letters of the
Shem HaMeforash, while Yosef, who sanctified it *b'tzina* in refusing the wife
of Potiphar, has only three (Sotah 10b, 36b). *Tzina*, a cognate of *tzenius*,
is the standing foil to *parhessya* throughout Shas (Eruvin 69a, Sukkah 49b,
Beitzah 16a, Taanis 16b, Kesubos 103b, Gittin 38a, Bava Metzia 46b, Bava Basra
42a, Sanhedrin 26b, Avodah Zarah 36b, 54a, Zevachim 88b, Erchin 16a-16b).

Rabbi David Golumb (*HaTorah VeHaTalmud* vol. 1 p. 39) reads the *has* element
of *parhessya* as the biblical *has* (Bamidbar 13:30, Shoftim 3:19, Amos 6:10,
Chavakuk 2:20, Tzefaniah 1:7, Zecharyah 2:17), the sound of hushing a crowd,
since one who would announce something to the masses must first quiet them.
Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 80:14) derives *pirsum* from פ-ר-ס with a mem appended.

### pumbi (פומבי)
Also absent from Tanach and Mishnah, and rare in Shas: twice in the Bavli and
twice in the Yerushalmi. Avodah Zarah 54b, as *Sefer HaAruch* reads it, speaks
of wicked men who sin in public affairs. Bava Basra 145b, on *shushbinin*: if
the wedding one man paid toward was done *b'pumbi*, his fellow owes him only if
his own wedding is likewise a public affair, and not if he chooses a *tzanua*
one. The Yerushalmi (Yoma 4:1) explains that the lots on Yom Kippur could have
been cast from a plain basket, and a special box was used to make the drawing
more of a public affair; likewise (Yerushalmi Shekalim 1:1, on Mishnah Shekalim
3:1) the terumas halishkah was taken three times a year rather than once for the
same reason. Midrash Tanchuma (Ki Sisa §31, Naso §17) draws the lesson the other
way: the first luchos were given with great public spectacle, *b'pumbi*, and
were broken, while the second, given in a humbler way, lasted.

Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Meturgaman*) writes that *pumbi*, which appears twice
in Targum (Tehillim 68:7, Iyov 5:23), means the same as *pirsum*, and in *Sefer
Tishbi* he identifies it with the Latin *pompa*. That is its origin: the Greek
*pompe*, a ceremonial public procession, whence also the English *pomp*. So in
sense it overlaps *pirsum*, but in origin it is a different word from a
different quarter, and it keeps the flavor of display.

## Rendering guidance

- *B'rabim*: **in public**, or "before many people." Keep it for Avos 3:11 and
  for anything about being seen by a crowd.
- *Tzibbur*: **the community**, or **the congregation** in a tefillah context.
  *Korban tzibbur* is a "communal offering," never "a public offering." *Shliach
  tzibbur* stays transliterated, or "the one who leads the congregation."
- *B'farhessya*: **openly**, or "in public view." *B'tzina*: **in private**.
  Render them as a matched pair whenever they appear together, since the sugya
  is built on the contrast.
- *Pirsum*: **publicizing**, **publicity**. *Pirsumei nisa* stays transliterated
  with a gloss, "making the miracle known."
- *B'pumbi*: **with public fanfare**, or "as a public spectacle." Not a plain
  "in public," which loses the display, and loses the point of the Tanchuma.
- Trap: English "in public" will absorb *b'rabim*, *b'farhessya*, and *b'pumbi*
  without a trace. Where two of them stand in one passage, or where a source is
  comparing the terms, assign "before many," "openly," and "with fanfare" and
  hold the assignment. And do not let "the public" absorb *tzibbur*, which is a
  body with standing, not an audience.
