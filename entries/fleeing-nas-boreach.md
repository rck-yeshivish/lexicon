# Fleeing: nas / boreach

## Bottom line

*Nas* is **fleeing** from a danger already upon a man, openly and in terror.
*Boreach* is **running away** from a feared future threat, with nobody actually
in pursuit, and usually done quietly so as not to be seen. Sforno and Malbim
agree on that core. *Boreach* is the one that carries stealth and a threat not
yet arrived. Where each is headed is disputed: Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer
has *nas* seeking refuge at a destination and *boreach* attending only to the
place fled from, while Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim holds the reverse, *nas* the
start of flight with nowhere prepared and *brichah* its end, the place prepared
in advance.

| רץ | ratz | run | running toward, where *nas* is running from (Malbim) |
| בורח | boreach | run away | running quietly from a future threat, nobody chasing (Sforno, Malbim); only away (Wertheimer), or toward a place prepared (Pappenheim) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Where each is headed.**

- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: *nas* seeks refuge at a destination; *boreach*
  attends only to the place fled from.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. נס): the reverse. *Nisah* is
  the start of flight, leaving the danger with no place prepared; *brichah* is
  its end, the place one prepared to flee to. Every *brichah* is a *nisah*, but
  not every *nisah* a *brichah*.
- *Sefer HaChochmah*: *nas* is to somewhere near, *boreach* to somewhere far.

## Distinctions

### nas (נס)
- Sforno (to Bereishis 31:21 and Yonah 1:3): *nas* is running from an active
  threat, or from someone actually chasing.
- Malbim: the word applies where the danger is immediate or was very close at
  hand, and the fleeing is plainly visible, obvious terror. Malbim adds that
  *nas* can also be used of fleeing something without intelligence, extreme
  weather for instance, since one need only put distance between himself and it.

- Malbim distinguishes *nas* from *ratz* as well: both are running, but *ratz* is
  running toward and *nas* is running from.
- Rabbi Naftali Hertz Treves (*Sefer HaGur*), however, points to at least two
  places where a cognate of *nas* means running toward rather than away (Shemos
  14:27, Yirmiyahu 16:19).
- Rabbi Klein finds that this sheds light on Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer's
  reading: *boreach* attends to the place a man is running *from*, with no
  interest in where he is headed, while *nas* attends to his seeking refuge at a
  particular destination, and may on that account be related to *nasa* with an
  ayin, "to travel."
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. נס): *nisah* is the start of
  flight, leaving the place of danger; *brichah* is its end, the place one
  prepared to flee to. Every *brichah* is a *nisah*, but not every *nisah* is a
  *brichah*, since danger that comes suddenly leaves one only to get away,
  anywhere. So one never *barach* from battle but *nas* (Bereishis 14:10;
  Devarim 28:7, 32:30; Bamidbar 10:35). That is why *nisah* was borrowed from
  *nisayon*: as a trial begins in doubt, so flight begins without knowing how it
  will end, and one may fall into worse danger, as the kings of Sedom and Amorah
  fled into the tar pits, and *ka'asher yanus ish mipnei ha'ari ufega'o hadov*
  (Amos 5:19).

*Sefer HaChochmah*, ascribed to Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms, has a different
line altogether: *nisah* is running away to somewhere near, *bericha* to
somewhere far.

### boreach (בורח)
- Sforno: running away out of fear of a future threat, with nobody chasing.
- Malbim: the danger is more abstract, less tangible, not yet immediate. Two
  further marks in Malbim. First, one who is *boreach* runs clandestinely, so
  that even a bystander would not necessarily see that he is on the run, where
  one who is *nas* is very obviously fleeing in terror; Rabbi Klein's
  illustration is the difference between a man running from a poisonous snake
  and an informant hiding in witness protection. Second, one who is *boreach* is
  running from an intelligent being, whether a man, an animal, or Hashem. Malbim
  ties the two together: fleeing something that can think sometimes requires
  going undercover, while fleeing a mindless danger requires only distance.

### The Aramaic
Aramaic draws none of these distinctions. The root ע-ר-ק is its one word for
running away, and it appears once in Tanach, at Iyov 30:3. Most of the Targumim
generally render both *nas* and *boreach* with *arak*, though Onkelos often
renders *boreach* as *azil*, simply "went."

## Rendering guidance

- נס: "flee," or "fled" plainly. Bamidbar 10:35, *veyanusu mesanecha mipanecha*:
  "and let those who hate You flee before You." The pursuit is present, so the
  strong verb is right.
- ברח: "run away," or "escape." Yaakov leaving Lavan (Bereishis 31:21) and Yonah
  from before Hashem (Yonah 1:3) are both *boreach*, and both are quiet
  departures ahead of a trouble not yet arrived.
- Trap: English "flee" absorbs both. Where a passage or a *meforash* is turning
  on the difference, split them: "flee" for *nas*, "slip away" or "run off" for
  *boreach*, which keeps Malbim's stealth.
- Trap: do not write "fled from an unseen pursuer" for *boreach*. Sforno's point
  is that there is no pursuer at all yet, only the fear of one.
- Trap: where *nas* means running toward (Shemos 14:27), "flee" is wrong. Render
  it "ran," and note the exception rather than forcing Malbim's rule onto the
  pasuk.
