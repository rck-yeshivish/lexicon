# Sickle: chermesh / magal

## Bottom line

Both mean **sickle**, and the weight of the sources treats them as one tool
under two names: *chermesh* is the Hebrew word, *magal* the Aramaic one taken
into Hebrew. That is why the Torah says *meihachel chermesh bakamah* (Devarim
16:9) while the Mishnah's ceremony of cutting the Omer asks the crowd twice
*magal zeh?* (Menachos 10:3). Do not translate one "sickle" and the other
"scythe"; the sources that distinguish them do so homiletically, not by tool.

## Distinctions

### chermesh (חרמש)
Twice in Tanach, both in Devarim: the start of the Omer count (16:9) and the
prohibition on a hired worker taking the owner's produce with a *chermesh*
(23:26). It never appears in the Mishnah, and only once in Tosefta (Bava Metzia
2:14).

Roots proposed:

- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 25:6): Hebrew words with an apparent four-letter root
  ending in shin are really triliterals with a shin added. Here the root is
  ח-ר-ם, *cherem*, destroying or banning, and the added shin makes it the
  instrument of cutting. 
- Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche: a merger of two biliterals, ח-ר (hole) and מ-ש
  (move), since the tool cuts the grain and lets it be moved from where it grew.
- Rabbi Moshe David Valle: a portmanteau of *cherem* and *aish*, placing
  *chermesh* on the side of din, with *magal* on the side of rachamim.
  Alternatively he reads it as a notarikon for *chafetz retzono marbeh
  sheilaso*, the more a man chases what he wants the needier he becomes.
- Rabbi Chaim Vital reads Devarim 16:9 as speaking of a man's lifespan: from
  twenty, when he becomes liable for his sins, fifty years run to seventy, the
  archetypal lifespan. His support is that *chermesh* is 548 in gematria, the
  same as *umalach hamaves*, and he holds that the Torah chose *chermesh* over
  *magal* precisely to bring death and punishment into the pasuk.

### magal (מגל)
Twice in Tanach, both of the harvest tool (Yirmiyahu 50:16, Yoel 4:13), and
repeatedly in Mishnah (Sheviis 5:6, Menachos 10:3, Keilim 13:1 and 15:4).

That it is the Aramaic form is the plain evidence: wherever *chermesh* appears,
the Targumim render it *magla*, and the *Sifrei* (to Devarim 23:26) defines
*chermesh* as *magal* outright. Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) notes the Arabic
*almunajil*, sickle, and the Rambam uses the same Arabic word to define *magal*
in his commentary to Peah 4:4 and Sheviis 4:6; on that showing the form should
carry a nun, *mangal*, dropped as often happens. The Rambam to Sheviis 5:6
glosses *magal yad* as a small *chermesh*, which says as plainly as possible
that the two words name one thing. Where Mishnaic Hebrew needed to distinguish
sizes it did so by a modifier, *magal yad* for the hand sickle against *magal
kotzer* for the larger tool, not by switching to *chermesh*.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the root is ג-ל, round, whence *gal* (a heap of stones
laid in a ring), *galgal* (wheel) and *megillah* (a rolled scroll); the *magal*
belongs there for the near-circle of its curved blade. He traces *galus* and
*geulah* to the same biliteral. The column adds its own suggestion, that the
cycle of galus and geulah is what ties them to ג-ל, and that the Rabbis may have
preferred *magal* over *chermesh* for the Omer ceremony because the word carries
the Pesach theme of that passage from galus to geulah. That last is offered as a
suggestion, not as a source.

## Rendering guidance

- Devarim 16:9: "from when the sickle is first put to the standing grain."
  Devarim 23:26: "you shall not raise a sickle against your fellow's standing
  grain." One English word for both, since the Sifrei and the Targumim treat
  them as one.
- Menachos 10:3: keep *magal* transliterated inside the ceremony, since the
  crowd is being asked to identify the very implement by name and the ritual
  reads flat as "Is this a sickle? Yes." A gloss on first appearance is enough.
- Trap: English "sickle" and "scythe" invite a distinction the Hebrew does not
  make. If a passage sets *chermesh* opposite *magal* it is almost certainly
  drawing a homiletic contrast (Rabbi Valle's din against rachamim, Rabbi Chaim
  Vital's malach hamaves), not naming two tools; render both "sickle" and carry
  the contrast in the transliteration.
- *Magal yad* is "hand sickle," *magal kotzer* the larger reaping tool; use the
  modifiers, not two different nouns.
