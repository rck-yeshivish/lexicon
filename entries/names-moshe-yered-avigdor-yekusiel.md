# Names of Moshe: Moshe / Yered / Avigdor / Chever / Avi-Socho / Yekusiel / Avi-Zanoach

## Bottom line

These are not synonyms but one man's ten names, and every one of them stays a
transliterated **name**, never an English gloss. The six of Divrei HaYamim I 4:18
(Yered, Avigdor, Chever, Avi-Socho, Yekusiel, Avi-Zanoach) are read as Moshe by
*Vayikra Rabbah* 1:3, each expounded from a Hebrew root; four more (Toviah,
Shemayah, Ben Nesanel, Levi) follow. The one thing not to get wrong: the Midrash
concludes that of all ten, the Torah and Hashem Himself call him only **Moshe**,
the name Basyah gave him, and that conclusion is the point of the whole passage.
Render the derashos by keeping the name in Hebrew and putting the root's meaning
in a gloss.

## Where the authorities disagree

**How many derashos each name carries.** *Vayikra Rabbah* 1:3 gives Yered three
readings: he brought the Torah down, he brought the Shechinah down, or, from
*lirdos*, he ruled over Israel as its leader (see Zevachim 102a). Megillah 13a
adds a fourth, that the mahn descended in his days, and Taanis 9a specifies in
his merit. Chever likewise has two: *chibbur*, that he joined Israel to Hashem,
which the Vilna Gaon (to Divrei HaYamim I 4:18) ties to his erecting the
Mishkan; or ע-ב-ר, on the interchange of ches and ayin, that he made punishment
pass over, most plausibly his pleading after the Eigel. Avi-Socho has two: from
*socheh*, a gazer, hence prophecy, so he is the father of the nevi'im; and
Megillah 13a, that he was a *succah* over Israel by the abundance of his merits
(see *Targum Rav Yosef* there), which the Vilna Gaon applies to his shielding
them after Taveirah (Bamidbar 11:2).

**Who gave which name.** The Midrash's own conclusion implies only Moshe came
from Basyah. The apocryphal *Divrei HaYamim Shel Moshe* assigns each name to a
different giver: Basyah, Moshe; Amram, Chever, since for this son's sake he
remarried the wife he had divorced; Yocheved, Yekusiel; Miriam, Yered, since she
went down to the river to see his fate; Aharon, Avi-Zanoach, since his father had
forsaken his mother; Kehas, Avigdor, since the decree against the boys was
repealed at his birth; the wet-nurse, Socho (*Zayis Raanan* notes she was hired
alongside Yocheved's own nursing); and Israel, Shemayah ben Nesanel. *Sefer
HaYashar* and *Yalkut Shimoni* to Shemos 166 carry the same tradition. Against
all of it, Ibn Ezra (to Shemos 2:22 and 4:20) rejects *Divrei HaYamim Shel Moshe*
outright as a source, since it comes neither from the nevi'im nor from Chazal.
The Bottom line follows Ibn Ezra and treats that assignment as unreliable.

**Whether Yehonasan is one of the names.** Rabbi Moshe Teitelbaum, on the
strength of *Yetziv Pisgam* (read on the second day of Shavuos) and its "humble
man Yehonasan," claims it as one of Moshe's ten. His grandson-in-law Rabbi
Chanoch Henoch Teitelbaum of Sassov answers that no rabbinic source says so. The
Bottom line follows the Sassover: it is not one of the ten. Note, though, that
Yehonasan and Nesanel are built the same way, *nasan* plus a Divine element,
*Yeho*- as a prefix in the one and *-el* as a suffix in the other, and that Bava
Basra 109b-110a identifies the idolatrous priest Yehonasan ben Gershom ben
Menasheh (Shoftim 18:30, where the nun of Menasheh is suspended) as Moshe's own
grandson, since Menasheh without the nun is Moshe.

**Whether Tamar was his original name.** Rabbi Gedaliah Ibn Yachya (*Shalsheles
HaKabbalah*) cites a tradition that Moshe's original name was Tamar or Tamur,
meaning "raising" in Egyptian. The column knows of no other source for it, and
neither does this entry.

## Distinctions

### The four additional names
*Vayikra Rabbah* adds four beyond the six of Divrei HaYamim I 4:18, bringing the
count to ten. **Toviah**, because at his birth his mother "saw him, that he was
good" (*tov*, Shemos 2:2). Others in Tanach bear it: the head of a family that
returned with Zerubavel (Ezra 2:60, Nechemiah 7:62), a kohen in the early Second
Beis HaMikdash period (Zechariah 6:10, 6:14), and the Ammonite who opposed
Nechemiah (Nechemiah 2:10-19, 13:4-7). **Shemayah** (Divrei HaYamim I 24:6),
because Hashem heard (*shama*) his tefillos; the man of that name in Divrei
HaYamim is read as Moshe because he had a part in establishing the twenty-four
mishmaros, which go back to Moshe. Others in Tanach carry it too: a true navi in
Yeravam's day (Melachim II 12:22 region, Divrei HaYamim II 12:5-15) and a false
one in Yirmiyahu's (Yirmiyahu 29:31-32). **Ben Nesanel** (Divrei HaYamim I 24:6),
because he gave (*nasan*) the Torah over from Hashem to Israel. **Levi**, because
he was Levi's grandson.

### Heiman
Bava Basra 15a identifies Heiman of Tehillim as Moshe. The name means the
trustworthy one, which is likely the ground of the Zohar's title for him, *Raya
Mehemna*, the trustworthy shepherd.

### The point of the list
*Vayikra Rabbah* 1:3 and *Shemos Rabbah* 1:26 close by noting that of all ten,
the Torah uses only Moshe, the name Basyah gave, as her reward for the kindness
of taking him in; even Hashem calls him by it. Rabbi Chaim Shmuelevitz reads each
name as a different facet of his greatness, and Moshe in particular as the trait
he absorbed from Basyah, giving up one's life to do chesed for another, which is
why he later asked to be erased from Hashem's book to save Israel after the
Eigel (Shemos 32:32).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: an English rendering absorbs the name and kills the derashah. Do not
  write "the Descender" for Yered or "Father of the Fence" for Avigdor. Keep
  Yered, Avigdor, Chever, and gloss: "Yered, from *yarad*, to descend."
- Where the Midrash gives two or three readings of one name, list them; choosing
  one silently is a translation of the Midrash, not of the pasuk.
- Names are roman and unitalicized. The words expounded from them (*geder*,
  *chibbur*, *socheh*, *tikvah*) are terms and take italics or transliteration
  markup.
- Divrei HaYamim I 4:18 read literally names four separate men. Chazal teach
  that Divrei HaYamim is written for derashah (*Vayikra Rabbah* 1:3), and Radak
  reads the pasuk's plain sense as those four. Do not present the Midrash as the
  pshat.
- The Egyptian derivation of the name Moshe is a separate question and lies
  outside this guide.
