# Cake: ugah / chararah / kikar / gluskos

## Bottom line

*Ugah* is not "cake" in the modern sense: its core is **round**, and as a food it is a
**flat round loaf**, which is why the Torah calls the matzos of Yetzias Mitzrayim *ugos
matzos* (Shemos 12:39). *Chararah* is Rashi's and the Mechilta's gloss on it. *Kikar* is a **loaf** with
body, *challah* a **round loaf**, *gluska* the Rabbinic **fine roll**.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether matzah must be round.** The column frames this as one small part of the
long-running controversy over machine-made matzos, which are typically square, some
having argued that because matzah is tied to *ugah* in the pasuk it must, or at least
should, be round. Rabbi Yehuda Aszad (*Yehudah Ya'aleh*, Orach Chaim
vol. 1 §157): the practice in his day was round matzos, since the Torah calls them
*ugos*, and round is faster to prepare, which suited a people rushed out of Mitzrayim.
He adds that the circle hints at the cycle of poverty (*lechem oni*, the poor man's
bread) and at the circle of life, our mourning on Pesach night over the loss of the
Beis HaMikdash; and he conjectures
that the Egyptians required many-sided loaves in homage to their many gods, so the Jews
baked round ones for the One God who has no beginning and no end. Against him, Rabbi
Shimon Sofer of Erloi denies any hidden reason: gentile loaves are mostly round too, and
from the Torah's own side the square is if anything favored, since tefillin must be
square, and so are the mizbeach, the choshen and the lechem hapanim, which Rabbi
Chizkiyah Medini of Chevron cites as precedent that rectangular matzos are fit (see also
*HaDrash V'HaIyun*, Tzav §29).

**How ugah differs from kikar.** The column consulted two scholars, Dr. Tamar Katzir
of the Academy for the Hebrew Language and Rabbi Shaul Goldman. It seems, on their
reading, that both
roots mean round and the two are near-synonyms in practice; but the two independently
suggested that *kikar* implies
something spherical, a three-dimensional loaf, while *ugah* implies a flat,
two-dimensional round. Which fits Choni's circle, a mere picture drawn in the ground,
and fits matzah, flat enough to look two-dimensional. HALOT implies the same, defining
*ugah* "a round flat loaf of bread" and *kikar* "a disk-shaped round loaf." How
*challah* divides from *ugah* the column leaves open, saying it is not readily clear.

**What gluskaos are.** Shabbos 30b and Kesubos 111b foretell that Eretz Yisrael will
yield ready-made *gluskaos* rather than raw stalks, and *Bereishis Rabbah* §88:2 has
Pharaoh's baker jailed over a pebble in his *gluskin*. But Rabbi Yitzchak of Pira, a
talmid of the Rashba (to Bereishis 3:21), implies they are a kind of clothing.

## Distinctions

### ugah (עוגה)
Seven occurrences in Tanach: Bereishis 18:6; Shemos 12:39; Bamidbar 11:8; Melachim I
17:13, 19:6; Yechezkel 4:12; Hoshea 7:8. Rashi and the *Mechilta* (to Shemos 12:39)
define it as a *chararah*, as does Targum (to Melachim I 17:13). Outside baking the word
is plainly round: an *ugah shel mayim* into which blood may pool (Chullin 2:9), which
Rashi (41a) calls a round water pit; the *ugiyos* one may not make around a grapevine on
Chol HaMoed (Moed Katan 1:1), small round water pits; and Choni HaMe'agel's *ug ugah*,
the circle he drew and would not leave until rain fell (Taanis 23a). Philologists such
as Shadal (to
Bereishis 18:6) and Dr. Alexander Kohut (*Aruch HaShaleim*) relate *ugah* to *chugah*,
circle or circuit; others, like Rabbi Aharon Marcus, connect it to *igul*.

*Chararah* itself is in the Mishnah a baked good of some sort (*Shabbos* 1:10, *Bava
Kamma* 2:3), and at *Peah* 5:8 a way of stacking produce which the Yerushalmi (there)
explains as round stacks. On that basis the column says it is safe to assume that
*chararah* and *ugah* in the sense of a baked good both name something round in shape.

### ugah and matzah
Shemos 12:39, said in the Haggadah: they baked the dough they brought out of Mitzrayim
as *ugos matzos*, because they were driven out. The link recurs in the midrashim:
*Bereishis Rabbah* §42:8 has Avraham occupied with "the mitzvah of *ugos*" when the
refugee came about Lot, which is why he was called Og; §48:12 reads Avraham's "knead and
make *ugos*" (Bereishis 18:6) as Erev Pesach, so that he was asking for matzos. Rabbi
Shalom Shabazi (*Chemdas Yamim* there): those *ugos* are never reported served because
Og ate them, a second account of the name.

### kikar (ככר), challah (חלה) and the Rabbinic words
*Kikar* appears five times, always as *kikar lechem* (Shemos 29:23; Shmuel I 2:36;
Yirmiyahu 37:21; Mishlei 6:26; Divrei HaYamim I 16:3). *Challah* appears alone (Vayikra
7:12, 24:5; Bamidbar 6:15, 15:20), as *challas lechem*
(Shemos 29:23; Vayikra 7:13, 8:26; Shmuel II 6:19), and as *challas matzah*
(Shemos 29:2; Vayikra 2:4, 7:12; Bamidbar 6:19).
Both words likewise imply a round shape (Ibn Ezra to Vayikra 2:4); for these against
*lechem* and *pas* see
[bread](bread-lechem-kikar-challah-pat.html). Pesachim 6b: the Rabbanan required destroying
chametz rather than nullification alone, lest a man find a good *gluska* on Pesach and
be tempted; Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Mussaf HeAruch*) derives the word from the Greek
*chollix*/*hollix* and
defines it in Hebrew as "an *ugah*," "a small round bread," and Rabbi Ernest Klein,
seemingly following him, traces it to the Greek *kollix*, a roll of bread. The column
offers as an alternative that Rabbinic *gluskos* may instead be related to the Ancient
Greek *glukus*, at first sweet new wine and then anything sweet, the English *glucose*
being coined from it only in the 1800s.

The column closes with parallels in other languages. English *cake* comes from the
Norse *kaka*, which linguists like Julius Pokorny take back to the Proto-Indo-European
root *gag*/*gog*, "round," on the interchange of g and k, which is the same pairing of
"baked good" with "round" that *ugah* shows; the diminutive *cookie* has its parallel in
Modern Hebrew *ugiyah*, the diminutive of *ugah*. German *kuchen* comes from the same
PIE root, and with the diminutive *-el* gives Yiddish *kichel*, a little cake; French
*quiche* is a cognate. Avraham Even-Shoshan, in his dictionary of Modern Hebrew, derives
*kugel* either from the Hebrew *k'igul*, "like a circle," or from the German *kugel*,
sphere or ball. The first is often dismissed as folk etymology, but Rabbi Shaul Goldman
points to a source for it: Rabbi Yechiel Michel Epstein, citing the custom of using
Pesach flour for the kugel of the Shabbos before Pesach, spells the word as though it
were *k'igul* (*Aruch HaShulchan*, Orach Chaim 429:8). In Modern Hebrew a kugel is a
*pashtida*, a word already used by Rashi (Pesachim 74b) and Tosafos (Eruvin 68a,
Pesachim 74b and 76b, Avodah Zarah 66b, Chullin 64a), derived from the Latin *pasta*,
which comes in turn from the Greek *paste*.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "cake" absorbs *ugah* and *gluska* alike and sweetens both; *ugos matzos* is
  "flat cakes of matzah," never "matzah cakes."
- Bereishis 18:6 is "knead and make cakes" in the older English versions; where the
  midrashic reading is in play, say "flat cakes" so the matzah link survives.
- Where *ugah* is not food (Chullin 41a, Moed Katan 1:1, Taanis 23a), render by the
  shape: "a round pit," "he drew a circle."
- *Kikar lechem* is "a loaf of bread"; using "loaf" for *ugah* in the same passage
  collapses the flat-against-round distinction. *Gluska* is "a fine roll."
