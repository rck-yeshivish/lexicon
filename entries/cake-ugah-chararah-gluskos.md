# Cake: ugah / chararah / kikar / gluskos

## Bottom line

*Ugah* is not "cake" in the modern sense: its core is **round**, and as a food it is a
**flat round loaf**, which is why the Torah calls the matzos of Yetzias Mitzrayim *ugos
matzos* (Shemos 12:39). *Chararah* is Rashi's gloss on it. *Kikar* is a **loaf** with
body, *challah* a **round loaf**, *gluska* the Rabbinic **fine roll**. The trap is
English "cake," which sweetens what *ugah* does not: say "flat cake" or "round loaf."

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether matzah must be round.** Rabbi Yehuda Aszad (*Yehudah Ya'aleh*, Orach Chaim
vol. 1 §157): the practice in his day was round matzos, since the Torah calls them
*ugos*, and round is faster to prepare, which suited a people rushed out of Mitzrayim.
He adds that the circle hints at the cycle of poverty (*lechem oni*), and conjectures
that the Egyptians required many-sided loaves in homage to their many gods, so the Jews
baked round ones for the One God who has no beginning and no end. Against him, Rabbi
Shimon Sofer of Erloi denies any hidden reason: gentile loaves are mostly round too, and
from the Torah's own side the square is if anything favored, since tefillin must be
square, and so are the mizbeach, the choshen and the lechem hapanim, which Rabbi
Chizkiyah Medini of Chevron cites as precedent that rectangular matzos are fit (see also
*HaDrash V'HaIyun*, Tzav §29).

**How ugah differs from kikar.** Rabbi Shaul Goldman, consulted for the column: both
roots mean round and the two are near-synonyms in practice, but *kikar* suggests
something spherical, a three-dimensional loaf, while *ugah* suggests a flat,
two-dimensional round. How *challah* divides from *ugah* he leaves open.

**What gluskaos are.** Shabbos 30b and Kesubos 111b foretell that Eretz Yisrael will
yield ready-made *gluskaos* rather than raw stalks, and *Bereishis Rabbah* §88:2 has
Pharaoh's baker jailed over a pebble in his *gluskin*. But Rabbi Yitzchak of Pira, a
talmid of the Rashba (to Bereishis 3:21), implies they are a kind of clothing.

## Distinctions

### ugah (עוגה)
Seven occurrences in Tanach: Bereishis 18:6; Shemos 12:39; Bamidbar 11:8; Melachim I
17:13, 19:6; Yechezkel 4:12; Hoshea 7:8. Rashi and the *Mechilta* (to Shemos 12:39)
define it as a *chararah*, as does Targum (to Melachim I 17:13). Outside baking the word
is plainly round: an *ugah shel mayim* into which blood may pool (Chullin 2:9), which
Rashi (41a) calls a round water pit; the *ugiyos* one may not make around a grapevine on
Chol HaMoed (Moed Katan 1:1), small round water pits; and Choni HaMe'agel's *ug ugah*,
the circle he drew and would not leave until rain fell (Taanis 23a). Shadal (to
Bereishis 18:6) relates *ugah* to *chugah*, circuit; Rabbi Aharon Marcus to *igul*.

### ugah and matzah
Shemos 12:39, said in the Haggadah: they baked the dough they brought out of Mitzrayim
as *ugos matzos*, because they were driven out. The link recurs in the midrashim:
*Bereishis Rabbah* §42:8 has Avraham occupied with "the mitzvah of *ugos*" when the
refugee came about Lot, which is why he was called Og; §48:12 reads Avraham's "knead and
make *ugos*" (Bereishis 18:6) as Erev Pesach, so that he was asking for matzos. Rabbi
Shalom Shabazi (*Chemdas Yamim* there): those *ugos* are never reported served because
Og ate them, a second account of the name.

### kikar (ככר), challah (חלה) and the Rabbinic words
*Kikar* appears five times, always as *kikar lechem* (Shemos 29:23; Shmuel II 6:19;
Yirmiyahu 37:21; Mishlei 6:26; Divrei HaYamim I 16:3). *Challah* appears alone (Vayikra
7:12, 24:5), as *challas lechem* (Shemos 29:23; Vayikra 7:13), and as *challas matzah*
(Shemos 29:2; Vayikra 2:4); for these against *lechem* and *pas* see
`bread-lechem-kikar-challah-pat.md`. Pesachim 6b: the Rabbanan required destroying
chametz rather than nullification alone, lest a man find a good *gluska* on Pesach and
be tempted; Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Mussaf HeAruch*) traces the word to Greek and
defines it as "an *ugah*, a small round bread," and Rabbi Ernest Klein follows him.
Rabbi Yechiel Michel Epstein spells *kugel* as *k'igul*, "like a circle," for the custom
of Pesach flour in the kugel of the Shabbos before Pesach (*Aruch HaShulchan* 429:8).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "cake" absorbs *ugah* and *gluska* alike and sweetens both; *ugos matzos* is
  "flat cakes of matzah," never "matzah cakes."
- Bereishis 18:6 is "knead and make cakes" in the older English versions; where the
  midrashic reading is in play, say "flat cakes" so the matzah link survives.
- Where *ugah* is not food (Chullin 41a, Moed Katan 1:1, Taanis 23a), render by the
  shape: "a round pit," "he drew a circle."
- *Kikar lechem* is "a loaf of bread"; using "loaf" for *ugah* in the same passage
  collapses the flat-against-round distinction. *Gluska* is "a fine roll."
