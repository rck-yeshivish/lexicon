# Immediately: teikef / miyad / le'alter / otyom

## Bottom line

All four are Rabbinic Hebrew, none of them in Tanach, and all four mean
"immediately." *Teikef* is **immediately after**, one thing hard on the heels of
another (it also means physically pressed close together). *Miyad* is
**straightaway**, literally "from hand," which suggests the readiness of what is
already in your hand. *Le'alter* is **on the spot**, which some commentators
explain as a contraction of *al atar*. *Otyom* is a technical Mishnaic term; Rav
Hai Gaon, the *Aruch*, Rashi and Rashba all hold it is a Greek loanword, and the
Ancient Greek εὐθέως, *eutheos*, means "immediately."

## Distinctions

### teikef (תיכף)
Carries both senses at once: "closely pressed together" in space and "rapidly
succeeding each other" in time. Two objects set right beside one another and two
events falling right after one another take the same word. This is Rabbi
Klein's governing idea, which he says may help shed light on some of the set:
the joining of space and time, an idea the Maharal of Prague (1525-1609) had
already expressed long before the space-time continuum entered physics in the
last hundred years, is reflected in Hebrew, where some words (and even some
English ones) carry both a spatial and a temporal meaning; *olam* (the whole
world / forever) and *lifnei* and *acharei* (before and after, in place and in
time) work the same way.

### miyad (מיד)
Hyper-literally "from hand," which suggests a link between time and space. What
sits in a man's hand is the most readily transferred thing there is, passing
straight to the next hand without an interval; so too an event that follows the
one before it with nothing in between. Rabbi Klein adds a thought of his own: he
once thought the English "off-hand" might be related to *miyad*, so that "I
don't remember off-hand" would mean "I don't immediately remember."

### le'alter (לאלתר)
Some commentators, among them Rabbi Yishaya Pick-Berlin (1725-1799) and Rabbi
Yaakov Emden (1697-1776), explain it as a portmanteau of *al atar*, "on the
spot." Again a word for place doing duty for time. Some Jews have the custom of
greeting one another on the first night of Rosh Hashanah with *l'shanah tovah
tikaseiv v'seichaseim le'alter l'chaim tovim u'l'shalom*.

### otyom (אותיום)
Appears once in the Mishnah, Niddah 2:2, and clearly means "immediately
thereafter"; some argue the vocalization should really be *evteos*.

- Rabbi Emmanuel Chai Ricci (1688-1743), in his commentary to the Mishnah, takes
  it as an abbreviation of a Hebrew phrase spelling out the halachic definition
  of "immediately" in that sugya.
- Rabbi Betzalel Ronsberg (1762-1821) disagrees, pointing out that it does not
  square with the Gemara's conclusions about that definition, and prefers Rav
  Hai Gaon (in the commentary to Niddah ascribed to him), *Sefer HeAruch*, Rashi
  (Yoma 6a, Kerisos 17b) and Rashba (*Toras HaBayis* 7:3), who all write that
  *otyom* is actually a Greek word. Indeed, the Ancient Greek εὐθέως, *eutheos*,
  means "immediately."

## Rendering guidance

- The doubled *teikef umiyad* (as in *teikef umiyad l'geulah*) is not
  redundancy for its own sake. Render it as an intensifier, "at once and without
  delay," rather than "immediately and immediately."
- *Miyad* on its own: "straightaway" or "at once." Keep "immediately" in reserve
  as the neutral fallback so it does not absorb every member of the set.
- *Le'alter*: "on the spot" preserves the *al atar* sense that Pick-Berlin and
  Emden give; "forthwith" is the register-neutral alternative in a halachic text.
- *Otyom*: transliterate and gloss, since the word's very identity is disputed.
  Translating it flat as "immediately" hides the machlokes between Ricci's
  notarikon and the Geonic-Rishonic reading of a Greek loanword.
- Trap: English has one workhorse, "immediately," and it will swallow *teikef*,
  *miyad* and *le'alter* alike. Where a passage lists them, or derives something
  from which one the Mishnah chose, spread them across "immediately after," "at
  once," and "on the spot."
