# Pulling and drawing: moshe / moshech / shoev / shoef

## Bottom line

*Moshech* is the general verb: **pull, draw**, taking something from its place
toward oneself. *Moshe* is narrow and almost always in water: **draw out of the
water** (Rashbam). *Shoev* is **draw water**, in Tanach from a well; *shoef* is
its air counterpart, **inhale**, and in *sheifah*, **yearning** (Wertheimer).
English "draw" swallows all four; where the Hebrew is *moshe*, keep the water.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| משה | moshe | draw out of water | only from within water (Rashbam, Rabbi Chelouche) |
| משך | moshech | pull, draw | the general term; includes moshe (Rabbi Chelouche) |
| שאב | shoev | draw water | water, from a well (Wertheimer) |
| שאף | shoef | inhale, yearn | air, not water; hence sheifah, a settled longing (Wertheimer) |
| משי | meshi | silk | what is drawn from the silkworm (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**The root of moshe.** The triliteralists (Ibn Chayyuj, Ibn Janach, Radak) give
מ-ש-ה; Menachem Ibn Saruk gives the biliteral מ-ש. Rashi (Shemos 2:10) takes up
the question over *mishisihu* and rules with the triliteralists, rejecting
Menachem by name.

**Whether moshe and moshech share a root.** Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche brings
both back to one biliteral מ-ש, "move or remove from place," keeping the
difference semantic only. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim separates them: on his rule
that a mem prefixed to a two-letter root reverses it (ח-ק engrave against מחק
erase, ר-ד rule against מרד rebel), *moshe* rests on ש-ה (equalize, whence
*shaveh*, *shav*, *shaanan*) and *moshech* on ש-ך (settled in place, whence
*shochen*, *mashkon*, *shechem*). Each verb undoes its own root's stability.

**Which stage of drawing shoev names.** Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 24:65, Devarim
28:22): the filling of the bucket, and Targum Onkelos supports him, rendering
*shoev* as *mamlei*, "fills." Rabbi Yaakov Berger infers from Ramban (Bereishis
24:17) that the core act is the lowering of the bucket into the well.

**Whether shoev and shoef are one word.** Rav Hirsch links them through the
interchange of beis and pei. *Midrash Sechel Tov* (to Bereishis 24:11), the
earliest to notice the pairing, records the likeness and rejects it on
principle, accepting letter-interchange derashos for proper names only. Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer keeps the two apart in practice, *shoev* for water and
*shoef* for air; the Bottom line follows him, the usage being consistent.

## Distinctions

### moshe (משה)
Rare as a verb. *"Ki min hamayim mishisihu,"* "for I drew him from the water"
(Shemos 2:10); *"yamsheini mimayim rabbim,"* "He drew me out of many waters"
(Shmuel II 22:17, Tehillim 18:17). Rashbam (Shemos 2:10) fixes the sense:
drawing out from within water; Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche makes *moshech* the
hypernym and *moshe* the water-specific species. At Yeshayahu 63:11 Radak in
*Sefer HaShorashim* records that some read *moshe* there as this verb, while
Targum, Rashi, Mahari Kara, Ibn Ezra and Radak himself read it as the name. Rav
Hirsch pairs מ-ש-ה with מ-צ-ה (squeeze) through shin and tzadi: one extracts a
solid from liquid, the other liquid from a solid.

### moshech (משך), shoev (שאב) and shoef (שאף)
*Moshech* has about forty appearances: pull something toward oneself, extended
to stretching, dragging, and leading; at Shoftim 5:14 it is used for writing,
*mosh'chim b'shevet sofer*, since ink is pulled from the pen.
*Shoev*, root ש-א-ב, some twenty appearances, nearly all of drawing water from a
well; later Hebrew widened it to a wick drawing up oil (*Shabbos* 2:4) and a
magnet, *even shoeves* (Sotah 47a). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to ש-ב,
"return," so that *shoev* halts flowing water inside a vessel. *Shoef*, root
ש-א-ף, is inhaling (Yirmiyahu 2:24, 14:6; Yeshayahu 42:14; Tehillim 119:131), and
yields *sheifah*, a longing taken inside (Koheles 1:5; Iyov 7:2, 36:20).

## Rendering guidance

- Keep "draw out of the water" for *moshe*; bare "drew" loses the name's point.
- *Moshech* takes "pull" when a person or object is moved, "draw" when the sense
  is toward oneself, "prolong" when it is time or sound.
- Where *shoev* and *shoef* stand near each other, render "draw" and "inhale";
  never let "draw in" cover both.
- Trap: a passage with both *moshe* and *moshech* reads as repetition in English
  unless the water is marked in the one and left out of the other.
- On why the name is Moshe (active) and not Mashui (passive): Rabbi Shlomo Ibn
  Parchon raises the difficulty; *Peirush HaTur* in the name of Rabbi Yosef
  Kimchi answers that Pharaoh's daughter did not command the grammar, with
  providence besides, since he would draw Yisrael out of Mitzrayim. *Peirush
  HaRokeach*: out of the sea. *Moshav Zekanim*: she foresaw his drawing water
  from the rock. *Midrash HaGadol*: his own merits drew him out. Chizkuni and Rav
  Hirsch: the active form is a charge, that one saved from water grow up to save
  others. Render "Moshe" and let a note carry the readings.
