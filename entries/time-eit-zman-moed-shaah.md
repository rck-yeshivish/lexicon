# Time: eit / zman / moed / shaah

## Bottom line

*Zman* is **time** in general, the span or flow (Wertheimer: the most general
of the three; Rav Hirsch; Maharal). *Eit* is **a time**, a point or portion
within the span, often the natural season for something (Malbim, Rav Hirsch:
"rain in its *eit*"). *Moed* is **appointed time**, the preset point fixed by
man or by Hashem, hence the *moadim* (Malbim, Wertheimer). *Shaah* beside
*eit* is **hour**, the unit of the day against the unit of the year (Rama).
The thing not to get wrong: בכל עת ובכל שעה is not a redundancy to be
collapsed into "at all times"; it is two measures, "at every season and every
hour" (Rama, against those who dropped the second word).

## Distinctions

### zman (זמן)
Root ז-מ-ן. Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: kin to *mezuman*, "ready, prepared" (and
"invited"); Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: from *zamam*, "to plan ahead." Time is a
means of preparation for what is to come. Maharal (*Netzach Yisrael* 26):
*zman* is the flow of time as an existing element of creation, *eit* time as
an abstract concept. Malbim: *zman*, like *moed*, is a predetermined time set
by man. Wertheimer: *zman* is the most general of the three. Rav Hirsch:
*zman* is time in general. Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *zman* is the entire duration
of an appointed period. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur notes that *zman* is at home in
the post-exilic books and Koheles; the Targumim render Biblical *moed* as
*zman*, which is why one may treat *moed* as the Hebrew and *zman* as the
Aramaic of one idea.

### eit (עת)
Root ע-ת. HaBachur rejects those who call *eit* a plain synonym of *zman*,
and cites sages who drew a fine, philosophical line he declines to spell out;
Maharal's abstract-versus-existent line (above) is that kind of distinction.
Malbim: *eit* is a natural unit of time whose existence nature itself shows
(*geshamim b'itam*). Pappenheim: *eit* also carries designation and
preparation, as the *ish itti* of Vayikra 16:21 is the man designated for the
*sa'ir*; *atid*, "future," derives from *eit* and from a verb meaning "to
prepare." Others: *zman* is a span, *eit* a point within it. Wertheimer:
*eit* is more specific than *zman*, less than *moed*. Rav Hirsch: *eit* is a
portion of time, related to *eit* the spade that breaks soil into pieces.
Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *eit* is any given point within an appointed span; and he
rejects the Rama's narrowing of *eit* to the seasons, since Tanach uses *eit*
of other natural occasions as well.

### moed (מועד)
Root י-ע-ד, "appoint." Malbim: a predetermined time, set by man, or by Hashem
for the festivals. Wertheimer: the most specific of the three, a preset point
fixed like a meeting down to the hour. Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *moed* is the
beginning of an appointed time (its *zman* is the whole duration, its *eit*
any moment within). Emden adds that these are the technical differences; in
ordinary usage the three words interchange.

### shaah (שעה)
Root ש-ע-ה. The article treats *shaah* only through the Rama (Rabbi Moshe
Isserles): in the second berachah of Birkas HaMazon and the last of Shemoneh
Esrei, *bechol eit u'vechol shaah*, *eit* is the seasons of the year and
*shaah* the hours of the day, two different measures and not a doubling. Rabbi
Yaakov Emden accepts that they differ but denies that *eit* is limited to
seasons. So *shaah* here is the small, clock-bound unit: **hour**. The article
gives no ruling on *shaah* standing alone in the sense "a while, a moment,
temporarily" (*lefi shaah*, *shaah achas*); the guide has no source for that
yet, and a rendering of it is a judgment call to be labeled as such.

## Rendering guidance

- בכל עת ובכל שעה: "at every season and at every hour" (Rama), or "at every
  time and every hour." Keep two nouns; do not write "always."
- עת alone: "a time," "the time [for]," "season" (עת לכל חפץ, "a time for
  every purpose"); בעתו "in its season," "at its proper time."
- זמן alone: "time" (the span, the flow); זמן קבוע "a set time"; בזמן הזה "in
  this time / at this season" (the berachah: "at this season" is customary).
- מועד: "appointed time," "set time"; the festivals are "the *moadim*" or
  "the appointed festivals," never bare "holidays" in a sefer.
- שעה beside עת: "hour." Standalone שעה in the "moment" sense: unsettled here;
  see above.
- Trap: English "time" absorbs עת, זמן, and מועד, and English "moment" would
  absorb עת and שעה. Where two stand together (עת וזמן, זמן ומועד), use the
  Wertheimer ladder: general "time," then "a time," then "appointed time."
- When the passage turns on preparation (Rabbi Moshe Shapiro, Pappenheim),
  a gloss such as "time (*zman*, the time made ready)" may be earned; do not
  build the etymology into the running English.
