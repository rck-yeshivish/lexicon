# Time: eit / zman / moed / shaah

## Bottom line

*Zman* is **time** in general, the span or flow (Wertheimer: the most general
of the three; Rav Hirsch: time in general; Maharal: the flow of time as an
existing element of creation). *Eit* is **a time**, a point or portion within
the span (anonymous "others": a point; Rav Hirsch: a portion); for Malbim it is
a natural unit of time that nature itself shows, "rain in its *eit*." *Moed* is
**appointed time**, the most specific preset point (Wertheimer), set by man or
by Hashem, hence the *moadim* (Malbim). The scheme is not agreed: Malbim puts
*zman* with *moed* as time set by man, and Rabbi Yaakov Emden makes *moed* the
beginning of an appointed time, *zman* its whole duration and *eit* any point
within it, while conceding that in practical usage all these words may be used
interchangeably. *Shaah* beside *eit* is **hour**, the hours of the day against
the seasons of the year (Rama). For the Rama, בכל עת ובכל שעה is not a redundancy; he rejects those who
say only *bechol eit* and reads two measures, "at every season and every hour,"
though Rabbi Yaakov Emden takes issue with his limiting *eit* to the seasons.

## Distinctions

### zman (זמן)
Root ז-מ-ן.
- Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: related to *mizuman*, "ready, prepared" (and used
  colloquially of someone "invited"); Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the root of
  *zman* is *zamem*, to "plot" or "think ahead." Rabbi Klein draws the upshot:
  in essence time is a means of preparation, for the future. (In Modern Hebrew
  cash is *mizuman* because, unlike checks or credit cards, it makes for a fast
  and easy transaction and can readily be used.)
- Maharal (*Netzach Yisrael* 26): *zman* is the flow of time as an existing
  element of creation, *eit* time as an abstract concept.
- Malbim: *zman*, like *moed*, is a predetermined time set by man. Rabbi Klein
  observes that this does not seem to account for the difference between *moed*
  and *zman*, and suggests that one could argue that *moed* is Hebrew and *zman*
  Aramaic: the Targumim commonly render Biblical *moed* as *zman*, although
  HaBachur points out that *zman* itself appears in some of the post-Exilic
  books of the Bible and in Koheles.
- Wertheimer: *zman* is the most general of the three.
- Rav Hirsch: *zman* is time in general.
- Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *zman* is the entire duration of an appointed time.

### eit (עת)
Root ע-ת.
- HaBachur disagrees with those who call *zman* a synonym of *eit*, and cites
  anonymous sages who mention a minute, philosophy-based distinction, which he
  does not spell out, writing that he lacks the energy to go into details.
  Maharal's abstract-versus-existent line (above) is a distinction of that sort.
- Malbim: *eit* is a natural unit of time whose existence nature itself shows
  (*geshamim b'ittam*).
- Pappenheim: *eit* shares some meaning with *zman*, because it too refers to
  preparation and designation, as the *ish itti* of Vayikra 16:21 is the man
  designated to send the goat to Azazel. *Atid*, "future," is derived from *eit*
  and is also a form of a verb meaning "to prepare."
- Others: *zman* is a span, *eit* a point within it.
- Wertheimer: *eit* is somewhat more specific than *zman*, less so than *moed*.
- Rav Hirsch: *eit* is a portion of time, related to *eeteit* or *ettim*, a
  spade that breaks up soil into smaller units.
- Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *eit* is any given point within an appointed span; and he
  takes issue with the Rama's assertion that *eit* means the seasons of the
  year, citing examples in which *eit* refers to other (natural) phenomena as
  well.

### moed (מועד)
Root י-ע-ד, "appoint."
- Malbim: a predetermined time, set by man, or by Hashem for the festivals.
- Wertheimer: the most specific of the three, a preset point like a meeting
  scheduled down to the hour and minute.
- Rabbi Yaakov Emden: *moed* is the beginning of an appointed time (its *zman*
  is the whole duration, its *eit* any point within). Emden concedes that these
  are only the technical differences; in practical usage all these words may be
  used interchangeably.

### shaah (שעה)
Root ש-ע-ה.

- The Rama (Rabbi Moshe Isserles) heard that some consider *bechol eit u'vechol
  shaah*, in the second berachah of Birkas HaMazon and the last of Shemoneh
  Esrei, redundant, and say only *bechol eit*. He rejects that: *eit* is the
  annual seasons and *shaah* the hours of the day, two different measures and
  not a doubling.
- Rabbi Yaakov Emden takes issue with the Rama's claim that *eit* refers to the
  seasons, citing other (natural) phenomena that *eit* names.

So *shaah* here is the unit of the day: **hour**.

## Rendering guidance

- בכל עת ובכל שעה: "at every season and at every hour" (Rama), or "at every
  time and every hour," which does not commit to the Rama's reading of *eit*
  that Emden contests. Keep two nouns; do not write "always."
- עת alone: "a time," "the time [for]," "season" (עת לכל חפץ, "a time for
  every purpose"); בעתו "in its season," "at its proper time."
- זמן alone: "time" (the span, the flow); זמן קבוע "a set time"; בזמן הזה "in
  this time / at this season" (the berachah: "at this season" is customary).
- מועד: "appointed time," "set time"; the festivals are "the *moadim*" or
  "the appointed festivals," never bare "holidays."
- שעה beside עת: "hour." Standalone שעה in the "moment" sense: unsettled here;
  see above.
- Trap: English "time" absorbs עת, זמן, and מועד, and English "moment" would
  absorb עת and שעה. Where two stand together (עת וזמן, זמן ומועד), use the
  Wertheimer ladder: general "time," then "a time," then "appointed time."
  Emden's concession (the three interchange in practical usage) means the
  ladder is a tool for doubled phrases, not a claim that every occurrence is
  technical.
- When the passage turns on preparation (Rabbi Moshe Shapiro, Pappenheim),
  a gloss such as "time (*zman*, the time made ready)" may be earned; do not
  build the etymology into the running English.
