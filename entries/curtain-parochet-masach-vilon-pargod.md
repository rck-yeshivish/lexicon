# Curtain: paroches / masach / vilon / pargod

## Bottom line

*Paroches* is a **dividing curtain**, the one that marks off one domain from
the next; *masach* is a **screen**, a heavy cloth that covers and shields. In
the Mishkan the *paroches* separates the Kodesh from the Kodesh HaKodashim and
the *masach* hangs over an entrance. *Vilon* and *pargod* are the later
Rabbinic words: *vilon* a plain **curtain**, *pargod* a **partition curtain**
standing where the *paroches* stands, and in aggadic contexts the screen
between Hashem and what is below. *Paroches* names a function, not a fabric.

## Distinctions

### paroches (פרוכת)
Rashi (to Shemos 26:31): a *paroches* is the curtain separating a king from his
people; in the Mishkan it divides the Kodesh from the Kodesh HaKodashim (Shemos
26:31-33). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives it from פ-ר-כ, a breaking off whose
point is to begin something new. So *avodas perech* (Shemos 1:13) was torture
because the Egyptians kept interrupting the Jews mid-task and pushing them onto
something else, and a *pircha* in the language of the Gemara is the question
that breaks the flow of a sugya and turns it elsewhere. On this reading a
*paroches* is a partition that breaks off one area and announces the start of
another: up to here the Kodesh, from here something new and holier. Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim concludes that *paroches* does not inherently mean "curtain"
at all, but any barrier that sets the border between two domains.

*Shoresh Yesha* reads *paroches* as glory, on two possible derivations: from
*pe'er* ("glory"), or by taking the peh as interchangeable with beis and
rearranging the letters to *rechev* ("vehicle"), so that the *paroches* is a
feature of Hashem's glory as the *merkavah* and the *keruvim* are.

### masach (מסך)
Rashi (to Shemos 26:36, and see Shemos 35:12): a *masach* is the curtain
covering the entrance to a building or a room. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives
it from ס-כ, "covering," the root behind *sukkah* (covered with *sechach*) and
the Modern Hebrew *maseichah* ("mask," covering the face). A *masach* is thus a
screen that keeps a thing from the eyes of onlookers, and more broadly any thick
cloth used for protection, whether laid over something as a cover (Shmuel II
17:19, Tehillim 105:39) or hung down its side as a curtain. The column notes
that in Modern Hebrew *masach* is any sort of "screen," a computer monitor
included.

### paroches hamasach (Shemos 35:12)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reads the doubled name as carrying both senses at once:
the curtain of the Kodesh HaKodashim is a *paroches* because it marks the line
between Kodesh and Kodesh HaKodashim, and a *masach* because its heavy fabric
shields the inner chamber from the dust raised by the *kohanim* working
outside.

### vilon (וילון)
Four times in the Mishnah (*Keilim* 20:6, 24:13, 27:9, and *Negaim* 11:11), and
the name of the first of the seven heavens (*Chagigah* 12b), because it serves
as the curtain over the entrance to the heavens. The origins of the word are
neither Hebrew nor Aramaic: the column has it a loanword from the Greek *velum*,
which is related to the English *veil*. *Velum* survives in English scientific
terminology for a thin veil-like membrane, and the English *reveal* and *unveil*
literally mean to undo a veiling.

### pargod (פרגוד)
The Rabbinic word used where Tanach has *paroches*. Rabbi Nosson of Rome in
*Sefer HaAruch*: a *pargod* is the curtain separating a *chasan* from everyone
else, which matches the *paroches* separating a king from the people and the
Kodesh HaKodashim from the Kodesh. Rav Hai Gaon, on the Mishnah's *pargod*
(*Keilim* 29:1), glosses it simply as *paroches*. It carries the same heavenly
weight as *vilon*: the Gemara (*Chagigah* 15a) relates that the apostate Elisha
ben Avuyah claimed he heard from beyond the *pargod* a call for everyone else to
return except him, and the Yom Kippur Mussaf piyut has Rabbi Yishmael ben Elisha
ascending and reporting what he heard from beyond the *pargod* about the decree
of the Ten Martyrs. Rashi defines it in these contexts twice, at *Bava Metzia*
59a as the separation between Hashem and the *malachim*, and at *Berachos* 18b
as the separation between Hashem and His Holy Presence. On etymology the column
gives two lines. Rabbi Ernest Klein derives *pargod* from the Greek
*paragaudos*/*paragaudion* and the Latin *paragauda*, a "laced garment" or
"tunic," the lattice of a curtain somewhat resembling that weave. Others explain
it from the Latin *pergere* ("come," "go forward"), which in turn might
ultimately derive from the Akkadian *paraku* ("to go across"), naming the
curtain's position at an entrance and the fact that one passes through it to
enter. (Another derivative of *pergere* is the Latin *pergola*, a roof that
protrudes from elsewhere, whence the English *pergola*.)

## Rendering guidance

- פרוכת: "curtain" in the Mishkan and in a shul, but where Rabbi Shlomo
  Pappenheim's sense is in play, "partition" or "dividing curtain," since his
  whole point is that the word names the division and not the cloth.
- מסך: "screen." Reserve it; do not let it become a second "curtain."
- וילון: "curtain," plain.
- פרגוד: "curtain" in a *chuppah* or Mishnah context; in the aggados of
  *Chagigah* 15a and the Mussaf piyut, keep it transliterated, "from behind the
  *pargod*," since no English word carries what Rashi loads onto it there.
- Trap: Shemos 35:12 puts *paroches* and *masach* in one construct phrase,
  *paroches hamasach*. "The curtain of the curtain" is nonsense; render "the
  partition screen" or "the dividing curtain that screens," and let Rabbi
  Shlomo Pappenheim's two functions both survive.
- Trap: do not render *masach* "veil." It shields from view but is heavy cloth,
  not gauze; "veil" belongs closer to *vilon*.
