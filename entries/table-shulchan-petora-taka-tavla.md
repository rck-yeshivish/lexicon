# Table: shulchan / petora / taka / tavla

## Bottom line

*Shulchan* is the Biblical **table**, and the Aramaic *petora* is its standard
equivalent; render both "table." *Taka* also glosses as *shulchan*, but on the
best reading it is the small personal **tray-table** set before one diner,
while *petora* is the long communal one. *Tavla* is a **board** or table top, a
flat surface without legs. Where *petora* and *taka* stand together the difference is
size and privacy, not vocabulary.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

| שולחן | shulchan | table | the Biblical word; a top on four legs (Rash MiShantz) |

| טכא | taka | tray-table | glossed *shulchan* by Rashi; in practice the individual's table (Yudelovitch) |
| טבלא | tavla | board, table top | a flat surface with no legs attached (Rash MiShantz) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether petora and taka are one word or two.** Rashi glosses both with
*shulchan*: *petorah* at Beitzah 29b, *taka* at Berachos 46b, Gittin 67b, and
Kiddushin 81a. The texts pull the same way: the story of Abaye at Rabbah's table
(Pesachim 115b) reads *taka* in our printed text, *petora* twice in the Rif
(Pesachim 25b in his pagination), and *petora* in the narration with *taka* in
Abaye's question in Rabbi David Abudraham's citation. Against this, Rabbi
Mordechai Dov Yudelovitch of Lida: *petora* is a large table, *taka* a small
private one, the portable tray set before a single person at a festive meal or
before an honored guest. The Bottom line follows Yudelovitch, because every
Talmudic *taka* fits an individual's table: Rava's own *taka* removed while the
Reish Galusa's still stood (Berachos 42a).

**Whether tavla is a shulchan.** Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*):
*tavla* is simply the Rabbinic Hebrew for the Biblical *shulchan*. Rash MiShantz
(to Ohalos 15:2): they differ, since a *shulchan* is a top fixed on four legs
and a *tavla* has no legs, even a bare plank. Follow the Rash where a halachic
passage turns on construction; HaBachur's equivalence is a translation gloss.

**The root of shulchan.** Ibn Janach and Radak: the triliteral ש-ל-ח, "send
away, dispatch, sword," though how the senses connect is not evident. Menachem
Ibn Saruk: a quadriliteral ש-ל-ח-נ of its own.

## Distinctions

### shulchan (שולחן)
All 21 occurrences in the Chumash are the ritual *shulchan* of the Mishkan
bearing the lechem hapanim; in the other 50 in Tanach it can be an ordinary
table at which a king or great man eats and feeds his household. The Mishnaic *shulchani*, "moneychanger" (Maaser Sheini 4:2, Bava Metzia 2:4,
Meilah 6:5, Keilim 12:5), is so called because he worked from behind a table on
which he laid the money.

### petora (פתורא)
The Targumim render *shulchan* as *petora* throughout, and Rashi (Beitzah 29b)
defines *petorah* as *shulchan*; *petoraah* is likewise *shulchani*, a
moneychanger (Rashi to Chullin 54b). Hence the Midrash (Bamidbar Rabbah 20:7; Tanchuma Balak 4) and Rashi (to
Bamidbar 22:5) on Balak sending to Bilaam *pesorah*: Bilaam was a kind of
moneychanger, the kings running to him to do business as merchants run to a
moneychanger. Shabbos 36a records that *patora* and its diminutive *patorta*
once meant a large table and a small one, and that after the Churban the two
senses switched.

### taka (טכא)
Rashi glosses it *shulchan* (Berachos 46b, Gittin 67b). Once the *taka* is removed at the end of a meal one has shown intent to bench
and may not eat again until he does (Berachos 42a); Rava, eating at the Reish
Galusa's, continued eating after his own *taka* was taken away, since he was
relying on the Reish Galusa's *taka*, which still stood. The word is not in
Tanach, but Shadal and Rabbi Ezra Reuven Dangoor read the obscure *tuku* of
"and they are *tuku* at Your feet" (Devarim 33:3) as related to it: Klal
Yisrael sit at Hashem's table to receive His directives and blessings, as a
guest at his host's.

### tavla (טבלא)
Mishnaic (Eruvin 5:1, Yoma 3:10, Keilim 2:3, Mikvaos 4:2) and Talmudic (Pesachim
57a). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) equates it with *shulchan* and
connects it to the Latin *tabula*; Rash MiShantz distinguishes it, as above.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: שולחן and פתורא = "table"; טכא = "table" in ordinary narrative, but
  "his own table" or "tray-table" where the sugya contrasts it with another's;
  טבלא = "board" or "table top," "table" only where no legs are at issue.
- Trap: English "table" absorbs all four without a murmur. Two places where it
  must not: Berachos 42a, where the point is that Rava's personal *taka* went
  and the host's remained, so "the table was removed" flattens the story; and
  Ohalos 15:2, where a *tavla* rendered "table" imports legs the Rash denies.
- Where Aramaic *petora* renders Hebrew *shulchan* in a Targum, use one English
  word for both; the Targum is translating, not distinguishing.
- שולחני = "moneychanger," but keep the table in view in the Bilaam derashah,
  where the whole reading depends on it.
