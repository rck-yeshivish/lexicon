# Compass directions: tzafon / darom (negev, teiman) / mizrach (kedem) / maarav (yam)

## Bottom line

Each direction has three Hebrew names, and each name says *why*: **east** is
*mizrach* (where the sun shines out), *kedem* (the sun's first, forward
position), or *panim* (facewards); **west** is *yam* (seaward, toward the
Mediterranean), *maarav* (where shadows blur), or *acharon* (the sun's last
place); **north** is *tzafon* (hidden from the sun), *smol* (left), or the
Aramaic *estan*; **south** is *darom* (the sun's daytime path), *negev* (the dry
country), or *teiman*/*yamin* (right). Render all of them by the compass point
in the body, and only unpack the sense where the passage or a derashah leans on
it. Bereishis 28:14 pairs *yam*, *kedem*, *tzafon*, *negev*; Devarim 3:27 pairs
*yam*, *tzafon*, *mizrach*, *teiman*.

## Distinctions

### East: mizrach (מזרח), kedem (קדם), panim (פנים)
*Mizrach* is from *zarach*, to shine: the quarter the sun first shines from.
*Kedem* is from *kodem*, early or forward, the sun's first station. *Panim*,
facewards, because Adam was created facing east, which makes east the default
frame of reference for the whole system (see the north and south entries
below). Note the consequence: *kedem* also means "of old," so a pasuk with
*kedem* may be reaching for antiquity, not geography.

### West: yam (ים), maarav (מערב), acharon (אחרון)
Rabbeinu Bachaya: *yam* literally means sea, and serves for west because the
Mediterranean lies on Eretz Yisrael's western side, so the word is local, not
absolute. *Maarav* is from *me'urav*, mixed: as the sun sets, the shapes of the
shadows run together. *Acharon* is literally "last," the sun's final place on
the horizon before it sets.

### North: tzafon (צפון), smol (שמאל), estan (אסתן)
Several accounts of *tzafon* are offered. One reads it as *tzafun*, hidden,
because in the far northern latitudes the sun's warmth is hidden, or because
the sun keeps south of the celestial equator so the north is hidden from it.
Another reads it from *tzifiyah*, gazing, since north is fixed by gazing upward
to the northern star. A third takes it from the mountain named Tzafon in
Tanach, which lies due north of Eretz Yisrael (the article adds a modern
geographic identification, omitted here as outside our sources). *Smol*, left,
follows from facing east. The Gemara's Aramaic *estan* (Kesubos 23a) is linked
by Rabbeinu Bachaya to *asuta*, health, since the north wind heals (Yevamos
72a); hence the custom of saying *Asuta* to one who sneezes.

### South: darom (דרום), negev (נגב), teiman (תימן) / yamin (ימין)
*Darom* is read as a compound of *derech yom*, the path of the day, since the
sun holds to the southern side of the sky through the daylight hours. *Negev*
is literally dry, because the southern part of Eretz Yisrael is arid desert, so
like *yam* it is a name taken from the land itself. *Teiman* and *yamin* are
"right," again from an east-facing stance.

## Rendering guidance

- In ordinary narrative render the compass point: *tzafon* **north**, *negev*
  **south**, *kedem* **east**, *yam* **west**. Do not translate *yam* as "sea"
  in a direction list or *negev* as "the Negev" where the pasuk means south.
- Where a passage plays on the sense, gloss once and move on: *tzafon* as
  **the hidden side**, *negev* as **the dry country**, *kedem* as **the
  forward side**.
- Trap: English "east" absorbs *mizrach*, *kedem* and *panim*, and "south"
  absorbs *darom*, *negev* and *teiman*. When two of them stand in one pasuk,
  or when a source contrasts *kedem* with *mizrach*, keep them apart by
  glossing the second: "east (*kedem*, the forward side)."
- Second trap: *kedem* carries both space and time. Check whether the pasuk
  means eastward or aforetime before settling on either.
- *Smol* and *yamin* are only north and south when the speaker faces east.
  Where the orientation is a person's own body, they stay left and right.
