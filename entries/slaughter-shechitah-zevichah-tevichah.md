# Slaughter: shechitah / zevichah / tevichah (tabach)

## Bottom line

*Shechitah* is **ritual slaughter** in the halachic sense, and for Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim it is also the **general word for putting a living thing to death**.
*Zevichah* is slaughter **for a purpose**, above all **sacrificial slaughter**.
*Tevichah* is slaughter **for eating**, killing an animal for food in a general
way, even outside ritual slaughter. Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach dissents entirely on
*tevichah* and reads it as **cooking**. The first two words are disputed as
well. Mahari Assad holds that *shechitah* is said of *kodashim* and *zevichah*
of *chullin*, and the *Sifrei* (Naso 23) counts *zevichah* and *shechitah* as
two expressions for one thing.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Which word is the sacrificial one.**

- Malbim and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: *zevichah* is slaughter for a purpose,
  above all for a *korban*. So too the Rokeach (*Sefer HaChochmah*): *zevach* is
  said of an offering.
- Mahari Assad (*Kovetz Ohr Yisrael* 39): *shechitah* is said of *kodashim* and
  *zevichah* of *chullin*. He asks why, then, *Chullin* opens *hakol shochtin*
  and *Zevachim* *kol hazevachim*.

**Whether zevichah and shechitah differ at all.**

- The *Sifrei* (Naso 23): two expressions the Torah uses for one thing. Ralbag
  (to Vayikra 1:1) likewise calls *zevichah* a synonym of *shechitah*.
- Malbim and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim set them apart, as above.

**What tevichah is.**

- Malbim and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: killing an animal for food.
- Ibn Janach: cooking, which Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer answers from
  rabbinic usage.

## Distinctions

### shechitah (שחיטה)
Root ש-ח-ט.

- Malbim: *shechitah* names the specific halachic act, cutting the animal's neck
  through the esophagus and the trachea.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740-1814), cited by Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi
  Mecklenburg, reads it wider: *shechitah* is a general term for any act that
  causes a living being's death, and halachic slaughtering is one case inside
  that term, not its whole definition. His two proofs: Chullin 17a rules that
  for the Noahide "his *nechirah* (stabbing) is his *shechitah*," and Moshe
  warns that the nations will say Hashem "slaughtered them (*vayishchatem*) in
  the wilderness" (Bamidbar 14:16), which cannot mean a literal neck-cut but a
  bringing-about of death.

Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia relates the rabbinic *nechirah*, stabbing an animal in
its neck (the typical Noahide way of killing an animal, as opposed to properly
slaughtering it), to *chor*, a hole, because it too involves making a hole;
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives [*nichirayim*](nose-af-chotem-nichirayim.html#%D7%A0%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9E), nostrils, from the same *chor*, a
nostril being a hole in one's face.

Shadal (to Bereishis 9:3 and Shemos 23:18): *shachat* is related to *sachat*,
squeezing, since slaughter presses the blood out; *zevach* is from *zav*, since
its purpose is to make the blood flow.

### zevichah (זביחה)
Root ז-ב-ח.
- Malbim: slaughtering with a stated purpose, whether a ritual korban or meat
  for a festive gathering.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim narrows it: *zevichah* is a species of *shechitah*,
  killing for the purpose of sacrifice.
- Ralbag, who does not offer full definitions of these words, stands in two
  places: to Devarim 12:21 he says *zevichah* does not *per force* name the same
  act as *shechitah* and can also include *nechirah* (stabbing), which is a
  halachically unacceptable way of killing (see Targum Yonasan to Bamidbar
  22:40); to Vayikra 1:1 he writes that *shechitah* and *zevichah* are in fact
  synonyms.
- Mahari Assad (*Kovetz Ohr Yisrael* 39): *shechitah* is said of *kodashim* and
  *zevichah* of *chullin*, and he asks why *Chullin* then opens *hakol shochtin*
  and *Zevachim* *kol hazevachim*. *Sefer HaChochmah* of the Rokeach (*Shaar
  HaChiluk* §5, p. 18): *zevach* is said of an offering, *tevach* of a small
  slaughter, and *shechitah* of either, large or small.
- *Sifrei* (Naso 23) counts *zevichah* and *shechitah* among the pairs where the
  Torah speaks in two expressions for one thing.

### tevichah (טביחה)
Root ט-ב-ח.

- Malbim argues that it is killing an animal for food in a general way, even
  outside the context of ritual slaughtering.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: it is a kind of *shechitah* defined by its reason,
  here ordinary human consumption.

The word appears three times in Chumash: *u'tevoach tevach* at Yosef's banquet
(Bereishis 43:16), the thief who slaughters the animal he stole (Shemos 21:37),
and the curse "your ox will be slaughtered (*tavuach*) before your eyes, and you
will not eat from it" (Devarim 28:31); basically all other instances in Tanach
fall outside the Chumash. The ordinary thief repays double (Shemos 22:3), but
one who slaughtered or sold the stolen animal pays four times its value for
ovacaprines and five for bovines. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (1866-1935)
uses the sense of *tevichah* to explain why the Torah chose that word there:
without it one might have thought the penalty falls only on a thief who
slaughtered the animal for no good reason or to anger its owner, and that a
thief who slaughtered it to eat the meat rather than to make a profit could
excuse himself. The word blocks the excuse, and the fourfold or fivefold payment
stands.

Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach (990-1050), cited by Wertheimer, dissents on the word
entirely: *tevichah* means **cooking** (compare Arabic *tovech*), so Yosef told
his steward to cook, not to slaughter. Wertheimer answers that in a wide range of
rabbinic sources *tevichah* plainly means slaughtering. In later Hebrew a
*tabach* is a butcher (one who cuts up meat already
slaughtered) or a cook; Modern Hebrew *mitbach* is a kitchen.

### tabach (טבח) as a title
Two occupations in Tanach are described with *tevichah*-related words: Potiphar,
the Egyptian courtier to whom Yosef was sold, is *sar ha'tabachim* ("minister of
*tabachim*," Bereishis 37:36), and Nevuzaradan, the Babylonian general who
destroyed the first Beis HaMikdash, is *rav ha'tabachim* ("chief of *tabachim*,"
Melachim II and Yirmiyahu).
- Rashi to Bereishis 37:36: Potiphar was in charge of all those who slaughtered
  the king's animals.
- Ibn Ezra and Ramban follow Targum Onkelos: he was Chief Executioner, or at
  least chief warden over those on death row, which seems also to be the meaning
  of Nevuzaradan's title. The link is plain, an executioner slays men as a
  slaughterer slays beasts.
- Ibn Ezra to Daniel 2:14 says instead that Potiphar was Pharaoh's Chief Cook,
  so, Rabbi Klein observes, Ibn Ezra seems to contradict himself.

Rabbi Klein notes that there might be an etymological link between *zevichah*
and *tevichah*: the two differ only in the first letter of the root and share
BEIS-CHES at the end, Hebrew *zevichah* is rendered in Aramaic as *devichah*
with zayin morphing into dalet, and dalet is sometimes interchangeable with tes.
It is therefore understandable that when the Mishnah (Eduyos 8:4) declares that
liquids in the Mikdash are ritually pure, Pesachim 17a disputes whether this
applies to *beis mitbachaya* (the Mikdash's slaughter-house) or *beis
midbachaya* (its sacrifice house).

## Rendering guidance

- *u'tevoach tevach* (Bereishis 43:16): the cognate accusative wants an English
  doubling, "slaughter a slaughtering" or, better in a chumash context, "have an
  animal slaughtered and butchered."
- Trap: "slaughter" absorbs all three. Reserve plain **slaughter** for
  *shechitah*, use **sacrifice** or **slaughter as a korban** for *zevichah*,
  and **butcher** or **slaughter for food** for *tevichah*.
- Where *zevichah* and *shechitah* appear together in one passage, do not
  flatten them; Ralbag to Devarim 12:21 makes the gap load-bearing (*zevichah* can take in *nechirah*, which is not valid *shechitah*), though the *Sifrei* (Naso 23) and Ralbag to Vayikra 1:1 treat the two as one thing, and Mahari Assad holds that *shechitah* is said of *kodashim* and *zevichah* of *chullin*.
- *sar ha'tabachim*: "chief of the butchers" follows Rashi, "chief executioner"
  follows Onkelos, Ibn Ezra and Ramban. Pick one and footnote the other; do not
  write a compromise that says neither.
- *nechirah* is **stabbing**, never "slaughtering," even where Chullin 17a calls
  it the Noahide's *shechitah*.
