# Horn: shofar / keren / yovel / chatzotzros

## Bottom line

*Shofar* stays transliterated; where a gloss is wanted it is **ram's horn**,
and the word carries the sense of a **hollow tube** (Ran, from *shefoferes*)
and of **beauty and fitness** (root ש-פ-ר; Ritva). *Keren* is the general
**horn**, still on the animal or off it, and by extension a **ray**, a
**corner**, **strength**, and in the Mishnah the **principal** of a debt or
investment. *Yovel* is a horn too (Shemos 19:13) and gives its name to the
fiftieth year. *Chatzotzros* are the silver **trumpets** of Bamidbar 10:2,
though the terms crossed after the Churban. The trap: never render *keren* and
*shofar* both as "horn" in Rosh Hashanah 3:2, since the whole halachah turns on
the bovine horn being *called* a *keren*.

## Distinctions

### shofar (שופר)
The column opens on the Mishnah (*Rosh Hashanah* 3:2), which disqualifies a
bovine horn for the mitzvah, and on the Gemara there, in both Bavli and
Yerushalmi, which grounds that in the bovine horn's being called a *keren*
(Devarim 33:17; see also *Gittin* 2:3, *Keilim* 11:7) and not a *shofar*, while
the ram's horn is called both, or at least is not called a *keren*. Its question
is why a word should disqualify if both words mean "horn."

Ramban: while attached, every horn is a *keren*; detached, two kinds part
company. One is hollow but naturally packed with a filling that must be drawn
out before it will sound, and that is a *shofar*; the other is solid keratin
that must be drilled through, and that stays a *keren* even detached. Any horn
that is a *keren* and not a *shofar* is unfit, and the bovine horn's
disqualification is not material (it is physically a *shofar*) but nominal: it
is *called* a *keren*.

Ran identifies *shofar* as a cognate of *shefoferes*, a hollow tube, common in
the Mishnah (*Shabbos* 2:4; *Keilim* 14:5, 17:17; *Parah* 5:4, 5:8, 6:1, 9:2;
*Mikvaos* 2:10, 4:5, 6:1-2, 6:5-7, 6:9).

The traditional derivation is from ש-פ-ר, pleasant, beautiful, good (Bereishis
49:21, Tehillim 16:6, Iyov 26:13), cognate with the Aramaic *shfar*, three times
in Tanach (Daniel 3:32, 4:24, 6:2); Targum regularly renders *yafeh* and *tov*
with *shapir*. *Vayikra Rabbah* 29:6, on the shofar of Rosh Hashanah (Tehillim
81:4), works exactly this way: along with the mitzvah of shofar Hashem told us
*shapru maaseichem*, better your deeds, in Tishrei. *Bereishis Rabbah* 98:17
reads Naftali's "*imrei shefer*" (Bereishis 49:21) of Devorah, who descended from
him, either her beautiful shirah after the victory over Sisera or her making
words of Torah clear and beautiful in judgment, and notes that the Torah was
given with *shofaros* (Shemos 19:19). Shifrah the midwife (Shemos 1:15), often
identified as Yocheved, Moshe's mother, is named on the same root, either for
beautifying newborns as they emerged from the womb or for the bettering of Klal
Yisrael through Torah and mitzvos, since she bore Moshe (*Sifrei Behaaloscha* 78,
*Shemos Rabbah* 1:13, *Koheles Rabbah* 7:3, *Midrash Shmuel* 23).

Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: *shapir* is a *shifal* form, the shin not radical, from
פ-א-ר, whence *pe'er* and *tiferes*, the initial shin marking an action that
produces *pe'er*. The column adds that a popular folk etymology ties the early
Ashkenazi surname Shapiro to *shapir*, though historians argue it more likely
comes from the name of the German town Speyer. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 5:1,
49:11), on shin/sin and beis/peh interchange, groups *shefer* with *sefer*,
*mispar*, *sofer*, and *shover*: a *sefer* is a composite whole made of parts
fitted together coherently, counting and number highlight the individual units
within a whole system, and *shover* breaks the parts off the whole. From that he
surmises that *shefer* is beauty as the fit of many parts into one order, against
*yofi*, the outward beauty others perceive; and with that inwardness he implies
it makes sense that *shofar* should point to the inside of the tube. Ritva notes
that *shofar* implies something beautiful and copacetic.

Linguists, on the column's report, offer another origin altogether: Avraham
Even-Shoshan (1906-1984) and Rabbi Ernest Klein (1899-1983) claim *shofar* is
cognate with the Akkadian *shapparu*, "wild sheep," which in turn seems related
to the late Biblical *tzfir* (Daniel 8:5, 8:8, 8:21; Ezra 8:35; Divrei HaYamim II
29:21) by the shin/tzadi interchange, and perhaps to the earlier *se'ir*, if peh
and ayin may interchange.

### keren (קרן)
Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, and Radak: root ק-ר-נ, both an animal's horn
and a ray of light, a beam projecting from its source as a horn juts from the
head. Extensions: strength (Yechezkel 29:21, Tehillim 132:17, 148:14), since the
horn gives an animal's gore its power; a horn-shaped vessel (Shmuel I 16:1,
16:13; Melachim I 1:39); the horn-like protrusions at the four corners of the
mizbeach (Shemos 38:2, Vayikra 8:15, Yechezkel 43:20). In the Mishnah *keren*
keeps "horn" (*Rosh Hashanah* 3:2, *Kesubos* 13:2, *Gittin* 2:3, *Keilim* 11:7),
names the goring category of damages (*Bava Kamma* 2:5), widens to any corner
while keeping the altar sense (*Kilayim* 6:7; *Yoma* 5:5; *Zevachim* 5:3, 6:2,
6:5, 11:3; *Tamid* 2:5, 4:1, 7:3; *Middos* 3:2-3, 4:3, 4:5; *Keilim* 17:9-10),
and means principal, whether the capital of an investment (*Peah* 1:1,
*Kiddushin* 4:12) or the principal of an obligation as against an added penalty
(*Terumos* 6:1-4, 7:1-4, 8:1, 11:2; *Maaser Sheni* 4:3; *Pesachim* 2:4;
*Yevamos* 11:5, 11:7; *Kesubos* 3:9; *Bava Kamma* 9:6-12; *Shevuos* 8:3-4).
Radak even insinuates a link to Latin *cornu*, from which English *corner* comes
by way of French. On the financial sense the column adds that Rabbi Ernest Klein
innovatively connects *keren* to the Akkadian *qerenu*/*qaranu*, "pile, heap" or
"threshing floor," which seems related to Hebrew *goren* by the kuf/gimmel
interchange. *Shofar* in the Mishnah likewise names a vessel, narrow above and
wide below like the horn (*Shekalim* 2:1, 6:1, 6:5, with Rambam's commentary).

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reduces it to the biliteral ק-ר, forceful impact from
weight or speed: *korah* (beam) and *tikrah* (ceiling), whose weight bears on
the supports; *kir* (wall); *kiryah* (city); *kurei* (a web, a wall for
catching insects); *krum* (a covering membrane); *kor* and *kar* (cold,
temperatures clashing with the body's warmth); *mikreh* and *keri* (a man
colliding with new circumstances); and *keren*, for the force of its goring.

Rabbi Moshe Shapiro, without explicitly invoking Rabbi Pappenheim, builds the
halachah out of this: a *keren* is the point of intersection where two opposing
forces meet, hence the goring category, which names not just the limb but the
point of departure from which an animal may stray; hence the crossroads at which
one faces two ways; hence a corner, the meeting place of two sides; hence the
principal, which can be lost or turn a profit. On Rosh Hashanah we galvanize
every energy toward one end, the proclamation of Hashem's kingship, where no
other possibility is valid, so a thing whose very name implies a fork in the road
cannot serve. The counterpart is the column's own addition, which it says Rabbi
Shapiro does not make explicitly: per Rav Hirsch, *shofar* is the exact opposite,
conjuring all the constituent parts working together in harmony, and Ritva's
"beautiful and copacetic" fits that, so the word *shofar* is the antithesis of
*keren*, implying a unification that precludes all other possibilities.

### yovel (יובל)
Also a horn (Shemos 19:13), and in some pesukim the animal it came from
(Yehoshua 6:4-13). Because the fiftieth year of the shemittah cycle is
consecrated and proclaimed with the shofar, the year itself is *yovel* (Vayikra
25:10-15, 25:50, 27:17-31), whence English "jubilee." Since the *yovel* horn was
used chiefly to tell the travelling nation when to move and the army when to
attack, Rabbi Pappenheim suggests it comes from the biliteral ב-ל, mixing, the
*yovel* being the catalyst that brings about an intermingling of one domain with
another.

### chatzotzros (חצוצרות)
The Torah's *chatzotzros* are of silver (Bamidbar 10:2), yet the Mishnah
(*Kinnim* 3:6) uses the word of a ram's horn after the animal's death, so they
can stand for *shofaros*. The Gemara (Shabbos 36a, Sukkah 34a) records that
what were called *shofaros* in the time of the Beis HaMikdash were later called
*chatzotzros*, and the reverse (*Chasam Sofer*, *Aruch LaNer* to Sukkah 34a).
Mabit notes we have since returned to the older usage, so a ram's horn is again
a *shofar*.

### The keren / cornu question
English "horn" comes from Latin *cornu* and Greek *keras*, which originally meant
something like pointy or protruding, and behind them stand *unicorn*, *capricorn*,
*rhinoceros*, *cornet* and *cornea*. It is tempting, the column says, to argue
that these derive from the Hebrew *keren*, and some have argued it; but linguists
take the Latin and Greek as tributaries of the Proto-Indo-European root *ker*, so
the n-sound does not seem integral to the original root, which makes the tie to
*keren* harder. The column's own closing thought is that on Rabbi Pappenheim's
reckoning the nun of *keren* is likewise not part of the core root, so perhaps
there is something to the connection after all.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shofar* transliterated always; "ram's horn" only as a first-mention gloss,
  and never "trumpet."
- *Chatzotzros*: "trumpets," in Bamidbar 10:2 "silver trumpets." Where the
  Gemara records the terms swapping, keep both transliterated.
- Trap: English "horn" absorbs *keren*, *shofar*, and *yovel*. In *Rosh
  Hashanah* 3:2 the argument is nominal, not physical, so both Hebrew words
  must appear: "a cow's horn is unfit, because it is called a *keren* and not a
  *shofar*."
- *Keren* in the Mishnah's monetary sense is "principal," in damages *keren*
  (the goring category, transliterated), for the mizbeach "the horns of the
  altar," elsewhere "corner." Do not carry one English word across the senses.
- *Keren* as strength (Tehillim 132:17, 148:14): "horn" reads well and the
  metaphor survives; do not flatten to "power."
- *Yovel*: transliterated for the year, "the horn" for Shemos 19:13.
