# Lottery: goral / pur / payis / cholesh

## Bottom line

*Goral* is the general Hebrew word for a **lot**, both the drawing and the thing
drawn, and also for the **portion** allotted to a man, often by lot. *Pur* is
the word Megillas Esther itself glosses as *goral*. Four rishonim call it
Persian; Rabbi Yaakov Lorberbaum of Lissa and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim hold that
it is Hebrew. *Payis* is the Mishnaic word for a **lottery**, above all the one
among the *kohanim* in the Beis HaMikdash, and *kalpi* is the **box** the lots
are drawn from. *Cholesh* is a rare word for casting lots (Yeshayahu 14:12).

## Distinctions

### goral (גורל)
The word for the lots that decided which *sa'ir* on Yom Kippur goes to Hashem
and which to Azazel (Vayikra 16:8), and for the mechanism by which Eretz
Yisrael was apportioned among the *shevatim* (Bamidbar 26:55). It carries the
second sense of the portion itself, the share allotted to a man, often by way of
a raffle or lottery. *Goral* is related to the Arabic *jaral* / *jarwal*,
"stones" or "pebbles," the stones used for drawing lots, and the English *lot*
and *plot* for a section of land are probably derived from "lottery." Rabbi
Yaakov Lorberbaum of Lissa: *goral* has a positive connotation, a lottery held
for a beneficial purpose, receiving a portion as at a raffle.

### pur (פור)
Esther 3:7: Haman "cast a *pur*, which is a *goral*."

- Four rishonim take the gloss at face value, that *pur* is a foreign word the
  Megillah renders into Hebrew: Rabbi Tuviah ben Eliezer (*Midrash Lekach Tov*
  to Esther 3:7), Rashbam (to Shemos 16:15), Ibn Ezra (to Esther 3:7), and
  Ramban (to Shemos 12:2).
- Rabbi Klein adds a query of his own, whether the word is Persian or Akkadian;
  some linguists derive *pur*, just as they derive *goral*, from a word for
  stone, in this case the Akkadian *puru*.
- Rabbi Yosef ben Yosef Ibn Nachmias adds why this one word out of the
  Megillah's many Persian words needed translating: the *yom tov* is named Purim
  after Haman's *pur* (Esther 9:26), so the word is too central to leave opaque.

Two dissenting lines.

- Rabbi Yaakov Lorberbaum of Lissa keeps both words Hebrew and makes them
  opposites: *pur* is a lottery cast with malicious intent, tied to *por
  hisporerah ha'aretz* (Yeshayahu 24:19), the land breaking apart. The Megillah
  calls Haman's act a *pur* for his malice and a *goral* because from his own
  side he stood to gain, the property of the Jews he meant to kill.
- Maharitatz (Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon) has Haman hold two separate lotteries, a
  *goral* for which day to approach Achashverosh and a *pur* for which day to
  carry out the decree, though he does not say why each got the name it did.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim mentions the authorities who call *pur* Persian and
disagrees with them: *pur* is Hebrew, from the biliteral פ-ר,
"breaking something down into smaller components": *perurim* (crumbs), *eifer*
(ash), *parur* (a pot for crumbs and small grains), *pri* (fruit, a microcosm
broken off the tree), *pe'er* and *tiferes* (an all-encompassing beauty that
breaks down into aspects), *hafarah* (nullifying a vow), *primah* (tearing a
garment into shreds), and *tefirah* (sewing, which repairs what *primah* did).
On his reading a *pur* is the box shaped like a *parur* into which the wooden or
stone lots were put; in Mishnaic Hebrew that box is the *kalpi*.

### payis (פייס)
The word the Mishnah and Gemara use for drawing lots, most prominently the
lottery deciding which *kohanim* serve (*Yoma* 2:2-4).

- Some linguists argue that *payis* comes from the Persian *pisa* or the Greek
  *psefos*, "small rock," which would make it mean the same as *goral* and
  *pur*.
- Against that, *Tosafos Yom Tov* (Rabbi Yom Tov Lipmann Heller, to *Shabbos*
  23:2) argues the etymology is Hebrew, from *piyus* ("appeasement"): the
  randomness of a lottery appeases everyone, since all have an equal chance.
- The Meiri (*Beis HaBechirah* to *Yoma* 22a) proposed a similar understanding,
  and earlier than both Rabbi Nosson of Rome in *Sefer HeAruch*, that a *payis*
  is a lottery whose purpose is to appease the entrants.

In Modern Hebrew *payis* is the national lotto.

### cholesh (חולש)
Yeshayahu 14:12 says Nevuchadnetzar was *cholesh* over the nations; the Gemara
(*Shabbos* 149b) explains that he drew lots each day to pick which nation's
royalty to victimize.

- Rabbi Shlomo HaAdani, a sixteenth-century commentator to the Mishnah, writes
  in *Meleches Shlomo* (to *Shabbos* 23:2) that *cholesh* is related to
  *lachash* ("whisper"), because, he claims, lotteries are generally cast in the
  quiet.
- Alternatively, Rabbi Yosef Yishai Rain suggests it is related to its Hebrew
  homonym *chalash* ("weak"): winning something in a raffle or lottery is weak
  proof that one really deserved it.
- Kedushas Levi (*Derushim LeChanukah*): *cholesh* is a lottery in the sense of
  the uncertainty of leaving things to chance. The Gemara calls that unsettled
  uncertainty *chalishus hada'as*, weakness of mind, because a man unsure
  whether he will succeed is not at rest.

### kalpi (קלפי) and chevel (חבל)
*Kalpi* is the Mishnaic name for the box the lots are drawn from (*Yoma* 39a),
a word derived from the Greek *kalpe* / *kalpis*, an urn used for
drawing lots; in Modern Hebrew it is the ballot box at government elections. *Chevel* ("portion") is a synonym of
*goral*; the Midrash (*Yalkut Shimoni*, Shemos §265, and *Pesikta d'Rav Kahana*
§3:1) counts four words for lots in Tanach, *cholesh*, *pur*, *goral* and
*chevel*, and says all four will be used against the sons of Eisav: *cholesh*
when Yehoshua weakened Amalek (*vayachalosh*, Shemos 17:13), *pur* and *goral*
in the Purim victory, and in time to come a *chevel*, since the birthpangs of the
Messianic arrival are called *chevlei leidah* (Hoshea 13:13). Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim takes "rope" as the principal meaning of ח-ב-ל and derives its other
senses from that: one of a rope's main uses is measuring, and a *chevel* was
typically used to survey and measure a plot of land, hence *chevel* as a lot of
land; and just as such a lot is a localized thing within a greater area, so
*chevel* as pain is a localized pain, like *chevlei leidah*.

## Rendering guidance

- גורל: "lot" for the drawing, "portion" for what falls out of it. Vayikra 16:8
  is "lots"; a man's *goral* in life is his "portion."
- פור: leave as *pur*, transliterated, and let Esther 3:7's own apposition
  carry it: "a *pur*, that is, a lot."
- פייס: "lottery" in the Beis HaMikdash context, and where the sugya turns on
  *Tosafos Yom Tov*'s etymology, gloss it once as the lottery that appeases.
- קלפי: "lot box," not "urn," which sounds classical rather than Mishnaic.
- Trap: English "lot" and "lottery" will swallow *goral*, *pur*, *payis* and
  *cholesh* together. Esther 3:7 puts *pur* and *goral* in one clause; if both
  become "lot," the pasuk says nothing. Transliterate the first.
- Trap: where a source (Rabbi Yaakov Lorberbaum) is making *pur* the malign
  lottery and *goral* the benign one, do not render both "lottery"; use "lot
  cast in malice" against "allotted portion," or the *chiddush* disappears.
