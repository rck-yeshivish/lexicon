# Oil: shemen / yitzhar

## Bottom line

*Shemen* is the general word, **oil**, which is why the Torah must say *shemen
zayis zach* to specify **pure olive oil**. *Yitzhar* is **olive oil** in itself,
and is never paired with *zayis* in Tanach. Every occurrence of *yitzhar* is
agricultural, the yield of the press, so where a passage lists produce, render it
"oil" as a crop and *shemen* as the substance. What a translator must not do is
let one English word erase the fact that *yitzhar* already carries "olive."

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

## Where the authorities disagree

Three accounts of the difference, and they do not reduce to one.

1. **By species.** Menachem Ibn Saruk defines *yitzhar* as olive oil, while
   *shemen* answers to *shuman* and *shamen*, fat, and names the fatty,
   glyceride property of any oil. So *yitzhar* means olive oil by definition and
   *shemen* means oil of any kind. The phrase *shemen zayis zach* proves the
   point from the other side: *shemen* alone does not mean olive oil, and
   *yitzhar* is never joined to *zayis*.
2. **By stage.** Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): *yitzhar* is oil as it comes out of
   the press. Every occurrence in Tanach is olive oil as an agricultural
   product, and Iyov 24:11 has a verb form of the word, which Rashi explains as
   the producing of olive oil. On this reading both words can mean olive oil, and
   *yitzhar* marks it in its freshest state.
3. **By local usage.** Rabbi Yitzchak Zev Yadler: *yitzhar* does not mean olive
   oil at all in itself, but whatever oil a given place burns for light. In Eretz
   Yisrael that was olive oil, so *yitzhar* in the Torah means olive oil; Shabbos
   26b records that Bavel used sesame oil, Media nut oil, Alexandria radish oil,
   and Cappadocia naphtha.

The Bottom line follows the first two, which agree on the English and differ only
on how narrow the word is, and notes the third where a source is discussing lamp
oil outside Eretz Yisrael.

## Distinctions

### shemen (שמן)
Close to two hundred occurrences with its derivatives, against roughly a tenth as
many for *yitzhar*. Ibn Saruk's link to *shuman* fixes its sense as the fatty
substance, which is why the Torah qualifies it for the Menorah: *shemen zayis
zach*, pure olive oil, pressed and clear.

### yitzhar (יצהר)
Rabbi Yosef Kimchi (*Sefer HaGilui*) roots the word in *tzohar*, light. His son
Radak explains him: oil is a peculiarly clear and shining substance, and for the
same reason olive oil is called "gold" (Zechariah 4:12). Ibn Ezra (to Iyov
24:11) takes *tzohar* in its other sense, "noon," since oil is burned for light
and that light resembles the sun at midday. *Yitzhar* is also a proper name nine
times in Tanach, Levi's grandson and Korach's father (Shemos 6:18 and elsewhere).
The Midrash has Korach read his father's name as an omen: oil floats over water,
and Moshe was drawn from the water, so he took it that he would come out on top.
Rabbi Yadler answers that the name pointed not at Korach but at his descendant
Shmuel HaNavi, who would anoint David with specially prepared olive oil and begin
the Davidic line.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "oil" absorbs both. In the produce lists where *dagan, tirosh
  v'yitzhar* stand together, render "grain, wine and oil"; the triad is fixed,
  and forcing "olive oil" there overtranslates one member against its neighbors.
- *Shemen* on its own is "oil," and only "olive oil" where *zayis* is present or
  the Menorah is the subject.
- Where a source contrasts the two words (a derashah on Korach's name, or a
  discussion of the press), keep them apart: *yitzhar* "fresh-pressed olive oil,"
  *shemen* "oil."
- Iyov 24:11, the verb form, is "they press out oil," following Rashi.
- The name is Yitzhar, roman and untranslated; do not gloss it "Oil" in the body,
  and save the wordplay for a note.
