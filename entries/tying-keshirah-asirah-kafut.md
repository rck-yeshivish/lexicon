# Tying: keshirah / asirah / kafut (ketirah, tzror, akeidah)

## Bottom line

*Keshirah* is **tying** where the thing tied goes along with it: **bind, tie,
join**. *Asirah* is **tying down** against the will of the one tied: **bind,
detain, restrain**, and it is the root of *assir* (prisoner) and *issur*
(prohibition). *Kafut* is the Mishnaic and Aramaic **tied down** of a man or an
animal held fast under compulsion. *Tzror* is **binding tight**, two things
knotted into one bundle. *Ketirah* is simply the Aramaic of *keshirah*. Malbim's
consent test is the one thing not to get wrong: consent belongs to *keshirah*,
coercion to *asirah*.

## Distinctions

### keshirah (קשירה)
Root ק-ש-ר, some sixty appearances in Tanach. Concrete: tying *tefillin* to the
arm (Devarim 6:8, 11:18), the scarlet thread on Yehudah's grandson's hand
(Bereishis 38:28), the rope in Rachav's window (Yehoshua 2:18, 2:21). Figurative:
Yaakov's soul "bound up" with Binyamin's (Bereishis 44:30), and Mishlei's charge
to tie *chessed*, *emes* and wisdom to oneself (Mishlei 3:3, 6:21, 7:3). The
commonest use in Tanach is *kesher*, a conspiracy: people who bind themselves
together to overthrow a government, as Asalyahu cried out *kesher, kesher* (II
Melachim 11:14). Malbim: this is exactly why the root is *keshirah* and not
*asirah*, since the conspirators enter the bond willingly; see also Radak to II
Melachim 9:14. Rabbinic Hebrew keeps the sense: *katir* is a conspiracy at
Yevamos 61a, on the appointment of Yehoshua ben Gamla as *Kohen Gadol*.

### asirah (אסירה)
Root א-ס-ר, close to ninety appearances. Its two settings are harnessing a wagon
to the animal that draws it (Bereishis 46:29, Shemos 14:6, II Melachim 9:21) and
tying down a captive or detainee (Tehillim 146:7, II Melachim 17:4, Shoftim
16:21). Hence *beis ha'asurim*, the jail, literally the house of those tied down
(Shoftim 16:21, 16:25; Bereishis 39:20, 40:5). Malbim: the whole difference from
*keshirah* is that *asirah* is done without the consent of what is tied; he links
it to *yissurin*, suffering, which likewise comes upon a man against his will,
and to *issur*, a prohibition, which is an action closed off to a person whether
he likes it or not. *Issur* in that sense is already Chumash Hebrew, in the
parashah of *nedarim* (Bamidbar 30:3-15). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces the root
to the biliteral samech-reish, "removal," since one who is tied down has his
freedom of movement removed; Rabbi Yehuda Ibn Chayyuj had already shown the aleph
to be extraneous to the root, from *beis hasurim* at Koheles 4:14 standing for
*beis ha'asurim*. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg extends the root to *mussar*,
which gives a man the means of tying down his *yetzer hara*.

### ketirah (קטירה)
Root ק-ט-ר. In Hebrew this root is smoke and incense; in Aramaic it is tying.
Three appearances in the Aramaic of Daniel: literally tied (Daniel 5:6), and
twice of a riddle or enigma, something knotted shut that must be untied (Daniel
5:12, 5:16). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Meturgaman*): this is Targum's standard
rendering of *keshirah*. Rabbi Yaakov Berger (*Milon Leshon HaMikra*): Hebrew
shin regularly becomes Aramaic tav (*shor* / *tor*), and tav and tes interchange,
so ק-ש-ר yields ק-ט-ר. On the lone Hebrew cognate (Yechezkel 46:22) the
commentators split between "smoke" and "tied." The name Keturah belongs here:
Hagar tied herself off from marrying anyone else so as to be able to return to
Avraham (*Bereishis Rabbah* 61:4; *Hadar Zekeinim* to Bereishis 25:1). Rav Hirsch
(to Bereishis 25:1) reads the name as *keshirah*, and reads Hagar itself with
*agur* (gathered in) and *chagurah* (a belt).

### kafut (כפות)
Four appearances in the Aramaic of Daniel (3:20-24), then common in Mishnaic
Hebrew: the hands of one being flogged are *kafus* to the pillar (Makkos 3:12),
the *Parah Adumah* was *kafus* before shechitah (Parah 3:9), and the Mishnah
contrasts it with *akeidah* for the *Korban Tamid* (Tamid 4:1); also Bava Kamma
6:5, Taharos 7:5. Rashi glosses it sometimes with *asirah* (Daniel 3:20; Sukkah
31a) and sometimes with *keshirah* (Daniel 3:23; Chullin 16a, Sanhedrin 77b,
Kiddushin 70a), so it does not sit cleanly on either side of Malbim's line. The
etymology that fits its usage ties it to *kefiyah*, compulsion, both being acts
of subjugation. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reads the biliteral kaf-peh as
"receptacle" and gathers under it *kaf* (palm), *kofeif* (bending, what turns a
palm into a receptacle), *akifah* (enforcement, bending others to an authority),
*kippah* (dome), *kofeh* (overturning a vessel), and *kafan* (famine, which bends
a man's stature); *kefiyah* is then the suppressing of dissent so that another
bends.

### tzror (צרור)
Root צ-ר-ר. *Maaser sheini* money is to be tied up in the hand (Devarim 14:25);
marrying two sisters "binds" them to each other (Vayikra 18:18), whence the
Mishnaic *tzaros*, co-wives tied together by one husband. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim: from the biliteral tzadi-reish, the union of tightly attached parts,
which is what a tight knot is.

### the narrower roots
Malbim: ע-נ-ד (Iyov 31:36, Mishlei 6:21) is tying on something worn as an
ornament; ח-ת-ל (*chitul*, Yechezkel 16:4, 30:21, Iyov 38:9) is wrapping in soft
cloth rather than tying tight. ע-ק-ד is tying down an animal for slaughter, hence
*Akeidas Yitzchak* (Bereishis 22:9) and the spotted animals marked where they
were tied (Bereishis 30:35-40, 31:8-12); Radak's father reads *beis eiked* (II
Melachim 10:12, 10:14) as the place shepherds tied sheep for shearing. ח-ב-ש is
saddling a donkey (Bereishis 22:3, Bamidbar 22:21), fastening a headdress (Shemos
29:9, Vayikra 8:13), and binding a wound, whence *tachboshes*, a bandage
(Yeshayahu 1:6, 30:26; Hoshea 6:1; Tehillim 147:3); Chizkuni (to Shemos 28:7)
reads *cheishev*, a belt, as its metathesis. ר-ת-ם is harnessing a wagon to an
animal (Michah 1:13).

## Rendering guidance

- Default: קשירה "tie" or "bind"; אסירה "tie down," "bind," "detain."
- קשר as a political noun: "conspiracy," and the tying metaphor is live; a
  derashah on the root can be carried with "bound together in a plot."
- Trap: English "bind" absorbs *keshirah*, *asirah* and *kafus* alike. Where a
  passage turns on Malbim's consent test, force the difference: *keshirah* "tie
  himself to," *asirah* "is tied down."
- Second trap: *issur* is normally just "prohibition," and the tie is dead
  metaphor in ordinary halachic prose. Revive it only where the source is
  actually darshening the root, as Malbim and Rabbi Mecklenburg do.
- Where *kafus* and *akeidah* stand together (Tamid 4:1), keep both: "not tied
  down but bound as for an *akeidah*," since the Mishnah's whole point is that
  they are two different acts.
- עקידה of Yitzchak stays *Akeidah*, not "the tying."
