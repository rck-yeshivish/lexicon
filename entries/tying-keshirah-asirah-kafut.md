# Tying: keshirah / asirah / kafut (ketirah, tzror, akeidah)

## Bottom line

For Malbim, *keshirah* is **tying** where the thing tied consents to it: **bind,
tie, join**; *asirah* is **tying down** without the consent of what is tied:
**bind, detain, restrain**, and it is the root of *assir* (prisoner) and *issur*
(prohibition). *Kafut* is the Aramaic and Mishnaic **tied down**; one way of
reading it ties it to *kefiyah*, compulsion; Rashi glosses
it sometimes with *asirah* and sometimes with *keshirah*; and Dr. Michael
Sokoloff connects it to a different Aramaic root altogether. *Tzror* is
**binding tight**, a union of strongly attached parts (Pappenheim). *Ketirah* is
the Aramaic of *keshirah*, Targum's standard rendering of it (HaBachur), by a
shin-to-tav shift, presuming tav and tes interchange (Rabbi Yaakov Berger). On Malbim's consent test, consent
belongs to *keshirah*, its absence to *asirah*.

## Distinctions

### keshirah (קשירה)
Root ק-ש-ר, sixty appearances in Tanach. Concrete: tying *tefillin* to the arm
(Devarim 6:8, 11:18), the red string tied to the hand of Yehudah's son
(Bereishis 38:28), the rope in Rachav's window (Yehoshua 2:18, 2:21). Figurative:
Yaakov's soul "tied" to Binyamin's, as Yehudah put it (Bereishis 44:30), and
Mishlei's charge to tie good virtues and wisdom to oneself (Mishlei 3:3, 6:21,
7:3). The commonest use in Tanach is *kesher*, a conspiracy: people who tie
themselves together, figuratively, to overthrow a government or reach a
political goal, as Asalyahu cried out *kesher, kesher* (II Melachim 11:14).
Malbim: *keshirah* is tying in a way that what is tied consents to it, as in a
*kesher*, whose conspirators willingly join one another in their plot and are
not forced into it; see also Radak to II Melachim 9:14.

### asirah (אסירה)
Root א-ס-ר, close to ninety appearances. It usually appears of tying a wagon to
the animal that draws it (Bereishis 46:29, Shemos 14:6, II Melachim 9:21) or
tying down a captive or detainee (Tehillim 146:7, II Melachim 17:4, Shoftim
16:21). Hence *beis ha'asurim*, the jail, literally the house of those tied down
(Shoftim 16:21, 16:25; Bereishis 39:20, 40:5). *Issur*, a prohibition, is
already in Chumash, of vows that forbid actions or things (Bamidbar 30:3 to 15);
Rabbi Klein explains that what is forbidden is as though tied down and made
inaccessible, and compares the English "my hands are tied."
- Malbim: the main difference from *keshirah* is that *asirah* is done without
  the consent of what is tied, as a captive is tied down against his will; so he
  relates it to *yissurin*, suffering, which likewise comes upon a man against
  his will, and to *issur*, where one's actions may be precluded, by law or by
  nature, against his will.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim ties the root to the biliteral samech-reish,
  "removal," since one who is tied down has his freedom of movement removed;
  that the aleph of *assur* is superfluous was already noted by Rabbi Yehuda Ibn
  Chayyuj, who adduces *beis hasurim* (Koheles 4:14) as the equivalent of *beis
  ha'asurim*.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg similarly relates *mussar* to tying, since it
  gives a man the ability to tie down his *yetzer hara*.
- Rabbi Klein also raises the notion that *erusin* may be a metathesized form of
  *asirah*.

### ketirah (קטירה)
Root ק-ט-ר. In Hebrew this root is smoke or incense; in Aramaic it is tying
(Rabbi Klein knows of no connection between the two). Three appearances in the
Aramaic of Daniel: literally tied (Daniel 5:6), and twice of a riddle or enigma,
figuratively tied in a knot that has to be opened (Daniel 5:12, 5:16). Rabbi
Eliyahu HaBachur (*Meturgeman*) had already noted that it is Targum's standard
rendering of *keshirah*. In many words Hebrew shin becomes Aramaic tav (*shor*,
*tor*); on that basis Rabbi Yaakov Berger of Kiryat Sefer (*Milon Leshon
HaMikra*) writes that it makes sense for ק-ש-ר to become ק-ט-ר in Aramaic,
presuming tav and tes interchange. On the one Hebrew cognate (Yechezkel 46:22)
the commentators disagree between "smoke" and "tied." And just as Biblical
*keshirah* can mean conspiracy, so can Rabbinic *katir*: the Gemara (Yevamos 61a)
speaks of a *katir* behind the appointment of Yehoshua ben Gamla as Kohen Gadol,
since his wife bribed King Yannai to arrange it.

The name Keturah (Bereishis 25:1), whom rabbinic tradition identifies with
Hagar, belongs here.
- *Bereishis Rabbah* 61:4: Hagar "tied herself up" by holding back from marrying
  anyone else, so that she might return to Avraham.
- According to some sources, a second reading, she physically closed herself off
  so as to remain chaste until Avraham took her back (see *Hadar Zekeinim* to
  Bereishis 25:1).
- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 25:1) relates the name to *keshirah*, and goes
  further, connecting her first name Hagar, by the interchange of hei, aleph and
  ches, to *agur* (gathered in) and *chagurah* (a belt), both of something tied
  or inward-looking, cut off from the outside.

### kafut (כפות)
Four appearances in the Aramaic of Daniel (3:20 to 24); Rashi explains it once
with *asirah* (Daniel 3:20) and once with *keshirah* (Daniel 3:23). It also
appears in several places in Mishnaic Hebrew: the hands of one being flogged are
*kafus* to the pillar (Makkos 3:12); the Mishnah says the Korban Tamid was not
*kafus* but bound by *akeidah* (Tamid 4:1); the *Parah Adumah* was *kafus* before
its shechitah (Parah 3:9); and it appears of tying down in other contexts (Bava
Kamma 6:5, Taharos 7:5). In the Gemara Rashi again sometimes defines it with
*asirah* (Sukkah 31a; see also Targum to Tehillim 118:27) and sometimes with
*keshirah* (Chullin 16a, Sanhedrin 77b, Kiddushin 70a).

Etymology, two proposals.

- One way is to tie *kafut* to *kefiyah*, duress or compulsion, since both
  subjugate and dominate another. That approach jibes with Rabbi Shlomo
  Pappenheim's reading of the biliteral kaf-peh as "receptacle," under which he
  gathers *kaf* (palm), *kofef* (bending, what turns a palm into a receptacle),
  *akifah* (enforcement, bending others to an authority), *kippah* (dome, a
  palm-like structure), *kofeh* (overturning a vessel, making a dome-like
  space), and *kafan* (famine, which bends a man's stature); *kefiyah* for him
  is suppressing dissent so that another bends.
- Dr. Michael Sokoloff, in his dictionary of Jewish Babylonian Aramaic, instead
  connects *kafut* to the quadriliteral Aramaic root כ-ר-פ-ת, found once in the
  Talmud (Niddah 48b), the reish sometimes dropping by assimilation; Rabbi Klein
  notes that as some commentators explain that one Talmudic word, it seems
  totally unrelated to *kafut*.

### tzror (צרור)
Root צ-(ו)-ר. *Maaser sheini* money is to be tied up in
the hand and taken to Yerushalayim (Devarim 14:25); marrying two sisters at once
"ties" them together (Vayikra 18:18), and in Mishnaic usage *tzaros* are
co-wives, tied together by marriage to the same man. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim:
from the biliteral tzadi-reish, the union of strongly connected parts, which, Rabbi
Klein says, a tight knot certainly fits.

### the narrower roots
- ר-ת-ם: harnessing a wagon to an animal (Michah 1:13). The same root gives the
  *rotem* plant (Melachim I 19:4 to 5; Tehillim 120:4; Iyov 30:4) and likely the place-name Ritmah, a stop in
  the wilderness (Bamidbar 33:18 to 19); the connection between the meanings
  remains obscure.
- ע-ק-ד: eight times in Bereishis, once of *Akeidas Yitzchak*, when Avraham tied
  down Yitzchak like a korban (Bereishis 22:9), and seven times of animals
  spotted on the parts by which they were usually tied down (Bereishis 30:35 to
  40, 31:8 to 12); elsewhere only in *beis eiked* (II Melachim 10:12, 10:14),
  which Radak's father explains as the place shepherds tied down sheep for
  shearing.
- ע-נ-ד: twice (Iyov 31:36, Mishlei 6:21), and some see a metathesized ע-ד-ן in
  Iyov 38:31. Malbim: tying on something that serves as an ornament. Modern
  Hebrew uses it for putting on a wristwatch.
- ח-ב-ש: saddling a donkey (Bereishis 22:3, Bamidbar 22:21, Shoftim 19:10,
  Shmuel II 16:1), tying a headdress on (Shemos 29:9, Vayikra 8:13, Yechezkel
  24:17), and binding a wound tight to heal it (Yeshayahu 1:6, 30:26; Yechezkel
  30:21; Hoshea 6:1; Iyov 5:18; Tehillim 147:3), whence *tachboshes*, a bandage.
  Chizkuni (to Shemos 28:7) explains *cheishev*, a belt, as a metathesis of this
  root.
- ח-ת-ל: Malbim: *chitul*/*chatulah* (Yechezkel 16:4, 30:21, Iyov 38:9) is
  wrapping in soft fabric, as against tying tight. In Modern Hebrew the noun is a
  diaper; for a possible metathesized form see II Melachim 10:22.
  *Chatul*/*chatulah*, a cat, appears many times in the Bavli
  (Berachos 56b; Shabbos 51a, 128b; Eruvin 100b; Bava Kamma 80a to 80b; Horayos
  13a to 13b; Chullin 52b to 53a; Bechoros 8a to 8b) and once in the Yerushalmi
  (Peah 3:7), never in Tanach or Mishnah. Rabbi Aharon Marcus connects it to this
  root: the cat is agile and dexterous, supple and flexible like soft cloth. He also writes
  that the Egyptian Hathor, a deified cat, comes from Hebrew *chatul*, the
  Egyptians pronouncing *l* as *r*, and that *chatul* entered Latin as *catulus*
  and spread from there: Italian *gatto*, Spanish *gato*, Swedish *katta*, Polish
  *kot*, German *Katze*, French *chat*, English *cat*.
- ר-כ-ס also means tying or fastening.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: קשירה "tie" or "bind"; אסירה "tie down," "bind," "detain."
- קשר as a political noun: "conspiracy," and the tying metaphor is live; a
  derashah on the root can be carried with "bound together in a plot."
- Trap: English "bind" absorbs *keshirah*, *asirah* and *kafus* alike. Where a
  passage turns on Malbim's consent test, force the difference: *keshirah* "tie
  himself to," *asirah* "is tied down."
- Second trap: *issur* is normally just "prohibition," and the tie is dead
  metaphor in ordinary halachic prose. Revive it only where the source is
  actually darshening the root, as Malbim does with *issur* (and Rabbi
  Mecklenburg with *mussar*).
- Where *kafus* and *akeidah* stand together (Tamid 4:1), keep both: "not tied
  down but bound as for an *akeidah*," since the Mishnah sets the two apart.
- עקידה of Yitzchak stays *Akeidah*, not "the tying."
