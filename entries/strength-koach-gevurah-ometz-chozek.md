# Strength: koach / gevurah / chozek / ometz (otzem, oz, eitan, adir, kabir, takif)

## Bottom line

*Koach* is **strength**, the general word: plain ability to do or withstand,
shared by man and beast (Rabbi Pappenheim; Rabbi Soloveitchik). *Gevurah* is
**might** or **heroism**: *koach* put into action, and for Chazal the conquest
of oneself (Avos 4:1; Rabbi Soloveitchik; Rabbi Mecklenburg). *Chozek* is the
standard word for being **strong**; *ometz* is strength above the normal
measure, **fortitude** or **resolve**, of the heart (Bedersi and Wertheimer;
Vilna Gaon), so חזק ואמץ reads "be strong and resolute", the greater word
second. *Eitan* **steadfast**; *adir* **mighty**, the mightiest of a group; *oz*
bold **might** of spirit, at root Divine; *takif* **overpowering**. Trap: one
English word for all; never let *koach* and *gevurah* both be "strength".

## Distinctions

### koach (כח)
Rabbi Pappenheim: the most general term, plain ability to do or withstand. Rabbi
Soloveitchik (*Catharsis*): in Tanach, physical strength, shared with the
animals; hence "who gives *koach* to the weary". Rabbi Mecklenburg: the power to
hold a state against what would break it; or mental fortitude, so Targum renders
*koach* (Devarim 8:18) as *eitzah*, and "with all his *koach*" (Shabbos 119b)
means full focus, not volume (Rashi, against Tosafos).

### gevurah (גבורה)
Avos 4:1: the *gibbor* conquers his yetzer. Rabbi Soloveitchik: given to man
alone, the power to rise above the survival instinct, best "heroism"; a *gibbor*
is not always the victor but always enters the fight with all his might; Hashem
is *Gibbor* because He holds back His anger (Yoma 69b; Yirmiyahu 32:18). Rabbi
Pappenheim: the act that actualizes potential *koach*, lent even to the Flood
waters (Bereishis 7:18-19). Rabbi Mecklenburg: not withstanding opposition but
defeating it. Rabbi Shapira-Frankfurter (Bereishis 25:23) alone makes it physical.

### chozek (חזק) and ometz (אמץ)
Bedersi and Rabbi Wertheimer: *chozek* is normal strength (a sick man restored is
*chazak*); *ometz* is strength beyond the normal. Vilna Gaon (Yehoshua 1:7;
Mishlei 24:5) and Malbim (*Yair Ohr*): *chozek* outer and physical, *ometz* of
the heart; Malbim elsewhere (Yeshayahu 28:2): *chozek* fades, *ometz* renews
itself. Rabbi Shapira-Frankfurter reverses part of this: *chozek* is strength of
will (courage to enter battle), *ometz* strength of heart (courage to stay).
Berachos 32b reads חזק ואמץ to Yehoshua as Torah and good deeds; Rabbi
Wertheimer: the greater the scholar, the more than ordinary strength he needs to
keep doing, hence *ometz*. Rabbi Pappenheim: *ometz* from מ-צ, squeezing out the
last drop (Zechariah 6:3); *chozek* withstands being overpowered, *tokef* overpowers.

### otzem (עצם), oz (עז), kabir (כביר)
Rabbi Dr. Asher Weiser: *otzem* is *ometz* by aleph/ayin and metathesis. Rabbi
Edel: strength through numbers (Shemos 1:8, 1:20; Devarim 7:1; Yoel 2:2), then
inherent strength; or from *etzem*, bone. Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: every ע-צ word
holds potential inside, so *otzem* is pent-up, unexpressed strength. Rabbi
Pappenheim: *otzem* is quantitative strength from unequal parts, *kabir* from
equal parts (Rashi to Berachos 8a). Rabbi Wertheimer: *oz* and *az* are
undeterrable strength of spirit, seen in the face (Koheles 8:1); the spies were
sent to test *chazak* (Bamidbar 13:18) and reported *az* (13:28), resolve, not
muscle. Rabbi Wessely, cited by Rabbi Edel: *oz* is primarily Divine,
supernatural power, against *gevurah* within nature; "Hashem gives *oz* to His
people" (Tehillim 29:11, printed 28:11 in the article) lifts them above nature;
"give *oz* to Hashem" (68:35) is figurative (Eichah Rabbah 1:33; Rabbi Yerucham
Levovitz). Rabbi Marcus: *oz*, *eitz*, *otzem* are one root (tzadi/zayin).

### eitan (איתן), adir (אדיר), takif (תקיף)
*Eitan*: Rashi (Devarim 21:4) strong, hard; Rambam (Hilchos Rotzeach 9:2)
strong-flowing; Rabbi Edel reconciles them as the river bank that holds the
water back, and holding back takes strength: Yosef (Bereishis 49:24), Yisro's
rock (Bamidbar 24:21), the Avos as *eitanim* born in Tishrei (Melachim I 8:2;
Rosh Hashanah 11a; Metzudas David). Rav Hirsch (Shemos 14:27): durable, like
bedrock. *Adir*: Rabbi Pappenheim, *chazak* is
stronger than another, *adir* the strongest of a whole group, fit for Hashem
(Shemos 15:6; Tehillim 8:2, 93:4); Rabbi Edel: kin to *hadar*. *Takif*: Targum's
word for *chozek* and *oz*, also Biblical (Esther 9:29; Koheles 4:12; Daniel
4:27); Rabbi Pappenheim, root ק-פ, a full circle, strength that surrounds and
overpowers; Rabbi Edel: of Hashem, all-encompassing sovereignty (Talmudic
*matkif*, one sage overpowering another's position).

## Rendering guidance

- *koach* "strength"; *gevurah* "might" ("heroism" for self-mastery), so the
  morning berachos read "who girds Yisrael with might" and "who gives strength
  to the weary" (Rabbi Soloveitchik); *chozek* "strength", *chazak* "strong";
  *ometz* "fortitude", "resolve"; *oz* "might", *az* "bold"; *eitan*
  "steadfast"; *adir* "mighty"; *kabir* "mighty in number"; *takif*
  "overpowering"; *otzem* "vastness" (number) or "innate strength".
- חזק ואמץ: "be strong and resolute" (Bedersi, Wertheimer, Gra), the greater
  word second; "strong and courageous" only if "courageous" is held for *ometz*
  throughout. Rabbi Shapira-Frankfurter's reversal is a minority reading.
- Trap: "strength" absorbs every word here, "power" absorbs *koach*, *oz* and
  *takif*. When several sit together (Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino counts 36 in *Ohel
  Moed*), assign each its English before drafting and hold it throughout.
