# Animal: behemah / chayah

## Bottom line

*Behemah* is **a domesticated animal**, one that lives among people and serves
them; *chayah* is **a wild animal**, one that lives in open country (Ibn Ezra
and Radak). Ramban suggests a different line, by diet: *behemos* eat only plants,
*chayos* eat flesh. The article leaves both live, the Netziv rejecting Ramban and
Rabbi Chaim Kanievsky defending him. The one thing not to get
wrong is that these are halachic categories before they are descriptions:
*cheilev* is forbidden only in a *behemah*, and *kisui hadam* applies only to a
*chayah*. Where a passage is halachic, do not flatten both to "animal."

## Distinctions

### behemah (בהמה)
Ibn Ezra and Radak: the domesticated animals, which live with people and have a
use for them, whether for their meat or as beasts of burden. Among the kosher
species, *behemah* is limited to three families, the same three that alone may be
brought as *korbanos*: the bovine, ovine and caprine families, that is cattle,
sheep, and goats. The category is not only kosher, though: other, non-kosher
animals such as pigs, camels, and donkeys are seemingly *behemos* as well, which
is why the domestication test, and not the sacrificial list, is what actually
defines the word. Ramban instead defines *behemos* as the herbivores, animals
that feed solely on plants and other flora.

### chayah (חיה)
Ibn Ezra and Radak: the wild animals, which live in uninhabited country, lions
and tigers and bears. Ramban: the carnivores, the meat-eaters. The Netziv favors
Ibn Ezra and Radak and doubts Ramban from Bava Kamma 19b, where Chazal state that
deer do not generally eat meat. On Ramban's rule a deer would then be a *behemah*,
yet a deer is a kosher animal and is not one of the three *behemah* families, and
everybody agrees it is a *chayah*; on the strength of that question the Netziv
rejects Ramban's approach. A recently published letter of Rabbi Chaim Kanievsky,
addressed to Rabbi Chaim Tzvi Lehrfeld, offers a defense of Ramban: a deer does
eat small insects and other creeping creatures, and that is carnivorous enough to
place it among the *chayos* even though it does not eat animal meat.

## Rendering guidance

- Where the halachic category is at stake, render the category: "domesticated
  animal (*behemah*)" and "wild animal (*chayah*)," or keep the Hebrew and gloss
  it once. "Beast" and "animal" will not carry *cheilev* or *kisui hadam*.
- In narrative or *mussar*, *behemah* is "beast" or "animal" and *chayah* is
  "wild beast"; where a *baal mussar* contrasts a man with a
  *behemah*, "beast" is the right word, since the point is the lack of a human
  faculty, not the animal's habitat.
- Trap: English "animal" absorbs both, and "cattle" absorbs *behemah* while
  losing the pig and the donkey. Where a pasuk or a Gemara lists *behemah*
  *chayah* together (a merism for the whole animal world), render "domestic
  animals and wild ones" rather than "animals and beasts," which says nothing.
- Colloquially, the article notes, a Yiddish-speaking bubby calls wild children
  *chayos* or *behemos*; in that register the word is a rebuke to stop acting
  like an animal, and "you animals" carries it better than either taxonomy.
- Second trap: do not carry Ramban's diet criterion into an English rendering,
  since "herbivore" and "carnivore" are his reading of the categories, not the
  words' meaning. Keep them in a note.
