# Beauty: hod / hadar (adar)

## Bottom line

*Hod* is **splendor** or **majesty**: beauty that is a thing's own, inner and
not seen with the eye, the sun's own light (Vilna Gaon; Malbim; Rav Hirsch, of
Hashem, "His essence"). *Hadar* is **glory**: outward, visible, reflected
beauty, the moon's light, the *esrog*'s fruit, the trappings of kingship without
its essence (Vilna Gaon; Malbim; Rabbi Simcha Maimon). *Adar* is *hadar* with
the Aramaic aleph and names the month. In the fixed pair הוד והדר keep the order
and the two words: "splendor and glory". Trap: "glory" absorbs both, and
"majesty" absorbs both; once one English word is assigned to *hod* in a passage,
*hadar* must get the other.

## Distinctions

### hod (הוד)
Root ה-ו-ד. Vilna Gaon: self-beauty, as the sun's light expresses the sun's own
essence; of a person, his inner good, his morality, which is then reflected in
his face. Moshe's *karnei hod* (Rashi to Shemos 34:34) is inner goodness shining
through physically. Malbim: inner beauty, the beauty of character (humble,
merciful, just), which the eye cannot see and only the intellect beholds; "give
of your *hod* to him" (Bamidbar 27:20) is no physical gift but the unseeable
spiritual beauty Moshe passed to Yehoshua. Rabbi Simcha Maimon: hence "the face
of Moshe is like the sun, the face of Yehoshua like the moon" (Bava Basra 75a),
Yehoshua's *hod* being received, as the moon's is. The same root gives *hodaah*,
admission and thanksgiving: bringing to the surface what one knows deep down to
be true, so again *hod* is what lies beneath. Sifrei (Devarim 33:17): *hod* is
the quality of kingship, the full sovereignty; Shlomo receives *hod malchus*
(Divrei HaYamim I 29:25). Of Hashem, Malbim: His *hod* is hidden, deeper than we
can perceive; Rav Hirsch: His *hod* is His essence.

### hadar (הדר) and adar (אדר)
Root ה-ד-ר. Vilna Gaon: reflected beauty, the moon's light whose true source is
the sun; *hadras panim* is the visible beauty of the face. Rabbi Moshe Shapiro:
Aramaic *hadar* means "to return", apt for the moon, which sends back what it
receives. Rabbi Mecklenburg citing Shadal: kin to the Aramaic for going
backwards, since one who beholds a thing in its full glory recoils in awe.
Malbim: outward beauty; the *esrog* tree is *eitz hadar* because its fruit is
beautiful to the eye; of Hashem, His *hadar* is how He interacts with creation
and reveals Himself; Rav Hirsch: His role in the world. Moshe's blessing to
Yosef, "like a firstborn ox, *hadar* is his" (Devarim 33:17), uses *hadar* and
not *hod*: Yehoshua of Efrayim would lead but with only the outer trappings of
kingship, so Moshe is called a king (Zevachim 102a) and Yehoshua never is.
Rabbi Simcha Maimon: for the same reason the Chashmonaim, kohanim and not of
royal stock, have *hadar malchus* (Daniel 11:20), kingship true in practice and
not in essence, against Shlomo's *hod malchus*. Yosef mirrors Yaakov (Bereishis
37:2 and Rashi) yet is not an Av: *hadar*, not *hod*; so the month of Adar,
when Moshe died and Yehoshua took over, belongs to the tribe of Yosef. *Sefer
HaKushyos* (Chassidei Ashkenaz): Adar is named for the *hadar* of the trees as
spring nears, or for Moshe, the glory of Yisrael, born in it. Eichah 1:6, "gone
from the daughter of Tzion is all her *hadar*", is read by the Midrash as
clothing, the outermost layer (as Hashem "wears" *hod* and *hadar*, Tehillim
104:1; Iyov 40:10; Mishlei 31:25).

### The wider cluster
Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) lists ten words for beauty: *yofi*, *hod*,
*hadar*, *adar*, *naeh* (*naaveh*), *ziv*, *ziz*, *tzvi*, *shefer* (*shapir*),
*tov*. This entry settles only *hod* and *hadar*; *shefer* and *tov* are in the
*tov* / *noam* / *shapir* entry, *tzvi* in the *ratzon* entry. *Ziv*, radiance,
is the old name of Iyar.

## Rendering guidance

- *hod*: "splendor" as the default, "majesty" where kingship is in view (Sifrei,
  *hod malchus*: "royal majesty"). *Karnei hod*: "rays of splendor".
- *hadar*: "glory"; "beauty" where the sense is plainly physical (*eitz hadar*,
  *hadras panim*: "a glorious countenance" or "beauty of face"). *Hadar
  malchus*: "royal glory".
- Together, הוד והדר (Tehillim 104:1 and the liturgy): "splendor and glory".
  Do not invert, and do not collapse to "glory and majesty" unless *hod* has
  been "majesty" throughout the passage.
- Trap: "glory" (and "majesty") will happily take both words. Assign before
  drafting: *hod* is the inner, owned light; *hadar* is the outer, reflected
  light. That inner/outer axis is shared by the Gaon, Malbim and Rav Hirsch, so
  it is safe to lean on.
- Second trap: *kavod* is also commonly "glory" (*kevod Hashem*). In a passage
  with *kavod* and *hadar* both present, give *kavod* "honor" and keep "glory"
  for *hadar*; see the *kavod* / *yakar* entry.
- *Hodaah*: "acknowledgment" or "thanksgiving" by context; the etymology (Malbim
  and the column) supports "acknowledgment" as the root sense.
