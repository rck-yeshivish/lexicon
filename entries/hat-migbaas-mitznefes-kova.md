# Hat: migbaas / mitznefes / kova

## Bottom line

*Migbaas* is the ordinary *kohen*'s **headdress** and *mitznefes* the *kohen
gadol*'s **turban**. *Kova* is possibly a **hard hat** or **helmet** rather than
a hat, since every one of its six appearances in *Tanach* is part of a warrior's
dress. Rashi and Ramban maintain that *migbaas* and *mitznefes* were the same
type of hat and are simply synonyms; the other authorities distinguish them.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether migbaas and mitznefes are two hats.**
- The Meiri and the Vilna Gaon: two hats. The ordinary *kohen*'s *migbaas* is
  a plain hat (Meiri), pre-sewn (Vilna Gaon); the *kohen gadol*'s *mitznefes*
  is a long cloth he winds around his head.
- Others: both were wrapped, and the *mitznefes* is distinguished by reaching
  down to the beard.
- Rambam: the *kohen gadol*'s *mitznefes* encircles the perimeter of the head
  without covering it, while the ordinary *kohen*'s hat actually covers the
  head.
- Rashi and Ramban: *migbaas* and *mitznefes* were the same type of hat, and
  the words are simply synonyms.

## Distinctions

The setting: the regular *kohen* wears four garments, pants, a belt, a hat and a
tunic; the *kohen gadol* wears four more, a breastplate, an apron, a robe and a
head-plate (Mishnah Yoma 7:5). Some explain that the *kohen gadol*'s clothes
differ from the regular *kohen*'s in that they do not wear the same type of hat.

### migbaas (מגבעת)
Root ג-ב-ע.

- The Meiri ties *migbaas* to *kova* (roots ג-ב-ע and כ-ב-ע) because the
  ordinary *kohen*'s hat is a plain one.
- The Vilna Gaon: a *migbaas* comes pre-sewn, ready to put on.
- Abarbanel connects the word to *givah*, a hill, because the hat carried two
  hill-like humps on top and tied under the chin.
- Rabbi Avigdor Miller: a hat means dignity. *Migbaas* is from *givah*, a hill,
  since it raises the head and makes a hill on top of it, and *pa'arei
  hamigbaos* speaks of the hat's beauty; *kova* is the same as *govah*, *givah*.
  So a hat makes a man taller, and wearing one shows dignity when speaking to
  Hashem.
- Rambam has the ordinary *kohen*'s hat actually covering the head.

### mitznefes (מצנפת)
Root צ-נ-ף.
- The Meiri: an especially long cloth that the *kohen gadol* winds (*tzonef*)
  around his head, so the name records the wrapping. The Vilna Gaon says the
  same, against the pre-sewn *migbaas*.
- Others hold both were wrapped, and the *mitznefes* is distinguished by
  reaching down to the beard.
- Rambam has it encircling the perimeter of the head without covering it.
- *Tosafos HaRosh* (Yoma 71b) links *mitznefes* to *tznif*, which alludes to the
  honor of wearing a royal diadem.

Against all of these, Rashi and Ramban maintain that *migbaas* and *mitznefes*
were both the same type of hat and are simply synonyms. Josephus writes that
the regular *kohen* wore a "Masnaemphthes," a
Greekized *mitznefes*, describing it as a cap that does not come to a point and
does not encircle the whole head, a crown-like bonnet covering most of the head
but not all of it. On that view, Rabbi Klein suggests, one may argue that even if
*migbaas* and *mitznefes* name the same hat they recall different elements of
it: *migbaas* denotes the hat-like form of the thing, while *mitznefes* focuses
on the honor that wearing it brings, in line with *Tosafos HaRosh* on *tznif*.

### kova (כובע)
Root כ-ב-ע. The everyday word for a hat in Modern Hebrew, but in *Tanach* it
appears six times and always as part of a warrior's kit (Shmuel I 17:5,
Yeshayahu 59:17, Yirmiyahu 46:4, Yechezkel 27:10 and 38:5, Divrei HaYamim II
26:14), so that it is possibly a "hard hat" or even a "helmet" rather than a "hat."

## Rendering guidance

- Do not let English "hat" absorb all three words. *Kova* in a pasuk is
  **helmet**; *migbaas* is **headdress** or **cap**; *mitznefes* is **turban**.
- Where the two priestly hats stand in one verse or one halachic list (Shemos
  28:4 and 28:40, Mishnah Yoma 7:5), keep two distinct English words even if you
  follow Rashi and Ramban that the object is the same, since the passage is
  distinguishing the *kohen gadol* from the ordinary *kohen*.
- Where a source treats *migbaas* and *mitznefes* as one hat under two names,
  the useful line is Rabbi Klein's own: *migbaas* names the shape, *mitznefes*
  names the honor (the honor half rests on *Tosafos HaRosh*).
