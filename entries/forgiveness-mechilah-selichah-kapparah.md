# Forgiveness: mechilah / selichah / kapparah

## Bottom line

*Selichah* is **forgiveness** proper: the bond between the sinner and the One
sinned against is mended (Rav Hirsch); it is the Biblical word (*salachti*,
Bamidbar 14:20). *Mechilah* is **pardon**: the penalty remitted, the debt
waived (Rav Hirsch); it is Rabbinic, never Biblical, and doubles as the word for
forgoing what one is owed. *Kapparah* is **atonement**: every consequence of
the sin wiped out (Rav Hirsch), at a price and usually through a korban
(Malbim). The liturgy's סלח לנו, מחל לנו, כפר לנו wants three English words,
"forgive us, pardon us, grant us atonement"; never let "forgive" cover all
three. Rav Hirsch's three-way map is followed here because it is the only
account that places all three words.

## Distinctions

### selichah (סליחה)
Root ס-ל-ח. Close to fifty Biblical occurrences, most famously Hashem's answer
to Moshe after the Meraglim, *salachti kidvarecha* (Bamidbar 14:20). Its
cognates cluster around the forgiveness a korban brings (Vayikra 4:20-35,
5:10-26); the column notes the linguists' link to Akkadian and Aramaic words
for sprinkling, the blood on the mizbeach. Rabbi Moshe Meth (*Matteh Moshe*)
and Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik of Tirna (*Sefer HaMinhagim*): *selichah* pairs with
*avon* (Bamidbar 14:19). Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: *selichah* is
quantitatively the stronger, it reaches the whole sin, but only by deferring
its punishment; the sin itself is left as it was. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon
Wertheimer: a reprieve, the sinner is not punished all at once but given time
to repay slowly. Rav Hirsch: the correction of the relationship between the
sinner and the one (or One) he wronged. The word also names the *piyyut*
genre, *Selichos*, once said inside the *selach lanu* berachah.

### mechilah (מחילה)
Root מ-ח-ל, of uncertain etymology. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur: absent from Tanach,
present in Rabbinic literature and the liturgy; besides forgiveness it means
cancelling a debt or forgoing what one deserves. Matteh Moshe and Sefer
HaMinhagim pair it with *pesha*, and since no pasuk has *mechilah* they prove
it from *mocheh peshaecha* (Yeshayahu 43:25), reading *mochel* as *mocheh*, to
wipe away; Rabbi Aharon Fuld (glosses to HaBachur): *mochel* is *mocheh* plus
*al*. Rabbi Moshe Yitzchak Avigdor and Rabbi Yehuda Aszad: kin to *yachel*
(Bamidbar 30:3), which Targum renders cancel, nullify. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's
rule that a leading *mem* inverts a two-letter root (*chok* engrave, *machak*
erase; *rad* rule, *marad* rebel) is applied by the column: ח-ל is hollowing
and profaning, so מ-ח-ל refills the sinner who had emptied himself of his
holiness. Mecklenburg: qualitatively the stronger, it can erase part of the
sin outright even if not all of it. Wertheimer: immediate and complete
forgiveness. Rav Hirsch: commuting the punishment the guilty party deserves.

### kapparah (כפרה)
Root כ-פ-ר. Matteh Moshe and Sefer HaMinhagim pair it with *chet* (Shemos
32:30); Rabbi Yechezkel Landau: elsewhere it goes with *avon* (Mishlei 16:6,
Yeshayahu 27:9, Tehillim 78:38), but the Chumash pairing with *chet* governs.
Malbim records two derivations. From *kofer*, ransom: *kapparah* is a price
paid for forgiveness, usually a korban (hence Yom HaKippurim, and the Erev Yom
Kippur *kapparos*). From *kapporet*, cover: *kapparah* covers the sin on the
surface so it is no longer seen, without removing it. Either way the word
recalls the price of sin and the transactional side of atonement. Rav Hirsch:
the total negation of any negative ramification of the sin. Mecklenburg and
Wertheimer do not place *kapparah* against the other two.

## Rendering guidance

- The triple: "forgive us, pardon us, grant us atonement." Keep three words.
- *Mechilah* between people or in money: "waive," "forgo," "excuse" ("he
  waived his honor," "she forgave the debt" in the plain financial sense).
  "Pardon" is for the penal sense.
- *Kapparah* is never "forgiveness": it carries the korban, the price, the
  covering. "Atonement" is the settled English and Yom Kippur fixes it.
- Trap: "forgiveness" absorbs all three, and "atone" is sometimes stretched to
  *selichah*. If a passage turns on the order or the strength of the words
  (Mecklenburg's deferral versus erasure, Wertheimer's slow versus immediate),
  the English must keep three distinct nouns.
- The sin pairings are a useful check: *selichah* for *avon*, *mechilah* for
  *pesha*, *kapparah* for *chet* (see the sin entry, `sin-chet-avon-pesha.md`).
