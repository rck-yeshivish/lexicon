# Forgiveness: mechilah / selichah / kapparah

## Bottom line

*Selichah* is **forgiveness** proper: the bond between the sinner and the One
sinned against is mended (Rav Hirsch); it is the Biblical word (*salachti*,
Bamidbar 14:20). *Mechilah* is **pardon**: the penalty remitted (Rav Hirsch);
it is Rabbinic, never Biblical, and doubles as the word for forgoing what one is
owed. *Kapparah* is **atonement**: every consequence of the sin wiped out (Rav
Hirsch), at a price and usually through a korban (Malbim). The liturgy's סלח
לנו, מחל לנו, כפר לנו uses all three. Rav Hirsch's three-way map is slightly
more comprehensive than the others: neither Mecklenburg nor Wertheimer addresses
how their two words differ from *kapparah* at all. Which of *selichah* and
*mechilah* is the fuller is disputed: *selichah* for Rav Hirsch and Rabbi Zev
Hoberman, for whom it restores the love that stood before the sin; *mechilah*
for Mecklenburg, Wertheimer and Rabbi Dosa the Greek, for whom *selichah* only
defers the debt. Rabbi Eliyahu Menachem Margulies, following the Vilna Gaon,
gives each its own object: *selichah* forgives the infraction, *mechilah*
forgoes the slight to Hashem's honor.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Which is the fuller forgiveness.**

- *Selichah*: Rav Hirsch, for whom it mends the bond where *mechilah* only
  commutes the penalty; Rabbi Zev Hoberman (*Ze'ev Yitraf*, Yom Kippur ch. 55),
  for whom *selichah* restores the love that stood before the misdeed while
  *mechilah* only erases it and leaves the relationship at neutral.
- *Mechilah*: Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg, for whom *selichah* reaches the
  whole sin but only defers its punishment; Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, for
  whom *selichah* is a reprieve and *mechilah* immediate and complete; and Rabbi
  Dosa the Greek (to Devarim 9:18): *selichah* defers the debt, *mechilah*
  forgives it at once.
- Rabbi Eliyahu Menachem Margulies (*Emunas Eliezer*, *Yerach HaEisanim* ch. 10)
  rejects Rabbi Hoberman's explanation, reasoning from the Yom Kippur liturgy,
  which calls Hashem the One who grants *mechilah* and also *selichah*, that
  *selichah* is the more encompassing.

**What each forgives.**

- Rabbi Margulies, following the Vilna Gaon: *selichah* forgives the infraction
  itself, *mechilah* is Hashem forgoing the slight to His honor. So *mechilah*
  goes with *pesha* and the relation of servant to king, *selichah* with *chet*
  and the relation of son to father. He ties *mechilah* to *machol*, a circle
  around a central point: every Jew holds a point of pure intent, and Rabbi
  Yitzchak Hutner (*Pachad Yitzchak*, Yom Kippur 25:2) explains that Hashem
  forgives by reviving it.

## Distinctions

### selichah (סליחה)
Root ס-ל-ח. Close to fifty Biblical occurrences, most famously Hashem's answer
to Moshe after the Meraglim, *salachti kidvarecha* (Bamidbar 14:20). Its
cognates appear many times for the forgiveness a korban brings (Vayikra 4:20-35,
5:10-26).
- Linguists connect *selichah* to the Akkadian *salahu* and the Aramaic
  *zelicha*, both meaning "sprinkling." This may be a reference to the
  sprinkling of the blood on the mizbeach, the main component of the korbanos.
- Rabbi Moshe Meth (*Matteh Moshe*) and Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik of Tirna (*Sefer
  HaMinhagim*): *selichah* pairs with *avon* (Bamidbar 14:19).
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: *selichah* may be quantitatively the stronger,
  since it reaches the whole quantity of the sin, but only by completely
  deferring its punishment to a later time; the sin itself is left as it was.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: a reprieve, the sinner is given time to repay slowly.
- Rav Hirsch: the correction of the relationship between the sinner and the one
  (or One) he wronged.

The word also names the *piyyut* genre, *Selichos*, originally said inside the
*selach lanu* berachah of Shemoneh Esrei and nowadays before davening begins or
after Shemoneh Esrei.

### mechilah (מחילה)
Root מ-ח-ל, which Ernest Klein's *A Comprehensive Etymological Dictionary of the
Hebrew Language* calls of uncertain etymology.
- Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur: absent from Tanach, present in Rabbinic literature and
  the liturgy; besides forgiveness it means cancelling a debt or forgoing what
  one deserves.
- Matteh Moshe and Sefer HaMinhagim pair it with *pesha*, and since no pasuk has
  *mechilah* they prove it from *mocheh peshaecha* (Yeshayahu 43:25), seemingly
  reading *mochel* as *mocheh*, to wipe away; Rabbi Aharon Fuld (glosses to
  HaBachur) suggests that *mochel* is *mocheh* plus *al*.
- Rabbi Moshe Yitzchak Avigdor and Rabbi Yehuda Aszad: kin to *yachel* (Bamidbar
  30:3), which Targum renders cancel, nullify.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim proposes that many three-letter roots opening with
  *mem* are really the remaining two letters with the *mem* flipping the sense
  to its opposite (*chok* engrave, *machak* erase; *leitz* scorn, *meilitz*
  defend; *na* movement, *mana* withholding; *rad* rule, *marad* rebel, and
  more). Pappenheim himself does not apply this to *mechilah*; Rabbi Reuven
  Chaim Klein suggests that perhaps we may: ח-ל is primarily hollowing out, and
  in many cases profaning something holy by emptying it, and a Jew who sins
  makes himself a hollow vessel, so מ-ח-ל would be the mechanism by which Hashem
  fills the hollow and re-infuses the penitent with holiness.
- Mecklenburg: qualitatively the stronger, it can erase part of the sin outright
  even if not all of it.
- Wertheimer: immediate and complete forgiveness.
- Rav Hirsch: commuting the punishment the guilty party deserves.
- Rabbi Dosa the Greek (to Devarim 9:18): *selichah* defers the debt, *mechilah*
  forgives it at once.
- Rabbi Zev Hoberman (*Ze'ev Yitraf*, Yom Kippur ch. 55): *selichah* erases a
  misdeed and also restores the love that existed before it, while
  *mechilah* only erases the misdeed and waives the grievance, leaving the
  relationship at neutral. Rabbi Eliyahu Menachem Margulies (*Emunas Eliezer*,
  *Yerach HaEisanim* ch. 10) disagrees, since the Yom Kippur liturgy calls
  Hashem the One who grants *mechilah* and also *selichah*, which implies
  *selichah* is the greater. Following the Vilna Gaon, he explains *selichah* as
  forgiving the infraction itself and *mechilah* as Hashem forgoing the slight
  to His honor; so *mechilah* goes with *pesha*, deliberate sin, and the
  relation of servant to king, and *selichah* with *chet*, inadvertent sin, and
  the relation of son to father. He ties *mechilah* to *machol*, a circle dance:
  as a circle surrounds a central point, every Jew, however wicked, holds a
  point of pure intent, and Rabbi Yitzchak Hutner (*Pachad Yitzchak*, Yom Kippur
  25:2) explains that Hashem forgives by reviving that buried intent, which
  calls the deliberateness of the sin into question.

### kapparah (כפרה)
Root כ-פ-ר.
- Matteh Moshe and Sefer HaMinhagim pair it with *chet* (Shemos 32:30); Rabbi
  Yechezkel Landau: elsewhere it goes with *avon* (Mishlei 16:6, Yeshayahu 27:9,
  Tehillim 78:38), but the Chumash pairing with *chet* governs.
- Malbim records two derivations. From *kofer*, ransom: *kapparah* is a price
  paid for forgiveness, usually a korban (*kapparah* also gives its name to Yom
  HaKippurim and to the Erev Yom Kippur *kapparos*). From *kapporet*, cover:
  *kapparah* covers the sin on the surface so it is no longer visible, but does
  not completely remove or wipe it away. Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein offers a
  harmonization of his own, that in English one "covers" a cost by offsetting
  it, so covering and paying a price can be two facets of one idea. Malbim's own
  summary is that *kapparah* recalls the price paid for sin and focuses on the
  transactional side of atonement.
- Rav Hirsch: the total negation of any negative ramification of the sin.

Mecklenburg and Wertheimer do not place *kapparah* against the other two.

## Rendering guidance

- The triple: "forgive us, pardon us, grant us atonement." Keep three words.
- *Mechilah* between people or in money: "waive," "forgo," "excuse" ("he
  waived his honor," "she forgave the debt" in the plain financial sense).
  "Pardon" is for the penal sense.
- *Kapparah* is never "forgiveness": it carries the korban, the price, the
  covering. "Atonement" is the settled English and Yom Kippur fixes it.
- Trap: "forgiveness" absorbs all three, and "atone" is sometimes stretched to
  *selichah*. If a passage turns on the order or the strength of the words
  (Mecklenburg's deferral versus erasure, Wertheimer's slow versus immediate),
  the English must keep three distinct nouns.
- The sin pairings are a useful check: *selichah* for *avon*, *mechilah* for
  *pesha*, *kapparah* for *chet* (see the sin entry, [sin](sin-chet-avon-pesha.html)).
