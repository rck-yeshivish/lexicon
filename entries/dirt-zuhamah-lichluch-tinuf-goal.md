# Dirt: zuhamah / lichluch / tinuf / go'al / elach

## Bottom line

*Go'al* is dirtying by something inherently disgusting (blood, excrement,
forbidden food): **defilement, befouling**. *Tinuf* and *lichluch* are dirtying
by something not disgusting in itself but unwanted where it landed (Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim): **soiling** and **grime**, with *lichluch* specifically wet
(Mishnah *Mikvaos* 9:4). *Zuhamah* is the unwanted residue that clings and
usually smells: **filth**. *Elach* is the spoiling of something homogeneous by
an incompatible admixture: **tainted, sullied**. The trap is that all five reach
for English "dirty"; the difference is whether the dirtying agent is disgusting
in itself (*go'al*), merely misplaced (*tinuf*, *lichluch*), or a foreign
element mixed in (*elach*).

## Distinctions

### elach (אלח)
Root א-ל-ח; three occurrences in Tanach (Iyov 15:16, Tehillim 14:3, 53:4).
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: not soiling by something disgusting, but the ruin that
comes when something of another type is mixed into an otherwise uniform
substance (grape juice poured into milk is now undrinkable, and only because
the combination is incompatible). Pappenheim takes the initial *aleph* as
extraneous and the working root as the two-letter ל-ח, "liquid" (as in *davar
lach*), so *elach* is an unwanted liquid sullying something else. Menachem Ibn
Saruk, Ibn Janach and Radak treat א-ל-ח as a full triliteral root. Rav Hirsch
(to Tehillim 14:3) links it to א-ל-ה, which yields *allah*, "curse," through the
interchange of *hei* and *ches*: both name a clash in which one party is set
against the other.

### lichluch (לכלוך)
Post-biblical; absent from Tanach, once in the Mishnah, then the standard word
for dirtiness in the Gemara and later. Pappenheim's ל-ח, "wet," fits its one
mishnaic use: *lichluch* of wet excrement on the skin is no *chatzitzah* for
immersion (*Mikvaos* 9:4), since the wet grime merges with the *mikvah* water,
while the same matter dried would interpose. Rashi uses *lichluch* to describe
the spiritual grime of forbidden food (to Daniel 1:8). The Gemara's anecdote in
*Nedarim* 66b turns on a woman named *Lichluchis*, "dirty," and Rabbi Yishmael
ben Rabbi Yose's ruling that the name fits.

### tinuf (טינוף)
Root ט-נ-פ, once in Tanach: "I have washed my feet, how shall I dirty them"
(Shir HaShirim 5:3). Three mishnaic uses: *tinofes*, spoiled produce tolerated
up to a twenty-fourth in a wholesale purchase (*Bava Basra* 6:2); uterine blood
that has *tinuf* (*Bechoros* 3:1); feet dirtied in a cave pool (*Machshirin*
4:5). Pappenheim: with *tinuf*, as with *lichluch*, the dirtying agent is not
itself repulsive; ketchup is food until it lands on a shirt. He derives it from
the two-letter root ט-פ, whose core sense he gives as "secondary, subordinate"
(hence *taf*, children; *tipah*, a drop; *matif*, hortatory speech), because the
soiling element is secondary to the object it clings to. Rabbi Shlomo Ibn
Parchon (recorded by Shadal to Bamidbar 35:33) connects *chonef*, flattery or
hypocrisy, to *tinuf*, moral pollution beside physical, on the interchange of
*ches* and *tes*.

### go'al (גועל)
Thirteen occurrences in Tanach. Pappenheim: dirtied by a pollutant that is
disgusting in its own right, excrement (Tzefaniah 3:1), blood (Yeshayahu 59:3,
Eichah 4:14). Daniel refused to *defile* himself (*yisga'al*) with the king's
food (Daniel 1:8); Hashem's garments are *befouled* (*ega'alti*) with Edom's
blood (Yeshayahu 63:3). Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg ties it to *geulah* and *go'el*
under ג-(א)-ל: a single axis of nearness and distance, with *go'al* naming what
one distances oneself from and redemption naming the drawing near; he also links
ג-ע-ל, "repugnant," through the interchange of *aleph* and *ayin*. Pappenheim
instead ties it to the two-letter ג-ל, "round," through *galal*, the round
pellets of animal droppings that no one disputes are filth.

### zuhamah (זוהמה)
Root ז-ה-ם, once in Tanach: to the sick man food looks like *zuhamah* (Iyov
33:20). Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): the foul sweat a body gives off, or the
residue thrown off in preparing certain foods. Pappenheim reduces it to the
single letter *zayin*, "movement," and reads it as the fluid that oozes out
after an unhealthy build-up, hence anything excess and unwanted. Donash Ibn
Labrat: *zuhamah* is specifically the bad *smell* of something dirty, while
*tinuf* is plain soiling. Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 7:12) relates ז-ה-ם to ז-ע-ם,
*za'am*, anger or curse: both name a thing rejected by the beholder. In the
Mishnah it covers a blemished, foul-smelling animal (*Bechoros* 6:12), fish odor
removed by an onion (*Terumos* 10:1), and treating saplings before *shevi'is*
(*Shevi'is* 2:4; manure per Rabbi Shimon of Shantz, foul-smelling oil to repel
insects per Rambam). The Gemara: cold water removes *zuhamah* from the hands
better than hot (*Chullin* 105a), and one who is *mezuham* may not recite a
berachah just as a *mezuham* Kohen may not serve (*Berachos* 53b), a halachah
taught by Rabbi Zuhamai, so named for it (Rabbi Yaakov Emden there, who also
reads the name Zaham of Divrei HaYamim II 11:19 as the same root). Pseudo-
Rashbam (to Tehillim 26:10) glosses *zimah* as *tinuf*, from which Rabbi
Steinberg suggests he read ז-מ-ה as a metathesis of ז-ה-ם.

Rabbi Tanchum HaYerushalmi (*HaMadrich HaMaspik*) treats *tinuf*, *lichluch* and
*zuhamah* as plain synonyms, and Ibn Janach and Radak equate *lichluch* with
*tinuf* in their respective *Sifrei HaShorashim*. Where they are equated, any
difference is one of provenance rather than sense: *tinuf* looks like biblical
Hebrew and *lichluch* like a later Aramaic borrowing, or the reverse, that
*lichluch* grows from biblical ל-ח while *tinuf* is the loanword the Targumim
prefer. Rabbi Shaul Goldman's summary of rabbinic usage: *tinuf* for bodily
fluids or contact with animals, *lichluch* for unwanted wetness, *zuhamah* for
an unwanted contaminant.

## Rendering guidance

- The famous *zuhamah* of the snake (*Shabbos* 146a, *Yevamos* 103b, *Avodah
  Zarah* 26b) is best left transliterated in a mussar or aggadic context; it is
  not ordinary dirt but a clinging contamination, and "filth" alone loses the
  sense of something injected and residual.
- Trap: English "defile" will swallow *go'al*, *tinuf* and *tamei* alike. Keep
  "defile" for *go'al* (the agent is genuinely repulsive), use "soil" for
  *tinuf*, and never let either stand for ritual *tumah*, which is a different
  category entirely.
- Second trap: "dirty" absorbs *lichluch* and *tinuf*. When a passage turns on
  the wet/dry distinction (*Mikvaos* 9:4), render *lichluch* as "wet grime" and
  keep "soiling" for *tinuf*.
- *Elach* rarely wants "dirty" at all. Tehillim 14:3, *ne'elachu*, reads better
  as "they have become tainted" or "gone rancid" than "they are filthy."
- *Mezuham* of a person (*Berachos* 53b) is "unclean and foul-smelling," not
  merely "dirty"; the odor is the operative fact.
