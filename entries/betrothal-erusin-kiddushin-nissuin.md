# Betrothal: erusin / kiddushin / nissuin (hekdesh, shidduchin)

## Bottom line

*Erusin* and *kiddushin* name the same halachic act, the first of the two stages
of marriage: **betrothal**, after which she is halachically his wife and adultery
applies, though the couple may not yet live together. *Nissuin*, effected by
chuppah, is the second stage: **marriage** proper. *Shidduchin* is what English
calls **engagement**, an agreement to marry later. The trap is rendering *erusin*
"engagement": modern Hebrew uses it that way, but Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 22:16)
already warns that the Torah's *erusin* is not that. Where the two words are
contrasted, *kiddushin* carries the *hekdesh* sense of setting apart and
*erusin* the sense of binding or tying.

## Distinctions

### erusin (אירוסין)
The Biblical term (Shemos 22:15, Devarim 20:7, 22:23-27, 28:30). Chazal read
*morashah* in Devarim 33:4 as though *me'orasah*, betrothed, so that the Torah is
betrothed to Klal Yisrael as a wife to her husband (*Berachos* 57a, *Pesachim*
49b). Rabbi Shalom Mansoura of Sanna: *erusin* is an expression of tying. Rabbi
Yitzchak Ratzabi gives two ways to take that. Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein reads
א-ר-ש as equivalent to א-ר-ז, since sin/samech and zayin interchange, and א-ר-ז
is something bound tightly (Yechezkel 27:24), related by metathesis to *eizor*, a
tight belt. Or, simpler, transpose the last two letters and get א-ס-ר, to bind:
the betrothed woman is bound to her husband, released only by a get or by his
death. Rabbi Yitzchak Vana: *arusah* is from *eres*, poison, since one who
approaches her illicitly incurs the death penalty as from a snake or scorpion.
Another Yemenite scholar cited by Rabbi Ratzabi: *arusah* as in *aris*, a
sharecropper who holds a partial claim, since the betrothed husband has a partial
claim on her (adultery applies) but not a full one (he does not yet inherit her).
Rabbi Ratzabi's own two suggestions turn on the interchange of aleph and ayin:
ע-ר-ס, to mix (as in *arisah*, kneaded dough), for the joining of man and wife,
or *eres*, a bed.

### kiddushin (קידושין)
The term of the Mishnah, and the name of the masechta. *Kiddushin* 2b: the
Rabbanan Savorai ask why the Mishnah (*Kiddushin* 2:1) uses it rather than the
Biblical word, and answer that it is from *hekdesh*, consecrated property: he
forbids her to all others as consecration forbids all men to benefit. Rabbi
Yitzchak Vana: the parallel to *hekdesh* is that betrothal, like consecration,
raises the severity of the prohibition on one who transgresses with her. Rabbi
Alexander Sender Shor and Rabbi Elazar Moshe Horowitz: mid'oraisa the betrothed
couple may already live together, and only the rabbinic enactment forbids her to
her own husband until nissuin (*Kallah* 1:1, Rashi to *Kesuvos* 8a); so only
after that enactment does she resemble *hekdesh*, forbidden to everyone
including her husband, and that is why the term is the Rabbis' and not the
Torah's. The Taz (to *Even HaEzer* 34:2) holds that the two-stage structure
itself confers a kedushah found nowhere else, which likewise suits a term coined
by those who instituted the structure.

Tosafos (to *Kiddushin* 2b) read *kiddushin* differently, from *kiddush* in the
sense of preparing or designating (Shemos 19:10, Bamidbar 11:18); *kedeishah*, a
harlot, is a cognate because she is set aside for a purpose (Rashi to Bereishis
38:21 and Devarim 23:18). Two supports: Rabbi Avraham HaLevi of Barcelona
(*Chiddushei HaRa'ah* to *Kiddushin* 2b) and Rabbi Yosef Ibn Ezra (*Atzmos
Yosef*) note that if *mekudeshes* meant consecrated, the groom would be
forbidding her to himself, which he plainly does not intend; and Rabbi Eliezer
Ashkenazi notes that consecration would call for *mukdeshes*, whereas the formula
reads *mekudeshes*, set aside for him. Tosafos (to *Kiddushin* 7b) nonetheless
apply the *hekdesh* paradigm in halachah: one who betroths "half" a woman with
the language of *kiddushin* effects a full betrothal, as consecrating half an
animal sanctifies all of it, but one who uses the language of *erusin* effects
only half.

### nissuin (נישואין)
The second stage, effected by chuppah, after which the couple live together.
*Kesubos* 4:5 defines it as the father handing the daughter over to the husband.
Only after nissuin is the rabbinic prohibition between the betrothed pair lifted.

### shidduchin (שידוכין)
The Talmudic term for what is colloquially called engagement, a couple who have
agreed to marry but have not done so.

## Rendering guidance

- *Erusin* and *kiddushin*, standing alone for the halachic act: "betrothal."
  Never "engagement," and avoid "inchoate marriage," which is heavy and obscure.
- *Nissuin*: "marriage," or "the second stage of marriage" where the two-stage
  structure is the subject. *Shidduchin*: "engagement" or "the match."
- Where a passage contrasts *erusin* with *kiddushin* (Tosafos to *Kiddushin*
  7b), English "betrothal" will absorb both. Keep the Hebrew terms and translate
  the concept: "betrothal effected in the language of *kiddushin*."
- *Mekudeshes* in the formula under the chuppah: "you are betrothed to me," or
  "designated to me," following Tosafos and the Ra'ah. "Consecrated to me" imports
  the very sense those Rishonim rule out.
- *Hekdesh* in this discussion: "consecrated property," so that the analogy to
  *kiddushin* stays visible.
- Trap: a Hebrew passage may use *kiddushin* for the act and *kedushah* for the
  holiness of the union in adjacent lines (the Taz). Do not render both by the
  same English word; the argument depends on their being felt as related but
  distinct.
