# One: echad / achad / chad (achat, echat, ach)

## Bottom line

*Echad* is the ordinary and overwhelmingly common word for **one**, and where
Chazal read it closely it means **the one that matters**, the choice or unique
one. *Achad* is the rarer form and splits in two (Rabbi Wolf Heidenheim): in the
construct ("one of the mountains") it carries the same weight as *echad*, **one
of the important ones**; in the absolute, recognisable because a *mem* follows
("*achad me...*"), it means **any one of them**, and implies the lesser, not the
greater. *Chad* is *echad* with the *aleph* dropped, Aramaic in almost every
occurrence. *Achat* / *echat* are the feminine, one word vowelised two ways.
Never let "one" flatten the *echad* / absolute-*achad* contrast where a derashah
turns on it.

## Distinctions

### echad (אחד)
634 occurrences. Plainly the numeral one, and beyond that the joining of parts
into a single whole. Chazal read a connotation of importance into it: *Megillah*
28a derives from "the *one* sheep shall you offer in the morning" (Bamidbar
28:4) that the *Korban Tamid* must come from the choicest animal, *echad*
marking the pick of the flock. So too "and *one* of the lads answered" (Shmuel I
17:18), which Rashi, citing *Sanhedrin* 93b, reads as the outstanding member of
Shaul's entourage, namely Doeg the Edomite. The word is bound up with Hashem
Himself: *Hashem Elokeinu Hashem Echad* (Devarim 6:4). On that strength the
Midrash (*Bamidbar Rabbah* 18:10; *Tanchuma Korach* 7) reads Moshe's "I did not
take a donkey of *echad* of them" (Bamidbar 16:15) as *hekdesh*, property
belonging to the Ultimate One.

### achad (אחד, construct)
65 occurrences in all. In the construct form it is simply "one of," and by
Heidenheim's account carries the same freight of importance as *echad*: "on
*achad* of the mountains that I will tell you" (Bereishis 22:2), which is Har
HaMoriah; "*achad* of the nation nearly lay with your wife" (Bereishis 26:10),
which Rashi reads as Avimelech the king himself; "like *achad* of the tribes of
Israel" said to Dan (Bereishis 49:16), which Rashi and *Bereishis Rabbah* 98:13
read as one of the *distinguished* tribes, Yehudah.

### achad (אחד, absolute)
Heidenheim's second form looks like a construct but is not; the tell is a
following *mem* ("one *from* them"), since a true construct swallows the "of."
Here the connotation inverts: *not* important, any ordinary member of the set.
"Take with you *achad* of the lads" (Shmuel I 9:3), a servant of no standing;
"he shall be brought to Aharon the Kohen or to *achad* of his sons the Kohanim"
(Vayikra 13:2), that is, any ordinary Kohen as against the Kohen Gadol. This is
why Bamidbar 16:15 says *echad* in its first clause and *achad* in its second:
had the first clause read *achad*, the Midrash's *hekdesh* reading, which turns
on greater honour, would collapse. Rabbi Moshe Sofer applies the same rule to
"behold the man has become like *achad* of them" (Bereishis 3:22): absolute
form, so Adam after the sin stood level with the *lowest* creatures rather than
above them.

Malbim is not consistent here. In *Ayelet HaShachar* (introduction to Vayikra
69) and to *Parashas Tazria* 43 he adopts Heidenheim's two forms, and his
comment to Bereishis 3:22 runs close to Rabbi Moshe Sofer's. But in *Artzos
HaChaim* (Orach Chaim 6:1) he explains the *achad* of *Asher Yatzar* as "the
most important" organs, citing Bereishis 22:2 and 26:10, though that *achad* is
not in the construct; on Heidenheim's rule it should mean the opposite.

### ach (אח)
Normally "brother" (also an exclamation of grief, and an "oven"). Rabbi Yonah
Ibn Janach and Radak (to Yechezkel 18:10 and in *Sefer HaShorashim*) insist that
in that one pasuk it means "one." Targum and Rashi there keep "brother." Ibn
Janach finds a second case in Tehillim 49:8; Radak rejects it and reads
"brother."

### chad (חד) and chadah (חדה)
Radak (to Yechezkel 33:30 and *Sefer HaShorashim*): *chad* is *echad* with the
initial *aleph* clipped for brevity. That is its only Hebrew occurrence; the
other five are the Aramaic of Daniel (2:31, 3:19, 6:3, 7:5, 7:16). Feminine
*chadah* appears nine times, all in the Aramaic of Daniel and Ezra; *chada* with
the definite *aleph* is not in Tanach but is common in the Bavli and Targumim.

### achat / echat (אחת)
The feminine. Ibn Janach (*Sefer HaShorashim*): feminine of *echad*/*achad*,
where the expected *echadet* drops its *dalet* to avoid *dalet* and *tav* back
to back. Radak in *Sefer HaShorashim* disagrees, making *achat*/*echat* the
feminine of *ach* and holding that *echad*/*achad* have no true feminine; in his
*Michlol* he treats all of them as one stock, *ach* being *echad* minus the
*dalet* and *chad* being *echad* minus the *aleph*. Practically, *achat* (over
200 times) is the standard form and *echat* (under 70) the positional variant,
typically at the end of a pasuk or under an *esnachta*, so the pair is one word
and not two senses.

### the biliteral reading
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces *echad*, *achad* and *chad* to the two-letter
root ח-ד, "singularity," what has no counterpart to make it two. From it he
derives *yachad* ("together," many sub-units become one), *chad*/*chidud*
("sharp," the whole force brought onto one point), and *chidah* ("riddle," which
takes all of one's mental force sharpened onto a single question). Pappenheim
does not treat *ach* here, holding it never means "one." Menachem Ibn Saruk and
Pappenheim keep *ach* ("brother," א-ח) separate from the *one* words; Rabbi
Shmuel Yehudah Steiger (*Avnei Shayish*) joins them, all of them naming a
closeness and joining like brotherhood.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: *echad*, *achad*, *achat*, *echat*, *chad* all render "one." The
  differences are connotative, not lexical, so do not manufacture a distinction
  in English where the source is not making one.
- Where a derashah is running, mark it: *echad* as "the one," "that one," or
  "the choicest"; construct *achad* as "one of the great," "one of the
  distinguished"; absolute *achad* as "any one of them," "a mere one of them."
- Trap: English "one of" absorbs both forms of *achad* and erases Heidenheim's
  whole point. When both forms sit in one pasuk, as at Bamidbar 16:15, translate
  them differently or the verse reads as pointless repetition. A workable
  rendering there: "I did not take a single donkey of theirs, and I did not
  wrong any one of them."
- *Chad* in a Hebrew context is a clipped *echad*; in Daniel, Ezra, the Bavli
  and Targum it is simply the Aramaic for one and needs no comment.
- *Ach* at Yechezkel 18:10 is a live dispute. Give "brother" (Targum, Rashi)
  unless following Radak and Ibn Janach, and footnote the other reading.
