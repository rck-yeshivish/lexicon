# Cutting tools: garzen / kardum / maatzad (kashil, keilapot)

## Bottom line

*Garzen* is the general Biblical **axe**, and equally the stonecutter's **pick**;
it is the only one of these words Chazal do not use in their own Hebrew.
*Kardum* is the double-headed **adze-axe** or **mattock**, adze on one face and
axe on the other. *Maatzad* is the **adze**, the woodworker's shaping tool and
the smaller implement of the pair. *Kashil* is the **large axe** and *keilapot*
the **hatchets**, both appearing once only, together in one pasuk. The one thing
not to get wrong: *maatzad* and *kashil* are separated by size, and a real
halachah in Bava Kamma turns on it, so never render both "axe."

## Where the authorities disagree

**The root of *garzen*.** Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) treats it as
a four-letter root ג-ר-ז-ן, every consonant part of the core. Radak (*Sefer
HaShorashim*) reads a three-letter ג-ר-ז, cutting and carving, with the final NUN
extraneous. Ibn Janach (*Sefer HaShorashim*) and Rabbi Shlomo Ibn Parchon
(*Machberes He'Aruch*) list the word under both and settle on neither. Ibn Janach
further ties ג-ר-ז to its metathesized twin ג-ז-ר, cutting, and Rashi (to
Tehillim 31:23) and Rabbeinu Tam follow Donash Ibn Labrat in doing the same. The
Bottom line follows Radak: four-letter roots are rare in Hebrew, and a *garzen*
plainly cuts.

**What a *kardum* is.** Radak (to Shmuel I 13:20) reads the Biblical *kardum* as
the Mishnah's tool (Keilim 13:3), double-sided, adze one way and axe the other.
Ibn Janach defines it a "spade," which Radak rejects. The digging sense is
Rabbinic and not Biblical: Avos 4:5 warns against making Torah a *kardum* to dig
with, and the Mishnah uses the word for a digging tool in many places (Peah 4:4,
Sheviis 4:6 and 5:4, Shabbos 17:2, Beitzah 4:3, Meilah 5:1, Keilim 13:3, 20:3,
29:4-7, Taharos 9:7, Parah 12:5). The Bottom line follows Radak for Tanach and
lets the Mishnaic digging sense stand on its own.

**Whose tool the *maatzad* is.** Rashi (to Yeshayahu 44:12) makes it one of the
blacksmith's tools, and elsewhere (to Shabbos 102b) a type of large *kurnas*.
Against this, the Mishnah always has the *maatzad* in a woodworker's hand
(Shabbos 12:1, Bava Kamma 10:10, Erchin 6:3, Keilim 13:4, 29:6), and both of its
appearances in Tanach are in the prophets' mockery of idol-makers, where a
carpenter is carving wood (Yeshayahu 44:12, Yirmiyahu 10:3). The Bottom line
takes it as the woodworker's adze; the smith's role is that he makes the tool,
not that he wields it.

**What *kashil* and *keilapot* are.** Menachem Ibn Saruk understands both as
names of weapons. Donash Ibn Labrat identifies them as woodcutters' tools, and
the Mishnah's use of *kashil* beside *maatzad* (Bava Kamma 10:10) bears him out.
The Bottom line follows Donash.

## Distinctions

### garzen (גרזן)
Four times in Tanach, and split evenly between the two crafts. Twice it cuts
wood: the woodsman whose *garzen* head flies off the handle and kills a man, so
that he must flee to a city of refuge (Devarim 19:5), and the prohibition against
felling a fruit tree in a siege (Devarim 20:19). Twice it cuts stone: Yeshayahu
10:15, where the axe boasts against the one who hews with it (the article prints
10:5), and Melachim I 6:7, where no iron tool was heard in the building of the
Beis HaMikdash. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 26:6) groups ג-ר-ז with the phonetically
near roots ק-ר-ס, ג-ר-ס and ק-ר-צ, since GIMMEL and KUF interchange, as do ZAYIN,
SAMECH and TZADI, and finds one sense running through all four: cutting,
breaking or separating by force. That the same word serves both wood and stone
is less strange once the root simply means to cut. Ibn Janach compares the
Arabic *karzam*, axe; Targum Yonasan (to Yeshayahu 44:12) and the Gemara (Yoma
37b) use the Aramaic *chatzina*.

### kardum (קרדם)
Five times in Tanach, first in Shmuel (I 13:20-21), then Shoftim 9:48, Tehillim
74:5 and Yirmiyahu 46:22, and once each in singular, masculine plural and
feminine plural (*kardumos*). Taken together the pesukim put it in the hands of
soldiers and of farmers both. The Mishnah vocalizes it *kordom*.

### maatzad (מעצד)
Root ע-צ-ד. An adze is not an axe: an axe blade lies parallel to its handle and
strikes perpendicular to the surface, while an adze blade sits perpendicular to
the handle and works at an oblique angle. The axe fells the tree and trims the
branches; the adze shapes the plank afterward. A tool that does both jobs is a
mattock in English, which is why *kardum* takes that word.

### kashil (כשיל) and keilapot (כילפות)
Both appear once, in the same pasuk, Tehillim 74:6, describing the wrecking of
the Mikdash. Their halachic weight comes from Bava Kamma 10:10, which asks
whether a craftsman keeps the scraps of a job he was hired for. He keeps them
when he worked with a *maatzad* and returns them when he worked with a *kashil*:
the *kashil* is the bigger tool and leaves scraps large enough to be worth
something to the one who hired him, while the *maatzad* leaves only fine
shavings he has no use for. *Keilapot* seems to be a foreign synonym for
*kardum*; Targum (to Shmuel I 13:20) renders *kardum* with a form of it, the PEH
interchanged with a BET.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "axe" will absorb *garzen*, *kardum*, *kashil* and *keilapot* at
  once. Reserve "axe" for *garzen*, give *kardum* "adze-axe" or "mattock",
  *maatzad* "adze", *kashil* "large axe" and *keilapot* "hatchets."
- Tehillim 74:6 puts *kashil* and *keilapot* side by side in one clause. The two
  must read differently in English ("with axe and hatchets they smashed"), or the
  pasuk loses its own pairing.
- Where *garzen* cuts stone (Melachim I 6:7, Yeshayahu 10:15), "pick" or "iron
  tool" reads better than "axe"; where it cuts wood (Devarim 19:5, 20:19), "axe."
- Avos 4:5: the familiar English "a spade to dig with" is a translation of the
  Mishnaic digging sense, not of the Biblical tool. Keep it, but do not carry
  "spade" back into a pasuk; there Radak's double-headed tool is meant.
- For *magal* and *chermesh*, the harvesting tools, see
  `sickle-chermesh-magal.md`; for the *kurnas* Rashi compares the *maatzad* to,
  see `hammer-makevet-patish-kurnas.md`; for the verbs of cutting, see
  `cutting-bokea-korea-gozer.md`.
