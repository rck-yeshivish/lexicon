# Flour: kemach / soles

## Bottom line

*Kemach* is ordinary **flour**, ground with the bran and germ still in it:
**whole wheat flour**, and by extension anything of the coarser grade.
*Soles* is the sifted, refined grade: **fine flour** (Rav Hirsch: white flour,
endosperm only). The difference is not chiefly fineness of grind but purity;
*kemach* has everything in it, *soles* has been sifted clean. Whenever a source
sets them opposite each other, *soles* is the better and *kemach* the lesser,
and English "flour" must not be allowed to carry both.

## Distinctions

### kemach (קמח)
Fourteen appearances in Tanach. Donash Ibn Labrat, arguing against Menachem Ibn
Saruk, took the cognate in Arabic to mean wheat; an anonymous student of
Menachem answered that the Arabic word means wheat generally while the Hebrew
means specifically wheat that has been ground, that is, flour. Donash's own
student Ibn Sheshes conceded the point from the Sotah offering, where the Torah
writes *kemach se'orim*, flour of barley, which cannot mean wheat at all. Rashi
(Sotah 14a): everything is mixed into *kemach*. It can be finely ground and
still be *kemach*, since husk and bran remain within the granules (Rashi to
Kesubos 112b). Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: *kemach* is adulterated flour
carrying subpar material. As a metaphor it is the lesser stock: "he does not
care about his *kemach*" (Yevamos 42b, Pesachim 84a, Sukkah 54a) of one who
speaks imprecisely, since he grinds *kemach* and not *soles* (Rashi there);
and of Kimchis, whose seven sons served as Kohen Gadol, "all *kemach* yields
*kemach*, but the *kemach* of Kimchis is *soles*" (Yerushalmi Yoma 1:1 and
Bamidbar Rabbah 2:26; the story is at Yoma 47a).

Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras (*Ohalei Yehudah*) reads *kemach* as a
compound of ק-מ-ה, standing erect, and מ-ח-ה, erasing, since grinding takes the
stalk that stood tall in the field and cuts it down; *kamah* is standing,
uncut grain (Shemos 22:5; Peah 2:7, 4:7, 5:2; Menachos 10:9). Alternatively he
derives it straight from י-מ-ח, erasure, on the interchange of kuf and yud.
The Kimchi family name (Rabbi Yosef Kimchi and his sons Radak and Rabbi Moshe
Kimchi) appears to come from this word; the article notes the proposed
pronunciation Kamchi but keeps Kimchi, since Rabbi Immanuel Frances rhymes it
with *simchi*.

### soles (סולת)
Fifty-two appearances in Tanach, more than half of them in Bamidbar. Every
menachah in the Torah is brought of *soles* except the Omer (Vayikra 23:10-11)
and the Sotah offering (Bamidbar 5:15), both of which are barley and both of
which the Torah calls *kemach*. On that basis Rashi (Vayikra 2:1) and Rashbam
(Bereishis 18:6) hold that *soles* always means wheat flour. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim objects from Shemos 29:2, which says *soles chittim*, flour of
wheat, implying that *soles* alone does not specify the species; on his reading
*kemach* at the Sotah marks a different grade, not a different grain. Menachos
6:7 describes sifting the flour for menachos over and over to yield the finest
*soles* (see Rashi to Menachos 76b); Rashi to Taanis 9b explains that the finer
white flour passes the sifter first and the coarser after (see also Tosafos
there), and Avos 5:15 uses the image: the sieve lets out the *kemach* and keeps
the *soles*. Rashi (Bava Metzia 87a) states plainly that *soles* is better than
*kemach*; Kiddushin 69b calls the Babylonian community pure *soles* against the
mixed dough left in Eretz Yisrael.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces *soles* to the biliteral ס-ל, repeated action:
*mesilah* is a road much trodden, *misalsel* (Rosh Hashanah 26b) is one who
twists his hair over and over, and *sal* is the bread basket (Bereishis 40:16,
Vayikra 8:2), in daily use. So *soles* is either the flour precious enough to
be kept in a *sal* rather than dumped in a sack like *kemach*, or the flour
named for the repeated pressing and grinding that produces it.

### geres (גרש) and semida (סמידא)
Rashi (Menachos 27a): *geres* said of barley (Vayikra 2:14) is to barley what
*soles* is to wheat. Ibn Janach defines *geres* with the word *soles* outright.
Menachem, Ibn Janach and Radak all treat ג-ר-ש and ג-ר-ס as one root meaning
breaking or grinding. The Aramaic *semida* Rashi glosses as *soles* (Shabbos
110b, Pesachim 74b, Taanis 24b, Gittin 56a), and Ibn Ezra (Vayikra 2:1) says
*soles* is white flour, called *semida* in Aramaic. Note the counter-usage:
Targum Yonasan to Bereishis 18:6 renders *kemach* as *semida* and *soles* as
*solta*.

## Rendering guidance

- Default pair: *kemach* = **flour** (or "whole wheat flour" where the contrast
  is live), *soles* = **fine flour**. "Fine flour" is the safe rendering in a
  korbanos context and matches the sifting the Mishnah describes.
- Do not render *soles* as "semolina." The English word does descend from the
  Aramaic *semida*, but semolina today is a coarse durum meal, the opposite of
  what *soles* means.
- Trap: English "flour" alone will swallow both words. In any passage that
  grades them (Avos 5:15, Bava Metzia 87a, the Kimchis maamar) keep the two
  renderings visibly different, and in a metaphor render the sense, "coarse
  stock yields coarse stock, but Kimchis's yields the finest."
- The two together: Bereishis 18:6, *kemach soles*. Bava Metzia 87a reads the
  doubling as Avraham offering the better grade and Sarah the lesser, so a
  translation following the Gemara must keep both words distinct. Radak reads
  the phrase as one thing, *kemach* to be made into *soles*; on his reading
  render "flour for fine meal." Say which you follow.
- Where the Torah writes *kemach se'orim* (Bamidbar 5:15), render "barley
  flour" and let a note carry the point that the coarse word is deliberate.
