# Hidden: tzafun / safun / tamun / ganuz (nistar, ne'elam)

## Bottom line

All six mean "hidden," and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim is the one authority who
separates them. *Tamun* is **buried, concealed within** something (usually the
ground). *Safun* is **stowed, hidden away** deliberately, its outer face
covered. *Tzafun* is **hidden away for safekeeping, stored up**: concealed in
order to preserve, the treasured hidden, not the merely unseen. *Ganuz* is
**stored away, put in the treasury** (Rabbinic: consigned to *genizah*).
*Nistar* / *mustar* is **hidden** from sight or from knowledge, the plain
general word. *Ne'elam* is **hidden from the mind, beyond one's grasp**. The
thing not to get wrong: *tzafun* is not a synonym of "lost from view"; it is
hidden *and kept*, so אור הצפון is light stored away, not light gone dark.

## Distinctions

### tamun (טמון)
Root ט-מ-ן, thirty-one times in Tanach; eight are a hidden trap (Yirmiyahu
18:22; Tehillim 9:16, 140:6), others burying, as Yaakov buried the idols of
Shechem (Bereishis 35:4), Moshe the Mitzri (Shemos 2:12), and the spies
themselves in flax (Yehoshua 2:6). Mishnaic *hatmanah* is embedding food in
an insulator (Shabbos 2:7, 4:1-2). The Mechilta and Bava
Kamma 60a-62a use *tamun* for what a fire consumed that the kindler could not
see. Pappenheim: from TET-MEM, "blocking," the root of *tumah* (a legal block
on what one may do), *atum* (sealed), and *tumtum*; a *tamun* object is
blocked from reach, and *tamun* alone usually implies hiding in the ground.

### safun (ספון)
Root ס-פ-ן, spelled with samech (Devarim 33:21; Melachim I 6:9, 7:3) or sin
(Devarim 33:19); Rashi (to Devarim 33:19) notes the two are one, and Rabbi
Yosef Kimchi (*Sefer Zikaron*) explains it by the samech/sin interchange.
Menachem Ibn Saruk defines *safun* by *tzafun*. Pappenheim: from SAMECH-PEH,
"edge, end": the outer side is obscured so the thing cannot be seen at all;
and *safun* implies hiding in an organized, deliberate way, where the others
can be hasty or ad hoc.

### tzafun (צפון)
Root צ-פ-ן, over thirty times in Tanach; Yocheved hid Moshe three months
(Shemos 2:2). Rabbi Yosef Kimchi ties it to *safun* via the tzadi/samech
interchange. Pappenheim gives it its own root, TZADI-PEH, "floating, covering":
hiding by embedding within something that covers it; and TZADI-PEH-NUN means
hiding specifically **for the purpose of preserving and saving**. That is what
*tzafun* adds over every neighbor: the object is put away because it is valued
and meant to be brought out again. Relatives: *tzafon* (north); Shadal (to
Vayikra 11:5) even derives *shafan* from it, the animal that hides in holes.

### ganuz (גנוז)
Root ג-נ-ז. In Tanach the noun is a royal storehouse: *ginzei* (Yechezkel
27:24; Esther 3:9, 4:7), *ganzach* (Divrei HaYamim I 28:11); Targum uses it
for *otzar*, *tzrurah*, *pikadon*. In the Mishnah it is the verb: Chizkiyahu
hid the Book of Remedies (Pesachim 4:9), the Aron was hidden (Shekalim 6:1-2);
*genizah* is the honorable burial of worn holy writings (Shabbos 9:6). Bava
Basra 11a: Munbaz "stored" (*ganzu*) for the World to Come. Rabbi Yehoshua
Steinberg links it to KAF-NUN-SAMECH, "gathered in"; *gizbar*, treasurer, is
its relative. *Ganuz* is hidden as treasure is hidden: catalogued and kept.

### nistar / mustar (נסתר, מוסתר, סתר)
Root ס-ת-ר, over a hundred times; *mistar* is a sheltered place (Yeshayahu
4:6). Pappenheim: the root covers both hidden from people's knowledge (*seter*,
a secret) and hidden from the senses (*hastarah*); the Rabbinic sense
"destroy" grows from the second. Rabbinic *mistorin*, the esoteric Torah
(Midrash Shmuel 28:1; Tanchuma Vayera 5), only sounds like this root; it is Greek.

### ne'elam (נעלם)
Root ע-ל-ם. Pappenheim: hidden in the intellectual sense, from AYIN-LAMED,
"above," as in *al*; the thing lies above one's range of perception. Rabbi
Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter (*HaRechasim LaVikah*, cited in *HaKsav
VeHaKabbalah* to Bereishis 49:6): *sod* is a secret people speak of quietly;
*ta'alumah* or *seter* is so hidden that one person alone knows it.

Aramaic *tamir* renders most of these in Targum (Rabbi Ernest Klein: ט-מ-ן
and ט-מ-ר by nun/reish interchange). Named, not treated: *pele*, *nechbeh*, *tzanua*.

## Rendering guidance

- טמון: "buried," "concealed [in]." ספון: "stowed away," "hidden [beneath]";
  where the hiding is orderly and deliberate (Pappenheim), "reserved" fits.
- צפון: "hidden away," "stored up"; where the context is a good held back in
  reserve, "laid up," "kept in store." Do not write "concealed" or "obscure"
  for צפון when the passage means treasure held back for its hour: the
  preserving sense (Pappenheim) is the whole point.
- גנוז: "stored away," "hidden in safekeeping"; writings, "consigned to genizah."
- נסתר / הסתר: "hidden," "concealed"; הסתר פנים "the hiding of His face"; סתר
  as a noun, "secrecy," "a hidden place" (see the secret entry).
- נעלם: "hidden [from him]," "unknown," "beyond his grasp"; it answers to the
  mind, not the eye. תעלומות "hidden things," "mysteries."
- Trap: "hidden" absorbs all six, and "concealed" absorbs טמון and נסתר. When
  a passage lists two (טמון וצפון, גנוז ונעלם), keep two English words: one
  for how it is covered, one for why. When צפון and גנוז stand together, both
  are treasure-words; render צפון "stored up" and גנוז "hidden away."
