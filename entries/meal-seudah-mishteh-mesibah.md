# Meal: seudah / mishteh / mesibah

## Bottom line

*Seudah* is a **meal** generally associated with bread, from *saad*, to sustain; it is the word
for the three Shabbos meals. *Mishteh* is a **feast** built on wine, from
*shoseh*, to drink; it is the word for the Purim meal and for the banquets of
Megillas Esther. *Mesibah* focuses on neither food nor drink: it is the
meal at which people **recline**, and only later came to mean a party in
general. In Rabbi Klein's summary, each word focuses on one thing: the bread,
the wine, or the posture.

## Distinctions

### seudah (סעודה)
Not a biblical word, though it is the word for each of the three meals of
Shabbos (*Shabbos* 117b). Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): Chazal modified the
biblical *saad* to form *seudah*. *Saad* is supporting or sustaining, both in a
rhetorical way (a proof that supports an argument) and in a physical way (food
that sustains the body). In a handful of places Tanach uses the verb *saad*
with bread: *v'lechem levav enosh yis'ad* (Tehillim 104:15), *s'ad libcha pas
lechem* (Shoftim 19:5), *v'sa'adu libchem* (Bereishis 18:5). The name Saadia,
most famously borne by Rabbeinu Saadia Gaon (882-942), is "support from
Hashem." So a *seudah* is named for what the festive meal does for the body.

### mishteh (משתה)
The word and its derivatives appear close to fifty times in Tanach, from the
verb *shoseh*, to drink.
- Rabbi Tzaddok HaKohen of Lublin (1823 to 1900): *seudah* and *mishteh* are
  both festive meals, but the first centers on eating and the second on
  drinking, which is why *seudah* is generally associated with bread and the
  Shabbos table while *mishteh* goes with wine and the Purim meal that
  commemorates the wine banquets of Megillas Esther.
- Rashi (to Shir HaShirim 1:2, Koheles 2:3 and Esther 5:4), however, writes that
  the defining element of an enjoyable, joyous *seudah* is the wine.
- Rabbi Levi Yitzchok of Berditchev (1740-1810, *Kedushas Levi*, Parshas
  Vayeira) reads *mishteh* as a meal of gladness, and on that basis explains
  that the party to which Esther invited Haman and Achashverosh is called a
  *mishteh* because it brought gladness to the Jews: through it Esther persuaded
  Achashverosh to execute Haman and rescind the decree, a victory that has
  brought gladness for generations and is celebrated each year on Purim.
- Shadal (to Bereishis 19:3): a *mishteh* is a light evening meal whose main
  course was wine, against the heavier afternoon meal called *seudah* or
  *lechem*.
- Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 26:14): *mishteh*, from
  drinking, has a parallel in Greek *symposion*, likewise from drinking: *syn*,
  together, and *potes*, drinking.

### mesibah (מסיבה)
Arguably once in Tanach (Rashbam to Shir HaShirim 1:12).

- Rashi (to Amos 2:8): the word names the fact that those at the meal would
  recline, the practice of *haseibah*. The Mah Nishtanah preserves the usage: on
  other nights we eat *bein yoshvin uvein mesubin*, sitting or reclining, and
  tonight only reclining. So a *mesibah* is a meal taken slouched on couches
  rather than sitting upright, and from there the word was borrowed for any
  gathering or party.
- One contemporary linguist argues instead that *mesibah* is related to
  *sovev*/*sevivah*, "around," and names the fact that everyone gathers around
  for a party; Rabbi Klein answers that this claim remains unsubstantiated.

## Rendering guidance

- Default renderings: *seudah* "meal" (or "festive meal" where the context is
  Shabbos or Yom Tov); *mishteh* "feast" or "banquet," and "drinking feast"
  where the wine is the point; *mesibah* "gathering" or "banquet," reserving
  "party" for later Hebrew.
- Trap: "feast" will swallow *seudah* and *mishteh* both. Where a sefer sets the
  Shabbos *seudah* against the Purim *mishteh* (Rabbi Tzaddok's distinction),
  the English must show the contrast: "the Shabbos meal" against "the Purim
  drinking feast," or keep both transliterated and gloss each once.
- *Seudah* is a term of art in halachah (*seudah shelishis*, *seudas mitzvah*).
  Leave it transliterated; "the third meal" flattens it.
- Megillas Esther's *mishteh hayayin* is "the wine feast," not "the banquet of
  wine," which reads as a construct in English where the Hebrew has an idiom.
- *Mesubin* in the Haggadah is "reclining," never "seated at table."
