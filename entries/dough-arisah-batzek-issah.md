# Dough: arisah / batzek / issah

## Bottom line

All three mean **dough**, and in most contexts plain "dough" is right for each.
Where a passage distinguishes them: *arisah* is dough as a **mixture** just
kneaded together (the Torah's word in the mitzvah of *challah*), *batzek* is
dough already worked into one batch and **rising** (the Torah's word for
ordinary dough), and *issah* is the Mishnaic word, dough as the thing that is
**kneaded and pressed**. Ibn Janach and Radak hold *arisah* and *batzek* are
simply synonyms; the distinguishing reading is the minority but is the one that
carries halachic weight.

## Distinctions

### arisah (עריסה)
The word Tanach uses whenever the mitzvah of *challah* is in view (Bamidbar
15:20-21, Nechemiah 10:38, Yechezkel 44:30) and nowhere else. Rabbi Nosson of
Rome (*Sefer HaAruch*): the root ע-ר-ס is an expression of mixing or joining.
On that basis Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg and Rav Hirsch take *arisah* to be
dough precisely as a mixture of ingredients kneaded together (see Rashi to
Berachos 37b). Mecklenburg adds *eres*, "bed," to the same root: a mattress
rests on interlaced planks, a mixture of parts; Rabbi Yachya ben Shalom Kohen
of Sanaa says the same independently. Rabbeinu Meyuchas bar Eliyahu takes a
different line: *arisah* is a form of *areivah*, the **kneading trough**, so
the word names the surface the dough is worked on rather than the dough itself.
Rabbi Yitzchak Ratzabi extends the mixing sense to *arusah*, a betrothed woman,
as one now joined to her husband-to-be, noting that the aleph of *arusah* and
the ayin of *arisah* interchange.

### batzek (בצק)
The ordinary word for dough in Tanach, five times (Shemos 12:34, 12:39,
Yirmiyahu 7:18, Hoshea 7:4, Shmuel II 13:8). Rabbi Mecklenburg: *batzek* is
dough already kneaded and worked into a single batch, which has begun to rise
or is about to. He grounds this in the root's other sense, **swollen**: the
Jews' feet did not swell (*batzek*) in the wilderness (Devarim 8:4, and the
parallel at Nechemiah 9:21; the article prints Nechemiah 15:21, a chapter that
does not exist). Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads every root containing beis and kuf
as breaking through (*boker*, *bezek*, *barak*, *bohak*, *bakesh*, *badek*,
*bokea*, *bakar*), and puts *batzek* there too: the cracks that open on the
surface of rising dough.

### issah (עיסה)
Not in Tanach as "dough" at all; it is the Mishnah's standard word (Challah
1:5, 2:2). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, Rabbi Mecklenburg and Rabbi Marcus derive
it from ע-ס-ס, to squeeze or trample (Yechezkel 23:3; Malachi 3:21), dough
being worked by pressing; the same root gives *assis*, pressed juice (Yoel
4:18, Yeshayahu 49:26, Shir HaShirim 8:2), and the modern *issui*, massage.
Pappenheim and Marcus further link the root to *asiyah*, making. So *issah*
names dough from the kneading, where *arisah* names it from the mixing. Rabbi
Baruch HaLevi Epstein takes the two to be one word: *arisah* is *issah* with an
added reish, a letter he holds is inserted in Hebrew and Aramaic for the beauty
of the language without changing the sense, citing Pseudo-Rashi to Divrei
HaYamim I 18:5 on *Darmesek*/*Damesek* and *sharvit*/*shevet*, and adding
*kursa*/*kisei*, *markoles*/*makoles*, *zutra*/*zuta*, and Uzziah/Azariah.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: **dough** for all three. Reserve a distinguishing rendering for a
  passage that actually turns on the difference.
- Where the contrast is live, render *arisah* **the mixed dough** or **your
  batch of dough** and *batzek* **the risen dough** or **the dough as it
  swells**. Rabbi Ratzabi draws a halachic consequence: separate *challah*
  immediately after kneading, while it is still *arisah*, before it becomes
  *batzek*.
- Trap: English "dough" absorbs all three, and "batch" absorbs *batzek* and
  *issah* both. If a sugya quotes a pasuk with *arisah* and then discusses it
  in Mishnaic *issah*, keep the two English words distinct so the reader can
  see which register each sentence is in.
- *Batzek* outside the bakery is **swollen**, not "doughy": "their feet did not
  swell."
- *Arisah* under Rabbeinu Meyuchas is **kneading trough**, not dough. Flag the
  reading rather than smuggling it into the main text.
