# Mount Sinai: Sinai / Chorev / Har HaElokim / Bashan / Gavnunim / Chemed

## Bottom line

One mountain carries many names. **Sinai** is the best known; Tanach also calls
it **Har HaElokim**, the Mountain of God, **Har Bashan** and **Har Gavnunim**
(Tehillim 68:16), **Har Chemed** (Tehillim 68:17) and **Chorev**, Horeb (Shemos
33:6). Various Midrashic sources explain how each name fits the one mountain,
most of them by a play on the name: *sinah*, hatred; *cherev*, a sword;
*chemdah*, desire; *giben*, a blemish, or *gevinah*, cheese. Some versions of the
Midrash add **Moriah**, which raises the question whether Moriah and Sinai are
two mountains or one; both answers are given.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Is Moriah the same mountain as Sinai?** Some versions of the Midrash count
Moriah among Sinai's names. That is difficult, since Moriah is understood to be
the place on which the Beis HaMikdash was built, in Yerushalayim and not in the
Sinai wilderness. Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi (1075 to 1141) implies the same
identification. The Zohar explains that Moriah is so called for the abundance of
sweet-smelling myrrh there, and in his *Yom Shabbaton* Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi
writes, "He spoke through His holiness on the Mountain of Myrrh / You shall
remember and guard the Seventh Day," placing the Shabbos commandments on Moriah.
- The simplest resolution is that two different mountains are both named
  Moriah.
- Rabbi Berachiah Baruch Shapiro (d. 1663) and Rabbi Naftali Katz (1649 to
  1718), among the most prominent Ashkenazi kabbalists: Moriah and Sinai are one
  mountain. When Hashem gave the Torah in the Sinai wilderness He uprooted the
  mountain from its place in Yerushalayim, brought it to the wilderness, and
  afterwards returned it.

## Distinctions

### Sinai (סיני)
The mountain's best-known name alludes to *sinah*, hatred, which comes from that
place. Opposition to the Jewish people stems from a deep hatred of and
resistance to the Torah and its values, an attitude that began as opposition to
the role Yisrael took on at Sinai.

### Har HaElokim (הר האלהים)
Where Yisrael accepted upon themselves the Godhood of the Creator. Of all the
mountains on which Hashem might have revealed His glory, Sinai was the most
fitting, since it had never been worshipped by idolaters, as other mountains
were. And *Elokim*, as against the Four-Letter Name, denotes Hashem's attribute
of judgment, alluding to His role at Sinai as Judge, revealing the civil laws of
the Torah (Shemos 21 onward).

### Har Bashan (הר בשן)
- *Ba sham*, "He came there," the commentaries noting that nun and mem are close
  enough to interchange: Hashem's arrival at the mountain to give the Torah.
- *Bi-shinav*, "with his teeth": everything Yisrael enjoys with its teeth, all
  material success, of which agricultural plenty is the type, comes in the merit
  of keeping the Torah.

### Har Gavnunim (הר גבננים)
- From *giben* (Vayikra 21:20), the blemish of abnormally long eyebrows that
  disqualifies a Kohen from service in the Beis HaMikdash: Sinai, clean of
  idolatry, "disqualified" every other mountain by contrast.
- The Midrash reads the name against *gevinah*, cheese: at the giving of the
  Torah every Jew with an ailment or handicap was healed. As cheese is made by
  separating the purest curds of the milk from their impurities, the whey, so at
  Sinai Yisrael stood in their purest state, with no physical blemish among
  them. Some explain the custom of eating dairy on Shavuos by this name and its
  comparison to cheese.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reduces ג-ב-ן to the biliteral ג-ב, a bulge or
  anything that sticks out and is plainly visible: *gavnunim* are bulging
  mountains that protrude upwards and are quite conspicuous, and he connects
  *gevinah*, cheese, as likewise "mountainous" in its composition.
- Alexander Kohut (*Aruch HaShalem*) suggests connecting *gavnunim* with the
  Arabic *jabal*, mountain, through the interchange of nun and lamed, so that
  *gavnunim* just means mountainous or hilly.

### Har Chemed (הר חמד)
Hashem desired (*chemdah*) to rest His Presence on that mountain in particular
(Tehillim 68:17).

### Chorev (חורב)
The name alludes to *cherev*, a sword: the Sanhedrin's authority to carry out
capital punishment comes from the Torah received at Sinai.

Shadal (to Shemos 3:1): Chorev and Sinai are two different mountains, or two
peaks of one mountain.

### Moriah (מוריה)
See the question above.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "Mount Sinai" will absorb every one of these names. Where a pasuk
  or a derashah lists them (Tehillim 68:16-17), keep each name as a name and give
  the word it is read against beside it (*sinah*, *cherev*, *chemdah*, *giben*,
  *gevinah*), or the derashah disappears.
- *Har HaElokim*: "the Mountain of God," keeping *Elokim* visible where the point
  is the attribute of judgment.
- *Chorev*: "Horeb" or "Chorev," as one mountain with Sinai, never a second place.
- "The Mountain of Myrrh" in *Yom Shabbaton* is Moriah; rendered only as a
  description it hides the identification the poem implies.
