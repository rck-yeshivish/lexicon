# Thought: machshavah / hirhur / raayon

## Bottom line

*Machshavah* is the deliberate, worked-out thought, kin to *cheshbon*:
**thought**. *Raayon* is the spark that surfaces unbidden and may or may not be
developed: **idea**, **notion** (Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer; Rashi and
Ramban, though, treat it as a plain synonym of *machshavah*). *Hirhur* is a
thought already shaped toward words, or a thought of the heart rather than the
brain (Vilna Gaon): **musing**, or in a mussar setting **stirring** (of the
heart). The one thing not to get wrong: *hirhur* is not a vaguer, lesser
"thought"; on the Gaon's first reading it is the *last* stage, thought at the
threshold of speech, and on his second it is the heart's thought, which is why
it is the word for the stirrings a person is answerable for.

## Distinctions

### machshavah (מחשבה)
Root ח-ש-ב. Vilna Gaon (commentary to Sefer Yetzirah): a pure, abstract
thought, as against *hirhur*, which has already been put into possible words;
alternatively, *machshavah* takes place in the brain, the seat of intellect,
while *hirhur* takes place in the heart, the seat of emotion. Wertheimer:
*machshavah* is the fruit of deliberate, focused thinking, related to
*cheshbon*, calculation; it is intended, purposeful pondering. On the track of
thought it is the middle station: a *raayon* followed, contemplated, and
developed to its conclusion (*hogeh*, *higayon*) matures into a *machshavah*.

### hirhur (הרהור)
Root ה-ר-ר (Rabbinic). Vilna Gaon: a thought that has been transferred into
possible words, so it is nearer to speech than *machshavah* is. Rabbi Pinchas
HaLevi Horowitz: *hirhur* is related to *herayon*, pregnancy; it is the
conception of a thought that will in time be born as speech. The Gaon's second
account puts it in the heart, not the brain. Taken together, *hirhurim* are
the unspoken words a person forms as he completes his train of thought and
readies himself to speak. Rashi (to Daniel 2:29-30) defines *raayon* as
*hirhur*, against his own equation of *raayon* with *machshavah* elsewhere.

### raayon (רעיון)
The surface pull toward *ratzon* (will) is real: Hebrew *tzadi* regularly
becomes Aramaic *ayin*, so *ratzon* is Aramaic *raavon*, one letter from
*raayon*. Rashi (to Berachos 55b) rejects that etymology outright: *raayon* is
not *ratzon* but *machshavah*. Ramban (cited to Bamidbar 2:14) likewise makes
*raayon* synonymous with *machshavah*. Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia (Yad Ramah
to Sanhedrin 38b): *raayon* is simply the Aramaic for *machshavah*, so the two
are one word in two languages. Wertheimer separates them: a *raayon* is the
beginning of a thought, the idea that materialises as one clears the mind to
rest, spontaneous and sometimes fleeting, which further deliberation may
develop into a formulated concept; the *machshavah* is that developed result.

## Rendering guidance

- *Machshavah* alone: "thought"; where the calculation sense is felt
  (*cheshbon*), "design" or "intention" can serve, but "thought" is the
  default.
- *Raayon* alone in Tanach or Aramaic: "thought," with Rashi and the Yad
  Ramah. In a later text that contrasts it with *machshavah*: "idea" or
  "notion," with Wertheimer, and keep "thought" free for *machshavah*.
- *Hirhur* alone: "musing" when the sense is silent inner speech; "stirring"
  when the heart sense governs (the Gaon's second reading). Do not render it
  "reflection," which suggests the deliberate *machshavah*.
- The three together, or a passage that ranks them: follow the track of
  thought, *raayon* (spark), *machshavah* (developed thought), *hirhur*
  (thought shaped into unspoken words), and give each its own English.
- Trap: "thought" absorbs all three, and "idea" absorbs *raayon* and
  *machshavah*. If the passage does not turn on the difference, "thought" for
  each is acceptable; if it does, one English word per Hebrew word.
- The pair *hogeh*/*higayon* names the process of following a thought to its
  conclusion: "contemplation," "reasoning it through," not a fourth noun for
  "thought."
