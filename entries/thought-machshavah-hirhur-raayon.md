# Thought: machshavah / hirhur / raayon

## Bottom line

*Machshavah* is the deliberate, worked-out thought, kin to *cheshbon*:
**thought**. *Raayon* is, for Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, the spark that
surfaces unbidden and may upon deliberation be developed: **idea**, **notion**;
but Rashi (to Berachos 55b) and Ramban treat it as a synonym of *machshavah*,
the Yad Ramah makes it simply the Aramaic for *machshavah*, and Rashi to Daniel
defines it as *hirhur*. *Hirhur* is, for the Vilna Gaon, a thought already
formulated into possible words, or alternatively a thought of the heart rather
than the brain: **musing**, or where the heart sense governs **stirring**.
On the Gaon's first reading *hirhur* is thought already put into words, and in
Rabbi Klein's synthesis of the three it is the *last* stage, thought at the
threshold of speech.

## Distinctions

### machshavah (מחשבה)
Root ח-ש-ב. The Vilna Gaon (commentary to *Sefer Yetzirah*) and others: a pure,
abstract thought, as against *hirhur*, which has already been formulated into
possible words. Alternatively, the Gaon writes, *machshavah* takes place in the
brain, the seat of intellect, while *hirhur* takes place in the heart, the seat
of emotion. Wertheimer: *machshavah* is the result of deliberate, focused
thinking, related to *cheshbon*, calculation; it is intended, purposeful
pondering.

### hirhur (הרהור)
Root ה-ר-ר (Rabbinic). The Vilna Gaon: a thought that has been transferred into
possible words, so it is nearer to speech than *machshavah* is. Indeed, Rabbi
Pinchas HaLevi Horowitz relates *hirhur* to *herayon*, pregnancy: it is the
conception of a thought that will in time germinate into speech. The Gaon's
alternative account puts it in the heart, not the brain. Rashi (to Daniel
2:29-30) defines *raayon* as *hirhur*, as opposed to *machshavah*, which seems
to contradict his own equation of *raayon* with *machshavah* at Berachos 55b.

### raayon (רעיון)
There is ample reason, Rabbi Klein grants, to take *raayon* as one's
intellectual will: Hebrew *tzadi* commonly becomes Aramaic *ayin*, so *ratzon*
is Aramaic *raavon*, spelled almost exactly like *raayon*, which would seem to
suggest a connection. But Rashi (to Berachos 55b) forcefully rejects that
etymology: *raayon* is not related to *ratzon* but means the same as
*machshavah*. Ramban (to Bamidbar 2:14) likewise makes *raayon* synonymous with
*machshavah*. Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia (Yad Ramah to Sanhedrin 38b): *raayon*
is the Aramaic translation of *machshavah*, which, Rabbi Klein notes, suggests
that the two are indeed synonyms, one Aramaic and one Hebrew. Wertheimer takes a
different approach: a *raayon* is the beginning of a thought, the idea that
simply materialises as one clears the mind to rest, spontaneous and sometimes
fleeting, which upon further consideration can potentially be developed into a
well-formulated concept; the *machshavah* is the product of deliberate thinking.

### the track of thought
Rabbi Klein's synthesis: the three words are not exactly synonymous but mark
three points on one track. First a spark of an idea surfaces, the *raayon*. If
one follows it, contemplates it and develops it to its conclusion (*hogeh*,
*higayon*), the result is a clearer, more mature idea, the *machshavah*. As one
completes his train of thought and prepares to say it, the thought is turned
into words, and those unspoken words are *hirhurim*.

## Rendering guidance

- *Machshavah* alone: "thought"; where the calculation sense is felt
  (*cheshbon*), "design" or "intention" can serve, but "thought" is the
  default.
- *Raayon* alone in Tanach or Aramaic: "thought," with Rashi, Ramban and the Yad
  Ramah. In a later text that contrasts it with *machshavah*: "idea" or
  "notion," with Wertheimer, and keep "thought" free for *machshavah*.
- *Hirhur* alone: "musing" when the sense is silent inner speech; "stirring"
  when the heart sense governs (the Gaon's alternative reading). Do not render
  it "reflection," which suggests the deliberate *machshavah*.
- The three together, or a passage that ranks them: follow Rabbi Klein's track
  of thought, *raayon* (spark), *machshavah* (developed thought), *hirhur*
  (thought shaped into unspoken words), and give each its own English.
- Trap: "thought" absorbs all three, and "idea" absorbs *raayon* and
  *machshavah*. If the passage does not turn on the difference, "thought" for
  each is acceptable; if it does, one English word per Hebrew word.
- The pair *hogeh*/*higayon* names the process of following a thought to its
  conclusion: "contemplation," "reasoning it through," not a fourth noun for
  "thought."
