# Names of Mordechai and Esther: Mordechai / Petachyah / Bilshan, Esther / Hadassah

## Bottom line

All five are names and stay transliterated in roman type; what a translator must not do is
treat the pairs as one name each. **Mordechai** is the Babylonian name the Megillah uses
throughout, **Petachyah** his Hebrew name, **Bilshan** a title of his mastery of languages.
**Esther** is the name the Megillah uses; **Hadassah** appears once, at Esther 2:7, whose
*hi Esther* says they are one woman. Which was her birth name is a Tannaic dispute, so a
translation should not settle it silently.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Which was her real name.** Megillah 13a gives three Tannaim. Rabbi Meir: her name was
Esther, and Hadassah is a description, the righteous being likened to a *hadas*
(Zechariah 1:8). Rabbi Yehuda: her name was Hadassah, and Esther is from *nistar*, since
she hid her people (Esther 2:20); Rabbi Avigdor Katz adds that Mordechai hid her from
the king's men. Rabbi Nechemiah: Hadassah was her name, and the nations called her
Esther after Istahar. The Vilna Gaon follows Rabbi Nechemiah, the *Chachmei Tzarfas*
commentary Rabbi Meir; Ralbag leaves it open; Rabbi Tanchum HaYerushalmi and Rabbi
Yitzchak Arama hold Hadassah was the birth name, Esther the name of the court.

**What Istahar is.** Rashi (to Megillah 13a) and *Sefer HeAruch*: the moon, from Aramaic
*sihara*. Rabbi Yaakov Emden: Venus, since Targum to Iyov 31:26 lists Istahar as a light
*beside* the moon; so too *Yalkut Shimoni* (Esther §1053) and *Targum Sheini*, which
explains the name by *Nogah*.

**Foreign name or Hebrew in disguise.** Rabbi Shlomo Alkabetz (*Manos HaLevi*) and Rabbi
Eliezer Ashkenazi (*Yosef Lekach*), with Ibn Ezra: she took Esther when Mordechai wanted her
origin concealed, the Aramaic for *hadas* being *assa*, so the new name still says myrtle.
Maharsha and Rabbi Meir Mazuz: it is Persian. Rabbi Yekusiel Yehuda Halberstam: her family
Judaized *Istahar* into *Esther*, while Mordechai, who let no loanwords into his Hebrew,
kept calling her Hadassah.

**Mordechai's other names.** Megillah 16b and Menachos 65a read Mordechai-Bilshan (Ezra
2:2, Nechemiah 7:7) as one man with a title; Ibn Ezra reads Bilshan as the next man
listed. On why the Megillah keeps the Babylonian name: *Torah Temimah* holds his Hebrew
name was Petachyah, and because Mordechai carries an idolatrous association the Gemara
sought a favorable allusion for it in the Torah; Rabbi Yehonatan Eybeschutz (*Yaaros
Dvash* vol. 2 drush 13) holds that as Moshe's given name is absent after "erase me from
Your book," so Mordechai's was left out.

## Distinctions

### Mordechai (מרדכי) and Merodach
Chullin 139b asks where Mordechai is hinted in the Torah and answers *mar dror*, pure
myrrh (Shemos 30:23), rendered *mira dachya* by Onkelos: a tzaddik gives off fragrance
as myrrh does. The same consonants are a Babylonian name in Tanach: Evil Merodach
(Melachim II 25:27; the article prints 28:27), Merodach Baladan (Yeshayahu 39:1), and
Yirmiyahu 50:2, where it is a deity beside Bel.

### Petachyah (פתחיה) and Bilshan (בלשן)
Shekalim 5:1: called Petachyah because he would *poteach* words and expound them, and knew
seventy languages. Menachos 65a: all the Sanhedrin knew the seventy, but Mordechai could
blend them, which is what Bilshan encodes; that is how he understood Bigsan and Seresh
plotting in a foreign tongue.

### Esther (אסתר) and Hadassah (הדסה)
Esther 2:7 is the single occurrence of Hadassah, and it carries its own gloss, *hi Esther*.
Chullin 139b answers the parallel question for her name with *haster astir*, "I shall surely
hide My face" (Devarim 31:18). Beyond the myrtle of the righteous, Megillah 13a offers her
greenish complexion (Rabbi Yehoshua ben Karcha, as Rashi reads him) and her average stature,
neither tall as a *lulav* nor stout as an *esrog* (Ben Azzai). Rabbi Tzadok HaKohen of
Lublin reads the two names as the lesson of Purim: when Hashem's face is hidden (Esther),
Israel still holds fast and does His will (Hadassah).

## Rendering guidance

- Transliterate all five, roman type, no translated gloss in the body; bring in "myrtle" or
  "hidden" only in a note, and say whose reading it is.
- Esther 2:7 is the trap: "he raised Hadassah, that is Esther." Collapsing the names, or
  rendering Hadassah as "Myrtle," destroys the pasuk's own clarification and the sugya on
  it; compare *hu haGoral* (Esther 3:7, 9:24).
- Where the Gemara plays on a name (*mira dachya*, *haster astir*), keep the Hebrew in
  brackets; these are derashos on sound.
- Do not write that Mordechai "was named after" a Babylonian deity; the pesukim show only
  that the name-form was current in Bavel.
