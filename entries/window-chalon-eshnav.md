# Window: chalon / eshnav

## Bottom line

*Chalon* is the ordinary **window**, an opening in a wall, and it is what a
translator should reach for unless a passage marks the difference. *Eshnav*
appears only twice in Tanach and is the narrower or latticed kind: render it
**small window** or, where the source says so, **lattice**. Shoftim 5:28 puts
both in one pasuk, Sisera's mother looking out of the *chalon* and then out of
the *eshnav*, so "window" twice loses what the navi is doing. What the *eshnav*
was is genuinely disputed, and a translation should not settle it silently.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| חלון | chalon | window | the general word, thirty-one times; a hole in a wall (Pappenheim, Radak) |
| אשנב | eshnav | small window, lattice | twice only; smaller (Kara, Meiri), a peephole (Rid), or latticed (Ibn Saruk, Valle) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether eshnav differs from chalon at all.** Rashi (to Shoftim 5:28), Ibn
Janach and Radak simply define *eshnav* as *chalon*, so the two are one word for
one thing. Everyone below holds otherwise, and the Bottom line follows them,
since the pasuk itself uses both in consecutive stichs.

**What kind of window an eshnav is.** Six readings:

1. **Smaller.** Rabbi Yosef Kara (to Shoftim 5:28) and the Meiri (to Mishlei
   7:6): an *eshnav* is a small window, *chalon* being the general term.
2. **A peephole.** Rabbi Yishaya of Trani: narrow at the outer end and wider at
   the inner, like an arrow slit. (Rabbi Shaul Goldman reads the Rid's foreign
   gloss as *balustraria*, a slit for shooting arrows through.)
3. **Latticed.** Menachem Ibn Saruk: the mesh or lattice openings of an upper
   floor. Rabbi Moshe David Valle: wooden latticework that a woman of means
   fixed to her window so she could look out without being seen, and he reads
   the word itself as an abbreviation of *ishah nosenes b'chalonoseha*, "a woman
   places this at her windows."
4. **Shuttered.** Rabbi Moshe Yitzchak Tedeschi Ashkenazi (*Hoil Moshe*): the
   root is ש-נ-ב, related through the interchange of nun and lamed to ש-ל-ב,
   "step" or "layer," so an *eshnav* is a window fitted with metal shutters.
5. **For the wind.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus: a metathesis of נ-ש-ב, the blowing of
   the wind, since an open window lets the air in. Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein
   says the same and adds that an *eshnav* is specifically a window for cooling;
   Rabbi Ernest Klein prefers this derivation.
6. **A lens.** Malbim in *Yair Ohr*: an *eshnav* is a window, mirror or lens
   that makes distant objects appear near, which would require real knowledge of
   optics. Rabbi Chaim Futernik notes that Malbim gives no source for this;
   the same explanation appears almost word for word in Rabbi Elazar Reines's
   *Shorashei Leshon HaKodesh* and *Mishlei Shlomo*.

The Bottom line follows readings 1 and 3, which are the oldest and which a
translation can carry ("small window," "lattice") without committing to more.

**Which of the two was the enchanted window.** The *Zohar* (Toldos 140b) says
that idolaters used a window and witchcraft to see what was hidden, and that
Sisera's mother did exactly this in looking out for her son; the same is said of
Avimelech seeing that Rivkah was Yitzchak's wife (Tzror HaMor to Bereishis 26:8;
*Sefer HaIkkarim* 4:43; Abarbanel and Alshich to Shoftim 5:28). Malbim (to
Mishlei 7:6) has it that through a *chalon* one sees what is revealed and
through an *eshnav* what is hidden, and (to Shoftim 5:28) that the *eshnav* was
the enchanted window while the *chalon* was an ordinary one. The difficulty is
that in Bereishis 26:8 Avimelech looks through a *chalon*. Rabbi Shmuel Landiado
of Aleppo (*Kli Yakar* to Shoftim 5:28) therefore reverses it: the *chalon* was
a divining mirror on her wall and the *eshnav* the real window onto the street,
so she consulted the one and then looked out of the other. A translator should
leave both words plain and put this in a note.

## Distinctions

### chalon (חלון)
Thirty-one appearances. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to the biliteral ח-ל,
circular movement and the empty space inside a circle, so a window is simply a
hole in a wall. He puts a wide family under that root: *chalil*, the hollow
flute; *machol*, a dance in a ring; *chalom*, a dream, thoughts going round and
round; *chillul*, the void left where holiness was; *challal*, a corpse emptied
of its life; *choli* and *machalah*; and *cheil*, the low wall that leaves an
empty space before the high wall. In *Cheshek Shlomo* he adds that *chalon* is
specifically a round window. Radak and Ibn Janach, who do not work with
two-letter roots, list it under the triliteral ח-ל-ל, emptiness.

### eshnav (אשנב)
Twice: Shoftim 5:28, Sisera's mother at the window, and Mishlei 7:6, where the
*ishah zarah* is watched through the window. Modern Hebrew's *eshnav*, the
service window of a bank or post office, is a neologism and should never be read
back into a pasuk.

## Rendering guidance

- חלון: "window," always.
- Shoftim 5:28: give the two words two English renderings. "She peered through
  the window… through the lattice" or "…through the casement" keeps the pair;
  "window" twice reads as mere repetition.
- Trap: English "window" absorbs both. Where a commentary is built on the
  difference, name the Hebrew word rather than presuming one reading.
- Mishlei 7:6: "lattice" is the safe rendering, and it is the one that carries
  the sense of looking out unseen.
- Do not render *eshnav* "service window" (modern) or "peephole" (the Rid's
  reading alone).
