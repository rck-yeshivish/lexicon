# Wisdom: chochmah / binah (tevunah) / daat

## Bottom line

Jewish tradition has long seen the Menorah as a symbol of wisdom (Bava Basra
25b); the three terms together form the basis of Jewish epistemology.
*Chochmah* is **wisdom**: for Rashi, after the *Sifrei*, what one hears and
learns from others, and Chazal's *chacham* gathers from every source; for
Malbim, practical wisdom, the smart way of acting. *Binah* (with *tevunah*, which most use almost interchangeably) is
**understanding**, understanding one matter from within another (Chagigah 14a),
building on what one already holds (a *navon* is **discerning**). *Daat* is
**knowledge**, and its readings are many: grasping the reason behind a lesson
(Rashi and Bartenura to Avos 3:17); *ruach hakodesh* (Rashi to Shemos 31:3) or
reconciliation (Rashi to Chagigah 12a), which Rabbi Shmuel Yaakov Burnstein
unites as connection; the joining of intellect and emotion (Rabbi Chaim
Friedlander); thinking of new ideas oneself (Rabbeinu Yonah); the certainty of
what *binah* concludes (Malbim); and, for the kabbalists and Ramchal, the
synthesis of the other two. Everyone agrees that *chochmah* is the *lowest* of
the three (Shemos Rabbah 41:3; Rashi to Shabbos 31a); whether *binah* or *daat*
ranks higher is disputed (the Maharal against some sources, see Maharsha).

## Distinctions

### chochmah (חכמה)
Root ח-כ-ם.
- Rashi (to Shemos 31:3, Devarim 1:13, Mishlei 1:5), whose source is the
  exchange between Rabbi Yosi and Arius in the *Sifrei* (to Devarim 1:13), and
  likewise Radak (to Melachim I 3:12): *chochmah* is what a person hears, that
  is, learns, from others.
- Chazal's *chacham* casts a wide net: he learns from every person (Avos 4:1),
  foresees consequences not yet come to be (Tamid 32a), makes his teachers
  wiser (Chagigah 14a).
- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini: *chacham* is the opposite of a simpleton,
  anyone who has mastered a body of knowledge, whether carpentry (Yeshayahu
  3:3, 40:20), snake-charming (Tehillim 58:6) or other technical skills
  (Shemos 31:6), even cunning and political ingenuity (Shmuel II 13:3), used
  for ill as well (Yirmiyahu 4:22); but when Tanach speaks of a *chacham*,
  especially in Koheles, it means a religious scholar, a sage who has mastered
  the Torah and the Divine arts.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (1740 to 1814): ח-כ is waiting or delaying, as in
  *mechakeh* (Yeshayahu 30:18; Chavakuk 2:3; Daniel 12:12; Iyov 32:4) and
  *chakah*, the fishing net one sets and waits by (Iyov 40:25; Yeshayahu 19:8;
  Chavakuk 1:15), so the *chacham* is the unhurried learner who waits to
  deliberate. He also derives *cheich*, the palate, from *chakah*, the open
  net resembling a mouth opened for food, and Rabbi Klein adds that Aramaic
  *chayach*, to smile, and Modern Hebrew *chiyuch* likely come from *cheich*.
- Malbim: *chochmah* is practical wisdom, applying only where its opposite was
  possible, the intelligence that chooses the smart way of acting over the
  foolish one; true *chochmah* comes only by Divine revelation, since
  otherwise one cannot know for certain that a way is the smartest, and
  non-revelatory *chochmah* is a borrowed term.

### binah (בינה) and tevunah (תבונה)
Root ב-י-ן.
- The *navon* is *meivin davar mitoch davar*, he understands one matter from
  within another (Chagigah 14a), deriving new ideas from what he has learned.
- Bedersi: from *bein*, "between"; the *navon* applies relevant data and
  excludes the irrelevant, telling one datum from another, and so reaches new
  conclusions.
- Pappenheim: from ב-נ, building (*boneh*; *even* and *teven*, building
  materials; *ben* and *bas*; *avnayim*, the birthing stool); *binah* builds on
  an idea by applying it elsewhere and extrapolating. (English *maven* comes
  from *meivin* by way of Yiddish.)
- Malbim: the abstract counterpart of *chochmah*, the faculty that reads an
  allegory or solves a riddle, and works all one has perceived, by the senses or
  the intellect, into a logically sound conclusion.
- On another model, *chochmah* is the raw content of the Written Torah with its
  limitless planes of interpretation, *binah* the Oral Torah that processes and
  explains it, and *daat* the balance between them (the *Zohar*, see *Matok
  MiDvash* to Yisro 85a; the Vilna Gaon, *Biurei Aggados* to Bava Kamma 92b, and
  to Mishlei 1:8, with the glosses to 10:13). On this model Rabbi Eliyahu Tzion
  Sofer explains that the Syrian-Greeks opposed *binah* in particular, denying
  the Oral Torah while they had the Written Torah before them in the Septuagint;
  Rabbi Klein adds that this is why *Maoz Tzur* calls the Jewish people *bnei
  binah* in telling of the victory over the Greeks.

*Binah* against *tevunah*: most authorities use them almost interchangeably.
- Rabbi Pappenheim, who admits he has seen no source on the difference, proposes
  his own from the initial tav: *binah* grasps the big picture of many
  components, *tevunah* breaks it down into its parts.
- The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 2:2-3, 2:6): *tevunah* is the reflection that
  qualifies one's *chochmah* or *binah*.
- In *Chemdah Genuzah* (to Mishlei 1:1; see also *Zohar* Vayakhel 201a) the Gaon
  writes that *binah* is understanding on one's own terms, *tevunah*
  understanding thorough enough to explain to others. Rabbi Shlomo Brevda (1931
  to 2013; *Leil Shimurim* p. 26) points out that this runs counter to the
  saying cited in the yeshiva world in the name of Rav Chaim Soloveitchik of
  Brisk (1853 to 1918), that a deficiency in explaining something shows a
  deficiency in understanding it.

### daat (דעת)
Root י-ד-ע. The Mishnah (Avos 3:17) makes *daat* and *binah* depend on each
other.
- Rashi and Rabbi Ovadia Bartenura (1445 to 1515; see also Rashbatz): *binah*
  derives a new idea from a lesson and *daat* grasps the reasoning behind it, so
  each fails without the other. At face value, then, it seems the two go hand in
  hand.
- Rashi gives two faces elsewhere: to Shemos 31:3 *daat* is *ruach hakodesh*, a
  lower form of prophecy; to Chagigah 12a, where *chochmah*, *tevunah* and
  *daat* head the ten qualities with which the world was created, it is
  reconciliation.
- Rabbi Shmuel Yaakov Burnstein (1946 to 2017) unites them: *daat* is connection
  (as "knowing" is intimacy, Bereishis 4:1, and kinship, Rus 2:1), joining all
  the pieces into one resolution where everything fits, and *ruach hakodesh* is
  a person connecting himself to Hashem and so seeing the whole. *Nefesh
  HaChaim* 1:6 explains that the *Eitz HaDaat* brings about the interconnection
  of good and evil, and the Vilna Gaon to Mishlei 2:5 similarly makes *daat* the
  reconciling of contradictory ideas. Rabbi Klein adds that connection presumes
  separation; indeed Havdalah is said in the blessing for knowledge: "if there
  is no *daat*, from where is there *havdalah*?" (Yerushalmi Berachos 5:2).
- Rabbi Chaim Friedlander (1923 to 1986): the connection is of intellect with
  emotion, knowledge fully internalised; Bedersi likewise ties *daat* to
  emotions and feelings.
- Rabbeinu Yonah (to Avos 3:17): the ability to think of new ideas independently
  (Rabbi Klein wonders whether Rabbeinu Yonah means the brain forging new
  connections).
- Malbim: the certainty of conclusions reached through *binah*.

Rank is disputed: some sources put *daat* above *binah* (see Maharsha to
Kesubos 5a); the Maharal (*Chiddushei Aggados* to Kiddushin 30a and Avodah Zarah
19b; *Tiferes Yisrael* ch. 56) puts *binah* above. The kabbalists (*Eitz Chaim*,
*Shaar HaAmidah* ch. 11) match ChaBaD to *chesed*, *gevurah* and *tiferes*. By
this model, Rabbi Klein explains, *chochmah* is knowledge received from others,
*binah* knowledge intuited from what one already knows with little outside
input, and *daat* the balance that harmonises them, the product of raw
*chochmah* processed through *binah*.
Ramchal (introduction to *Klach Pischei Chochmah*) calls *daat* the offspring of
the two, and Rabbi Shaul Levi Mortera (1596 to 1660) writes that *chochmah* is
acquired, *binah* natural, and *daat* their synthesis. Dr. Michael G. Samet, a
brother of Ohr Somayach's mashgiach Rav Yehuda Samet, told Rabbi Klein that he
once pointed out to the Yale professor Robert J. Sternberg how closely his
Triarchic Theory of Intelligence resembles this threefold scheme, and that
Sternberg was quite taken aback.

## Rendering guidance

- The triad חכמה תבונה ודעת (Shemos 31:3): "wisdom, understanding, and
  knowledge," in that order.
- The pair חכמה ובינה (Devarim 4:6): "wisdom and understanding" (Rabbi Klein
  has "wisdom and insightfulness"). The absence of *daat* is load-bearing (Rabbi
  Yaakov Chaim Sofer: the nations reach *chochmah* and *binah* but not *daat*;
  his brother Rabbi Eliyahu Tzion Sofer, from Eichah Rabbah 2:13: not even
  *binah*), so do not pad the pair with "knowledge."
- נבון וחכם (Bereishis 41:33, 41:39): "discerning and wise" (Rabbi Klein has
  "insightful and wise"), in Hebrew order. For Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer the
  same silence about *daat* is the point: Pharaoh could not see that Yosef was
  also a *yodea*.
- *Binah* and *tevunah* alone: both "understanding." Where a passage contrasts
  them, say whose distinction is followed: Rabbi Pappenheim (the whole against
  its parts), the Vilna Gaon to Mishlei 2 (*tevunah* the reflection that
  qualifies), or the Gaon in *Chemdah Genuzah* (*tevunah* the understanding one
  can explain to others).
- *Daat* alone: "knowledge"; if the passage turns on connection,
  reconciliation, or certainty, keep *daat* transliterated with a gloss.
- *Chacham* in Tanach, above all in Koheles: a sage, a religious scholar of
  Torah (Bedersi). Of a craft or a scheme it is the skilled or the cunning.
- Trap: loose English "wisdom" absorbs *binah* and *daat*, and loose
  "knowledge" absorbs *chochmah* and *daat*. Where a derashah orders or ranks
  them, give one English word per Hebrew word.
