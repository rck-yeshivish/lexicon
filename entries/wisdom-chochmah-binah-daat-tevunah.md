# Wisdom: chochmah / binah (tevunah) / daat

## Bottom line

*Chochmah* is received knowledge, what one takes in from a teacher or from any
source: **wisdom**. *Binah* (with *tevunah*) is understanding one thing from
within another, building on what one already holds: **understanding** (a
*navon* is **discerning**). *Daat* is the joining of the two, knowledge made
certain and internalised: **knowledge**. The one thing not to get wrong:
*chochmah* is the *lowest* of the three, not the crown (Shemos Rabbah 41:3;
Rashi to Shabbos 31a), so keep the Betzalel order, "wisdom, understanding, and
knowledge" (Shemos 31:3), and never let "wisdom" swallow *daat*.

## Distinctions

### chochmah (חכמה)
Root ח-כ-ם. Rashi (to Shemos 31:3, Devarim 1:13, Mishlei 1:5, from Sifrei to
Devarim 1:13; likewise Radak to Melachim I 3:12): *chochmah* is what a person
hears, that is, learns, from others. Chazal's *chacham* casts a wide net: he
learns from every person (Avos 4:1), foresees what is not yet born (Tamid
32a), makes his teachers wiser (Chagigah 14a). Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini:
*chacham* is the opposite of a simpleton, anyone who has mastered a body of
knowledge, carpentry (Yeshayahu 3:3), snake-charming (Tehillim 59:6), even
cunning (Shmuel II 13:3; Yirmiyahu 4:22); but when Tanach, Koheles above all,
says *chacham* plainly, it means a Torah sage. Pappenheim: ח-כ is waiting
(*mechakeh*; *chakah*, the net one sets and waits by), so the *chacham* is the
unhurried learner who deliberates. Malbim: *chochmah* is practical, the
intelligence that picks the smart way of acting where a foolish way was
possible; in its full sense it comes only by revelation.

### binah (בינה) and tevunah (תבונה)
Root ב-י-ן. The *navon* is *meivin davar mitoch davar*, he understands one
matter from within another (Chagigah 14a). Bedersi: from *bein*, "between";
the *navon* tells the relevant datum from the irrelevant and so reaches new
conclusions. Pappenheim: from ב-נ, building; *binah* builds on an idea by
extending it. Malbim: the abstract counterpart of *chochmah*, the faculty
that reads an allegory, solves a riddle, and works everything perceived into a
sound conclusion. Zohar and the Gaon: the Oral Torah that processes the
Written; hence *Maoz Tzur*'s "*bnei binah*" against the Greeks, who had the
Septuagint but scorned the Oral Torah (Rabbi Eliyahu Tzion Sofer). *Binah*
against *tevunah*: most treat them as one word. Pappenheim (his own proposal):
*binah* grasps the whole, *tevunah* resolves it into parts. Vilna Gaon (to
Mishlei 2:2-3, 2:6): *tevunah* is the reflection that qualifies *chochmah* or
*binah*; in Chemdah Genuzah (to Mishlei 1:1, with Zohar Vayakhel 201a):
*binah* is understanding for oneself, *tevunah* understanding thorough enough
to teach to others.

### daat (דעת)
Root י-ד-ע. Rashi and Bartenura (to Avos 3:17, with Rashbatz): *daat* grasps
the reasoning behind a lesson, where *binah* extrapolates from it; hence the
Mishnah's mutual dependence. Rashi gives two faces elsewhere: to Shemos 31:3
*daat* is *ruach hakodesh*; to Chagigah 12a it is reconciliation. Rabbi
Shmuel Yaakov Burnstein unites them: *daat* is connection (as "knowing" is
intimacy, Bereishis 4:1, and kinship, Rus 2:1), joining all the pieces into one
resolved whole; *ruach hakodesh* is a man connecting himself to Hashem and so
seeing the whole (so Nefesh HaChaim 1:6 on the *Eitz HaDaat*, the Gaon to
Mishlei 2:5, and Yerushalmi Berachos 5:2). Rabbi Chaim Friedlander: the connection is
of intellect with emotion, knowledge internalised (Bedersi likewise ties
*daat* to feeling). Rabbeinu Yonah (to Avos 3:17): originating new ideas
oneself. Malbim: the *certainty* of conclusions reached through *binah*. Rank
is disputed: Maharsha (to Kesubos 5a) puts *daat* above *binah*; Maharal
(Chiddushei Aggados to Kiddushin 30a, Avodah Zarah 19b; Tiferes Yisrael ch.
56) puts *binah* above. The kabbalistic scheme (Eitz Chaim, Shaar HaAmidah ch.
11) sets ChaBaD against *chesed*, *gevurah*, *tiferes*: received, intuited,
balanced; Ramchal (introduction to Klach Pischei Chochmah) calls *daat* the
offspring of the two.

## Rendering guidance

- The triad חכמה תבונה ודעת (Shemos 31:3): "wisdom, understanding, and
  knowledge," in that order.
- The pair חכמה ובינה (Devarim 4:6): "wisdom and understanding." The absence
  of *daat* is load-bearing (Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer: the nations reach
  *chochmah* and *binah* but not *daat*; Rabbi Eliyahu Tzion Sofer from Eichah
  Rabbah 2:48: not even *binah*), so do not pad the pair with "knowledge."
- נבון וחכם (Bereishis 41:33, 41:39): "discerning and wise," Hebrew order;
  the same silence about *daat* is the point (Pharaoh could not see that
  Yosef was also a *yodea*).
- *Binah* and *tevunah* alone: both "understanding." Separate them only when
  the passage contrasts them, and then follow the Gaon: *tevunah* is the
  understanding one can teach.
- *Daat* alone: "knowledge"; if the passage turns on connection,
  reconciliation, or certainty, keep *daat* transliterated with a gloss.
  *Chacham* in Koheles and Chazal: "sage," not "scholar" (Bedersi).
- Trap: loose English "wisdom" absorbs *binah* and *daat*, and loose
  "knowledge" absorbs *chochmah* and *daat*. Where a derashah orders or ranks
  them, one English word per Hebrew word; and "intellect" is *sechel*, not
  any of these.
