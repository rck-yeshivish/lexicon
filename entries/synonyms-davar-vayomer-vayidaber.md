# Synonyms: the method (davar, vayomer / vayidaber, melucha / moshel, ba / atah)

## Bottom line

The column's founding premise: in Lashon HaKodesh a word is not a convention but
the essence of the thing, which is why *davar* means both **word** and **thing**;
every element of creation is a Divine word set into matter. Two Hebrew words for
one idea therefore always differ. Render *vayomer* **he said** (brief) and
*vayidaber* **he spoke** (extended); *melucha* **kingship** accepted by the
people and *moshel* **ruler** whether accepted or not; *ba* and *atah* both
**came**, the second being Aramaic and chosen for that reason. The rule a
translator must not break: never render two different Hebrew words with one
English word in a passage that has set them side by side.

## Distinctions

### the five resolutions
Where two Hebrew words look identical in meaning, the answer usually takes one of
these forms:
1. They only seem alike, and a slight, barely visible difference separates them.
2. They name the same thing but focus on different properties of it.
3. In verbs, the same act done to a different degree or with a different intent.
4. Taxonomy: one is the general term (hypernym), the other a species that falls
   inside it (hyponym).
5. The Torah has borrowed a word from another language to make a point, and the
   loanword means what a Hebrew word already meant.

### vayomer (ויאמר) and vayidaber (וידבר)
Malbim: *vayomer* is a short, brief verbal expression; *vayidaber* is a long,
drawn-out address. Example of resolution 3.

### melucha (מלוכה) and moshel (מושל)
Tehillim 22:29, "for to Hashem is *melucha*, and He is *moshel* over the
nations." Ibn Ezra: both are sovereignty, but a *melech* is a popular sovereign
whose rule his constituents willingly took upon themselves, while a *moshel*
rules on regardless of whether his people object. Example of resolution 1 or 2.
See `entries/king-melech-moshel.md`, where the Vilna Gaon works the same pair
out at length.

### ba (בא) and atah (אתה)
Devarim 33:2: "Hashem *ba* from Sinai... and He *atah* from the holy
multitudes." The *meforshim*: the two words mean the same, but the first is
Hebrew and the second Aramaic, and the Torah reaches for Aramaic here to hint
that Hashem first offered the Torah to the other nations before giving it to
Klal Yisrael. Example of resolution 5.

### davar (דבר)
The premise itself. One word carries "word" and "thing" because the two are the
same: creation is Divine speech crystallized into material existence. On this
footing, close study of what a word actually means is not an indulgence but
required.

## Rendering guidance

- Fix a stable English word to each Hebrew word across a passage and hold it
  there. *Vayomer* is "said," *vayidaber* is "spoke"; if the English needs
  variety, get it from the sentence, not by swapping the pair.
- Where two synonyms sit in one pasuk, assume the second is doing work. Devarim
  33:2 and Tehillim 22:29 both look like poetic repetition and are not.
- Trap: English "speak" and "say" are interchangeable in ordinary prose, so a
  translator will pick by rhythm. That destroys Malbim's distinction wherever a
  derashah turns on which verb the Torah used.
- Trap: an Aramaic word inside a Hebrew pasuk is a signal, not a stylistic
  accident. Do not smooth *atah* into the same English as *ba* without a note.
- Where a word is the general term and its partner a species of it (resolution
  4), the English must keep the hierarchy: a general word for the hypernym, a
  narrow one for the hyponym, never two words of equal breadth.
