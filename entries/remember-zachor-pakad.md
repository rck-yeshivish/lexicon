# Remember: zachor / pakad

## Bottom line

*Zachor* is remembering in the mind: **remember, call to mind**. *Pakad* is
remembering that issues in action: **attend to, take note of** (and in the
right context "count," "appoint," "punish," "deposit"). Malbim: *zechirah* is
in thought, *pekidah* is in deed. The trap is rendering both as "remembered"
and flattening a pasuk that turns on the difference; when Hashem *pakad*
someone, something happened to them.

## Distinctions

### zachor (זכור / זכר)
Root ז-כ-ר. Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra: *zachor* is to bring back to mind something
one knew and forgot, as against *shamor*, to keep in mind what one already
holds. Malbim: *zachor* is purely mental recall, the fact or idea called up and
nothing further; and in his second approach, *zachor* is sustained remembering
over a long span. Rabbi Naftali Hertz Treves (*Sefer HaGur*) takes *zachor*
itself to imply remembering as an act of mercy, which is how he settles Rashi's
comment that Hashem's remembering Noach (Bereishis 8:1) was a turn from
judgment to mercy even though the pasuk uses the name Elokim.

The noun *zecher* is not always "memory." Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*):
*zecher* sometimes means the **scent** of a thing (Vayikra 6:8, Hoshea 14:8,
Tehillim 20:4), a memory being a whiff of the thing and not the thing.

### pakad (פקד)
Root פ-ק-ד. Its range in Tanach is unusually wide: to count (Bamidbar 1:21,
2:32, Shmuel I 15:4), to be missing (Bamidbar 31:49, Yeshayahu 38:10), to
command (Tehillim 19:9, 119:56, Ezra 1:2), to appoint (Bamidbar 1:50, 31:14,
Esther 2:3, Tehillim 109:6), to punish (Shemos 20:5, Hoshea 2:15), to bring
about death (Bamidbar 16:29, Yirmiyahu 46:21), to visit (Rashi to Nedarim 39b;
Kli Yakar to Bamidbar 16:29), and to deposit (Vayikra 5:23). Malbim ties the
whole spread to one core: to remember something in order to **deal with it**.
Counting is the plainest form of attending to a thing, instructing and
appointing is another, giving a man his deserts (punishment, or death, or a
visit) another, and handing an object to someone to mind is another still.
Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer sums it up: *zechirah* is in thought, *pekidah*
is in action. In Malbim's second approach *pakad* is a single passing act of
recall, enough to do what must be done and then let go. Rabbi Asher Weiser
connects *kapdanus*, being particular, to פ-ק-ד by metathesis: the *makpid*
attends to every detail.

Rabbi Saadia ben David Al-Dhamari reads the pair by elapsed time instead:
*pakad* is remembering after a long stretch, *zachor* after a short one. Hence
*pakad* for the redemption from Mitzrayim (Shemos 3:16) after centuries and for
Sarah (Bereishis 21:1) after decades, but *zachor* for Noach after a year in
the teivah (Bereishis 8:1) and for Rachel after a few years (Bereishis 30:22).
Note that Chazal treat the two as close enough to be interchangeable for one
purpose: Rosh Hashanah 32b rules that *pikdonos* count as *zichronos* for the
Mussaf berachah.

### zachar (זכר, "male")
Spelled identically to *zecher*, and the Gemara plays on it: Bava Basra 21b has
Yoav sparing the Amalekite women because his teacher had read *zecher Amalek*
(Devarim 25:19) as *zachar*. Rashbatz (to Avos 5:12) reads the shared spelling
as substantive, and Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg reads it as legacy, sons
carrying the father's name and so his memory. Rabbi Moshe de Leon (*Sefer
HaRimmon*) makes the pair giver and receiver, and on that basis explains
*Yaaleh V'Yavo*: **tovah** is direct bestowal, so we say *zachreinu bo l'tovah*;
**berachah** is the growth of what one already holds, so we say *ufakdeinu bo
livrachah*. *Kedushas Levi* (to Bereishis 21:1) reads *pakad* as revealing a
gift that already existed from Hashem's timeless vantage and was only hidden
from the recipient.

## Rendering guidance

- *Zachor* in the six mitzvos of remembrance: **remember**, plain.
- *Pakad* of Hashem toward a person: **took note of**, or **remembered** with
  the consequence named, since the pasuk always supplies one (Sarah conceives,
  the redemption begins). "Visited" is the old rendering and reads archaic.
- *Pakad* in a census or a list: **counted**, **mustered**; *nifkad*:
  **missing**, not "unremembered."
- Trap: English "remember" will absorb both words. In a passage that contrasts
  them, or in *Yaaleh V'Yavo* where both appear in one breath, hold the
  distinction: *zachreinu* "remember us," *ufakdeinu* "and take account of us."
- *Zecher* is not automatically "memory." Check whether the pasuk wants
  **trace** or **scent** (Hoshea 14:8) before defaulting.
- The article gives Shemos 20:7 for *zachor es yom haShabbos*; the locus in the
  standard Masoretic division is Shemos 20:8. It also gives Devarim 4:9 for
  remembering Sinai and Devarim 9:7 for the wilderness provocations, both of
  which use *hishamer/al tishkach* forms alongside the remembrance theme.
