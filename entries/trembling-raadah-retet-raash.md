# Trembling: ra'adah / ziyah / retet (ra'ash, rogez, ga'ash)

## Bottom line

*Ra'adah* is **trembling**, the deep shake of fear. *Ziyah* is a **shudder**, a
quiver felt rather than seen, and the Targum's word for both *vayecherad* and
*ra'ash*. *Retet* is the **lighter** tremble (Wertheimer). *Ra'ash* is
**quaking** or **tumult**, vigorous movement of any kind. *Rogez* is
**agitation**, physical in Hebrew and angry in Aramaic. Yoma 4b strings four
together and Shmuel II 22:8 puts three in one pasuk, so a translator needs
distinct English words, not one repeated.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| רעדה | ra'adah | trembling | felt inside, unseen (Wertheimer), or inner fear breaking out (Malbim) |
| זיע | ziyah | shuddering | anxiety from outside; a harp string's quiver (Pappenheim) |
| רתת | retet | trembling, lighter | lighter than ra'adah and chil (Wertheimer) |
| רעש | ra'ash | quaking, tumult | vigorous movement of any kind; an earthquake; later, noise |
| רגז | rogez | agitation; anger | trembling extended to any agitation (Shadal); anger in Aramaic (Rashbam) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether ra'adah is visible.** Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: *ra'adah* is
felt within the body and not discernible to an onlooker (Daniel 10:11), against
*chil*, which can be seen. Malbim: *ra'adah* is precisely where inner fear grows
so full that it shows itself in tremors. Either way, render it "trembling."

**Retet with a tav and retet with a tet.** Each spelling appears once: with a tav
in Hoshea 13:1, with a tet in Yirmiyahu 49:24. Radak and Ibn Janach (*Sefer
HaShorashim*) define the tav form as *ra'adah*, following the Targum, which
renders the *ra'ad* of Shemos 15:15 as *reteta*; Menachem Ibn Saruk defines it
as *chil*. On the tet form Ibn Janach breaks ranks: it is pain in the joints,
not trembling, and Rabbi Pappenheim takes it as a disease of sudden repeated
flexing of nerve and muscle. Bedersi (*Chotam Tochnit*), Rabbi Yosef Kimchi and
Rabbi Shlomo Ibn Parchon hold the two spellings are one word; so the Bottom line.

**The order of intensity in Shmuel II 22:8 (Tehillim 18:8).** Malbim (cited in
*Sefer HaCarmel*, and in his *Yair Ohr*) reads the pasuk as ascending: *ga'ash*
slightest, *ra'ash* stronger, *rogez* strongest. Rabbi Reuven Landau (*Degel
Machaneh Reuven* §61) holds the reverse between the last two. The Bottom line
follows Malbim, who works from the pasuk's own sequence.

**What ziyah was at Sinai.** Onkelos renders *vayecherad* as *ziyah* both when
the mountain trembled (Shemos 19:18) and when the people trembled (19:16), so
the *ziyah* of Yoma 4b may be either. The Mechilta (*BaChodesh* §9) reads
*vayanu'a* in Shemos 20:15 as *ziyah*, which favors the people.

## Distinctions

### ra'adah (רעדה) and retet (רתת, רטט)
Root ר-ע-ד, nine appearances: *ra'ad* seizing the mighty of Moav (Shemos 15:15),
the land that quivers at Hashem's glance (Tehillim 104:32), and the *ra'adah* of
Tehillim 2:11, which Yoma 4b expounds as the Torah having been given "with
dread, with fear, with *retet* and with *ziyah*." *Retet*, for Rabbi Wertheimer,
is lighter than *ra'adah* or *chil*, and in Chazal it splits: a kohen is
disqualified once his hands and feet shake (*yirtat*) with age, no fear in it
(Chullin 24a-24b), while *mirtat* is fear with no shaking in it.

### ziyah (זיע) and its family
Rabbi Pappenheim's biliteral ז-ע, movement triggered by anxiety, its measure the
quiver of a harp string held tight, felt by touch and not seen. From it: *za*,
as Mordechai neither rose nor stirred before Haman (Esther 5:9); *zeiyah*, sweat
(Bereishis 3:19), which is why the two are often confused in quoting Yoma 4b;
*za'avah* in the tochachah (Devarim 28:25), which Onkelos renders *ziyah* and
Rashi glosses as shaking in fear; *zava'ah*, an earthquake (Berachos 54a).

### ra'ash (רעש), ga'ash (געש), rogez (רגז), chareid (חרד)
*Ra'ash* is absent from Chumash but appears close to fifty times after it, and
covers vigorous movement generally: the earthquake in Uzziyah's day is simply
"the *ra'ash*" (Amos 1:1). Targum Yonasan and *Devarim Rabbah* 11:10 render the
*kol ra'ash gadol* of Yechezkel 3:12 as *ziyah*, which is how it entered *U'va
L'Tzion*. *Ga'ash* appears nine times, and is also a place name. *Rogez*: Shadal
(to Bereishis 45:24) makes the physical trembling primary and the emotional
sense derived, as in *lev ragaz* (Devarim 28:65); Rashbam there notes that
biblical Aramaic uses it for anger, whence *b'rogez racheim tizkor*.

## Rendering guidance

- Yoma 4b's list: give each term its own English. "With dread, with fear, with
  trembling and with shuddering" keeps four; repeating one word loses the
  drashah. In Shmuel II 22:8, three verbs, three words, ascending per Malbim.
- Trap: English "shaking" absorbs *ra'adah*, *retet*, *ra'ash* and *rogez* at
  once. Reserve "quake" for *ra'ash*, "shudder" for *ziyah*, "tremble" for
  *ra'adah* and *retet*, and let *rogez* take "agitation" by context.
- רגז in Aramaic or a tefillah: "anger"; in a Hebrew pasuk, "trembling."
