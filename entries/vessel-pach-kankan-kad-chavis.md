# Vessel: pach / kankan / kad / chavis (kaneh)

## Bottom line

*Pach* is a small pottery **flask** or **jug**, used in Tanach only in *pach
hashemen*. *Kankan* is a Mishnaic **jug** or **jar**; *kad* a **jug** or
**pitcher**; *chavis* a **barrel**. The trap is over-differentiating: Chazal
themselves use these words interchangeably, because the vernacular never fixed
them (Bava Kamma 27a), which is why *Maoz Tzur* can sing of *kankanim* where the
Gemara says *pachim*. *Kaneh* is not a container but a hollow **reed** or
**tube**.

## Where the authorities disagree

**The root of pach, and what it means.** The triliteralists take it as פ-כ-ה
(Radak, *Sefer HaShorashim*), the biliteralists as a two-letter פ-כ (*Machberet
Menachem*, Rashi to Yechezkel 47:2), and both connect it to *mefakim*, the flow
of liquid. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim also takes the root as פ-כ but gives it the
sense of *reversal*, parent of *hafuch*: a flask so narrow-necked that it must be
turned over to empty. He extends it to *puch*, the kohl stored in such a flask,
and thence to *puch* and *nofech* as gemstone names, for their kohl-like color.

**Why Maoz Tzur says kankanim.** Poetic license: *kankanim* rhymes with
*shoshanim* and *pachim* does not. Rabbi Tzvi Elimelech Shapiro of Dinov rejects
this, since *shemanim* would have rhymed too; the column answers that *shemanim*
had already ended the previous line. Either way the words are near-synonyms.

**Whether the Menorah's branches may be hollow.** Rambam (*Beis HaBechirah* 3:4)
rules a Menorah made *chalulah* is fit. Rabbi Yosef Karo: *chalulah* means not
made from a single hunk of gold, permitted only when the Menorah is fashioned
from another metal. The Mabit (*Kiryas Sefer*): the branches may be made hollow
*lechatchilah*, even out of one hunk. Rabbi Massoud Chai Rokach and Rabbi
Avraham Dovber Kahane-Shapiro explain that Rav Karo declined the Mabit's reading
for want of a source in Chazal; the Mabit needs none, since *kaneh* itself
carries hollowness, and Rashbam and Ibn Ezra (to Shemos 25:32) say outright that
the *kanim* were hollow within.

## Distinctions

### pach (פך)
Three occurrences in Tanach, each in *pach hashemen*: Shmuel's anointing of Shaul
(I Shmuel 10:1) and Yonah's anointing of Yehu (II Melachim 9:1, 9:3). Jastrow:
"flask," "jug." In the Mishnah (Shabbos 17:5, Beitzah 1:7, Bava Kamma 8:6,
Keilim 2:2, Negaim 12:5) it is a pottery jug for oil, and Rashi (to Avodah Zarah
71b) makes it smaller than a *chavis*. Chullin 91a (cited by Rashi
to Bereishis 32:25) has Yaakov recross the Yabok for *pachim ketanim*; as the
Tosafists (*Daas Zekeinim*, *Hadar Zekeinim*) and Rabbeinu Bachya cite it, the
derashah reads *levado* as *l'kado*, "for his *kad*," on the resemblance of beis
and kaf. So Chazal gloss *kad* with *pach*.

### kankan (קנקן)
Absent from Tanach. In the Mishnah it stores wine (Maaser Sheini 1:3-4, 3:12-13,
Avodah Zarah 2:4), oil (Kesubos 13:4, Bava Metzia 3:8, Shevuos 6:3), or water
(Mikvaos 2:7, 2:9); Tosafot (Avodah Zarah 32a): typically pottery. Rabbi Ernest
Klein traces the form back to Hebrew *kaneh*, so a *kankan* is defined by its
hollow receptacle.

### kad (כד) and chavis (חבית)
The Mishnah opens a ruling with one word and continues it with the other
(Terumos 11:7-8, Demai 7:8, Bava Kamma 3:1, 3:5, 10:4); Bava Kamma 27a: in
common speech the two were switched about and not fixed. *Kankan* joins the set,
since Maaser Sheini 3:13 interchanges it with *chavis*, and Maaser Sheini 1:3-4,
Kesubos 13:4 and Shevuos 6:3 with *kad* (*Tosafos Yom Tov* to Kesubos 13:4).

### kaneh (קנה)
Over fifty occurrences in Tanach: "reed," "stem/branch," "tube," all pipe-like
and hollow in the middle; in Rabbinic Hebrew it also names the windpipe. The
Torah calls the Menorah's branches *kanim* (Shemos 25:31-36), which is what lets
the Mabit rule them hollow.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "jug" will absorb *pach*, *kankan* and *kad*, and often that is
  right. Distinguish only where the passage turns on size: *pach* small flask,
  *kad* jug, *chavis* barrel.
- Where a sugya shifts from one word to another, do not repair it in English;
  the shift is the point, and Bava Kamma 27a says so.
- *Pach hashemen* in a Chanukah context: "flask of oil," not "can" or "cruse."
- *Kaneh* is never a vessel: "reed," "stem," "branch," or "windpipe."
