# Friend: reya / chaver / amis

## Bottom line

The three run on a scale of closeness. *Amis* is the loosest: **associate,
acquaintance**, the man one deals with rather than confides in. *Chaver* is the
middle and the default: **friend**, one joined to you in some shared thing.
*Reya* is the closest: **companion** or **confidant**, the one you are with
constantly and to whom you tell your secrets. Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms and
Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer both set them in this order. Do not render all
three "friend" in a passage that lists them.

## Distinctions

### amis (עמית)
Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms (commentary to the Siddur): one who comes now and
then, not often, so it carries less intimacy than *chaver*. Rabbi Wertheimer:
*amis*, unlike *chaver*, is not built on a word for connection at all; it
denotes the man with whom one has business dealings.

### chaver (חבר)
Root ח-ב-ר, joining. Rokeach: a *chaver* is one who has left his original place
and attached himself (*chibur*) elsewhere, as a member of a body is called a
*chaver* of it; alternatively, two who were apart and have now drawn close,
old friends reunited. Wertheimer: connection in one shared respect, the way two
men are friends because they learn in the same place. Chazal apply *chaver* to
a wife as well (Shabbos 63a). One of Yisro's seven names is Chever, because he
became a *chaver* of Hashem.

### reya (רע)
The most intimate. Rokeach: the one you are with constantly and to whom you
reveal your secrets. Wertheimer: complete affection, the highest form of
companionship. Tanach bears this out: *reiacha asher kenafshecha*, "your friend
who is as your own self" (Devarim 13:7), among those whose pull toward avodah
zarah is hardest to resist; and *ve'ahavta lereiacha kamocha* (Vayikra 19:18).
Chazal in places apply the mitzvah of loving one's *reya* as oneself to one's
wife (Kiddushin 41a, Niddah 17a), and *reiasi* is the ordinary way a man refers
to his wife. Targum Onkelos renders *rei'eihu* sometimes as *chavrei* and
sometimes as *rachamohi*, "one who loves him," according to the context.
Yisro's name Reuel is read as his having become a *reya* of Hashem.

The article flags one usage it cannot fit the scale: Shemos 11:2, where the
Jews are told to ask their Egyptian *rei'eihu* for vessels, uses the most
intimate of the three.

### The root of reya
ר-ע ordinarily means bad or evil. Mishlei 13:20 already plays on it: *ro'eh
chesilim yeiro'a*. Rashi: *yeiro'a* there is not "will become evil" but "will
be broken" (see also Maharsha to Berachos 63a on Mishlei 18:24). Hence the
Talmudic *kosel ra'ua*, a broken wall, and *teruah*, the broken-up shofar
blasts against the unbroken *tekiah*. The column offers two ways to hold the
opposites together, both of them tentative: "it seems" that this is one more
Hebrew word carrying a sense and its reverse, or, as he then suggests himself,
a *reya* is precisely one whose separate existence is a brokenness off from
you, really meant to be attached to you.

The column closes on the theme of friendship with two lines from public life:
the American poet Ralph Waldo Emerson (1803-1882), "The only way to have a
friend is to be one," and President Bill Clinton's "*Shalom Chaver*."

## Rendering guidance

- Set renderings before translating. The column's own English is *chaver* =
  **friend**, *amis* = **acquaintance**, *reya* = **confidante**; where the
  secret-keeping sense is not what is live, **associate** for *amis* and
  **companion** for *reya* carry the same scale.
- *Ve'ahavta lereiacha kamocha*: the settled English is "love your fellow as
  yourself." Keep it; the entry's scale is for passages that contrast the
  words, not for retranslating a pasuk everyone knows.
- Trap: English "friend" will absorb all three, and "companion" will absorb
  *chaver* and *reya* together. When a source grades them (Yisro's names,
  Rokeach's list), render each differently or the whole point disappears.
- Trap (ours, not the column's): *chaver* in a Mishnaic context often means
  something else entirely, the man trusted in matters of tumah and maasros
  against an *am ha'aretz*. That *chaver* is not on this scale.
- A wordplay on ר-ע as "broken" (Mishlei 13:20 and the *teruah* connection)
  cannot be carried in English. Transliterate and gloss.
