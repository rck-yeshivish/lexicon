# Friend: reya / chaver / amis

## Bottom line

The three run on a scale of closeness. *Amis* is the loosest: **associate,
acquaintance**, the man one deals with rather than confides in. *Chaver* is the
middle and the default: **friend**, one joined to you in some shared thing.
*Reya* is the closest: **companion** or **confidant**, the one you are with
frequently and to whom you tell your secrets. Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms and
Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer both set them in this order.

## Distinctions

### amis (עמית)

- Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms (commentary to the Siddur): one who comes now
  and then, not often, so it carries less intimacy than *chaver*.
- Rabbi Wertheimer: *amis*, unlike *chaver*, is not built on a word for
  connection at all; it denotes the man with whom one has business dealings.

### chaver (חבר)
Root ח-ב-ר, joining.

- Rokeach: a *chaver* is one who has left his original place and attached
  himself (*chibur*) elsewhere, as a member of a body is called a *chaver* of
  it; alternatively, two who were apart and have now drawn close, old friends
  reunited.
- Wertheimer: connection in one shared respect, the way two men are friends
  because they learn in the same place.

Chazal apply *chaver* to a wife as well (Shabbos 63a). One of Yisro's seven
names is Chever, because he became a *chaver* of Hashem.

### reya (רע)
The most intimate.

- Rokeach: the one you are with frequently and to whom you reveal your secrets.
- Wertheimer: it tends to denote complete affection, the highest form of
  companionship.

Tanach itself implies as much: *reiacha asher
kenafshecha*, "your friend who is as your own self" (Devarim 13:7), among those
whose influence might draw a man to avodah zarah; and *ve'ahavta lereiacha
kamocha* (Vayikra 19:18). Chazal in places apply the mitzvah of loving one's *reya* as oneself to one's
wife (Kiddushin 41a, Niddah 17a), and *reiasi* is the ordinary way a man refers
to his wife. Targum Onkelos renders *rei'eihu* sometimes as *chavrei* and
sometimes as *rachamohi*, "one who loves him," according to the context.
Yisro's name Reuel is read as his having become a *reya* of Hashem.

Rabbi Klein flags one usage he is not sure what to make of: Shemos 11:2, where
the Jews are told to ask their Egyptian *rei'eihu* for vessels, uses the most
intimate of the three.

### The root of reya
ר-ע ordinarily means bad or evil. Mishlei 13:20 already plays on it: *ro'eh
chesilim yeiro'a*. Rashi: *yeiro'a* there is not "will become evil" but "will
be broken" (see also Maharsha to Berachos 63a on Mishlei 18:24). Likewise the
Talmudic *kosel ra'ua*, a broken wall, and *teruah*, the broken-up shofar
blasts against the unbroken *tekiah*. Rabbi Klein offers two ways to hold the
opposites together, both of them tentative: "it seems" that this is one more
Hebrew word carrying a sense and its reverse, or, as he then suggests himself,
a *reya* is precisely one whose separate existence is a brokenness off from
you, really meant to be attached to you.

Two lines from public life speak to the theme of friendship:
the American poet Ralph Waldo Emerson (1803-1882), "The only way to have a
friend is to be one," and President Bill Clinton's "*Shalom Chaver*."

Rabbi Mattisyahu HaYitzhari (on *Avos* 6:1): a *rei'a* is one who cleaves to his
fellow and whose thoughts never part from him, in study, in public affairs, or
in bodily matters; the word is from *ra'ayon*, thought (Koheles 1:17; Tehillim
139:2, 139:17). So *bechol eis ohev harei'a, ve'ach letzarah yivaled* (Mishlei
17:17): a true *rei'a* loves at every time, and hurries to his friend's trouble
as though born that moment to be his brother. He reads *rei'im ahuvim* in the
wedding blessings the same way: each seeks to be loved by the other as he loves
him, thinking himself wanting in his friend's eyes.

## Rendering guidance

- Set renderings before translating. Rabbi Klein's own English is *chaver* =
  **friend**, *amis* = **acquaintance**, *reya* = **confidante**; where the
  secret-keeping sense is not what is live, **associate** for *amis* and
  **companion** for *reya* carry the same scale.
- *Ve'ahavta lereiacha kamocha*: the settled English is "love your fellow as
  yourself." Keep it; the entry's scale is for passages that contrast the
  words, not for retranslating a pasuk everyone knows.
- Trap: English "friend" will absorb all three, and "companion" will absorb
  *chaver* and *reya* together. When a source grades them (Yisro's names,
  Rokeach's list), render each differently or the whole point disappears.
- Trap: *chaver* in a Mishnaic context often means
  something else entirely, the man trusted in matters of tumah and maasros
  against an *am ha'aretz*. That *chaver* is not on this scale.
- A wordplay on ר-ע as "broken" (Mishlei 13:20 and the *teruah* connection)
  cannot be carried in English. Transliterate and gloss.
