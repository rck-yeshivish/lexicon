# Explanation: peirush / beiur / hesber / pesher / pitaron

## Bottom line

*Peirush* is the plain **explanation** or **commentary**: it makes known what a
text says on its surface, its legal and practical sense (Malbim: exoteric).
*Beiur* is **elucidation**: it goes beneath the surface to bring up what is
hidden (Malbim), at length and in writing, so that the matter is clear (Shadal).
*Hesber* is **explanation of the reasoning**, the *svara*: it answers *why*,
where *beiur* answers *what* (Rabbi Reuven Subar). *Pesher* and *pitaron* are
**interpretation**, the solving of an enigma; *pitaron* in Tanach is dream
interpretation only (Radak, Malbim). Do not render *beiur* as "commentary"; it
promises more than a *peirush* does.

## Distinctions

### peirush (פירוש)
Root פ-ר-ש (kin to *parashah*, *prishah*, *parush*, *parash*, *peresh*).
Shadal: the means of making known what was unknown, kin to פ-ר-ש with a sin,
"spreading out," since new information spreads the enigmatic matter open; so
the blasphemer and the wood-gatherer waited because Hashem had not yet
*porash*, made known, their sentence (Vayikra 24:12, Bamidbar 15:34). Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim: the root means "separating"; the verdict was separated out
from the possible outcomes, and *meforash*, "explicitly," is the true reading
separated from the ambiguous alternatives. Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi:
clarifying in an unambiguous way, as evidence is spread out (Devarim 22:17).
Malbim (*Yair Ohr*): *peirush* reveals the exoteric, the surface facts and the
normative implications, what everyone can see once it is spread out.

### beiur (ביאור)
Root ב-א-ר, which the classical lexicographers (Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Radak)
read as two senses, wellspring (*be'er*) and elucidation (*ba'er*). Pappenheim
drops the aleph to the biliteral ב-ר, "separated, choicest, clear" (*barah*,
*bari*, *bar*, *bahir*, *birah*): a *beiur* separates the correct explanation
from the mistaken ones. Shadal: *beiur* is a clear manner of *writing*,
expanding on an idea with words; the verb appears only three times in Tanach,
each about writing to clarify (*ba'er heitiv* on the stones, Devarim 27:8;
Moshe's *be'er* of the Torah, Devarim 1:5; Chavakuk's tablets, Chavakuk 2:2).
Mecklenburg cites Shadal. Tedeschi-Ashkenazi: the core is *ohr*, light; a
*beiur* sheds light (the very etymology of "elucidate"). Malbim: *beiur* is
clarification of the *esoteric*, the core elements beneath the surface, like a
*be'er* that draws hidden waters up into daylight. What *beiur* adds over
*peirush*, then, is depth and extent: it digs, and it writes out what it
finds. Commonly pronounced *biur*.

### hesber (הסבר)
From the Aramaic *svar*, to think or hold a position (Daniel 7:25; Talmudic
*sover*, *mistaber*, *svara*, the *Rabbanan Savora'ei*, the *savri* before
Kiddush). The Hebrew ש-ב-ר means waiting or hoping (Tehillim 145:15, 119:166)
and planning (Esther 9:1); *sever panim yafos* (Avos 1:15) per Meiri is a face
that makes others think you are glad to see them. Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim
145:15): *sover* joins separate pieces of information into one logical
thought (kin to *sofer*, counting, and *tzover*, gathering). Rabbi Reuven
Subar: *beiur* answers what is being said, *hesber* answers why, laying out
the *svara* behind it; the Aramaic *sover* also means "bear, lift up," and a
*hesber* lifts the idea by supplying its rational support (*masa u'matan*).
Hence *ba'er heitiv* is linked to translation (Rashi to Devarim 27:8): rendering
the *what* in a plainer register, not arguing the *why*.

### pesher (פשר) and pitaron (פתרון)
Radak and Malbim: *pitaron* in Tanach is interpretation of a dream (Bereishis
40-41, fourteen times; Berachos 55b's *posrei chalomos*); the Gemara extends
*poser* to reinterpreting a teaching (Eruvin 32b, Kesubos 107b), and Ibn Saruk,
Rashi, Rashbam, and Rabbeinu Tam call a word's definition its *pitaron*.
Shadal: *poser* contracts *posei'ach ohr*, "opens light." Rav Hirsch (to Shemos
13:2): kin to *peter rechem*, opening and revealing. *Pesher* appears once in
Hebrew (Koheles 8:1; Rashi: Moshe's *psharah*, mediation after the Eigel) and
in Daniel's Aramaic for dreams; Ibn Saruk defines each by the other; Ibn Janach
and Radak define *pitaron* as *peirush* / *beiur*. Tedeschi-Ashkenazi: from ש-ר,
untie (*sharei*, *matir*); Rabbi Eliyahu Bachur: shin/tav interchange with
*poser*, or metathesis of *peirush* (so too Ibn Melech, Bedersi). Rabbi
Avraham Vilner links *efshar* and *poshrim* (middle states) to *pesher*.

## Rendering guidance

- פירוש: "explanation," or "commentary" for the written genre (Rashi's
  *peirush*). מפורש: "explicitly," "expressly."
- ביאור: "elucidation" (best), "clarification"; as a genre title keep it
  transliterated. Never "commentary" when it stands beside *peirush*.
- הסבר: "explanation" with the reasoning audible: "the reasoning," "an account
  of why"; סברא: "reasoning," "logic," or transliterate in a learning context.
- פשר / פתרון: "interpretation," "meaning," "solution"; for a dream, always
  "interpretation."
- Trap: English "explanation" absorbs *peirush*, *beiur*, and *hesber*. Where
  a text pairs them (a *peirush* that is not yet a *beiur*; a *beiur* without a
  *hesber*), grade the English: explanation (surface), elucidation (depth,
  written out), reasoning (why).
- Together in a list, keep Malbim's ladder: *peirush* outside, *beiur* inside;
  and Subar's axis: *beiur* what, *hesber* why.
