# Land: eretz / adamah

## Bottom line

*Eretz* is **land** as territory: a country, a nation's settled land, and by
extension the earth itself down to its core. *Adamah* is **ground**: the arable
surface, the soil worked for crops. The Gra, Malbim and Rabbi Moravsky make
*eretz* the broader word, and the last two confine *adamah* to the thin top
layer; the Maharshal inverts it.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| ארץ | eretz | land | territory, a nation's country (Vilna Gaon); reaches down to the core (Malbim); surface only (Maharshal) |
| אדמה | adamah | ground | farmland (Vilna Gaon); thin top layer only (Malbim); ground cursed for Adam's sin (Pri Megadim) |

## Distinctions

### eretz (ארץ)

- The Vilna Gaon: *eretz* connotes land as national settlement, and in the
  construct it names a particular nation's land, so "country" fits it well; an
  unqualified *eretz* often means Eretz Yisrael in particular.
- Malbim: *eretz* takes in the whole planet, from the surface down to the
  innermost core, while *adamah* generally means only the crust whose dirt is
  farmed.

Bereishis Rabbah 5:8 relates *eretz* to *ratzon* and *ratz*, running: when the
world was created the land wanted to do Hashem's will, and ran to cover as much
of the earth's surface as it could until Hashem told it to stop.

The berachos put the difference on display. Over bread one says that Hashem
brings forth bread *min ha'aretz*, from the land; over vegetables, that He
creates the fruit *ha'adamah*, of the ground.

- Rabbeinu Bachaya and the Ritva: bread should by rights have taken the more
  specific *adamah*, the word for a field, and the reason it does not is that
  Chazal shaped the berachah on the language of the pasuk (Tehillim 104:14),
  which says bread comes from the *eretz*.
- Rabbi Yechiel Michel Moravsky of Lublin answers from the meanings themselves.
  *Adamah* is limited to the immediate top layer of dirt, while *eretz* reaches
  what lies underground; wheat, whose roots run deep (Yerushalmi Taanis 1:3 puts
  them at fifty handbreadths), is therefore said to come from the *eretz*, while
  vegetables, which do not necessarily need such depth, are fruit of the
  *adamah*. Rabbi Yisrael Lipschitz explains similarly.

### adamah (אדמה)

- The Gra: *adamah* denotes specifically uninhabited land set aside for
  agriculture, against *eretz* as settled national territory.
- The *Pri Megadim* adds a different axis entirely: *eretz* is land in its
  supernal, unblemished state, which is why it suits the Holy Land, while
  *adamah* is the stained and imperfect ground that Hashem cursed for Adam's sin
  (Bereishis 3:17). On his reading the vegetable berachah says *adamah* because
  it acknowledges Hashem's kindness now, growing produce out of cursed ground,
  while the bread berachah looks back before the sin, when whole loaves rose
  from the earth, and forward to the days of Mashiach when they will again
  (Shabbos 30b), and so says *eretz*, the land that will by then break free of
  its curse and return to its pure, unblemished state.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives *adamah* from the biliteral ד-ם, similarity,
whence *domem*, inactive: the *adamah* is where plants grow and are active, while
the *adamah* itself remains passive. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg builds on this
to explain why an unqualified *eretz* in the Torah often means the Holy Land and
*adamah* the lands outside it. *Eretz* derives from ר-ץ, persistency, alluding to
the earth's persistent motion, and in the Holy Land people must be constantly
active to fulfill the agricultural mitzvos; outside it they remain inactive with
respect to those mitzvos, and the land is *adamah*.

### the dissent
The Maharshal holds the reverse: *eretz* is limited to the surface and the
topmost three cubits of soil (compare Rashi to Bereishis 6:13), while *adamah*
is the more general word and takes in the depths as well. On his reading bread
comes from the *eretz* because grain shoots up out of the surface, and
vegetables are of the *adamah* because they draw their nourishment from deeper
down. Rabbi Yitzchok Dov Bamberger, the Wurzburger Rav, follows him.

### the word-switch at Korach
The Torah moves between the two words as the ground opens under Korach: Moshe
warns that the *adamah* will open its mouth, the *adamah* under them splits, and
then the *eretz* opens its mouth and swallows them, and the people flee lest the
*eretz* swallow them too (Bamidbar 16:30-34). Where the episode is recalled it
is the *eretz* that swallowed them (Bamidbar 26:10, Devarim 11:6). Rabbi Yaakov
Chaim Sofer, Rosh Yeshivah of Yeshivas Kaf HaChaim in Yerushalayim, works from
Rabbi Moravsky's distinction: the opening came in two stages, the surface layer first
and then the subterranean depths, and the switch of words records it.

## Rendering guidance

- *Eretz*: **land**, or **country** where a nation's territory is meant, or
  **earth** where the whole world is (*shamayim va'aretz*). *Eretz Yisrael* stays
  as it is.
- *Adamah*: **ground**, or **soil** where farming is in view. "Land" for
  *adamah* should be reserved for the idiom "the face of the earth."
- *Adam min ha'adamah*: keep the echo. Where a passage builds on man being taken
  from the *adamah*, "ground" preserves it and "earth" muffles it.
- Trap: English "earth" absorbs both words outright, and "land" absorbs both
  nearly as well. At Bamidbar 16:30-34 the entire question is why the pasuk
  switched, so the English must switch too: "ground" for the first stage,
  "land" for the second.
- Trap: do not carry one commentator's scale of breadth into another's passage.
  Malbim and Rabbi Moravsky are opposed to the Maharshal on which word reaches
  deeper.
