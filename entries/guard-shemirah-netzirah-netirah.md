# Guarding: shemirah / netzirah / netirah

## Bottom line

*Shemirah* is the general word: **guard**, **watch**, **keep**, and by extension
**wait for** something whose hour has not yet come; for the *Siddur HaRokeach*
and Rabbi Wertheimer it is bounded in time. *Netzirah* is, for the Vilna Gaon
and Wertheimer, the stronger watch, and for the Rokeach and Wertheimer the
perpetual one: **safeguard**, **watch over** (unceasingly). *Netirah*, for
Malbim, is holding something in the mind: **harbor** (a grudge), **hold on to**. *Lo sitor* (Vayikra 19:18) does not mean "do not guard" but "do
not keep it stored up inside."

## Distinctions

### shemirah (שמירה)
Root ש-מ-ר, one of the commonest roots in Tanach (over four hundred
occurrences). It covers watching, guarding, overseeing and looking after, and
also a man's watching himself, being careful not to do what is dangerous or
forbidden.

Malbim: *shemirah* typically attaches to a tangible thing that can be damaged,
stolen or lost. When silver or vessels are handed over for safekeeping the Torah
uses *shemirah* (Shemos 22:6), and the *shomer* of the *sugyos* is named from
that word; so too "*Hashem yishmarcha mikol ra, yishmor es nafshecha*"
(Tehillim 121:7).

A second, temporal sense: *shemirah* as holding on while waiting for the right
moment. "*Ve'aviv shamar es hadavar*" (Bereishis 37:11), where Yaakov dismissed
Yosef's dreams outwardly but kept them, waiting for the day they would come
true (see Mahari Kara to Koheles 5:7). The *Siddur HaRokeach* reads
"*im avonos tishmor Kah*" (Tehillim 130:3) the same way, as waiting until the
time for reckoning arrives. Eli watched Chanah's lips and *waited* (*shomer*)
for her to finish (Shmuel I 1:12), and Eliyahu *waited* (*shamar*) for Rabbi
Yose to finish his tefillah in the ruin (Berachos 3a).

The *Siddur HaRokeach* holds that *shemirah* is temporary guarding against a
particular threat until it passes, not an everlasting guardianship, and Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (*Biurei Shemos HaNirdafim*) likewise holds that it is
the more temporally limited watch.

### netzirah (נצירה)
Root נ-צ-ר, about sixty occurrences; the initial *nun* often drops. The Vilna
Gaon (to Mishlei 2:8) postulates that *netzirah* is a more substantial watching
than *shemirah*. On "*lintzor orchos mishpat vederech chasidav yishmor*" he
reads two types of upright Jew, the *talmid chacham* wholly given to Torah and
the *chassid* given to *mitzvos*; many devote themselves to *mitzvos* and only
a select elite to Torah study, and the *yetzer hara* presses hardest on
*talmidei chachamim* (Sukkah 52a), so Shlomo HaMelech gave the stronger word
*netzirah* to the wisdom that shields the scholar and reserved *shemirah* for
the *chassid*, who needs less protection from the *yetzer hara*.

Another approach: Rabbi Shimon Dov Ber Analak of Shidlitz (1848 to 1907)
theorizes that *shemirah* implies a passive form of watching over something,
*netzirah* an active form.

The *Siddur HaRokeach* (on "*chasdecha va'amitcha tamid yitzruni*", Tehillim
40:12): *netzirah* is watching over something forever, not guarding it from one
passing danger. Indeed *netzer*, a shoot, from the same root (Yeshayahu 11:1,
14:19, 60:21; Daniel 11:7), can be cultivated only under constant tending. Wertheimer,
without citing the Rokeach, says the same, and applies it to "*yishmeru
chukav vesorosav yintzoru*" (Tehillim 105:45): *mitzvos* are kept *ad hoc* as
each arises, so *shemirah*; Torah study is unceasing (Yehoshua 1:8), so
*netzirah*. Wertheimer also calls *netzirah* the best kind of watching, from
"*yitzrenhu ke'ishon eino*" (Devarim 32:10).

### netirah (נטירה)
Root נ-ט-ר, the rarest of the three: nine occurrences in the Hebrew portions of
Tanach and one in the Aramaic (Daniel 7:28); its *nun* also often drops. Malbim (to
Vayikra 19:18, Tehillim 103:9, Yirmiyahu 3:5, Shir HaShirim 1:6, and in *Sefer
HaCarmel*) presumes that *netirah* is not physical guarding at all but the
abstract holding on to a conception in the mind. Hence *lo sitor*, harboring a grievance without
watching anything tangible. Rashi's classic illustration of the pair: to answer
"I will not lend you my ax, as you did not lend me your sickle" is *nekamah*;
to say "here it is, I am not like you" is *netirah*, because the ill will is
still being kept inside.

The difficulty on Malbim is Shir HaShirim 1:6, where *notrah* and *natarti* are
used of watching vineyards, tangible things. Malbim answers that Shir HaShirim
is wholly allegory: those vineyards are a person's own goals and aims, tended
like a vineyard until the fruit ripens, and the "mother's sons" who divert him
are his own faculties, the imagination pulling him toward aims that are not his.
So the object of the *netirah* really is a conception.

On Wertheimer's approach, Rabbi Klein notes, the allegory is not needed: for him
*shemirah* is usually for something tangible, *netirah* can extend to something
abstract, and *netzirah* is the best watching of all.

### The Aramaic collapse
The Targumim render *shemirah*, *netzirah* and *netirah* indiscriminately with
נ-ט-ר forms (see Rabbi Eliyahu Bachur, *Meturgeman*), which might mean that
whatever nuances hold in Hebrew disappear entirely in Aramaic, the meanings
melding together; *neturei karta* is simply "the guardians of the city."
Rabbi Dr. Asher Weiser (*Mikra V'Lashon*) goes so far as to write that נ-צ-ר
and נ-ט-ר are almost used synonymously, especially since *tzadi* and *tes* are
often interchangeable, with only a slight nuance between them: *netirah* is
localized, specific guardianship, *netzirah* is watching in a general sense.

### The names
Several names look related to
*shemirah*: the places Shamir (Yehoshua 15:48; Shoftim 10:1 to 10:2), Shomron
(Samaria, over 110 times in Tanach), Shamrayin (its Aramaic name, Ezra 4:10,
4:17) and Shimron (Yehoshua 11:1, 12:20, 19:15); and the personal names Shemer,
Shomer, Shimron, Shamir, Shimras, Shimri, Shemiramos, Shmaryahu and Shmaryah
(the last two abbreviated in Yiddish as Shmerel), with the feminine Shimris. In
rabbinic tradition the name of Nevuchadnetzar's wife is related to *shemirah*,
though there is a dispute as to what it was: Shemirah, Shemirmos or Shemiram
(*Vayikra Rabbah* 19:6). From *netirah* comes the Geonic name Natronai; beyond
that Rabbi Klein knows of no Hebrew name from *netirah* or *netzirah*, but he
adds that the surname of Hasan Nasrallah of Hezbollah seems to derive from an
Arabic cognate of *netzirah*, parsing as *netzirah*, protection, plus *allah*, a
construction that parallels Shmaryahu and Shmaryah, *shemirah* plus a
theophoric Yah or Yahu.

## Rendering guidance

- Vayikra 19:18: *lo sitor* is "do not bear a grudge" or "do not harbor it," and
  the point Rashi is making is that outward compliance with the grudge intact is
  the *aveirah*. Never render it "do not guard."
- Trap: English "keep" covers all three, and "watch over" covers *shemirah* and
  *netzirah*. Where a pasuk pairs them, split them: Mishlei 2:8 as "to safeguard
  the ways of justice, and the way of His devoted ones it watches"; Tehillim
  105:45 as "that they keep His statutes and guard His teachings."
- Where *shemirah* means waiting (Bereishis 37:11, Tehillim 130:3, Shmuel I
  1:12), "kept the matter in mind" or "waited" is right and "guarded" is wrong.
  Yaakov is not protecting anything; he is holding the thing until its time.
- If a passage follows the Vilna Gaon or the Rokeach, *netzirah* should read as
  the heavier or the permanent watch, and the English should carry that
  ("watched over always," "never let go of"). If it follows Rabbi Analak, the
  contrast to draw is active against passive instead. Do not blend the two.
