# Trust: bitachon / machseh / tikvah / tocheles / chikui / semichah / mishan / saad / sever / kesel

## Bottom line

*Bitachon* is **trust**, inner confidence that the future will be good; for
Malbim it is a state of the heart and says nothing about action. *Machseh* is
**refuge**. For Malbim it is trust lived out; for the Vilna Gaon it is trust in
Hashem beyond what He promised, where *bitachon* is trust that He will do what
He did promise. *Tikvah* is **hope**, *tocheles* **expectant waiting** with some
doubt, and *chikui* **waiting** in full certainty; Rabbi Wertheimer grades the
three by how certain one is. *Semichah* is **leaning** and *mishan* **leaning
for support**, and Malbim and Rabbi Pappenheim divide them differently: for
Malbim *semichah* is done with one's whole weight and *mishan* only partly; for
Pappenheim *somech* covers any leaning, even by choice, and *mishan* is leaning
out of need. *Saad* is **support** given, for Malbim to one unaware of it and
for Pappenheim to one who needs *mishan*; or, as Rabbi Moshe Tzuriel suggests,
it is **help**, since the beneficiary also does his part. *Sever* is **hopeful
looking-to**, and *kesel* is **assurance** that nothing bad will happen
(Malbim). Chovos HaLevavos counts all ten as ten levels of trust in Hashem.

## Distinctions

### bitachon (בטחון) and machseh (מחסה)
- Malbim: *betach*/*bitachon* is an emotional state, confidence about a
  successful future and peace with oneself, specifically inner serenity in the
  face of turmoil; always positive, the opposite of harried insecurity; it says
  nothing of what one does about it. *Machseh* is one who actively lives by his
  *bitachon*.
- The Vilna Gaon (*Biur HaGra* to Mishlei 14:26) divides by the ground of the
  confidence: *bitachon* is trust that Hashem will do what He promised;
  *machseh* is trust in Him even where He promised nothing, and it is better to
  have *machseh* in Hashem than *bitachon* in a human patron's explicit word
  (Tehillim 118:9).
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: *machseh* is from ח-ס, as in *chas v'shalom*, caring
  enough to preserve from destruction; to take Hashem as *machseh* is to rely on
  His caring.

### tikvah (תקוה), tocheles (תוחלת), chikui (חכוי)
- Wertheimer: all three mean waiting, graded by certainty. *Tocheles* (verb
  *yachel*): one relies on it coming but not wholeheartedly; hence *yachel
  Yisrael* (Tehillim 130:7), for there is sometimes reason to doubt our
  salvation, since our sins might block what we await. *Tikvah*: one relies
  wholly on the fulfilment, though he cannot say it is certain. *Chikui*: one is
  sure it will happen and is only waiting.
- Pappenheim: *tikvah* is from ק-ו, *kav*, a straight line (the builder's cord;
  the rope of Yehoshua 2:18; the *mikveh*, the body of water gathered as waters
  travel in a straight line to one place, Bereishis 1:9 to 10, Shemos 7:19,
  Vayikra 11:36); hope sees a straight line from the current situation to the
  one anticipated.

### semichah (סמיכה), mishan (משען), saad (סעד)
*Somech* is "next to," hence leaning.
- Malbim, from Chagigah 16b (one leans on the korban with all his weight):
  *semichah* is leaning with all one's might, *mishan* only partly.
- Pappenheim: *mishan* is from ש-ע, enjoyment (with *sha'ashua*, [*yeshuah*](salvation-yeshuah-teshuah-hatzalah-purkan.html#%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%94),
  *shav'ah*): the satisfaction of being supported; a [*mishenes*](staff-mateh-shevet-makel.html#%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%A2%D7%A0%D7%AA) is the
  walking-stick, a great satisfaction to the elderly who struggle to walk.
  Pappenheim also divides *somech* from *mishan*: *somech* is any leaning or
  relying, even when done willingly and not forced (Moshe on Yehoshua, Devarim
  34:9; Yisrael on the Leviim, Bamidbar 8:10, where they would not have fallen
  had they not leaned), while *mishan* is leaning on another for actual support
  because one is too weak to support oneself.
- Malbim: *saad* is primarily holding up an inanimate thing so it does not fall,
  so also a patron's support of a beneficiary who does not know of it; *mishan*
  is a beneficiary who knowingly relies on another, usually Hashem (Michah
  3:11).
- Pappenheim: *saad* is the act of helping one who needs *mishan* (so *soed*,
  refreshing with food, Bereishis 18:5, Tehillim 104:15; *seudah*).
- Rabbi Moshe Tzuriel suggests *saad* is better translated "helps," for the
  beneficiary also contributes to his own betterment and does not rely wholly on
  outside help.

### sever (שבר) and kesel (כסל)
- Peirush HaRokeach: *sever* is spelled like *shever*, food; men look to Hashem
  in hope and He gives them their food (Tehillim 145:15).
- Wertheimer: *sever* is strong trust that grows from recognising Hashem as
  creator and sustainer of all.
- Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 145:15): *sover* is a mental process that computes
  separate pieces of information and joins them into one complete, logical
  thought, so that one who puts his hope in Hashem comes to the intellectual
  conclusion that He is the holistic One who powers all parts of creation.

*Kesel*, confidence or reliance, seems to be a cognate of *kesil*, fool, both
from כ-ס-ל.

- Malbim: in *tikvah* one awaits a good; in *kesel* one is sure, with almost
  foolish naivety, that no harm will come.
- Rabbi Moshe Wolfson accounts for the connection differently: to rely on Hashem
  wholly one must be willing to appear illogical or foolish; though the fool in
  general walks in the dark (Koheles 2:14), one considered a fool for following
  Hashem walks in the light, and so Kislev, when the light of Chanukah
  illuminates the dark winter, is especially the month to work on *kesel*.

## Rendering guidance

- בטחון: "trust" (in a mussar text keep *bitachon* transliterated once the
  term is established). בטח as adverb: "securely," "in safety."
- מחסה: "refuge," "shelter"; לחסות בה׳: "to take refuge in Hashem." Do not
  write "trust" for it beside בטח (Tehillim 118:9 sets them against each
  other).
- תקוה: "hope"; תוחלת: "expectation," "waiting hope"; חכוי / מחכה: "awaiting,"
  "waiting for."
- סמיכה / סומך: "leaning," "leans on"; משען / נשען: "support," "relies on";
  סעד / סועד: "support," "sustains," "helps" (Tzuriel).
- שבר / משבר: "look to in hope"; כסל: "assurance," "confidence" (Mishlei 3:26).
- Trap: English "trust" (and "rely") absorbs at least six of these. When a text
  lists them (the ten of Chovos HaLevavos; a pasuk with two, like Mishlei 14:26
  or Tehillim 118:9), give each its own English and keep the author's order; where the
  order is a ladder (Wertheimer's *tocheles* < *tikvah* < *chikui*), make it
  climb in English too.
