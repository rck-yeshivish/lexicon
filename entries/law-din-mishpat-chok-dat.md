# Law: din / mishpat / chok / dat / nimus

## Bottom line

*Chok* is **statute** (or **decree**): a law whose reason is not revealed, set
and unchanging (Yoma 67b, Rambam). *Mishpat* is **ordinance** or **judgment**:
the rational, mostly interpersonal law, and the verdict a judge reaches by
weighing two sides (Rambam, Rabbi Moshe Shapiro). *Din* is **judgment** or
**law** in the sense of the case and its penalty: the dispute itself (Bedersi),
the penal outcome (Wertheimer). *Dat* is **law, edict, royal decree**; in
Megillas Esther the king's law, in Devarim 33:2 Divine law. Never "religion."
*Nimus* (Rabbinic, from Greek *nomos*) is **law, rule** as society sets it. Do
not let "law" swallow a pair: חוקים ומשפטים is "statutes and ordinances."

## Distinctions

### chok (חק)
Root ח-ק-ק, "engrave." Yoma 67b on Vayikra 18:4-5: *chukim* are the mitzvos the
*yetzer hara* mocks as pointless (pork, *shaatnez*, *chalitzah*, the *metzora*,
the *sa'ir la'azazel*); the answer is "I am Hashem, I decreed them (*chakaktiv*)."
Rambam (*Shemonah Perakim* 6, *Moreh* 3:26), Rabbeinu Bachaya, Ibn Yachya, and
Radak (to Tehillim 119:1): a mitzvah whose reason is hidden. *Peirush
HaRokeach*: *issur v'heter* versus *dinim*. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: *chok* is
borrowed for any quota set from above (the brick tally of Shemos 5:14), as
arbitrary as an unexplained law; being engraved, it is unchanging, hence
"*chok olam*" (Shemos 12:14). Rabbi Eliyahu Menachem Margules (*Emunas
Eliezer*): obedience without a reason is unconditional and so "engraved" in
the person (cf. Avos 5:16). Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 119:5): kin to *chag*, a
circle of mitzvos around us. Rabbi Yisrael Salanter: rungs to the *mishpatim*.

### mishpat (משפט)
Root ש-פ-ט, "judge." Yoma 67b: the laws that would have been written had the
Torah not commanded them (idolatry, arayos, murder, theft, blasphemy). Rabbi
Moshe Shapiro: not "a law with a reason" by definition but a decision between
two viable options, as a *shofet* decides between two legitimate litigants
(*Tikkunei Zohar*, *Patach Eliyahu*: the Middle Pillar). Bedersi (*Chotam
Tochnit*): against *din*, *mishpat* is the verdict rendered that settles the
dispute. Pappenheim: what ethics demands; a worker's wage is a *mishpat*.
Wertheimer: the judiciary's discretion among legal outcomes. Onkelos renders
*mishpat* as *din* (Shemos 21:1) and as *halachah* (21:9).

### din (דין)
Root ד-י-ן. Pappenheim: DALET-NUN is judicial decision and its execution;
hence *dan*, *dayan*, *madon*, *adon* (one with jurisdiction over another),
*medinah* (the area under one court). Bedersi: the controversy between two
litigants, so a litigant is a *baal din* (Avos 4:22) and sentencing is *gmar
din*. Wertheimer: the penal system. Maharal: law the intellect presumes true;
or, civil law between man and his fellow. Rabbi Yosef Nechemias (to Esther
1:13): the letter of the law. Twice in Chumash (Devarim 17:8).

### dat (דת)
Pappenheim: the biliteral DALET-TAV, practice by force of custom, of a person,
a family, or a nation; tipping the bearer of good news is a *dat*. Twenty
times in Esther, once in Ezra 8:36, and in the *krei* of אשדת (Devarim 33:2,
so Menachem Ibn Saruk; Ibn Janach and Radak do not list it). Ibn Ezra (to
Esther 1:3): the law of the land, where *din* is the verdict on the offender.
Maharal: the established norm of a polity; or, law between man and himself
(ethics, religion). Rabbi Yosef Zecharia Stern: pragmatic governance, where
*din* is justice set in law. Wertheimer: the rule of law itself, revealed or
societal. Rabbi Tzadok: *din* is law by reason, *dat* the ruler's prerogative
to legislate by fiat; Haman's charge (Esther 3:8) was that the Jews keep the
king's *din* but not his *dat*; for Torah, *dat* and *din* are Written and
Oral Torah together. Rav Hirsch (to Devarim 33:2): primarily Divinely given
law, borrowed in Esther for royal law (kin to *dut*, the accessible cistern).
On אשדת, Ramban, Onkelos, Rashi, and Abarbanel keep the two-word *krei*;
Shadal's one-word *eshed* Ibn Janach had already rejected. Bedersi, rejecting
Ibn Janach's "covenant," treats *dat* as effectively *din*.

### nimus (נימוס)
Rabbinic, from Greek *nomos*. Gittin 6:5: "do for her as the *nimus*" is too
vague to authorize a *get*. Rabbi Eliyahu Bachur (*Tishbi*): Targum uses it
for *chok* (Vayikra 18:3), *mishpat* (Shmuel I 2:13), and *torah* (Tehillim
1:2). Rabbi Nissim of Marseilles: *nimus* is society's rules, *dat* Hashem's.

## Rendering guidance

- חוקים ומשפטים: "statutes and ordinances" (or "decrees and laws"). Keep the
  order and keep two words; the pair is the Yoma 67b contrast.
- דת ודין (Esther 1:13): "law and judgment." Rabbi Yosef Nechemias reads the
  order as deliberate (practical *dat* before theoretical *din*); do not reorder.
- Standalone דת in Esther: "edict," "decree," or "law." Never "religion"; the
  modern *dati* sense is not in the pasuk. Devarim 33:2: "a fiery law."
  Standalone דין in mussar: "judgment" (*middas hadin*) or "the law" (its
  strict letter); בעל דין "litigant," גמר דין "sentence."
- Trap: "law" covers all five, and "judgment" covers both *din* and *mishpat*.
  Where a passage sets two against each other, give each its own English.
  "Statute" is *chok* only; *dat* is "edict" (issued), not "engraved."
  Pappenheim's test case: wage = *mishpat*, tax = *chok*, gratuity = *dat*.
