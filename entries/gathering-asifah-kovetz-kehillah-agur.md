# Gathering: asifah / kovetz / aggudah / kehillah / eidah / agur

## Bottom line

*Asifah* is **gathering in**, bringing scattered things to where they belong;
*kovetz* is **collecting**, with no bringing-in implied. *Aggudah* goes further:
the gathered are **bound** into one unit. *Kehillah* is an **assembly** of
people, *eidah* a **congregation** joined for a purpose, *agur* is
**stockpiling** for later. English "gather" absorbs the first two, so where they
stand in consecutive pesukim the difference must show in the English.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| אסיפה | asifah | gathering in | to its proper place, and indoors (Rashi, Malbim) |
| קבץ | kovetz | collecting | no bringing in (Malbim); from a distance (Radak); bit by bit (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| אגודה | aggudah | bundle, binding | fastened into one unit (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| קהילה | kehillah | assembly | people only (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| עדה | eidah | congregation | joined for a purpose (Bedersi); the righteous (Vilna Gaon) |
| אגר | agur | stockpiling | stored away for later; inanimate (Bedersi) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Asifah against kovetz.** Four accounts, and they do not agree.

1. **Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel, with Rashi and Malbim.** *Asifah* gathers
   disparate things to put them where they belong, which is why Succos is *Chag
   HaAsif* (Shemos 23:16, 34:22), the last chance to bring produce in before the
   rains (Rashi); of an individual it means only arriving (Malbim, Bamidbar
   11:30). So *le'esof* brings in and *lekavetz* does not: Achashverosh gathered
   the maidens with *kovetz* (Esther 2:3), from afar but only one came inside.
2. **The Vilna Gaon** (Chavakuk 2:5): *asifah* brings things inside, *kovetz*
   puts what is inside in one place; Yaakov calls his sons twice, *he'asfu*
   (Bereishis 49:1) for those outside, then *hikavtzu* (49:2).
3. **Radak** (*Sefer HaShorashim*) rejects the "proper place" idea: the
   difference is distance, *asifah* for what is near, *kovetz* for what is
   dispersed, which is the reverse of Malbim's reading of Esther 2:3.
4. **Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim.** *Asifah* takes in all the parts at once,
   *kovetz* accumulates them little by little; he derives it from ס-ף, a
   threshold, the group's moving boundary as more are added.

The Bottom line follows the first two, which agree that *asifah* brings in and
between them cover both worked examples.

**Whether asifah of death means arrival or reunion.** Rashi (Bereishis 49:29;
see 49:33, Bamidbar 27:13): the soul is gathered into the store where souls are
kept. Radak: he is gathered to his fathers, a reunion.

**Kehillah against eidah.** Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini: *kehillah* is any
gathering of people, *eidah* a body joined for a specific cause. The Vilna Gaon
(Mishlei 5:14): *eidah* is a gathering of the righteous. The Bottom line follows
Bedersi, fitting the ordinary usage *adas Yisrael*.

## Distinctions

### aggudah (אגודה)
More than assembly: the gathered are tied into one, physically (three of the four
species bound for the lulav, Succah 11b, 13a) or by an agreement binding people's
fates. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to ג-ד, attaching, whence *gid* (sinew)
and *gedud* (a squad joined as one).

### kehillah (קהילה)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: used of human beings only, never of animals or objects,
and kin to *kol*, sound, since people gather at the sound that summons them (the
trumpets of Bamidbar 10:2). Shlomo is *Koheles* because his teaching was given in
gatherings (Melachim I 8:1), or because he gathered wisdom (Rashi, Koheles 1:1).

### agur (אגר)
Shlomo's other name, Agur (Mishlei 30:1), for having gathered up the Torah's
wisdom; *Midrash Agur* 4 reads both names as a sign that the nation was gathered
as one in his days. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim:
collecting little by little for later use. Rabbi Avraham Bedersi: inanimate
objects only, wine (Devarim 28:39) or silver (Shmuel I 2:36), while *asifah*,
*kovetz* and *kehillah* also take living things. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's root
is ג-ר, temporary lodging, whence *ger*, *goren*, and the coin *geirah*.

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 49:1-2 is the test case; "gather yourselves" twice destroys it.
  Render *he'asfu* "come in together" and *hikavtzu* "draw close together."
- *Chag HaAsif*: "the Festival of the Ingathering," not "of the Gathering."
  *Asifah* of death: "he was gathered to his people," passive.
- *Aggudah*: "bundle" for the lulav, "band" for people, never "gathering."
- Trap: "assembly" absorbs *kehillah*, *eidah* and *asifah* alike. Keep
  "assembly" for *kehillah*, "congregation" for *eidah*, and give *asifah* a
  verb rather than a noun wherever the English allows.
