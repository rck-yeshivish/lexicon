# Gathering: asifah / kovetz / aggudah / kehillah / eidah / agur

## Bottom line

On the reading of Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel, Rashi and Malbim, *asifah* is
**gathering in**, bringing scattered things to where they belong, and *kovetz*
is **collecting**, with no bringing-in implied. *Aggudah* goes further: the
gathered are **bound** into one unit. *Kehillah* is an **assembly** of people;
*eidah*, for Bedersi, a **congregation** joined for a purpose; and *agur*,
**stockpiling** for later.

| אגר | agur | stockpiling | stored away for later; inanimate (Bedersi) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Asifah against kovetz.** Five accounts, not all in agreement.

1. **Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel, with Rashi and Malbim.** *Asifah* gathers
   disparate things to put them where they belong, which is why Succos is *Chag
   HaAsif* (Shemos 23:16, 34:22), the last chance to bring produce in before the
   rains (Rashi); of an individual it means only arriving (Malbim, Bamidbar
   11:30). So *le'esof* brings in and *lekavetz* does not necessarily:
   Achashverosh gathered the maidens with *kovetz* (Esther 2:3), from afar but
   only one came inside.
2. **The Vilna Gaon** (Chavakuk 2:5): *asifah* brings things inside, *kovetz*
   puts what is inside in one place; Yaakov calls his sons twice, *he'asfu*
   (Bereishis 49:1) for those outside, then *hikavtzu* (49:2).
3. **Radak** (*Sefer HaShorashim*) seems to reject the "proper place" idea,
   disagreeing with Rashi even on death, which he explains as being gathered
   together with one's forefathers. On his account the difference is distance: *asifah* for
   what is near, *kovetz* for what is dispersed further off.
4. **Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim.** *Asifah* takes in all the parts at once,
   *kovetz* accumulates them little by little; he derives it from ס-ף, a
   threshold, the group's moving boundary as more are added.
5. **The Rokeach** (Bereishis p. 60), much as Pappenheim: *kibutz* is gathering
   little by little from here and there, *asifah* gathering much together.
   Beside them, *mikveh* is what gathers of itself into one place, *tzvirah*
   heaping up high, *kenisah* gathering into one place until it is needed, and
   *agirah* a gathering that needs searching and care.

The first two agree that *asifah* brings in.

**Whether asifah of death means arrival or reunion.**

- Rashi (Bereishis 49:29; see 49:33, Bamidbar 27:13): the soul is gathered into
  the store where souls are kept.
- Radak: he is gathered to his fathers, a reunion.

**Kehillah against eidah.**

- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini: *kehillah* is any gathering of people,
  *eidah* a body joined for a specific cause.
- Along the same lines, the Vilna Gaon (Mishlei 5:14): *eidah* is a gathering
  of the righteous.
- Rabbi Zev Leff: *asifah* is a physical gathering of many bodies, and an *edah*
  a gathering united by one idea, all testifying to one thing or aiming at one
  goal, *ya'ad*, tied to *edus*.

## Distinctions

### aggudah (אגודה)
More than assembly: the gathered are tied into one, physically (three of the four
species bound for the lulav, Succah 11b, 13a) or by an agreement binding people's
fates. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to ג-ד, attaching, whence *gad*, a
spiritual force attached to a particular physical entity; *gid*, the sinew that
holds the parts of the body together; *gedud*, a squad joined as one; and
*haggadah*, presenting new information by weaving it into a broader narrative.

### kehillah (קהילה)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: used of human beings only, never of animals or objects,
and kin to *kol*, sound, since people heed the sound that tells them to band
together (the trumpets of Bamidbar 10:2); alternatively, because a gathering of
many people is always noisy. He derives *kol* itself from ק-ל, light as against
heavy, either because sound is light enough to travel in air and not sink, or
because it moves fast, unweighed down. Shlomo is *Koheles* because his teaching
was given in gatherings (Melachim I 8:1), or because he gathered up many forms
of wisdom (Rashi, Koheles 1:1).

### agur (אגר)
Shlomo's other name, Agur (Mishlei 30:1), for having gathered up the Torah's
wisdom; *Midrash Agur* 4 reads both names as a sign that the nation was gathered
as one in his days.

- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: gathering little by little for long-term
  storage; similarly Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: collecting or stockpiling for
  later use.
- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi: inanimate objects only, wine (Devarim 28:39) or silver
  (Shmuel I 2:36), while *asifah*, *kovetz* and *kehillah* also take living
  things.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's root is ג-ר, temporary residence, whence *ger*, a
  sojourner, and *goren*, the granary that houses the grain until its processing
  is done; and, in the gathering sense, *gargir*, a grape with the juice
  gathered inside it, and the small coin *geirah* (Shemos 30:13), also called
  *agurah* (Shmuel I 2:36), which is worth almost nothing singly and has value
  only collected together. (The Aramaic *agra*, wages or reward, as in Avos 5:22
  and Targum Onkelos to Bereishis 15:1, may derive from *agurah*; and a hundred
  Modern Hebrew *agurot* make a shekel.)

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 49:1-2 is the test case; "gather yourselves" twice destroys it.
  Render *he'asfu* "come in together" and *hikavtzu* "draw close together."
- *Chag HaAsif*: "the Festival of the Ingathering," not "of the Gathering."
  *Asifah* of death: "he was gathered to his people," passive.
- *Aggudah*: "bundle" for the lulav, "band" for people, never "gathering."
- Trap: "assembly" absorbs *kehillah*, *eidah* and *asifah* alike. Keep
  "assembly" for *kehillah*, "congregation" for *eidah*, and give *asifah* a
  verb rather than a noun wherever the English allows.
