# Bowing: kidah / kriah / hishtachavaah / segidah

## Bottom line

The Gemara (Megillah 22b, Berachos 34b) teaches that the three are three kinds of bowing: *kidah* is bowing the face, *kriah* is kneeling, and
*hishtachavaah* is full prostration with hands and feet stretched out. Maharsha
explains that each bows the head and some add more: *kidah* with the body otherwise
erect, *kriah* on the knees, *hishtachavaah* stretched out on the floor. *Segidah* is
Aramaic for *hishtachavaah* according to Radak; Malbim holds that the act may be
the same but the purpose differs.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Is *segidah* just the Aramaic for *hishtachavaah*?**
- Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): *segidah* is Aramaic for *hishtachavaah*. The
  Targum generally renders *hishtachavaah* as *sagid* (Bereishis 18:2, 23:7, 23:12,
  24:26 and more).
- Against that, Yeshayahu 44:17 uses *hishtachavaah* and *segidah* side by side,
  which suggests, in Rabbi Klein's view, that they are not synonyms from two
  languages.
- Malbim: the physical act of the two may be the same, and the difference lies
  in the motive. *Hishtachavaah* is bowing as a means of giving honor to that
  toward which one bows. *Segidah* is bowing done as a religious rite said to bring special benefits from Above, rather than to give honor as such.

## Distinctions

The Gemara (Megillah 22b, Berachos 34b) says the three words denote three
different kinds of bowing. Maharsha (Rabbi Shmuel Eidels, 1555 to 1631) explains
that all three involve bowing the head, and some involve more than that.

### kidah (קידה)
The Gemara learns that *kidah* is bowing the face from "and Bas Sheva bowed
(*vatikod*) her face to the ground" (Melachim I 1:31). Tosafos point out that
"face" appears with *hishtachavaah* as well (Yeshayahu 49:23), and conclude that
the Gemara's definition of *kidah* as bowing the face rests on a tradition, not
on derivation from the verse.

Maharsha: in *kidah* only the head bows, to the ground, while one otherwise
stands erect, without bending the knees. The Gemara (Sukkah 53a) relates that few
could do this, but Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel would perform *kidah* in the Beis
HaMikdash courtyard, supporting himself on his two thumbs, his face to the
ground, kissing the floor, and standing straight again; and that Levi once
demonstrated *kidah* before Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi and was left paralyzed.

- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740 to 1814) and Malbim (1809 to 1879)
  derive *kidah* from *kadkod*, the skull, since one tilts the top of the head
  downward.
- Linguists relate *kidah* to the Akkadian *qadadu*, to incline.

### kriah (כריעה)
Kneeling, as Shlomo HaMelech is described "kneeling (*kore'a*) on his knees"
(Melachim I 8:54). The word *kera* refers to the leg or knee of living creatures
and of objects such as tables and chairs. Maharsha: *kriah* is a bit easier than
*kidah*: one bends the knees to the floor and then bows the face to the ground.

### hishtachavaah (השתחוויה)
Prostrating oneself on the floor with hands and feet stretched out. Maharsha:
one goes down on the knees, bows the head to the ground, and then stretches out
hands and feet on the floor. It is the word for Avraham's bowing before the
children of Ches (Bereishis 23:7, 23:12) and for his servant's bowing in thanks to
Hashem (Bereishis 24:26, 24:48, 24:52); at 24:26 the Torah adds *kidah* to it.

Rav Hirsch (1808 to 1888) explains the symbolism of *kidah* against
*hishtachavaah*; he does not discuss *kriah*. Both are signs of submission, but
they express it differently. *Kidah* moves the head, the seat of the intellect,
and so denotes submitting one's intellect or will to the inclinations of another.
*Hishtachavaah* prostrates the whole body, and so symbolizes dedicating every
faculty of one's physical existence to a cause. As one is willing to bend his
posture for another, so is he willing to break his will and submit to another's
wishes.

### segidah (סגידה)
*Sagad* or *segidah* appears only four times in Tanach, all in Yeshayahu 44 to
46. On whether it differs from *hishtachavaah*, see above. The Modern Hebrew
*misgad*, a mosque, is related to it; its more direct ancestor is the Arabic
*masjid*, of which English "mosque" is a corruption.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "bow" absorbs all four. Where *kidah* and *hishtachavaah* stand
  together, as in *vayikod ha'ish vayishtachu* (Bereishis 24:26), "bowed and
  prostrated himself" keeps the Gemara's two distinct acts; "bowed and bowed
  down" loses them.
- "Worship" for *hishtachavaah* hides the physical act the Gemara defines and
  Malbim's point that it gives honor. In Yeshayahu 44:17, where *segidah* and
  *hishtachavaah* appear together, Malbim's reading needs two different words.
