# Washing: kevisah / rechitzah / shetifah / hadachah / merikah (kinuach)

## Bottom line

*Kevisah* is **laundering**: objects only, never a body, with the water going
right through the cloth. *Rechitzah* is **washing** a body or a part of one, by
immersion or by pouring. *Shetifah* is **sluicing**, cleaning under a strong
flow of much water. *Hadachah* is **rinsing**, the same surface work with little
water. *Merikah* is not water-cleaning at all by Rabbi Avraham Bedersi but
**scouring**, buffing off rust or tarnish. *Kinuach*, Rabbinic only, is
**wiping**. The trap: English "wash" absorbs the first four at once, and Vayikra
6:21 and Yeshayahu 4:4 each put two of them in a single verse.

## Distinctions

### kevisah (כביסה)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: *kevisah* is never used of the body, only of objects,
above all clothing. It can be a whole garment immersed (Shemos 19:10, Vayikra
11:25, 13:6) or one part of one cleaned (Vayikra 6:20), and it works either by
bringing the garment to standing water or by pouring water on it. In his second
formulation the contrast with *rechitzah* is depth: *rechitzah* rinses a
surface, *kevisah* drives the water through and through, as it must with cloth.
Rabbi Aharon Marcus ties כ-ב-ס to *keves*, a young sheep, for the washing of its
wool; *Pesikta Rabbasi* darshens *keves* from *kevisah*, the korban washing
away sin.

On Shemos 19:10, *v'chibsu*: *Yevamos* 46b first tries to prove from it that
Klal Yisrael immersed at Sinai, then rejects the proof, since the washing may
have been for cleanliness alone. Rabbi Shlomo HaKohen of Vilna finds the same in
Targum Onkelos, who renders *v'chibsu* as *v'yichavrun*, "they shall whiten,"
not his usual rendering for laundering that purifies. The Netziv adds that the
pasuk says *simlah*, not *beged*; tumah would not stop at the outer layer, so
this was laundering in honor of the day.

### rechitzah (רחיצה)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the body, whole (immersion in a mikveh, Vayikra 15:13,
Bamidbar 19:7) or in part (feet, Bereishis 18:4; face, Bereishis 43:31), and by
either method, entering existing water (Shemos 2:5) or having water poured on
one (Shemos 30:21). Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer allows it both outside (the
kiyor poured over hands and feet, Shemos 30:19) and inside (water put into the
innards of a korban to clean them), where *shetifah* is exterior only.

### shetifah (שטיפה)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the root ש-ט-פ means flow or stream; *shetifah* is
holding a body or an object under running water and shaking it so the current
does the cleaning. Unlike the other two it always brings the item to the water,
never the water to the item, and it covers both bodies (Vayikra 15:11) and
objects (Vayikra 6:21, 15:12). Wertheimer: as an active verb it cleans the
exterior only, but in the passive it implies inside and out both, so much water
being involved; and it means more water than *rechitzah*, being the word of
*mayim rabim* (Tehillim 32:7). Radak (to Melachim I 22:38) likewise takes it as
the stronger washing. Rabbi Avraham Bedersi: *shetifah* runs an already clean
item under a strong current to make it look cleaner still.

### hadachah (הדחה)
Menachem Ibn Saruk and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, both working from two-letter
roots, take it from ד-ח, to push away: *hadachah* washes off whatever water can
carry away. Wertheimer: *hadachah* and *shetifah* both work the exterior, but
*hadachah* uses a minimum of water. His proof is Yeshayahu 4:4, *rechitzah* for
the filth of the daughters of Tzion and *hadachah* for the blood of
Yerushalayim, blood coming away with little water and filth needing much.
Bedersi: *rechitzah* is scrubbing off dirt in earnest, *hadachah* a plain rinse.
*Sefer HaShem* (ascribed to Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms) makes the difference
the substance removed, from Divrei HaYamim II 4:6, Mishlei 30:12 and Yeshayahu
4:4: *hadachah* for blood, *rechitzah* for filth.

### merikah (מריקה) and tamruk (תמרוק)
Vayikra 6:21 requires a metal vessel used for a chatas to undergo *merikah* and
*shetifah* in water. Rashi (to *Zevachim* 96b): *merikah* is the inside of the
vessel, *shetifah* the outside. Rambam (commentary to *Zevachim* 11:7) makes it
a matter of degree instead: *merikah* purges clinging particles, *shetifah* is
filling it with water and scrubbing further. Bedersi separates *merikah* from
the whole group: it is not water-cleaning but buffing and polishing, softening a
surface and taking off rust, which is how *tamruk* in Esther 2:3 and 2:12 is a
cosmetic regimen that smooths the skin. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives both
from ר-ק, emptying a thing of its blemishes. Menachem Ibn Saruk files *merikah*
and *tamruk* under מ-ר-ק in separate categories, implying no link; Rashi (to
Vayikra 6:21) does link them. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 21:25 and Vayikra 6:21)
compares *merikah* to *morag*, a threshing sledge, making it a deep cleaning
that goes below the surface.

### kinuach (קינוח)
Rabbinic only: *Berachos* 8:3, *Shabbos* 21:2-3, *Bava Basra* 5:10, *Keilim*
28:2, *Parah* 3:9. Cleaning by wiping off what soils. Rashi (to Yeshayahu 47:11)
takes the root as finality: what is wiped away is gone. Hence *kinuach seudah*,
the wiping of the meal, for dessert.

## Rendering guidance

- Vayikra 6:21, ומרק ושטף במים: "it shall be scoured and sluiced with water."
  Two words for two acts. Rashi has them as inside and outside, Rambam as two
  grades of thoroughness, so avoid an English pair that picks one reading.
- Yeshayahu 4:4: "when Hashem will have washed away the filth of the daughters
  of Tzion and rinsed out the blood of Yerushalayim." Keep "washed" for
  *rechitzah* and "rinsed" for *hadachah*; the distribution is Wertheimer's
  whole argument.
- כביסה: "launder" or "wash [clothes]," never "bathe." רחיצה of a person:
  "wash" or "bathe." The rule is object against body; an English "wash" for both
  loses it.
- Trap: "rinse" will take both *shetifah* and *hadachah*. Reserve "rinse" for
  *hadachah* and use "sluice," "flush" or "wash under running water" for
  *shetifah*, which carries *mayim rabim* with it.
- תמרוק in Esther: "beauty treatment" or "cosmetic regimen," not "purification";
  Bedersi and Rashi's tie to *merikah* make it a smoothing of the skin.
- קינוח: "wipe." Never for water-washing; the root's point is that what is
  wiped is finished with.
