# Son: ben / bar

## Bottom line

Both mean **son**, and the difference is language, not sense: *ben* is Hebrew,
*bar* is its Aramaic equivalent, which is why Targum renders *ben* as *bar*
everywhere. Render both "son of," and keep whichever form the text itself uses
in a name. What must not be lost is that *bar* in Aramaic also means **outside**
or **separate**, which is why halachah prefers *ben* in a get and why the
darshanim read a lesson into *bar mitzvah*.

## Distinctions

### ben (בן)
The Hebrew word, and the word of Tanach. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to
the two-letter root ב-נ, building and producing: a child is what a marriage
produces. From the same root he brings *binyan* (building), the building
materials *even* (stone) and *teven* (straw), *avnayim* (a birthing stool, which
helps the producing along), and *binah*/*tevunah* (understanding), which are the
products of thought. He holds that *bas* (daughter) is also from ב-נ and should
properly be *banas*, the nun dropping out in the singular and returning in the
plural *banos*.

### bar (בר)
The Aramaic word, at home in Aramaic texts. Wherever *ben* appears in Tanach the
Targumim render it *bar*. The one apparent exception is Mishlei 31:2, a Hebrew
pasuk that uses *bar*, and Malbim there argues that the word is Hebrew and means
"choicest," not the Aramaic for son.

*Bar* in Aramaic also means outside or separate, in constant use in the Gemara;
a *baraisa* is a Tannaic teaching from outside the Mishnah. Rabbi Pappenheim
traces this second sense to the two-letter root ב-ר, exclusion or separation:
*bar* is grain separated from its chaff, *bor*/*boris* is a cleaning agent that
separates off filth, and *bari* (against *shema*) is the certainty in which all
other options have been excluded; to which one may add the common *bar minan*,
"apart from us." On this reading *bar* means son because the child was separated
from his mother's person at birth.

That double meaning has a halachic consequence. Rabbi Yaakov Margolis of
Regensburg (*Seder HaGet*) rules that in a get one writes "so-and-so *ben*
so-and-so" and not *bar*, even though *bar* is standard in other shtaros,
because a get must be as clear as possible and *bar* carries more than one
meaning. The Maharshal upholds the ruling for a different reason: nobody would
actually read "outside" there, but *bar* could be taken as an abbreviation for
*ben reb*, and honorifics are kept out of a get. The Maharik disputes the
ruling; the Rema codifies it twice (*Even HaEzer* 126:30 and 129:7).

### bat / bas (בת) and the bar mitzvah
The Aramaic for daughter is *barat*, but the Targumim tend to leave the Hebrew
*bas* untranslated (Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur, *Meturgaman*), so *bas* serves in
Aramaic too; hence *bas mitzvah* even in an Aramaic phrase. Why the boy is a
*bar mitzvah* rather than a *ben mitzvah* has three answers on record. Rabbi
David Ovadiah of Tiberias, in the name of Rabbi Meir Simcha of Dvinsk: because
*bar* also means outside, teaching the boy that he now stands on the threshold
of adult life, able to choose which path he takes. Rabbi Alexander Sender
Feuerstein: the Aramaic word says he must master not only the Written Torah,
mostly Hebrew, but the Oral Torah, mostly Aramaic. Rabbi Meir Mintzberg: *ben*
connotes the son as the product of his parents' building, and *bar* the
opposite, the son as separate and independent of them, which is exactly what the
day marks, that his growth is now his own responsibility.

## Rendering guidance

- Render both "son of," and do not translate the connector in a name. "Rabbi
  Shimon bar Yochai" stays *bar*; "Yehoshua bin Nun" stays *bin*. Do not
  normalize one to the other, and note that Rashbi appears in both forms.
- Trap: an English "son of" absorbs *ben* and *bar* alike, so any source
  discussing why a get says *ben*, or why the boy is a *bar mitzvah*, cannot be
  translated word for word; keep the Hebrew and Aramaic words themselves in the
  sentence and explain around them.
- *Bar* in its other sense: "outside," "external," and in a Talmudic context
  often best left as *baraisa*, *bar minan*, *milsa d'bar*.
- *Ben* in the construct before a noun works as an adjective in Hebrew (a
  *ben mavess*, a *ben shanah*); render by sense, not by "son of." See
  *HaKsav VeHaKabbalah* to Shemos 12:43.
