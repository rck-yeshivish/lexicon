# Phylacteries: totafos / tefillin

## Bottom line

*Totafos* is the word of Chumash and belongs to the head alone. *Tefillin* is the Mishnaic word, plural because it
names the pair of head and arm together; the singular of one box is *tefillah*.
The Torah never calls the arm box *totafos*, which is one
of the two reasons Rabbi Pinchas Avraham Meyers and Rabbi Moshe Rachamim Shayo
give for the berachah before laying them saying *tefillin* and not the biblical
term.

## Distinctions

### totafos (טוטפות)
Three times in Chumash (Shemos 13:16, Devarim 6:8, 11:18), each time of the head
alone.

- Targum Onkelos and Targum pseudo-Yonasan render it *tefillin*.
- A second early tradition among the ancient translations, that of Targum
  Neofiti and the Peshitta, renders it instead by the Aramaic or Syriac
  equivalent of *zikaron*, "remembrance," which implies they took the core
  meaning to be remembering; Ibn Ezra (to Shemos 13:16) echoes that approach. On
  that reading it is unclear what the etymological root of *totafos* might be,
  or even what language it comes from.

Beyond that, several lines of explanation:

**An ornament worn on the forehead.** Tosafos (Menachos 34b) look for the word's
cognates in Mishnaic Hebrew and find *totefes*, an article of women's jewelry
(Shabbos 6:1, see Shabbos 57b), and the Aramaicized *totafta*, a bracelet
(Targum to Shmuel II 1:10, and see Yechezkel 24:17). Ibn Janach and Radak (each
in *Sefer HaShorashim*) take the same line and assign the word a root of its
own, ט-ט-ף, which occurs nowhere in Tanach but here; so too Rashbam (to Shemos
13:9) and possibly the Zohar (Bo 43a). Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785-1865)
develops it: the tefillin are an ornament, a Jew's way of announcing to the world
that he is proud to stand in Hashem's service. This also explains why the Gemara
(Megillah 16b) sees *yakar* (Esther 8:16), "precious, honorable," as a reference
to tefillin.

**Something that has to do with seeing.** Tosafos' second suggestion, adopted by
Chizkuni and Rabbi Yosef Bechor Shor (to Shemos 13:16), ties
*totafos* to gazing, the head
tefillin sitting by the eyes and looking outward. The Gemara (Berachos 6a) reads
"the nations of the world will see that the name of Hashem is called upon you"
(Devarim 28:10) as the gentiles seeing a Jew in his tefillin, and in light of
this, Rabbi Klein reasons, it makes sense that the biblical word for them might
be related to seeing.

**Dripping.**
- Menachem Ibn Saruk (920-970; *Machberes Menachem*, cited by Rashi to Shemos
  13:16) takes the word from the biliteral ט-ף, which yields four categories:
  *taf* (children), *notef*/*tipah* (dripping), *netifos* (adornments and
  niceties), and *matif* (speech, preaching). He explicitly classes *totafos*
  with the last of these, but offers no further justification for the assertion.
- A separate approach connects *totafos* to the "dripping" sense of (נ)ט-ף:
  Rabbi Yissocher Ber Eilenberg (1550-1623; *Tzeidah LaDerech* to Shemos 13:16)
  quotes in the name of his teacher Rabbi Mordechai Yaffe (1530-1612) that
  tefillin picture the trickle-down of how Hashem sustains the world: the black
  leather box above stands for His dominion rising over all of existence, the
  straps descending from it for the influence that reaches down into the world's
  inner core, represented by the wearer's torso.
- *Daas Mikra* (to Shemos 13:16) more simply reads *totafos* as head jewelry
  that would typically hang down, as though dripping, connecting it to *netifos*
  (Yeshayahu 3:19).

**Secondary or subordinate.** Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (1740-1814; *Chesheik
Shlomo*) champions Ibn Saruk's biliteralism and gives ט-ף the core sense of what
is secondary or subordinate to a main element: *taf*, the children secondary to
the household's master and mistress; a *tipah*, the small insignificant droplet
that falls from a larger body of liquid; *matif*, prophetic or hortatory speech
delivered in a controlled, slow way that resembles slowly falling drops; and (in
*Yerios Shlomo*) *tinuf* (dirtiness), with a middle nun added, since what soils an
object is secondary to the object. A *totefes* on this reading is a sort of hat
fastened on the head with straps, the hat being the main frontispiece and the
straps merely secondary. He then concludes: the Torah is saying the head tefillin
should be worn where people would normally wear their *totafos*, not that
*totafos* is the name of the tefillin.

**A foreign word.** All the readings above seem to presume *totafos* is a native
Hebrew word; the Gemara supplies one that seemingly treats it as a loanword.
- Rabbi Akiva (Menachos 34b, cited by Rashi to Devarim 6:8) parses *totafos* as
  *tot*, two in Coptic, and *fot*, two in *Afriki* (which Rabbi Klein glosses as
  Phrygian), alluding to the four parshiyos written on the parchments inside
  (or, per Rashi to Shemos 13:16, the four chambers of the head tefillin).
- Others place the word in Aramaic: Rabbeinu Chaim Paltiel (to Shemos 13:16)
  relates it to *t'fei* ("more," "addition"), since tefillin make a person more
  likely to remember the exodus, and Rabbi Mecklenburg to the Aramaic for
  appeasement (Targum to Bereishis 34:22, Mishlei 1:10).
- Others again read it as Egyptian: Rabbi Avraham Saba (1440-1508; *Tzror HaMor*
  to Shemos 13:16) connects it to Egyptian headdresses, and Abarbanel (there)
  holds it is the Egyptian word for "brain."
- A similar explanation has been proposed more recently by Professor Hubert
  Grimme (1864-1942), cited by Professor Jeffrey H. Tigay, who derives *totafos*
  from the Egyptian *ddft*, "snake," a reference to the Uraeus snake often worn
  on the headdress in ancient Egypt.
- It has even been suggested that *totafos* somehow relates to the names of the
  Egyptian deities Thoth and Ptah, but Rabbi Klein has not seen this cited in
  any serious scholarship on the topic.

### tefillin (תפילין)
Common in the Mishnah (Berachos 3:1, Shabbos 6:2, 16:1, Eruvin 10:1, Shekalim
3:2, Megillah 1:8, Moed Katan 3:4, Nedarim 2:2, Sanhedrin 11:3, Shevuos 3:8,
Yadayim 3:3). The plural is not incidental: technically it names the pair, head
and arm, and the Mishnah's word for a single box is *tefillah* (Menachos 4:1,
Keilim 16:7, 18:8, Mikvaos 10:2, 10:3-4); the possessive *tefillav*, "his
tefillin," appears at Erchin 6:4.

- Tosafos (Menachos 34b), in the same passage that treats *totafos*, derive the
  word from פ-ל-ל, argument or proof: the tefillin are a tangible testament to
  the association of Klal Yisrael with Hashem. Similarly the Rosh (*Hilchos
  Tefillin* §2) and his son the Tur (*Orach Chaim* §25) take it from *plilah*,
  argument, since tefillin quell any argument about the specialness of Klal
  Yisrael, serving as a physical sign that Hashem rests His presence upon them.
- The Maharal (*Be'er HaGolah* §4:2) proposes that the root's core sense is
  intellectuality or thoughtfulness: *tefillah* is a thoughtful plea, judges are
  *pelilim* (Shemos 21:22) because they use their minds to decide the law, and
  tefillin sit at the head and beside the heart to fix mind and heart on
  remembering Hashem; Rabbi Yaakov Emden (*Mor U'Ketziah, Orach Chaim* §25, and
  *Siddur Yaavetz* on the berachos before laying tefillin) and Rabbi Yisroel
  Lipshitz (*Tiferes Yisrael* to Menachos 4:1, *Yachin* §2) offer comparable
  explanations.
- Rabbi Nosson of Rome (1035-1106) in *Sefer HeAruch*, his lexicon of Rabbinic
  Hebrew, instead files *tefillin* under ת-פ-ל, and Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur
  (1469-1549) in *Sefer HaTishbi* questions why he did not give the root as פ-ל-ל
  (the root Tosafos give). Explaining the Aruch's position, Dr. Alexander Kohut
  (1842-1894) in *Aruch HaShalem* argues that ת-פ-ל is a permutation of ט-פ-ל
  (*tafel*, a secondary element attached to a primary one), and refers to the
  way tefillin are attached or tied to a person's body.
- Rabbi Yosef Teomim-Frankel (1727-1792), author of the *Pri Megadim*, suggests
  in one of his epistles that *tefillin* likewise derives from ת-פ-ל in its
  sense of "unsalted": because one dons tefillin every single weekday the
  mitzvah becomes like something routine and bland, with no special taste to it,
  and one must perform it anyway simply because Hashem decreed so. He further
  suggests connecting *tefillin* to *pele*/*hafla'ah*, wonderment, exaggeration
  and separation, from the root פ-ל-(א); Rabbi Mecklenburg applies that to the
  way Klal Yisrael consciously separate themselves from the ways of evil and
  from impure thoughts by binding tefillin on, and Rabbi Mordechai Abadi
  (1826-1884) to the way only Klal Yisrael separate Hashem from everything else
  and single Him out by declaring His uniqueness.
- Rashi (to Bereishis 30:8) in fact connects *tefillah* with *pesil* and the
  root פ-ת-ל, thread or cord, twisted, wrestling; some read Yehudah's *ptil*
  (Bereishis 38:18, usually rendered "wick" but here generally understood as
  "belt") as his tefillin, *ptil* being readable as an anagram of *tefillin*
  (*Paneach Raza*, *Baal HaTurim* there), and *Noam Elimelech* (to Bamidbar
  18:8), echoing Tosafos, reads tefillin as what ties Klal Yisrael to Hashem and
  Hashem to them. Indeed, the Gemara says that He too wears tefillin, whose
  contents declare the uniqueness of Klal Yisrael as ours declare His oneness
  (Berachos 6a).

Professor Moshe Weinfeld (1925-2009) notes that in the Aramaic writings found at
Elephantine the term *tefillah* describes a silver amulet, and that in Ugaritic
*tefili* refers to a sort of ornament worn on the head or forehead. That would
mean, Rabbi Klein observes, that the etymology of *tefillin* may in some ways line up with
that of *totafos*, which may also mean an ornament.

## Why the berachah says tefillin

The berachos before laying tefillin say *al mitzvas tefillin* and *l'haniach
tefillin*, using the Rabbinic word rather than the biblical *totafos*, which is
noteworthy because berachos usually prefer biblical terminology to Rabbinic. Two
contemporary rabbanim, Rabbi Pinchas Avraham Meyers (Chief Rabbi of the Hague) and
Rabbi Moshe Rachamim Shayo, give the same two answers: first, that
*totafos* refers only to the head tefillin and never to the arm tefillin, so the
Rabbis preferred the more inclusive *tefillin*; second, that *totafos* is more
likely of foreign origin, per Rabbi Akiva, while *tefillin* seems more closely
related to Hebrew.

## Rendering guidance

- In Chumash: *totafos* as **frontlets**, or transliterated, with a note that it
  denotes the head tefillin. Onkelos' *tefillin* is a translation of the mitzvah,
  not of the word.
- In Mishnah, Gemara, and halachah: *tefillin*, left as is. The English
  "phylacteries" is a foreign borrowing that reads oddly; use it only
  where a title or a quoted translation demands it.
- Trap: "tefillin" in English will absorb *totafos* wherever it appears, and the
  distinction between them is exactly what a passage on the berachah, or on Rabbi
  Pappenheim's reading, is about. Where a source quotes the pasuk and then the
  Mishnaic word in one breath, render the pasuk's word "frontlets" and the
  Mishnaic word "tefillin" and hold the pair.
- Singular: one box is *tefillah*, not "a tefillin." In English, "the head
  tefillin" and "the arm tefillin" read better than a forced singular.
- On why the berachah says *tefillin* and not *totafos*, see above: Rabbi Pinchas
  Avraham Meyers and Rabbi Moshe Rachamim Shayo give two answers, that *totafos*
  covers only the head, and that *totafos* is more likely of foreign origin (per
  Rabbi Akiva) while *tefillin* is closer to Hebrew.
