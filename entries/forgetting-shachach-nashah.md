# Forgetting: shachach / nashah

## Bottom line

*Shachach* is the ordinary word, over a hundred times in Tanach and the word
Devarim uses for every warning not to forget: **forget**. *Nashah* is rare and,
on the majority reading, heavier: **forget entirely, put out of mind for good**.
Malbim: what is *shachach* can still be recalled, what is *nashah* cannot. Rav
Hirsch adds the moral note, that *nashah* attaches to a man who held the thing
lightly, so the forgetting is his own fault. A minority (Rabbi Eliyahu Katz)
denies any difference at all.

## Distinctions

### shachach (שכח)
Root ש-כ-ח. The standard verb. Devarim uses it for forgetting Hashem (6:12,
8:11, 8:14, 8:19, 32:18), Sinai (4:9), the Torah (31:21), the covenant with the
Avos (4:31) and with Yisrael (4:23), how the Jews angered Hashem in the
wilderness (9:7), the halachos of maaser (26:13), and what Amalek did (25:19).
Rav Hirsch (Bereishis 8:1): *shachach* is forgetting inadvertently; elsewhere
(Devarim 4:9) he defines it as forgetting because one's attention went to
something else. Malbim (Iyov 11:6): what is forgotten by *shachach* can be
retrieved later.

Rabbi Yekusiel Yehudah Teitelbaum of Sighet (*Yitav Lev* to Shemos 10:1) reads
ש-כ-ח against its metathesis כ-ח-ש, deny or weaken: forget Hashem and your
obligations to Him, and you will end by denying Him outright. On the same
interchange, *Daas Mikra* explains *tishkach* in Tehillim 137:5 ("let my right
hand be forgotten") as *tikchash*, let it be weakened.

Rabbi Moshe Shapiro on the Aramaic *shachiach*, common or frequent, which shares
the root: one would expect the frequent thing to be the best remembered. Rather,
the mind files what recurs under "normal," and what is taken for granted is
easily lost; Chullin 75b says that a bizarre matter is the thing people surely
remember.

### nashah (נשה)
The rare word. The unambiguous case is Yosef naming Menashe: *ki nashani
Elokim es kol amali ve'es kol beis avi* (Bereishis 41:51). Rabbi Avraham
Menachem Rappaport (*Minchah Belulah*) held that this is the only cognate in
Tanach; the article shows others. Devarim 32:18 pairs both words in one pasuk,
*tzur yeladcha teshi vatishkach Kel mecholelecha*, where *teshi* is the same
root with the *nun* dropped for a *tav* (Rashi there). Further cases: Eichah
3:17; Yirmiyahu 23:39; Yeshayahu 44:21; Iyov 39:17 and 11:6. The *gid hanasheh*
(Bereishis 32:33) is read by the Zohar (Bereishis 170b) as food for the yetzer
hara that makes a man forget his duties to Hashem, so the name itself is from
*nashah*. The Gemara's *bei nasha* for a married woman's family of origin was
explained in the same sense, that on marrying she leaves the first household
behind: so *Tosafos Chachmei Anglia* to Kiddushin 24a, then Rabbi Yehuda
Chalava and Rabbeinu Bachya ibn Chalava (each to Bereishis 41:51), and later
Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Musaf HaAruch*) and Rabbi Shmuel HaLevi (*Nachalas
Shivah*).

On the root itself: Menachem Ibn Saruk reduces *nashani* and *teshi* to the
single letter *shin*; Ibn Chayyuj, Ibn Janach and Radak give נ-ש-ה or נ-ש-ש.
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo*) gives the biliteral נ-ש, moving
something out of its natural condition, and forgetting is information moved out
of its place in the mind; on that root he also puts *gid hanasheh* (the sinew
that moves from its place), *nosheh* (a lender, who moves his money out of his
hand), *anush* (a sickly man, whose health has been displaced) and *enosh*
(mankind, weak beside higher beings). Elsewhere he derives *teshi* from ת-ש,
weakening; Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi Ashkenazi (*Hoil Moshe*) makes weakening the
primary sense of נ-ש itself.

### The distinction, and the dissent
Malbim (Iyov 11:6): *shachach* is recoverable, *nashah* is irretrievable.
Rav Hirsch (Bereishis 8:1): *shachach* is inadvertent, *nashah* is what happens
to a man who treated the matter flippantly, so he bears more blame for having
forgotten; and (Devarim 4:9) *shachach* is displacement by another focus while
*nashah* is a weakened memory. Rabbi Eliyahu Katz disagrees with both: the
Targumim regularly render *shachach* and its cognates with *nashah*-words, so
in his view *nashah* is simply the Aramaic for *shachach* and no distinction
exists. The Bottom line follows Malbim and Rav Hirsch, the majority.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: *shachach* = **forget**. Reserve the heavier English for *nashah*.
- *Nashah* standing alone (Bereishis 41:51): "Hashem has made me **forget**
  entirely," or "has put out of my mind." Menashe's name carries the finality;
  a bare "forget" loses why Yosef chose the rarer word.
- The two in one pasuk (Devarim 32:18): the Hebrew moves from *teshi* to
  *vatishkach*, so do not render both with the same English verb. "You grew
  unmindful of the Rock that bore you, and forgot the God Who brought you
  forth" keeps them distinct without importing a claim about order.
- Trap: English "forget" will absorb both words, and "neglect" will quietly
  smuggle in Rav Hirsch's moral reading where the source has not made it.
  Use "forget" for *shachach*; choose the *nashah* rendering by which
  authority the passage follows, and say so in a note.
- Do not render *shachach* as "lose" or "blank on"; the register is wrong in a
  sefer, and Devarim's warnings are commands about a state of mind, not lapses.
