# Haircut: giluach / gizah / galav / sapar

## Bottom line

*Giluach* is **shaving**, a person's hair cut down to the skin; *gizah* is
**shearing**, an animal's wool, and a cut that leaves something standing.
*Galav* is a **barber** (or his razor), a *hapax legomenon* in Yechezkel 5:1.
*Sapar* is not Biblical at all: it is the Targumic and Mishnaic word, "barber"
and "to give a haircut," and it is the ordinary word today. The trap is that
English "cut hair" covers all of them; the *metzora* and the Nazir are *giluach*
and never *gizah*, and the difference is halachically load-bearing.

## Distinctions

### giluach (גילוח)
Root ג-ל-ח, some twenty-three appearances in Tanach, thirteen of them in Chumash,
mostly in the *metzora* and Nazir purification passages (Vayikra 14:8 and 14:9,
Bamidbar 6:9, 6:18 and 6:19). Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 9:21), on the
interchange of hei, ayin and ches, groups ג-ל-ה (*gilui*, reveal), ג-ל-ע
(*gala*, open) and ג-ל-ח under one theme of exposing and bringing forward: to
shave is to *expose* the skin the hair had covered. He reads *ta'ar*, razor,
from ע-ר-ה the same way, laying bare. Rabbi David Golumb (*Targumna* to Vayikra
14:9) adds a second strand: ג-ל-ח is ח-ג-ל by metathesis, and ח-ג-ל a form of
ע-ג-ל, "round," so *giluach* carries both the revealing of *gilui* and the
circular motion (*igul*) of cutting the hair of the head.

(On ח-ג-ל: no word from it appears in Tanach, but Chazal speak of the widow who
is *chaglah*, who goes around and earns herself a bad name, Yerushalmi Sotah 3:4,
and the *Aruch* has an entry for it from his girsa of Bereishis Rabbah 18:3.
Rabbi Avraham Abulafia derives the name Chaglah, one of the daughters of
Tzelafchad, from ח-ג-ל as a permutation of ע-ג-ל. In medieval Hebrew and Yiddish
*galach* came to mean a gentile priest, from the shaved head; Rashi seems to be
the first to use it so.)

### gizah / gezizah (גזיזה)
Root ג-ז-ז. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim gives two different distinctions in two
different works, and they are worth keeping apart:
1. *Yerios Shlomo*: the difference is **whose** hair. *Giluach* in Tanach is
   always a person's hair (Yosef before Pharaoh, Bereishis 41:14; the *metzora*,
   Vayikra 14:9; the Nazir, Bamidbar 6:18; the prohibition on shaving the beard,
   Vayikra 21:5), while *gizah* is normally an animal's wool (the shearing at
   Bereishis 38:12 and 31:19; *reishis hageiz* given to the Kohen, Devarim 18:4).
   He grants that *gizah* is used of a human haircut in a borrowed sense, as at
   Iyov 1:20.
2. *Cheshek Shlomo*: the difference is **how close**. *Giluach* severs the hair
   as near the skin as it can go, a close shave; *gizah* cuts and leaves a
   remnant standing, stubble. He takes the core sense of the two-letter ג-ז as
   trimming that leaves part attached and part detached, and reads the whole
   family from it: *geiz* (Tehillim 72:6), the grass left after mowing; *gozez*,
   shearing; *gazam*, the locust that eats some of the crop and leaves the rest;
   *geza*, a tree with its top cut off; *gazis*, hewn stone, part shaved down and
   the rest intact.

### galav (גלב)
Root ג-ל-ב, one appearance only: "take a sharp sword, a razor of a *galav* shall
you take" (Yechezkel 5:1). Radak: a *galav* is a barber, and "razor of a *galav*"
means the specially sharp blade barbers used to cut hair quickly. Radak (to
Yechezkel 5:1 and in *Sefer HaShorashim*) also traces *maglev* to this root,
apparently a barber's razor; the *Aruch* instead explains the *maglev* that the
angel held over Pharaoh (Bereishis Rabbah 41:2) as a bridle for driving a donkey.

Targum Yonasan uses forms of *galav* for *giluach* (Vayikra 19:27, Bamidbar 6:19)
and even for *ta'ar*, razor (Bamidbar 8:7), which suggests the word is Aramaic;
Rashi and Mahari Kara (to Yechezkel 5:1) say it is Greek. Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim
74:6) connects ג-ל-ב to ק-ל-פ, "peel" in Rabbinic Hebrew, since a haircut lays
bare what was covered much as peeling does.

### sapar (ספר)
Absent from Tanach, standard in the Targumim and the Mishnah, and the everyday
word now (*sapar*, barber; *tispores*, haircut). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur
(*Meturgaman*) notes that Onkelos and Targum Yonasan are inconsistent, sometimes
rendering *giluach* with *sapar* and sometimes leaving the Hebrew root standing,
and that the Targumic *maspar*, razor (Bamidbar 6:5, Shoftim 13:5), is from this
root; Targum Neofiti is the consistent one, always *sapar*. The Mishnah uses it
throughout: the *anshei mishmar* and *anshei maamad* may not take a haircut
during their week (Taanis 2:7), no haircut in the week of Tisha B'Av (Taanis
4:7), one may not see a Jewish king while he is being shorn (Sanhedrin 2:5),
restrictions on a haircut from a non-Jew (Avodah Zarah 2:2), plus *sapar* as
barber, *misperes* as razor and *misparayim* as scissors (Keilim 13:1).

Golumb (*Targumna* to Shemos 9:29 and Vayikra 14:9) reconciles the Targum's
inconsistency: for Yosef's haircut Onkelos writes *sapar* because in Mitzrayim
they cut hair with *misparayim*, scissors, while for the *metzora* he leaves
*giluach* standing, since the *metzora* must be shaved smooth "like a gourd"
(Sotah 16a), which scissors cannot do. Golumb further reads the string ס-פ-ר as
"round": *l'saper*, to tell a story that goes around from mouth to mouth; *sfar*,
a border city ringed by its wall; and *sapar*, the barber who cuts around the
head from ear to ear.

## Rendering guidance

- *Metzora* and Nazir passages: "shave," never "cut his hair." Sotah 16a's "like
  a gourd" is the standard, and Golumb hangs Onkelos's whole word choice on it.
- Animal wool: "shear." *Reishis hageiz* is "the first of the shearing."
- *Gizah* of a person (Iyov 1:20): "cut off his hair," since Pappenheim calls
  this the borrowed use; keep the animal register out of a *pasuk* about a
  mourner.
- Yechezkel 5:1: "a barber's razor" (Radak). "Razor" alone loses that the word
  names the man.
- Trap: English "cut" absorbs *giluach* and *gizah* both. Where Pappenheim's
  second distinction is at issue, use "shave clean" against "trim" or "crop," so
  the close cut and the cut that leaves stubble stay apart.
- Trap: *l'saper* means both "to give a haircut" and "to tell." In a Mishnaic
  citation check which one the context wants before reaching for "recount."
