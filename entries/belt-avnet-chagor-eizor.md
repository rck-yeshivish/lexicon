# Belt: avnet / chagor / eizor

## Bottom line

*Avnet* is the **sash** of the priestly service, *chagor* the general **belt**
for anything that goes around the body, and *eizor* the **girdle** at the
waist (Malbim); in *Tanach* only mighty men wear the *eizor*. Rashi and Tosafos
seem to separate the last two another way: the *chagorah* is worn inside the
clothing, the *eizor* on top of it. The Mishnah Berurah writes that *avnet*
could refer to pants as well as to a belt. *Sinar* is post-Biblical, a special
**waistband** Ezra instituted for women.

## Distinctions

The setting: the regular [*kohen*](priest-kohen-komer.html#%D7%9B%D7%94%D7%A0) wears four garments, pants, a belt, a hat and a
tunic; the *kohen gadol* wears four more, a breastplate, an apron, a robe and a
head-plate (Mishnah Yoma 7:5). By some opinions the *kohen gadol* does not wear
the same type of belt as the regular *kohen*.

### avnet (אבנט)
Malbim: a belt worn for ritual purposes, which is why it is the belt of the
*kohanim*. The Torah refers to the belt worn by all the *kohanim* as an
*avnet*. The Mishnah Berurah (*Orach Chaim* 46:8) writes that *avnet* could
refer to pants as well as to belts.

### chagor / chagorah (חגור, חגורה)
Root ח-ג-ר.
- Malbim: the general term, anything that goes around the circumference of the
  body, whether at the torso, the heart, the waist or the feet. Masculine
  *chagor*, feminine *chagorah*; the gender does not change the sense.
- Rashi (to Shabbos 120a) and Tosafos (to Shabbos 59b) seem to understand that
  a *chagorah* is a belt worn inside one's clothing, while an *eizor* is a belt
  worn on top of one's clothing.
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus argues that the root of *chagorah* is [*chug*](around-saviv-heikef-afef-chug.html#%D7%97%D7%95%D7%92), a circle,
  which may be related to *chag*, a festival, because on festivals celebrants
  dance in a circle.

In Modern Hebrew, *chagurah*, a corruption of *chagorah*, is a seat belt, as in
a car.

### eizor (אזור)
Root א-ז-ר. Malbim: specifically the belt at the waist, and one generally drawn
tight.
Because it is fastened tightly, girding an *eizor* becomes a figure for
performing a feat of strength, and in *Tanach* it is worn only by mighty men,
never by women or children.

Shadal (to Shemos 15:6 and 15:11) explains [*adir*](strength-koach-gevurah-ometz-chozek.html#%D7%90%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8) as the Aramaic form of
*eizor*, Hebrew zayin often becoming dalet in Aramaic: *adir* is
all-encompassing power, just as an *eizor* is a belt that entirely encompasses
the circumference of the body. The same is true of [*aderes*](garment-aderet-glima-itztala.html#%D7%90%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%AA), a cloak, which
likewise encompasses the body.

In Modern Hebrew *eizor* means an area or region, in the way English speaks of
the "Bible Belt" or the "Rust Belt."

### sinar (סינר)
In post-Biblical writings *sinar* is a different item again, a special belt
Ezra instituted that women should wear, something like a chastity belt.

## Rendering guidance

- "Belt" will happily swallow *avnet*, *chagor* and *eizor*. Reserve **sash**
  for *avnet* in a Mikdash context, **belt** for *chagor*, and **girdle** for
  *eizor*, especially where the verse is using *eizor* for strength; there
  "girded" carries the metaphor and "belted" does not.
