# Place: makom / atar / duchta

## Bottom line

*Makom* is the only native Hebrew word for "place," and it is the default:
**place**, or **site/location/spot** where English wants variety. *Atar* is the
standard Aramaic word for the same thing, the Targum's regular rendering of
*makom*; *duchta* is the Bavli's Aramaic word for "place," and probably a
loanword. The one thing not to get wrong: these are not shades of meaning but
shades of *language*. Where a passage sets *makom* against *atar*, the contrast
is Hebrew against Aramaic, not one kind of place against another.

## Distinctions

### makom (מקום)
Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak (each in *Sefer HaShorashim*) derive *makom*
from the triliteral root ק-ו-ם, taking the opening MEM as non-radical; Menachem
Ibn Saruk has no entry for the word at all, so his root for it is unknown.
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, working from the biliteral ק-ם, supplies the sense that
ties the family together: the core idea is "height," the up-and-down dimension of
a thing. From it come *komah* (physical stature), *kamah* (standing grain),
*kumah* (standing or rising, and figuratively holding one's stature under
pressure), *kiyum* (established, enduring), and *makom*, the spot in which a
thing has been planted and thereby firmly established, then borrowed outward to
mean any location at all. Rabbi Pappenheim adds *nekamah* (revenge) to the
family: the debt owed a wrongdoer is kept "established" until it can be repaid.
*Makom* appears roughly four hundred times in Tanach.

### atar (אתר)
An Aramaic word. Outside the phrase *derech ha'atarim* (Bamidbar 21:1) it never
appears in the Hebrew of Tanach, nor in the Mishnah, but it appears five times in
the Aramaic sections (Daniel 2:35; Ezra 5:15, 6:3, 6:5, 6:7). Rabbi Eliyahu
HaBachur (*Meturgaman*): *atar* is the Targumim's standard rendering of *makom*
(so Targum to Bereishis 1:9, 13:4, 20:11; Devarim 12:2; Shoftim 19:13; Shmuel I
7:16; Shmuel II 17:12; Melachim I 13:16; Yirmiyahu 22:3). The cognate *asra*
stands in *Kaddish D'Rabbanan*.

On *derech ha'atarim* itself the commentators split. Most (*Eichah Rabbah* 1:21,
Targum Onkelos, Menachem Ibn Saruk, Rashi, Rashbam, Rabbeinu Yosef Bechor Shor,
Ibn Ezra, Radak) treat the ALEPH as non-radical and read the word from *la'tur*,
"to spy," so the phrase names the route the spies had taken. A second group
(Rabbi Toviah ben Eliezer in *Pesikta Zutrata*, Rabbeinu Meyuchas, and Rabbi
Menachem Ricanati, all to Bamidbar 21:1) glosses *atar* with the word *makom*
outright, giving "the way of the sites." Rashi himself glosses *atar* with
*makom* elsewhere (to Daniel 2:38; Ezra 6:3, 6:8, 8:15; *Kesuvos* 68a; *Bava
Metzia* 67a; *Bava Basra* 29a, 103a; *Avodah Zarah* 7b, 31b).

Rabbi Yaakov Yehudah de Kassif takes *atar* as Hebrew, grouping three roots
built of ALEPH, TAV and REISH around the idea of a marked boundary: *toar*, the
form a body's contours outline; *hasraah*, the warning that draws the line a
sinner must not cross; and *atar*, a place defined by the borders that set it off
from everywhere else.

### duchta (דוכתא)
The Bavli's usual Aramaic word for "place," with the shortened *duch* at
*Berachos* 42b. Rashi defines both with the word *makom* (to *Moed Katan* 16b,
*Kesuvos* 77a, *Sanhedrin* 96b). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur's *Meturgaman* has no
entry for it, though it does render *makom* in Targum to Mishlei 27:8 (and
*ducha* in Targum to Melachim I 14:28 and Divrei HaYamim II 12:11). Rabbi Dr.
Ernest Klein reads the Aramaic *atar* against verb cognates meaning "tracing,"
which would make the "place" sense of *duchta* a widening of a word about
locating something rather than about the location itself.

### asher (אשר)
The common Biblical conjunction, but in two places Targum renders it *atar*
(Targum to Shmuel I 23:13 and Yechezkel 23:40; see Rashi there and to *Kiddushin*
76b). This may be interpretation rather than translation, reading *asher* in
context as "that [place where]"; Rabbi Aharon Marcus treats *asher* and *atar* as
genuinely cognate, the Hebrew SHIN answering the Aramaic TAV.

## Rendering guidance

- *Makom* standing alone: "place." Reach for "site," "location," or "spot" only
  where the English sentence needs the variety; none of them is a different word
  in the Hebrew.
- Where a Rishon glosses *atar* or *duchta* with *makom*, he is translating.
  Render both "place" and do not manufacture a distinction the gloss denies.
- Trap: English "place" absorbs all three, so a passage whose point *is* the
  difference (an Aramaic word explained by a Hebrew one, a Targum's choice) needs
  the Hebrew or Aramaic kept in the rendering, at least once.
- *Derech ha'atarim* (Bamidbar 21:1): follow the majority and render "the way of
  the spies," noting the minority "the way of the sites" if the passage turns on
  it. Do not present the minority as the plain sense.
- *Atra* in *Kaddish D'Rabbanan*: "place," in the sense of a locale or community.
- The Rabbinic *le'alter* ("immediately") is plausibly *al atar*, "on the spot";
  render it as an expression of time, not of place.
