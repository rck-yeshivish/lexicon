# Poison: eres / sam hamaves / rosh / chamat / ra'al

## Bottom line

*Eres* is **venom**, in practice snake venom, and it is Talmudic, not Biblical.
*Sam hamaves* is the general **poison**, literally the deadly drug, and never
venom. *Rosh* is **poison** in Tanach, drawn either from a snake's head or from
a poisonous plant. *Chamat* is **venom** as what an angered snake emits. *Ra'al*
is disputed: poison, or a draught that leaves a man limp and reeling. The trap:
English "poison" absorbs all of them, and Chullin 3:5 turns on *sam hamaves*
being precisely not venom.

## Distinctions

### eres (ארס)
Not in Tanach or Mishnah; it appears in Hebrew passages of the Bavli and a few
times in the Yerushalmi, there usually with a yod after the aleph, so likely
*iras*. The Gemara's usage fixes the sense. Avodah Zarah 30b: three kinds of
*eres*, a young snake's which sinks in a liquid, an old snake's which floats, a
middle-aged one's between (Sukkah 50a, Bava Kamma 115b). Avodah Zarah 31b: all
*sheratzim* have *eres*, but only a snake's kills. Sanhedrin 78a and Bava Kamma
23b ask whether a snake releases venom only when angered. So *eres* is venom,
usually a snake's; this is what Bigsan and Seresh meant for the king's cup
(Megillah 13b).

Rashi (to Shabbos 62b) derives *eres* by metathesis from *ka'as*, anger, since a
snake emits venom only when angered. Rabbeinu Efrayim reverses it: *ka'as* comes
from *eres*, since anger puts poison in a man's heart. The Gemara already uses
the connection, expounding the name of Kalev's daughter Achsah (Yehoshua
15:16-17, Shoftim 1:12-13, Divrei HaYamim I 2:49) as one whose beauty made every
man who saw her angry at his wife, playing ע-כ-ס against כ-ע-ס. Rabbi Menachem
Azariah of Fano reads her name as marking her a gilgul of Tzipporah, who saved
Moshe from death by *nachash* through the milah (Shemos 4:24-26).

### sam hamaves (סם המות)
Literally the potion of death. Not in Tanach; once in Mishnah, where an animal
that swallowed *sam hamaves* or was bitten by a snake is no tereifah but is
still forbidden as dangerous (Chullin 3:5). The two are listed separately there,
which shows *sam hamaves* is not venom. In the Gemara: whether a radish is *sam
hachaim* or *sam hamaves* (Eruvin 56a), Torah learned without proper intent
(Taanis 7a), a mother's oath on her child's life (Gittin 35a), and liability for
killing another's animal by feeding it (Bava Kamma 47b, 56a).

Because sin and samech interchange, *Midrash Sechel Tov*, *Midrash Aggadah* and
*Daas Zekeinim* read *vayusam lefanav le'echol* (Bereishis 24:33; the article
prints 23:33) as a hint that Besuel tried to poison Eliezer; a commentary
ascribed to Rashi on Divrei HaYamim II 22:10 has Asaliah killing this way too.
Pappenheim takes ס-מ / ש-מ as putting a thing in its place, and derives *samim*,
the ketores ingredients (Shemos 30:7, 30:34, 40:27; Vayikra 16:12), from their
being kept in designated places. So *sam hamaves* is the general word, covering
any lethal preparation and naming none in particular.

### rosh (ראש / רוש), chamat (חמת)
*Rosh*: Devarim 29:17, Iyov 20:16, Eichah 3:19 and elsewhere. Targum and Rashi
(to Yirmiyahu 8:14) take it as poison drawn from a snake's *rosh*, its head, so
essentially venom. It is normally spelled with an aleph, like the word for head,
once with a vav (Devarim 32:32). Others take it as a plant extract, Rabbi Yehuda
Feliks identifying it with poppy or conium, and Pappenheim traces it to ר-ש,
head, for that plant's round top. *Chamat* (Devarim 32:33, Tehillim 58:5) is
venom named for what a snake emits when its *cheimah* is aroused, the same anger
motif as Rashi's *eres*, here in the word itself.

### ra'al (רעל) and tarelah (תרעלה)
Nachum 2:4, Zechariah 12:2; *tarelah* in Tehillim 60:5, Yeshayahu 51:17, 51:22.
Commonly rendered poison, but Rashi (to Yeshayahu 51:17 and Nachum 2:4) says it
is a draught that leaves a man weak and unable to move, as though bound. A near
word *re'alah* (Yeshayahu 3:19) is an ornament one wraps oneself in. Radak (to
Yeshayahu 51:17 and in *Sefer HaShorashim*) cites the poison reading but also
defines it as shaking or reeling, possibly from *ro'ed*.

### eches (עכס)
A separate dispute, since Rashi reads it as venom. Yeshayahu 3:16 has
*uvragleihem te'akasnah* as a hapax verb; 3:18 has *ha'achasim* as an ornament,
which Rashi and Ibn Ezra explain as special shoes. The Gemara (Shabbos 62b, Yoma
9b): the Bnos Tzion put perfume in their shoes and kicked their feet at the young
men in Yerushalayim's markets, infecting them with the yetzer hara, which works
like *eres*. Hence Rashi (to Yeshayahu 3:16) glosses *eches* as snake venom and
the verb as poisoning them to sin; the Vilna Gaon joins the two senses by
taking *eches* as shoes cased in snakeskin. Ibn Ezra and Radak dissent, tying the
verb to the noun: the women drew attention by the noise of their shoes, which
Radak says chimed like bells. At Mishlei 7:22 Rashi again reads venom, while
Rabbi Yeshayah of Trani takes it as a prisoner's ball and chain, akin to the shoe
sense (so too Ibn Ezra there and Radak in *Sefer HaShorashim*), a sin bound to a
man and resurfacing on the Day of Judgment (Sotah 3b). The Midrash renders *efeh*
as *echis*, viper (*Mechilta* to Shemos 15:22).

### la'anah (לענה)
Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) counts *la'anah* a synonym of *rosh*,
*chamat* and *ra'al*, all poison. This is his own novelty: Rashi and Radak call
*la'anah* extremely bitter but not deadly. Default to **wormwood**.

## Rendering guidance

- *Eres*: "venom." Never "poison" in a sugya where a snake is in view, since
  Avodah Zarah 31b turns on which creatures' *eres* is lethal.
- *Sam hamaves*: "deadly poison," or "a deadly drug" where the substance is fed
  or administered. Chullin 3:5 lists it beside a snakebite, so rendering it
  "venom" makes the Mishnah repeat itself.
- *Rosh* in a pasuk: "poison," with the *rosh*/head wordplay noted where Rashi's
  derivation is cited; paired with *la'anah* (Devarim 29:17), "poison and
  wormwood," which keeps Rashi and Radak against Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino.
- *Chamat taninim* (Devarim 32:33): "the venom of serpents," not "poison."
- *Ra'al* / *tarelah*: "a reeling draught" or "cup of staggering" following
  Rashi, "poison" only if the reader is told which reading is taken.
- Trap: English "poison" will take *eres*, *sam hamaves*, *rosh*, *chamat* and
  *ra'al* without protest, and "venom" will take *eres*, *rosh* and *chamat*.
  When two appear in one passage, force the split: venom for what a snake emits,
  poison for what a man administers.
- *Eches* (Yeshayahu 3:16): pick a side and say so. "They poison with their
  feet" is Rashi; "they tinkle with their feet" is Ibn Ezra and Radak.
