# Grapevine: gefen / sorek / shdeimah (sarig)

## Bottom line

*Gefen* is the plain word: **grapevine**. *Sorek*, by most readings, is a
**choice vine**, the best stock; Rabbi Klein suggests that perhaps all the
different explanations of *sorek* are somehow describing the same thing. *Sarig*
is the **tendril**. Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) lists *gefen*, *sorek*
and *shdeimah* together as "grapevines", but not everybody agrees that
*shdeimah* refers to a grapevine. Rashi makes it a **grain field**, Rabbeinu
Bachya and the grammarians a **vine**, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim simply rich,
nourishing **ground**, and *Ohalei Yehuda* and *Shoresh Yesha* a poor field.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What shdeimah means.** Four views.
- **Grain field**: Rashi (to Devarim 32:32, Yeshayahu 16:8, Chavakuk 3:17),
  consistently.
- **Grapevine, a true synonym of gefen**: Rabbeinu Bachya (to Devarim 32:32),
  who reads the pasuk as deliberate doubled verbiage, with Ibn Janach (*Sefer
  HaShorashim*), Ibn Ezra and Radak (to Yeshayahu 16:8).
- **Any nourishing ground**: Pappenheim: land juicy enough to feed what grows in it, neither a vine nor Rashi's grain field.
- **A poor field**: *Ohalei Yehuda* and *Shoresh Yesha*, a portmanteau of
  *sadeh*, field, and *mah*, what, seemingly invoking the shin and sin
  interchange, with mem interchanging with peh to link ש-ד-ם to ש-ד-ף,
  wind-blasted (Ibn Janach and Radak make a similar point at Yeshayahu 37:27,
  though not for every instance).

**What sorek is.**
- Targum Yonasan (to Yeshayahu 5:2, Yirmiyahu 2:21), Ibn Janach and Radak: the
  choicest grapevine, and per Ibn Ghiyyat in Radak's name one whose grapes have
  no pits.
- Rashi: shoots good for planting as trunks in their own right (Yeshayahu 5:2),
  or the vine's especially long tendrils (Bereishis 49:11), while at Yirmiyahu
  2:21 he seems to follow the choice-vine approach, explaining *sorek* as shoots
  from a good *gefen*.
- Earlier, Rabbi Shmuel ben Chofni Gaon (998 to 1034, to Bereishis 49:11): the
  *softest* branches of the vineyard, which he notes is the sense of *al-sarik*
  in Syrian Arabic; Rabbi Klein adds that the word possibly refers to the Sirac
  or Syrac grapes now known as Syrah or Shiraz.
- Ibn Saruk: simply *sarig*, a tendril.

Rabbi Klein suggests that perhaps all these readings are somehow describing the
same thing.

**The root of gefen.**
- The early grammarians (Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Radak) hold the triliteral
  ג-פ-ן.
- Pappenheim derives it from the biliteral ג-ף, outward protrusion, with *gaf*
  (side, Mishlei 9:3), a bird's wing, *guf* (the body as the outward projection
  of the person), [*mageifah*](plague-dever-mageifah-negef.html#%D7%9E%D7%92%D7%A4%D7%94) (a plague spreading by contact), and [*agaf*](wing-kanaf-ever-agaf.html#%D7%90%D7%92%D7%A4) (a
  flank of troops spread like wings; in Modern Hebrew a department within a
  larger body).
- *Shoresh Yesha*, implicitly invoking the gimmel and kaf interchange, relates
  ג-פ-ן to כ-פ-ן, famine, since wine makes a man hungry (Pesachim 107b to 108a).

In Ugaritic and Canaanite mythological texts the *gefen* is deified as an
idolatrous god named Gapnu, understood there to be a messenger of Baal.

## Distinctions

### gefen (גפן) and sorek (שורק)
*Gefen* is the default: its cognates appear 55 times in Tanach, against six for
*shdeimah* (Devarim 32:32; Yeshayahu 37:27, 16:8; Chavakuk 3:17; Yirmiyahu
31:39; Melachim II 23:4) and three for *sorek* and *sorekah* (Bereishis 49:11,
Yeshayahu 5:2, Yirmiyahu 2:21), making it the most common of the three. While
*gefen* plainly means a grapevine, the meaning of *sorek* is somewhat unclear
(see the dispute above). Ibn Saruk also defines *shdeimah* as a *sorek*.
*Sorek* is also taken as a type of high-quality red grape (Bereishis 49:11,
Yeshayahu 5:2, 16:8, Yirmiyahu 2:21), as *serukim* is used of the red horses of
Zecharyah 1:8; it seems to be cognate with the Arabic *sharaqa*, "shining
brightly," and the Persian *sorkh*, "red" or "scarlet." It appears twice as a
place-name: Nachal Sorek, Delilah's valley between Ashkelon and Gaza (Shoftim
16:4), and seemingly Masreikah, the town of Samlah king of Edom (Bereishis 36:36,
Divrei HaYamim I 1:47).

*Sechel Tov* (to Bereishis 49:11): *gefen* is any vine, laden with grapes or
not, while a *soreikah* is a vine without grapes (*ve'anochi netaticha sorek*,
Yirmiyahu 2:21), since vines are planted bare and not laden. *Arugas HaBosem*
rejects Dunash's view that *kanah*, *netzer* and *sorek* are one, since *sorek*
is a kind of vine. The Rokeach (on the Hoshanos, p. 50): the *kanah* of Tehillim
80:16 is the wood by the root; Rashi and Ibn Ezra there take it as the firmly
set vine. The Rokeach (Eikev, p. 193): *gefen* is named for stopping the wine in
a *magufah*, a sealed jar, to keep its taste and strength. Rashi (to Yirmiyahu
2:21): *sorek* is shoots of a good vine.

Rabbeinu Bachya (to Devarim 32:14): *enav* is the single grape; the grapes
hanging together are an *eshkol* in the Torah's language and a *parkil* in the
language of Chazal (*Sukkah* 10a); what the clusters hang from is a *gefen*
while attached and a [*zemorah*](pole-mot-bad-neis-toren-shapud.html#%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%94) once cut, as in *vayichresu misham zemorah
ve'eshkol anavim echad* (Bamidbar 13:23). At Bamidbar 13:23 he brings a Midrash
(Zohar, Shelach 160b) that it is a *zemorah* while attached and a *mot* once
cut.

### shdeimah (שדמה)
See the dispute above. Pappenheim's route to it runs through the biliteral ש-ד,
whose core sense he gives as abnormality: *shoded* (commandeering), [*sheid*](demon-sheid-lilis-ruach.html#%D7%A9%D7%93) (a
demon, said to disrupt the natural order), *shidah* and *shidos* (Koheles 2:8,
women who danced as swiftly as *sheidim*), and *shad* (the gland whose
secreting of nourishment departs from what other body parts do). From that last
sense he derives *shod* (Yeshayahu 16:4, 60:16) and *leshad* (Tehillim 32:4)
for mother's milk, borrowed for the *mann* (Bamidbar 11:8); *eshed* (Bamidbar
21:15), where streams converge as the fluids of milk converge; and *shdeimah*,
a field juicy enough to nourish growth.

### sarig (שריג) and srak (סרק)
*Sarig* appears twice in Chumash, in the butler's dream of a *gefen* with three
*sarigim* (Bereishis 40:10, 40:12), and once in Yoel 1:7; it seems to belong
with ש-ר-ג, entangling (Iyov 40:17, Eichah 1:14). *Srak* in Mishnaic Hebrew is
a non-fruit-bearing tree (*Kilayim* 6:3 and 6:5, *Shevi'is* 1:3, *Sotah* 8:3,
*Bava Basra* 2:7, *Bechoros* 4:8). Describing the abundance of the Holy Land in
Messianic times, Kesubos 111b expounds *sorekah* of Bereishis 49:11, spelled
with a sin, as though it were *srak* with a samech; read literally as it appears
before us, the passage says that even trees that bear no fruit will yet bear
fruit. That reading has seemingly nothing to do with grapevines, so why is it
tied to *sorekah*?

- Maharsha (Rabbi Shmuel Eidels, 1555 to 1631): the rabbis expounded the word
  as though it were spelled with a samech, in an allusion to a *srak* tree, and
  he concedes that the plain sense of the pasuk they expound is about
  grapevines, not other trees.
- Alternatively Rivan there, Rashi's son-in-law (see also *Shitah Mekubetzes*
  there in Rashi's name): even grapevines that would otherwise count as barren
  will still yield a plentitude of grapes.

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 49:11 pairs *gefen* and *sorekah*. Do not print "grapevine" twice.
  "He will tie his donkey to the vine, and his young donkey to the choice vine"
  keeps the escalation that Targum Yonasan and Radak hear in *sorek*.
- Devarim 32:32 pairs *gefen* and *shdeimos*: following Rashi, "of the fields
  of Amorah"; following Rabbeinu Bachya and the grammarians, both are vines.
- Trap: English "vine" will swallow *gefen*, *sorek*, and *sarig* together.
  Reserve "vine" for *gefen*, "choice vine" for *sorek*, "tendril" for *sarig*.
  Where a passage turns on Kesubos 111b, keep *sorekah* and *srak* distinct in
  the English or the drashah on the spelling disappears.
