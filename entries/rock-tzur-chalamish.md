# Rock: tzur / chalamish (against sela)

## Bottom line

*Even* is the general term for any rock. A *sela* is a hard rock, and Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim and Malbim both write that a *tzur* is harder still. *Tzur* is
**flintrock** or **hard rock**, and it is also a divine epithet; Rambam adds a
second sense, the quarry a stone is hewn from, hence **source**. *Chalamish* is
conventionally **flint**, but Rabbi Pappenheim makes it a subdivision of *tzur*
covering the very hardest stones. Malbim explains that a *sela* is porous and
can hold water while a *tzur* cannot, and this, Rabbi Klein notes, fits neatly
with Rabbi Chaim Vital's calling a *tzur* the stone that gives fire.

## Distinctions

### tzur (צור)
Both Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim and Malbim: a *tzur* is harder than a *sela*.

- For Rabbi Pappenheim the hardest types of rock are called *tzur*, and he ties
  the word to *tzarur*, cluster or bundle, since its components are packed so
  tightly that the resulting stone is quite hard; he identifies one type of
  *tzur* as *Hornstein*, German for "horn stone," that is, chert. Pieces of
  chert generally have very sharp edges, so they can be used for cutting in
  place of metal knives.
- Rabbi Yosef Kimchi (1105-1170), father of Radak (Rabbi David Kimchi,
  1160-1235), writes in *Sefer HaGalui* that *tzur* / *tzor* means specifically
  a *sharp* stone. That fits its two circumcision contexts: Tzipporah takes a
  *tzur* to be mohel for her son (Shemos 4:25), and Yehoshua's mass milah is
  done with *charvos tzurim*, literally stone swords (Yehoshua 5:2-3).

Malbim: a *sela* is the porous kind that can hold water inside; a *tzur* is so
hard that water in it is impossible. This fits neatly, Rabbi Klein says, with
Rabbi Chaim Vital (1543-1620), who wrote that a *tzur*, or *tzur hachalamish*,
is the stone that produces fire: a stone with water in it certainly could
not make fire. Rabbi Yisrael Menachem Mendel Sacharov (d. 1966) points out that
*hatzur* has the same gematria as *aish*, fire.

Then the extended senses.
- Rambam (*Moreh Nevuchim* 1:16): *tzur* means a mountain and a kind of hard
  rock, and also the quarry from which stones are cut, which is how Yeshayahu
  can say "look to the *tzur* from which you were hewn... look to Avraham your
  father and to Sarah who bore you" (Yeshayahu 51:1-2); from there the word was
  borrowed for any root or source, and Hashem is called *Tzur* as the Source of
  everything (Devarim 32:4, 32:18, 32:30; Shmuel I 2:2; Yeshayahu 26:4).
- Rabbi Moshe Teitelbaum (1759-1841), building on Rambam: the *tzur* is the
  source of stones, while *even* and *sela* are the stones themselves.
- Rabbi Chaim Friedlander (1923-1986): a *tzur* is the bedrock a building's
  foundation rests on, and Hashem is called *Tzur* because He is the surest
  foundation, relied on continuously the way a building leans on its footing at
  every moment.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: Hashem is *Tzur* because as the Prime Force He ties
  (*tzorer*) all of creation into one, everything depending on Him to exist; and
  *Berachos* 10a reads Chanah's "there is no *tzur* like our God" (Shmuel I 2:2)
  as *tzayir*, Fashioner.
- Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bamidbar 31:8): the name of Tzur,
  one of the Midianite kings killed in the war with Midian, is related to
  *tzur*, rock.

### chalamish (חלמיש)
*Chalamish* is commonly translated "flint," a glassy rock formed from silicate
fossils. Like a *tzur*, flint stones are generally sharp, because when they
break they form conchoidal fractures, which always have sharp edges; both chert
and flint have historically been popular stones for making arrowheads. And in
Modern Hebrew it is *tzur* that refers specifically to flint.

Nonetheless, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim argues for a different identification:
*chalamish* does not refer specifically to flint but is a sub-category of *tzur*
covering the hardest types of rock within that category, so that it even
includes diamonds, called *yahalom* in Modern Hebrew after one of the precious
gems mentioned in Shemos 39:11, and generally considered the hardest natural
mineral. By a small stretch of the imagination, Rabbi Pappenheim links the word
to *chelmon*, egg yolk, both being round and smooth. It appears bound to *tzur*
in construct, *tzur hachalamish* and *chalamish tzur* (Devarim 8:15, 32:13), and
in the absolute as simply *chalamish* (Tehillim 114:8, Iyov 28:9, Yeshayahu
50:7).

Mohs' Scale of Mineral Hardness rates minerals by hardness, with talc, the
softest, at 1. On that scale the calcite-based rocks Rabbi Klein elsewhere
identifies as *sela* rate a 3,
while chert, a type of *tzur*, is much harder and rates a 7, and diamonds take
the top at 10. If the conventional identification of *chalamish* as flint is
accurate, *chalamish* should rate a 7; but as Rabbi Pappenheim puts it,
*chalamish* can reach as high as 10, because it takes in minerals as hard as
diamond.

## Rendering guidance

- Fix one English word per Hebrew word and hold it: *even* "stone," *sela*
  "crag," *tzur* "flintrock" (or "hard rock"), *chalamish* "flint" or "hardest
  rock." Devarim 32 uses *tzur* repeatedly and *chalamish* once (32:13); a
  translation that says "rock" throughout makes the shirah unreadable as an
  argument.
- צור as a name of Hashem: capital **Rock**, as in "the Rock, His work is
  perfect" (Devarim 32:4). Do not lowercase it and do not substitute
  "Stronghold" or "Refuge," which belong to other words. There are three
  separate accounts of why Hashem is called *Tzur*, Rambam's source-sense, Rabbi
  Chaim Friedlander's foundation, and Rabbi Pappenheim's *tzorer*, and "Rock"
  leaves room for all three.
- צור הַחלמיש (Devarim 8:15, 32:13): "the flinty rock" or "rock of flint." Do
  not collapse the construct into one word; the pair is a superlative, the
  hardest sort of the hard sort.
- Trap: *tzur* and *sela* in the same passage. Follow Malbim and let the English
  mark it: a *sela* can hold water, a *tzur* cannot, and per Rabbi Chaim Vital
  the *tzur* is the fire-stone. If both are "rock," the pesukim look like
  repetition. (This applies to the *sela* Moshe was told to speak to at
  Bamidbar 20:8.)
- Where *tzur* means a quarry or lineage (Yeshayahu 51:1), render "source" or
  "the rock you were hewn from," and gloss it once; a bare "rock" hides Rambam's
  reading and with it the parallel to Avraham and Sarah in the next pasuk.
- צורים as knives (Yehoshua 5:2-3): "flint knives" or "stone knives," not
  "swords of rock," even though the Hebrew says *charvos*.
