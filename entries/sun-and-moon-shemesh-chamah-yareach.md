# Sun and moon: shemesh / chamah / cheres and yareach / levanah / sahar

## Bottom line

Biblical Hebrew has three words for the sun and three for the moon, and Rabbi
Aharon Marcus explains that each names its luminary by a different aspect.
*Shemesh* is the **sun** as servant, doing its assigned work; *chamah* is the
**sun** as the hot one; *cheres* is the **sun** as the baker of pottery, or, as
others explain, as the pottery-colored one at sunrise and sunset. *Yareach* is
the **moon** as the traveler on its path; *levanah* is the **moon** as the white
one; *sahar* is the **crescent**, the moon named by its shape, though others
note it is an Aramaic loanword sometimes used for the moon. Rabbi Marcus adds
that the words may also represent different stages in the history of man's
relationship with the sun and moon. Rabbi Klein notes that some might reject
that historical part as seeming to contradict the Divine origin of the Hebrew
language, but the basic point still holds: the two sets of three somewhat
parallel each other.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why the sun is called *cheres*.**

- Rabbi Aharon Marcus: because the sun's heat bakes pottery, and pottery was a
  mainstay of ancient civilization, so the sun is named for what it makes
  possible.
- Others: because the sun takes on a reddish, pottery-like hue at sunrise and at
  sunset, so it is named for how it looks.

Rabbi Klein gives no ground for choosing between them.

**Whether *sahar* is Hebrew.**

- Rabbi Aharon Marcus counts it as the third Hebrew word for the moon, the one
  that names it by its shape.
- Others hold *sahar* is not really Hebrew but an Aramaic loanword sometimes
  used for the moon.

On Rabbi Marcus's view the word carries the crescent shape; on the loanword view
it is simply a word sometimes used for the moon.

**Whether the staged history is admissible.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus holds that the
six words may also represent different stages in the history of man's
relationship with the sun and moon. Rabbi Klein notes that some might reject
this historical aspect of the explanation, because it would seem to contradict
the Divine origin of the Hebrew language, and answers that the basic argument
still holds: the three words for the sun and the three for the moon somewhat
parallel each other in their focus on different aspects of the sun and the
moon.

## Distinctions

### The luminaries are unnamed at first
In the account of creation (Bereishis 1) neither *shemesh* nor *yareach* appears.
The Torah calls them only the great luminary and the small luminary, as if to say
that their whole purpose is to give light and that they carry no importance of
their own. On Rabbi Aharon Marcus's reading, that is the point: at first the sun
and the moon had no names at all.

### shemesh (שמש) and yareach (ירח)
The first pair coined, and coined against idolatry. When, as the generations
progressed, people began to worship the sun and the moon under the names Baal,
master, and Asherah, early monotheists who fought idolatry coined names that
identify the two luminaries yet downplay their importance: *shemesh*, the
servant, highlighting its subservience to Hashem, and *yareach*, related to
*orach*, a path, for the moon's orbital path in the heavens. Both names in time
came to be used as names of the idolatrous gods of the sun and the moon
themselves, and archeologists have identified the
worship of Shammash and Yarikh in ancient Canaan and Mesopotamia. This is what
necessitated the coining of the next pair. Place-names reflecting the old
practices are still in use: Beis Shemesh, the house of the sun, and Yericho,
related to *yareach*, were apparently ancient centers of the sun and moon cults
before the Jewish People entered Eretz Yisrael.

- The classical lexicographers Menachem Ibn Saruk, Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak
  trace *yareach* to the triliteral root י-ר-ח (and *reiach*, smell, to ר-י-ח).
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim instead sees both as derivatives of the biliteral ר-ח,
  air, whose commonest form is *ruach*, wind: the moon is called *yareach*
  because its movements somehow control the *ruach*, the tides and winds.

### chamah (חמה), cheres (חרס), levanah (לבנה) and sahar (סהר)
The second layer: *chamah* for the sun's scorching heat and *cheres* for its
ability to bake pottery (others: for its reddish, pottery-like hue at sunrise
and sunset), *levanah* and *sahar* for the moon's perceived color and shape. In
short, *shemesh* focuses on the sun's utilitarian role in the celestial system,
carrying out Hashem's wishes, *chamah* on its heat, *cheres* on its connection
with pottery; *yareach* on the moon's orbital path, *levanah* on its color,
*sahar* on its shape. In time these words too, unsurprisingly, came to be
associated with celestial idolatry, as worshippers of the sun and moon used
ritual objects in their cults. The Torah (Vayikra 26:30) refers to the
*chaman*, an idol erected on a roof to worship the sun, and Yeshayahu (3:18)
criticizes the people for wearing *saharonim*, talisman necklaces shaped like
the moon.

The Tashbetz (*Magen Avos*, Jerusalem 2007, p. 223): *shemesh* serves Hashem,
*chamah* makes things hot, *cheres* breaks things, as earthenware must be
broken, since it can never be fully cleaned.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 38:17): *levanah* is the
source of Latin *luna*, the v and u sounds interchanging in Latin.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 18:1 and 38:17): *yom*
sometimes means the sun, and in Sanskrit *jama* means sun; *sahara* has
Indo-European parallels in Persian *sohre* and Sanskrit *souhra*, meaning moon.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: "the sun" and "the moon" for all six. The aspects are etymological,
  and forcing them into every rendering makes the English strange.
- Trap: English has one word for the sun and one for the moon, so a pasuk that
  pairs two of the Hebrew words will collapse into "the sun and the moon"; where
  the pairing matters, name the aspects.
- *Sahar*: "crescent" or "crescent moon" on Rabbi Marcus's reading, where shape
  is what the word contributes; on the view that it is an Aramaic loanword for
  the moon, plain "moon" is defensible.
- Watch the homographs. *Cheres* spelled the same way is the skin affliction of
  Devarim 28:27, treated in [boils](boils-shechin-garav-cheres.html), and in Mishnaic
  Hebrew *cheres* is an earthenware shard in the ordinary sense. *Yerach*, the
  month, is the same root as *yareach* and is treated in
  [month](month-chodesh-yerach.html); for *lavan*, white, see
  [white](white-lavan-chivar-tzochar.html), and for the place-name, [Jericho](jericho-yereicho-yericho.html).
