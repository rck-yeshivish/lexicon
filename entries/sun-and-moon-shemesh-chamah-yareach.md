# Sun and moon: shemesh / chamah / cheres and yareach / levanah / sahar

## Bottom line

Three words each, and each names its luminary by a different aspect. *Shemesh*
is the **sun** as servant, doing its assigned work; *chamah* the **sun** as the
hot one; *cheres* the **sun** as the baker of pottery, or the pottery-colored one
at sunrise and sunset. *Yareach* is the **moon** as the traveler on its path;
*levanah* the **moon** as the white one; *sahar* the **crescent**, the moon by
its shape. All six are Rabbi Aharon Marcus's account. In ordinary translation all
three suns are "the sun" and all three moons "the moon"; surface the aspect only
where the passage turns on it.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why the sun is called *cheres*.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus: because the sun's heat
bakes pottery, and pottery was a mainstay of ancient civilization, so the sun is
named for what it makes possible. Others: because the sun takes on a reddish,
pottery-like hue at sunrise and at sunset, so it is named for how it looks. The
Bottom line carries both, since the article gives no ground for choosing and each
reading fits a different kind of pasuk.

**Whether *sahar* is Hebrew.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus counts it as the third Hebrew
word for the moon, the one that names it by its shape. Others hold *sahar* is not
really Hebrew but an Aramaic loanword taken up for the moon. Either way it means
the crescent, so the rendering is unaffected; the argument matters only where
somebody builds on its being native.

**Whether the staged history is admissible.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads the six
words as successive coinages, and some would reject that historical frame
outright, since it sits badly with the Divine origin of Lashon HaKodesh: words
of the holy tongue are not invented by early monotheists in response to
circumstance. The article concedes the objection and answers that the basic
argument survives it. Whatever one holds about the history, the six words do
distribute themselves over different aspects of the sun and the moon, and that is
the part a translator needs.

## Distinctions

### The luminaries are unnamed at first
In the account of creation (Bereishis 1) neither *shemesh* nor *yareach* appears.
The Torah calls them only the great luminary and the small luminary, as if to say
that their whole purpose is to give light and that they carry no importance of
their own. On Rabbi Aharon Marcus's reading, that is the point: at first the sun
and the moon had no names at all.

### shemesh (שמש) and yareach (ירח)
The first pair coined, and coined against idolatry. When people began to worship
the sun and the moon under the names Baal, master, and Asherah, the two luminaries
were given names that keep them in their place: *shemesh*, the servant, subject
to Hashem, and *yareach*, from *orach*, a path, the one that runs its appointed
course through the heavens. Neither name grants the thing named any standing of
its own. Both names were in time taken up by the sun-cult and the moon-cult
themselves, which is what forced the coining of the next pair. Two place-names in
Eretz Yisrael preserve the old cults: Beis Shemesh, the house of the sun, and
Yericho, tied to *yareach*.

### chamah (חמה), cheres (חרס), levanah (לבנה) and sahar (סהר)
The second layer, and each of the four names its luminary by an observable
property rather than by a role: heat and pottery for the sun, whiteness and
crescent shape for the moon. In time these too were drawn into the worship of the
heavenly bodies by way of ritual objects. The Torah forbids the *chaman*
(Vayikra 26:30), an idol set up on a roof for the worship of the sun, and
Yeshayahu rebukes the daughters of Tziyon for wearing *saharonim* (Yeshayahu
3:18), moon-shaped amulets on a necklace.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: "the sun" and "the moon" for all six. The aspects are etymological,
  and forcing them into every rendering makes the English strange.
- Trap: English has one word for the sun and one for the moon, so a pasuk that
  pairs two of the Hebrew words will collapse. Yeshayahu 24:23 sets *levanah*
  against *chamah* in one clause, "the white one will be mortified and the hot
  one ashamed"; rendering both "the sun and the moon" loses the pairing outright,
  so name the aspects there. (For the pair of shame-words in that same pasuk see
  `shame-bushah-klimah-cherpah.md`.)
- *Sahar* is "crescent" or "crescent moon", never simply "moon", since shape is
  the whole of what the word contributes.
- Watch the homographs. *Cheres* spelled the same way is the skin affliction of
  Devarim 28:27, treated in `boils-shechin-garav-cheres.md`, and in Mishnaic
  Hebrew *cheres* is an earthenware shard in the ordinary sense. *Yerach*, the
  month, is the same root as *yareach* and is treated in
  `month-chodesh-yerach.md`; for *lavan*, white, see
  `white-lavan-chivar-tzochar.md`, and for the place-name, `jericho-yereicho-yericho.md`.
