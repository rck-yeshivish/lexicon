# Pleasure: eden / oneg / pinuk

## Bottom line

For Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, *eden* (with *ednah* and *maadanim*) is primarily
pleasure of the body: **sensual pleasure, delight of the senses**. In Tanach,
Rabbi Klein observes, it almost always concerns taste and touch; *maadanim* are
**delicacies**. Pappenheim holds that *oneg* is primarily pleasure of the soul,
mind and heart, even when it arrives by way of the body: **delight, enjoyment**.
*Oneg Shabbos* is therefore delight in Shabbos rather than physical pleasure,
though it may come through good food. Rav Hirsch explains *oneg* differently, as
being surrounded by favorable conditions. *Pinuk* is **pampering, indulgence**.
Pappenheim takes it as playfulness and flattery rather than pleasure; Rabbi
Klein observes that this fits its one pasuk but does not account for the
Targumim, which use *pinuk* words to render both *oneg* and *eden*. *Hanaah* is
the commonest word for **pleasure** in rabbinic literature, and its root is
disputed.

## Distinctions

### eden (עדן), ednah (עדנה), maadanim (מעדנים)
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740-1814) holds that *eden* and *oneg* both
name enjoyment, whether or not the enjoyment is ultimately good for a person,
and that *eden* is primarily physical pleasure, that of the five senses: taste,
hearing, smell, sight and touch. Its root is ע-ד-ן, which he traces to ע-ד,
joining or connecting (as in *ad*, *yaad*, *eidah*, *vaad*, *moed*, *eidus*):
physical pleasure forges a connection between the one enjoying and the object
enjoyed. Rabbi Klein adds that a careful look at usage in Tanach shows *eden/ednah*
almost exclusively concerning taste and touch, which fits Pappenheim, since
those are the two senses in which the object comes into direct contact with the
organ. *Maadanim* are the delicacies of a king's table (Tehillim 36:9,
Yirmiyahu 51:34, Eichah 4:5; Bereishis 49:20, Asher's *maadanei melech*).
Sarah's עדנה (Bereishis 18:12): Onkelos renders youthfulness; Radak, the smooth skin
of youth; Ibn Ezra, the pleasures or enjoyment of youth; Rabbi Yehuda Chalava, son
of the thirteenth-century Maharam Chalava, conjugal pleasure specifically. Rabbi
Klein raises the possibility that some of these sources are associating *ednah*
with the Aramaic *idna*, time, and reading youth as a bygone time. *Adin/adinah*
(Yeshayahu 47:8) is the delicate, dainty one, easily overwhelmed by sensation.
(*Adina* is nowhere a proper name in Tanach or in Chazal, though it turns up
twice in the quasi-midrashic *Sefer HaYashar*, as the names of Lavan's wife and
of Levi's.) Pappenheim: *eden* stays "physical" even when the bodily pleasure
also yields spiritual pleasure (a tasty medicine, Shabbos and Yom Tov food,
marital intimacy as a mitzvah); *Gan Eden* thus names both the physical delights
of that paradise and its intellectual bliss.

He works the interplay out in both directions. Some things the body enjoys and
the soul does not: sleep, which restores the body but dulls the mind; drinking
past the point of drunkenness; sin, which may pay in some tangible enjoyment but pains
the soul. Some things the soul enjoys and the body does not: hard physical labor
to build wealth, a needed medicine that tastes foul, mitzvos that bring
spiritual ecstasy but not necessarily bodily comfort. And there the symmetry stops: a
physical pleasure can carry a spiritual one with it, but there is no such thing
as a spiritual pleasure that produces a physical one.

Rabbi Nissim of Marseilles (*Maaseh Nissim* p. 271): *Eden* is related to
*idan*, time, since Gan Eden is the middle world joining this world to the
highest, where the heavenly bodies by which we keep time are found.
Alternatively, *Eden* is this world, from *idun*, pleasure, the place of bodily
delight.

### oneg (עונג)
Root ע-נ-ג.

- Pappenheim: pleasure of the soul, the abstract pleasures that only the soul
  enjoys: understanding, grasping a hard idea, wealth, honor, love, vengeance on
  enemies, peace, a friend's success. *Oneg* remains spiritual even when rooted
  in something physical: *oneg Shabbos* (Yeshayahu 58:13) is delight in Shabbos,
  which may come through good food; the pampered *anug* (Devarim 28:54) is one
  whose emotional well-being needs bodily coddling. And *oneg* also stands
  wholly apart from the body: delighting in peace (Tehillim 37:11), in love
  (Shir HaShirim 7:7), in honor (Yeshayahu 66:11), in Hashem Himself (Tehillim
  37:4, Iyov 22:26), all of them purely abstract pursuits.
- Rav Hirsch (1808-1888) explains *oneg* differently: the state of being
  surrounded by favorable and pleasant conditions, which he compares to *anak*,
  a necklace, an object that surrounds the wearer's neck.
- *Anak* can also mean a grant, as in the master's charge to *haanek taanik* the
  departing Hebrew bondsman (Devarim 15:14); explaining that word, Rabbi Yaakov
  Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785-1865) writes that the kuf of *anak* interchanges with a
  gimmel to give *oneg*, the gift the freed bondsman gets to enjoy.

### pinuk (פינוק)
Root פ-נ-ק. A *hapax legomenon*, once in Tanach: מפנק מנער עבדו (Mishlei 29:21),
warning a master not to be *mefanek* his slave lest the slave come to rule over
him, that is, not to overindulge him or flatter him too much for his efforts,
since he will grow used to the treatment and come to expect it. Pappenheim claims that *pinuk* means playfulness and flattery, and not pleasure at all.
That fits the pasuk's context well, Rabbi Klein observes, but it does not account for the Targumim,
which consistently use *pinuk* words to render *oneg* and *eden*: *anug* becomes
*mefanak/mefunak*, *maadanei melech* becomes *tafnukei malkin*; in *Mah Yedidus*,
*tafnukei maadanim* is the same word twice, Aramaic and Hebrew, "delicacies."
(Rabbi Klein once took the English *finicky* for a derivative of *pinuk*, and
on looking into it found that *finicky* comes from English *fine* instead. Go
figure.)

### hanaah (הנאה)
The commonest word for pleasure in rabbinic literature, in all its
conjugations. Its etymology and literal meaning are debated, and there is no
consensus on its root; some even say the word is Greek.

- Alexander Kohut (1842 to 1894) theorizes that its core meaning is to profit.
  Since it could be confused with *onaah*, in essence profiting by cheating
  someone, which carries a negative sense, the two were given different
  spellings: *hanaah* always with a hei, *onaah* with an alef. Kohut uses this
  to explain why Rabbi Nosson of Rome (1035 to 1106), in *Sefer HeAruch*, seems to
  take the root of *hanaah* as ה-נ-י.
- Ibn Ezra (to Shir HaShirim 1:4, as Rabbi Klein gives the reference) and Radak
  (to Tehillim 33:1) suggest that *naveh* or *neos*, fitting or suitable, is
  from א-ו-ה, desire (as in *taavah* and *ivah*), so that *naveh* is what is
  desirable. Radak suggests alternatively that the root of *naveh* is נ-א-ה,
  beautiful. Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg of the Veromemanu Foundation suggests
  that *hanaah* comes from one of these two etymologies, but then defers to
  Rabbi Mecklenburg.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg puts the root of *hanaah* at ה-נ. *Hein* and
  *hineh*, from that root, do not mean "behold"; they are a transition word
  joining a cause to its effects, like "because" or "since." As a corollary
  they also mean agreement or willingness, which tend to be requisites for a
  cause to bring about an effect. *Hanaah* in the sense of pleasure comes from
  the same root: what is pleasurable is what one considers agreeable, because
  it agrees with one's senses, and whose coming about one even wills. Rabbi
  Dr. Ernest Klein, in *A Comprehensive Etymological Dictionary of the Hebrew
  Language for Readers of English*, takes a similar approach.

## Rendering guidance

- עונג שבת: "delight in Shabbos" or "Shabbos delight"; never "Shabbos pleasures" as
  if it named the food. The food is the vehicle (Pappenheim).
- מעדנים: "delicacies"; מעדני מלך: "royal delicacies."
- עדן / עדנה of a person: "sensual pleasure," "delight of the senses"; Bereishis
  18:12 follows whichever commentator the passage follows (youth, smooth skin,
  conjugal pleasure). גן עדן stays *Gan Eden*.
- פינוק: "pampering," "indulgence"; מפנק as a verb, "to pamper," "to indulge."
  Mishlei 29:21 reads "who pampers his slave from youth."
- Together: תפנוקי מעדנים (*Mah Yedidus*): "delectable delicacies," one doubled idea
  in two languages, not two kinds of pleasure.
- Trap: English "pleasure" absorbs *eden* and *oneg*, and "delight" can do the
  same. When both appear, or when a passage argues body against soul, keep *eden*
  on the side of the senses and *oneg* on the side of the soul.
- Trap: *adin* (Yeshayahu 47:8) is "delicate, dainty," not "pleasant."
