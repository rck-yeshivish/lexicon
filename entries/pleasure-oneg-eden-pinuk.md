# Pleasure: eden / oneg / pinuk

## Bottom line

*Eden* (with *ednah*, *maadanim*) is pleasure of the body, above all of taste and
touch: **sensual pleasure, delight of the senses**; *maadanim*, **delicacies**.
*Oneg* is pleasure of the soul, mind and heart, even when it arrives by way of the
body: **delight, enjoyment**; *oneg Shabbos* is therefore delight in Shabbos taken
through good food, never "physical pleasure." *Pinuk* is for Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim not pleasure at all but playfulness and flattery, though the Targumim
use it for both *oneg* and *eden*: **pampering, indulgence**. The one thing not to
get wrong: the body/soul split between *eden* and *oneg* (Pappenheim) is the whole
distinction; do not let "pleasure" cover both.

## Distinctions

### eden (עדן), ednah (עדנה), maadanim (מעדנים)
Root ע-ד-ן, which Pappenheim traces to ע-ד, joining or connecting (as in *ad*,
*yaad*, *eidah*, *vaad*, *moed*, *eidus*): physical pleasure forges a connection
between the one enjoying and the object enjoyed. Usage in Tanach bears this out;
*eden/ednah* almost always concerns taste and touch, the two senses in which the
object touches the organ. *Maadanim* are the delicacies of a king's table (Tehillim
36:9, Yirmiyahu 51:34, Eichah 4:5; Bereishis 49:20, Asher's *maadanei melech*).
Sarah's עדנה (Bereishis 18:12): Onkelos renders youthfulness; Radak, the smooth skin
of youth; Ibn Ezra, the pleasures of youth; Rabbi Yehuda Chalava, conjugal pleasure
specifically. *Adin/adinah* (Yeshayahu 47:8) is the delicate, dainty one, easily
overwhelmed by sensation. Pappenheim: *eden* stays "physical" even when the bodily
pleasure also yields spiritual pleasure (a tasty medicine, Shabbos food, marital
intimacy as a mitzvah); *Gan Eden* thus names both the physical delights of paradise
and its intellectual bliss. He adds the asymmetry: bodily pleasure can bring
spiritual pleasure, but no spiritual pleasure ever produces a bodily one.

### oneg (עונג)
Root ע-נ-ג. Pappenheim: pleasure of the soul, the abstract pleasures that only the
soul enjoys: understanding, grasping a hard idea, wealth, honor, love, vengeance on
enemies, peace, a friend's success. *Oneg* remains spiritual even when rooted in
something physical: *oneg Shabbos* (Yeshayahu 58:13) is delight in Shabbos, which
may come through good food; the pampered *anug* (Devarim 28:54) is one whose
emotional well-being needs bodily coddling. And *oneg* also stands wholly apart from
the body: delighting in peace (Tehillim 37:11), in love (Shir HaShirim 7:7), in honor
(Yeshayahu 66:11), in Hashem Himself (Tehillim 37:4, Iyov 22:26). Rav Hirsch reads
it otherwise: the state of being surrounded by favorable conditions, as *anak*, a
necklace, surrounds the neck. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: *anak* as "grant"
(Devarim 15:14, *haanakah*) becomes *oneg* by kuf/gimmel interchange, the freed
bondsman's gift to enjoy.

### pinuk (פינוק)
Root פ-נ-ק. Once in Tanach: מפנק מנער עבדו (Mishlei 29:21), a master who
overindulges his slave until the slave comes to rule him. Pappenheim: not pleasure
but playfulness and flattery, which fits the pasuk. Against him stand the Targumim,
which consistently use *pinuk* words for *oneg* and *eden*: *anug* becomes
*mefanak/mefunak*, *maadanei melech* becomes *tafnukei malkin*; in *Mah Yedidus*,
*tafnukei maadanim* is the same word twice, Aramaic and Hebrew, "delicacies."

## Rendering guidance

- עונג שבת: "delight in Shabbos" or "Shabbos delight"; never "Shabbos pleasures" as
  if it named the food. The food is the vehicle (Pappenheim).
- מעדנים: "delicacies"; מעדני מלך: "royal delicacies."
- עדן / עדנה of a person: "sensual pleasure," "delight of the senses"; Bereishis
  18:12 follows whichever commentator the passage follows (youth, smooth skin,
  conjugal pleasure). גן עדן stays *Gan Eden*.
- פינוק: "pampering," "indulgence"; מפנק as a verb, "to pamper," "to indulge."
  Mishlei 29:21 reads "who pampers his slave from youth."
- Together: תפנוקי מעדנים (*Mah Yedidus*): "delectable delicacies," one doubled idea
  in two languages, not two kinds of pleasure.
- Trap: English "pleasure" absorbs *eden* and *oneg*, and "delight" can do the
  same. When both appear, or when a passage argues body against soul, keep *eden*
  on the side of the senses and *oneg* on the side of the soul.
- Trap: *adin* (Yeshayahu 47:8) is "delicate, dainty," not "pleasant."
