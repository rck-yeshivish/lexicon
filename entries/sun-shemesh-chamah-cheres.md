# Sun: shemesh / chamah / cheres

## Bottom line

Biblical Hebrew has three words for the sun, and Rabbi Aharon Marcus explains
that each names it by a different aspect. *Shemesh* is the **sun** as servant,
doing its assigned work; *chamah* is the **sun** as the hot one; *cheres* is the
**sun** as the baker of pottery, or, as others explain, as the pottery-colored
one at sunrise and sunset. Rabbi Marcus adds that the words may also represent
different stages in the history of man's relationship with the sun. The three
words for the moon, *yareach*, *levanah* and *sahar*, somewhat parallel these
three, each naming the moon by a different aspect in the same way.

## At a glance

| חרס | cheres | sun | pottery: it bakes pottery, or it is pottery-colored at dawn and dusk; breaks things (Tashbetz) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why the sun is called *cheres*.**

- Rabbi Aharon Marcus: because the sun's heat bakes pottery, and pottery was a
  mainstay of ancient civilization, so the sun is named for what it makes
  possible.
- Others: because the sun takes on a reddish, pottery-like hue at sunrise and at
  sunset, so it is named for how it looks.

Rabbi Klein gives no ground for choosing between them.

**Whether the staged history is admissible.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus holds that the
words may also represent different stages in the history of man's relationship
with the sun and moon. Rabbi Klein notes that some might reject this historical
aspect of the explanation, because it would seem to contradict the Divine origin
of the Hebrew language, and answers that the basic argument still holds: the
three words for the sun and the three for the moon somewhat parallel each other
in their focus on different aspects of the sun and the moon.

## Distinctions

### The sun is unnamed at first
In the account of creation (Bereishis 1) *shemesh* does not appear. The Torah
calls the sun only the great luminary, as if to say that its whole purpose is to
give light and that it carries no importance of its own. On Rabbi Aharon
Marcus's reading, that is the point: at first the sun had no name at all.

### shemesh (שמש)
The first word coined, and coined against idolatry. When, as the generations
progressed, people began to worship the sun under the name Baal, master, early
monotheists who fought idolatry coined a name that identifies it yet downplays
its importance: *shemesh*, the servant, highlighting its subservience to
Hashem. In time *shemesh* itself came to be used as the name of the idolatrous
sun-god, and archeologists have identified the worship of Shammash in ancient
Canaan and Mesopotamia. This is what necessitated the coining of the next
words. A place-name reflecting the old practice is still in use: Beis Shemesh,
the house of the sun, was apparently an ancient center of the sun cult before
the Jewish People entered Eretz Yisrael.

### chamah (חמה) and cheres (חרס)
The second layer: *chamah* for the sun's scorching heat and *cheres* for its
ability to bake pottery (others: for its reddish, pottery-like hue at sunrise
and sunset). In short, *shemesh* focuses on the sun's utilitarian role in the
celestial system, carrying out Hashem's wishes, *chamah* on its heat, *cheres*
on its connection with pottery. In time these words too, unsurprisingly, came
to be associated with celestial idolatry, as worshippers of the sun used ritual
objects in their cults: the Torah (Vayikra 26:30) refers to the *chaman*, an
idol erected on a roof to worship the sun.

The Tashbetz (*Magen Avos*, Jerusalem 2007, p. 223): *shemesh* serves Hashem,
*chamah* makes things hot, *cheres* breaks things, as earthenware must be
broken, since it can never be fully cleaned.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 18:1 and 38:17): *yom*
sometimes means the sun, and in Sanskrit *jama* means sun.

### The parallel with the moon
Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads the three words for the
[moon](moon-yareach-levanah-sahar.html) the same way, and the two sets
somewhat parallel each other. The moon too is called only the small luminary at
creation. Its first name, [*yareach*](month-chodesh-yerach.html#%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%97), from [*orach*](path-derech-orach.html#%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%97), a path, was coined in the
same fight against idolatry as *shemesh*, and like *shemesh* it was later taken
up as the name of a god, Yarikh. Its later names, *levanah* and *sahar*, name it
by its color and its shape, as *chamah* and *cheres* name the sun by its heat and
its pottery, and they too came to be tied to idolatry, in the *saharonim* of
Yeshayahu 3:18.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: "the sun" for all three. The aspects are etymological, and forcing
  them into every rendering makes the English strange.
- Trap: English has one word for the sun, so a pasuk that uses two of the Hebrew
  words, or sets one of them against a word for the moon, will collapse into
  "the sun" or "the sun and the moon"; where the choice of word matters, name
  the aspect.
- Watch the homographs. *Cheres* spelled the same way is the skin affliction of
  Devarim 28:27, treated in [boils](boils-shechin-garav-cheres.html), and in
  Mishnaic Hebrew *cheres* is an earthenware shard in the ordinary sense.
