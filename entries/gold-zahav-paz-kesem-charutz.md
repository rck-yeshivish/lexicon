# Gold: zahav / paz / kesem / charutz (betzer)

## Bottom line

*Zahav* is the general word: **gold**. *Paz* is the purest and rarest grade,
**fine gold**, and where it stands beside *zahav* in a pasuk the pair is
ascending, so render "gold and fine gold," not two flat "golds." *Kesem* is also
fine gold, named for the malleability that lets it be beaten thin. *Charutz* is
gold as mined, dug out of the ground. *Betzer* is contested and should not be
rendered "gold" without a note: gold, silver, a stronghold, and unrefined ore
are all live readings of Iyov 22:24. The trap: English has one word where Hebrew
has five, and Tehillim 19:11 and 119:127 pair two of them deliberately.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether paz is gold at all.** Most read it as gold, and Radak (to Tehillim
19:11) specifies good, unadulterated gold; Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer calls
it the finest and rarest gold there is. Ibn Ezra (to Tehillim 19:11 and Shir
HaShirim 5:11) reads it as a precious stone. Rabbi Moshe David Valle takes it as
royal jewels that happened to be made of gold. The Bottom line follows the
majority.

**Where paz comes from.** Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the biliteral פ-ז means quick
movement, which is why David dancing before the Aron is *mifazez* (Shmuel II
6:16); *paz* is gold so pure it shimmers in the sun as though dancing, and
*mufaz* (Melachim I 10:18), *meufaz* (Yirmiyahu 10:9), and *ufaz* (Daniel 10:5)
all describe that glitter. Ibn Janach and Radak separate the words: פ-ז-ז is its
own root meaning strength, so *mifazez* is dancing with vigor and *mufaz* and
*ufaz* describe gold unalloyed and therefore stronger. Radak adds a third
possibility, that *meufaz* means "from Ufaz," which Targum identifies with
Ophir. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 49:23-24, and see his comment to Tehillim 19:11)
says plainly that *mifazez* and *paz* must be connected but that he does not know
how.

**What betzer means** (Iyov 22:24, and see 22:25, 36:19). Ibn Janach, Radak, and
Ralbag: gold. Ibn Ezra: silver. Menachem, followed by Rashi: a stronghold, from
the other sense of ב-צ-ר. Rabbi Yishaya of Trani: not gold itself but the crude
ore from which gold is refined, which is the reading that best fits the pasuk's
"on the ground" and "with the rocks of the brooks."

**Whether charutz is gold or a gemstone.** In *yerakrak charutz* (Tehillim
68:14) Menachem Ibn Saruk reads a gemstone; Donash Ibn Labrat and others read
gold, so the phrase means greenish gold (see *Tosafos* to Nedarim 32a). Radak
defines it as gold while recording the gemstone view.

**What kesem means.** Most read gold. Ibn Janach first defines it as jewels and
then concludes it is more likely gold. Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra holds that in Iyov
31:24 specifically it means silver, while agreeing it is gold elsewhere.

**How zahav is derived.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus: from ז-ה, "this," because
something that sparkles calls attention to itself. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: from
ז-ב, "flow," because in refining, the pure gold flows away from its dross. Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: from *zav*, flow, in a different sense, gold found by
flowing water, following Ramban on the good gold of Havilah along the Pishon
(Bereishis 2:11-12), which Ramban locates in the sand and on the river's banks.
A popular reading, cited by *Peirush HaRokeach*, Rabbi Todros Abulafia, Rabbi
Binyamin HaRofeh Anav, the Maharal, and Rabbi Eliezer Papo, parses it as *zeh
hav*, "give this," for gold as legal tender.

## Distinctions

### zahav (זהב)
Over four hundred occurrences, with the Aramaic *dahav* as its counterpart (the
zayin becoming a dalet). Yoma 44b-45a lists seven kinds: plain *zahav*, *zahav
tov*, *zahav Ophir* (Divrei HaYamim I 29:4), *zahav mufaz*, *zahav shachut*
(beaten gold, Melachim I 10:16-17, Divrei HaYamim II 9:15-16), *zahav sagur*
(eight times in Melachim I 6 and 7, Divrei HaYamim II 4 and 9), and *zahav
parvayim* (Divrei HaYamim II 3:6), which is either gold from Parvayim or gold
the color of a cow's blood. *Shemos Rabbah* 35:1 gives a variant list,
substituting *zahav tahor* and *zahav mezukak* for plain *zahav* and *zahav
Ophir*. *Tikkunei Zohar* (*Tikkun* 70) carries a parallel tradition of seven
shades in David HaMelech's hair. Ibn Janach and Radak note that *zahav* is
borrowed for anything clean and pristine (Yirmiyahu 51:7, Zechariah 4:12).

**Ophir** is a person, Havilah's brother, son of Yoktan son of Ever (Bereishis
10:29, Divrei HaYamim I 1:23), and a place from which both *zahav* (Melachim I
9:28, 10:11, 22:49, Divrei HaYamim II 9:10) and *kesem* (Yeshayahu 13:12,
Tehillim 45:10, Iyov 28:16) were brought. Rabbi Pinchas Eliyahu Horowitz (*Sefer
HaBris*) identifies it with Peru; Rabbi Yosef Chaim of Baghdad takes *zahav
Ophir* to be white gold. Other identifications the article surveys come from
outside this guide's sources.

### kesem (כתם)
Nine occurrences. The same root כ-ת-ם gives *ketem*, a stain (Yirmiyahu 2:22,
and the Mishnah's blood stain, Niddah 8:1). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim unites them:
the monoliteral כ means striking, כ-ת means beating by repeated rubbing, and
*kesem* is gold pure enough to be beaten very thin. Such fine gold catches the
eye, so the word was borrowed for anything that stands out against a clean
background, that is, a stain. Modern Hebrew's *katom*, orange, comes from the
same root, and *tapuz*, the fruit, contracts *tapuach zahav*, the golden apple
of Mishlei 25:11.

### charutz (חרוץ)
Six occurrences as gold. Rabbi Aharon Marcus: from ח-ר-צ, cutting or digging
with a sharp instrument, since gold is dug out of the earth; Rabbi Wertheimer
says the same. Rabbi Yishaya of Trani takes it from the word's other sense: gold
is called *charutz* because chasing it makes a man diligent and industrious.

### Color as the distinction
The Mishnah (Yoma 4:4) records that the fire pan for the ketores was of greenish
gold on ordinary days and of reddish gold on Yom Kippur, so Chazal do recognize
gold in distinct hues. A modern proposal maps the words onto those hues; it is
set aside here (see Sources), but the Mishnah's own datum stands, as does Radak
on *yerakrak charutz* as greenish gold.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "gold" absorbs all five. Where two stand in one pasuk, differentiate.
  Tehillim 19:11, "more desirable than gold, than much fine gold"; Tehillim
  119:127, "more than gold and fine gold."
- Do not render *charutz* "gold" in a passage where its other sense, diligent, is
  being played on; the Rid reads the two senses as one word, and a derashah may
  turn on it.
- In Iyov 22:24 leave *betzer* transliterated or render it "ore," and footnote
  the four readings. "Gold" there quietly picks Ibn Janach over Rashi.
- *Kesem* in Niddah and *kesem* in Iyov are the same consonants doing opposite
  work, precious gold and a defiling stain. Never carry one rendering into the
  other; note the shared root only where a source makes the point.
- The compound grades (*zahav sagur*, *zahav shachut*, *zahav parvayim*) are
  best left in Hebrew with a gloss, since English has no vocabulary for them.
