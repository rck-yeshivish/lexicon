# Collateral: mashkon / chaval / avot / rahan / apoteiki

## Bottom line

*Mashkon* is the general **collateral** or **pledge**. *Chaval* is the Biblical
verb for **distraining**, seizing a pledge by force once payment is due. *Avot*
is a **pledge** handed over by agreement. *Rahan* is security **deposited up
front**. *Apoteiki* is property **designated** for collection on default and
never surrendered. *Chaval* is taken, *avot* and *rahan* given.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| משכון | mashkon | collateral, pledge | the general term; the item "dwells" with the creditor (Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur) |
| חבל | chaval | distrain, seize as security | forcible, and after the debt falls due (Rashi; Tedeschi-Ashkenazi) |
| עבוט | avot | pledge, security | given by mutual agreement (Tedeschi-Ashkenazi); binds the borrower like an *eved* (Rav Hirsch) |
| רהן | rahan | deposit given up front | given before the loan and left continuously (Tosafos Yom Tov; Rav Hirsch) |
| אפותיקי | apoteiki | designated property | earmarked for collection, not surrendered (Rashi; Sefer HeAruch) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether chaval "take a pledge" is the same root as chevel "rope."**
- The early lexicographers, Menachem Ibn Saruk, Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak
  among them, derive *chaval* from ח-ב-ל but see the root's other senses, rope,
  ship's captain (*rav-chovel*, the rope-master), region, band of people, birth
  pangs, injury, as distinct from taking a pledge.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives them all from "rope": a rope measures (a
  *chevel* of land, *tachbulah*, measured counsel), and what is measured is
  partial (*chavlei*, localized pains; *chavalah*, partial damage; a *chevel* of
  people, a sub-group only partly reflecting the larger body), so *chaval* is an
  act of injuring the debtor by a measured incursion into his property to settle
  a debt.
- Tedeschi-Ashkenazi sets *chaval* against *avot* as forcible against agreed,
  and Rabbi Klein brings in support Rashi (to Shemos 22:25), for whom *chaval*
  is taking a pledge once payment has fallen due. Tedeschi-Ashkenazi has the
  creditor pulling the debtor's property into his own domain as by a rope, and
  also considers that *chaval* may be related to *chov*, debt, the two standing
  side by side in Yechezkel 18:7.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 22:25) offers two ways: the item is tethered to the
  lender, in that the borrower may no longer sell or damage it, or else the
  collateral, as a tangible token of the borrower's responsibility, ties the
  borrower to his debt as a rope ties a thing down.

**What apoteiki is.** Rashi (Bava Kamma 11b, Bava Basra 44b) and *Sefer HeAruch*
read it as a portmanteau of the Aramaic *a'po te'hei ka'i*, "upon here shall you
be," that is, a declaration to the creditor that he is to collect his debt from
here, from this particular item.
Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur in *Meturgeman* cites that tradition as well but notes
that the word is in fact Greek. Rabbi Dr. Daniel Sperber, in his *Dictionary of
Greek and Latin Legal Terms in Rabbinic Literature*, likewise identifies it as
the Greek ὑποθήκη (*hypotheke*), literally to put
down or to pledge, built on the *hypo-* prefix, "down," familiar from
*hypothesis*, *hypothetical* and the rest; the Latin *hypotheca* gives the
English *hypothec*, which Merriam-Webster defines as an obligation, right or
security given by contract or by operation of law to a creditor over the
debtor's property without transfer of possession or title.

## Distinctions

### mashkon (משכון)
Not in Tanach; Mishnaic (Sheviis 10:2, Bava Metzia 6:7, Shevuos 6:7). Rabbi
Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*): the root is ש-כ-נ, "dwell, settle," with a
prefixed mem, since the collateral dwells in the creditor's house until
redeemed; the mem was then felt as a root letter, giving *lemashken*, to take or
designate a collateral (Peah 8:8,
Kesubos 11:3, Arachin 5:6, among a dozen other Mishnayos). Modern Hebrew
*mashkanta*, a mortgage, descends from it. (The English
*mortgage* has a morbid history of its own, from the Old French
*mort*, dead, as in *mortal* and *mortuary*, plus *gage*, a pledge or security.)

### chaval (חבל)
Root ח-ב-ל. The Torah's word in several of its restrictions on a creditor's
taking: a
blanket taken as pledge must be returned nightly (Shemos 22:25); a millstone or
other food-preparing utensil may not be taken (Devarim 24:6), nor a widow's
garment (24:17). Rashi (to Shemos 22:25): taking a pledge to pay off a debt once
payment has fallen due, after the fact, as against taking one at the time of the
loan itself. Verbs of *chaval* in this sense come fourteen times in Tanach, on
Even-Shoshan's concordance.

### avot (עבוט)
Root ע-ב-ט. The noun occurs four times, all in Ki Seitzei (Devarim 24:10-13),
the verb five times, all in Devarim (15:6, 15:8, 24:10).

- Rashi (Bava Kamma 115a) glosses it *mashkon*.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 22:25, Devarim 15:6, Tehillim 2:3) and
  Tedeschi-Ashkenazi both tie *avot* to *eved*, tes and dalet interchanging: an
  *eved* is tied down to his master's whims and stripped of his freedom, and so
  the borrower is bound to his lender, whom the pasuk itself calls his master
  (Mishlei 22:7); an *avot*, likewise, is an item detained by the creditor, its
  owner no longer free to use it.
- Rav Hirsch further connects *avot* to its homonym *avos* with a final tav, a
  thick rope used to tie things down.

Two other words seem to come from ע-ב-ט with quite different meanings: a verb in
Yoel 2:7 meaning to stop, and the noun *avtit* in Chavakuk 2:6, possibly thick
mud. The Mishnaic *avit* (*Bava Metzia* 5:7, *Keilim* 23:2, *Taharos* 10:4-5) is
a container for produce.

### rahan (רהן)
The Mishnah (Eduyos 8:2) uses it of a Jewish girl handed to non-Jewish creditors
as security until her family could pay, the question there being whether she may
afterward marry a Kohen.

- Tosafos Yom Tov (Rabbi Gershon Shaul Yom Tov Lipman Heller): a *mashkon* is
  given for a debt already owed, *rahan* is what a would-be borrower places with
  a would-be lender so that he will trust him enough to lend, which he finds
  implicit in the Rambam (*Hilchos Maaser Sheini* 3:18).
- Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 37:35) links it to *raanan*, "fresh": as freshness is
  continuous moisture, so *rahan* stays with the creditor until the debt is paid
  in full.

### apoteiki (אפותיקי)
Mishnaic (Gittin 4:4), also *apoteka'i* or *upoteke*. A specific item the
borrower sets aside, from which the creditor may collect on default (Rashi to
Gittin 40b and Kesubos 54a). The asset stays with the debtor; only the right to
it is pledged.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: משכון = "collateral" (or "pledge" in a Mishnaic register), חבל =
  "distrain," עבוט = "pledge," רהן = "deposit," אפותיקי = "designated property."
- Trap: English "pledge" will absorb *mashkon*, *avot*, and *rahan*, and "take a
  pledge" will absorb *chaval* too. Two tests keep them apart: did the creditor
  take it or did the debtor hand it over (*chaval* against *avot*), and was it
  before the loan or after default (*rahan* against *mashkon*)?
- When *chaval* and *avot* meet in one sugya (Shemos 22:25 against Devarim
  24:10-13), render *chaval* with a verb of seizing and *avot* with a noun of
  giving; the contrast is the halachah. And do not render *apoteiki*
  "collateral" unqualified: the creditor never holds it.
