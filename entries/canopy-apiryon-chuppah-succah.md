# Canopy: apiryon / chuppah / succah

## Bottom line

*Succah* is a **booth**, named for its *sechach*, and Rabbi Tzvi Mordechai
Libber explains that the word carries the sense of shelter, a covering that
protects from what is above. *Chuppah*, from *chofeh*, to cover, is a
**canopy**; on his reading it says only that the thing is covered on top, with
nothing about protection. Rabbi Pheobus Meir Parenzo and the Maharsha gloss
*apiryon* as a *chuppah*, but Rabbi Klein, whose working premise is that no two
words in Hebrew mean exactly the same thing, takes an *apiryon* to be more than
a canopy: the Mishnah suggests a **mobile enclosure** a woman travels inside,
and on the reading of those who tie the word to the Sanskrit *paryanka* it is a
**palanquin**, a covered litter carried on poles. On that reading, their gloss
states the *apiryon*'s halachic function at Amram and Yocheved's remarriage and
does not equate the two words; Rashi (to *Yevamos* 110a) likewise makes the
*apiryon* a sedan, tied to a *chuppah* only in the halachic sense. Two other
readings stand apart: Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives *apiryon* from *pri*, a
bed on which children are brought forth, and some cited in *Machberes Menachem*
make it a crown.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What an apiryon is.**

- A *chuppah* (Parenzo, the Maharsha).
- A mobile enclosure or palanquin, the *chuppah* gloss stating its halachic role
  and not its meaning (Rabbi Klein); so Rashi (to *Yevamos* 110a), who explains
  *bei kursia* as an *apiryon*, a sedan.
- A bed on which children are brought forth, from *pri* (Rabbi Shlomo
  Pappenheim, *Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. פר).
- A crown, as some explain (*Machberes Menachem*).

## Distinctions

### succah (סוכה)
Rashi (to *Succah* 2a): what defines a *succah* is its *sechach*, the covering
above; a *succah* is a booth whose main shade comes from what is overhead. Rabbi Tzvi
Mordechai Libber of Milwaukee draws the distinction from a second Rashi (to
Shemos 35:12), where *sechach* is a protective covering: *succah* names a
structure whose main purpose is to shield a man from what is above him.

### chuppah (חופה)
From the verb *chofeh* or *mechapeh*, to cover, used both physically (a cloth
covers a table) and figuratively (covering for a thief with an alibi). On Rabbi
Libber's reading, *chuppah* records only that the thing is covered on top and
carries no implication of shelter. A *succah* and a *chuppah* may be the same
sort of structure; the words look at it differently, the one at its protection,
the other at its being covered.

### apiryon (אפריון)
A *hapax* in Tanach: *apiryon asah lo hamelech Shlomo me'atzei haLevanon*, with
its pillars of silver, its covering of gold, its seat of purple, and its inside
inlaid with love (Shir HaShirim 3:9 to 11), in a passage that closes on the day
of his wedding. The Gemara (*Sotah* 12a; *Bava Basra* 120a) says of Amram
remarrying Yocheved that he seated her in an *apiryon*. Rabbi Pheobus Meir
Parenzo (1500 to 1575, in his glosses in the 1566 Venice *Yalkut Shimoni*,
Shemos §165) and the Maharsha both gloss *apiryon* as a *chuppah*.

Against reading them as simple synonyms stands the Mishnah (*Sotah* 9:14): in
the Hadrianic persecutions of about the second century the Chachamim decreed that a
kallah not travel through
the city inside an *apiryon*, and later rescinded that ruling for reasons of
tznius. This passage, Rabbi Klein says, suggests that an *apiryon* is more than a canopy: a mobile enclosure a woman may travel in.

Some argue that *apiryon* is related to the Sanskrit *paryanka*, English
palanquin or sedan chair: a covered litter for one passenger, a large box on two
horizontal poles borne by four or six men, used by important persons in India
and other Eastern cultures. On that reading the kallah of the Mishnah led her
procession from inside such a celebratory box; the Chachamim first outlawed it
because the excessive joy of her parade was out of place during the persecutions,
and later lifted the prohibition for reasons of modesty. And on that reading,
when Parenzo and the Maharsha wrote that an *apiryon* is a *chuppah* they did
not mean that the words mean one thing, but that in the context of Yocheved's
marriage to Amram the sedan chair she was seated in took on the halachic role a
*chuppah* has at a wedding.

Rashi (to *Yevamos* 110a) explains *bei kursia*, the chair, as an *apiryon*, a
sedan, and ties it to *chuppah* only in the halachic sense, a marriage effected
by the bride sitting in her husband's carriage, and not as meaning that an
*apiryon* is a canopy.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. פר): the root of *apiryon* is
*pri*, a bed on which one brings forth fruit, children.

*Machberes Menachem* brings that some explain an *apiryon* as a crown.

Rashi (to Niddah 20b) ties the name of Ifra Hurmiz to *apiryon*: *ifra*, he
says, is Greek for grace, as in *apiryon nimtayei leRabbi Shimon* (Bava Metzia
119a).

## Rendering guidance

- Default renderings: *succah* stays transliterated, glossed "booth" once;
  *chuppah* stays transliterated, glossed "wedding canopy" once; *apiryon*
  "canopied litter" or "palanquin" if you follow the *paryanka* reading, which
  also suits Shir HaShirim 3:9, where the pasuk goes on to list its seat.
- Trap: "canopy" will take *chuppah* and *apiryon* both, and "covering" will
  take *chuppah* and *sechach* both. Where a source glosses one by the other
  (*apiryon zu chuppah*), render the gloss as a statement of function: "an
  *apiryon*, which served as a *chuppah*."
- *Sechach* is never "roof." It is the covering, and Rashi's protective sense is
  the point wherever the halachos of Succos are being explained.
- Where *chofeh* is figurative (covering for another's wrongdoing), do not carry
  the canopy image into the English; "shield" or "cover for" is right, "canopy"
  is not.
