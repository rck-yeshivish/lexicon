# Teaching: hora'ah / limmud / alef (and hadrachah)

## Bottom line

*Hora'ah* is **instruction** given once and expected to stick; in Mishnaic
Hebrew it narrows to **halachic ruling**. *Limmud* is **teaching by drilling**,
repeating until the lesson is engraved and second nature. *Alef* is teaching
that happens **incidentally**, by example or by inference, without anyone
setting out to teach. *Hadrachah* is not teaching at all but **guidance** in
applying what one already knows. The one thing not to get wrong: *hora'ah* and
*limmud* are stages, not synonyms, and Shadal reads a whole tragedy in Tanach
off the difference.

## Distinctions

### hora'ah (הוראה)
Ibn Janach and Radak: from the root yud-reish-hey, which also yields throwing
(*yarah vayam*, Shemos 15:4), teaching (*yoreh dei'ah*, Yeshayahu 28:9), and
rain (*yoreh* and *moreh*, Devarim 11:14; Yoel 2:23). Menachem Ibn Saruk traces
all of these to a one-letter reish element and adds a fourth sense, spittle or
discharge (*rir*, *rar*: Vayikra 15:3; Shmuel I 21:14; Iyov 6:6). None of the
grammarians ties the senses together thematically; the column itself proposes
movement in one direction as the common thread, throwing away from oneself, rain
and spittle downward, and teaching from teacher to pupil and not back.

In Mishnaic Hebrew *hora'ah* is specifically the authority to rule in halachah
(*Avos* 4:7 and 6:6; *Horayos* 1:1 and 1:4; *Parah* 7:9), and masseches
*Horayos* treats the consequences of a mistaken ruling. Modern Hebrew keeps the
neutral sense of teaching or instructing.

Radak: *Torah* itself (and Aramaic *Orayta*) is from yud-reish-hey, so *Torah*
is best rendered "a teaching" or "instruction." Radak also weighs Ibn Janach's
alternative, the root tav-vav-reish, investigation or scouting, from which come
system and order, and the word *tor*, one's turn within a fixed arrangement; on
that reading *Torah* is law set out as an ordered code. Rav Hirsch (Bereishis
26:5; Devarim 4:1) takes a third root, hey-reish-hey, to impregnate: Torah is
the seed of goodness planted in a person.

### limmud (למוד)
Malbim, in the name of Shadal: *limmud* is going over the lesson again and again
until it is engraved on the pupil's heart and he truly knows it, where *hora'ah*
is teaching a thing once and trusting it will be retained. Shadal reads Melachim
II 12:3 accordingly: Yoash did right in Hashem's eyes all the days that Yehoyada
taught him (*horahu*), and no longer after Yehoyada died. The wording is a
criticism of the method. Yehoyada used *hora'ah* and not *limmud*, never
drilling the essential lessons into the boy-king, so once he was gone there was
nothing engraved to hold.

Rav Hirsch makes the parallel point from Moshe's own usage: in Devarim, Moshe
describes his teaching of Klal Yisrael with cognates of *limmud*, not with
*Torah*, because he did not stop at planting seeds but saw the work through.
Both Shadal and Rav Hirsch treat *hora'ah* as one step in the process and
*limmud* as its completion. The early grammarians note that the *malmad
habakar*, the ox-goad of Shoftim 3:31, is from the same root: training an animal
by repetition.

### alef (אלף)
Iyov 33:33 and 35:11; Mishlei 22:25. Malbim: this is teaching done
inadvertently, where a person or thing serves as a prototype from which a lesson
is drawn, though no one set out to teach it. In Rabbinic Hebrew, deriving such a
lesson is *yalfusa* (verb *yalif*), usually inferring from a pasuk or a
derashah; Modern Hebrew *ulpan* comes from the same root.

Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) gives the root as aleph-lamed-peh,
with the aleph sometimes dropping so that lamed-peh serves as well; on that
basis he and Radak connect *talpiyos* (Shir HaShirim 4:4, its only occurrence)
to this root. Chazal read *talpiyos* as *tel* and *piyos*, the mound to which
all mouths turn in prayer, that is, the Beis HaMikdash (Berachos 30a). Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim traces *alef* to the two-letter element lamed-peh, a strong
hold hard to break free of: hence *yalefes*, a boil leaving a permanent scar
(Vayikra 21:20); *aluf*, a chieftain ruling from strength; *elef*, a thousand,
the following a warlord needs to claim the title; the lamed-peh-tav verbs of
seizing (Shoftim 16:29; Rus 3:8); and *lefes*, the turnip, fixed firmly in the
ground. On this account the learning called *alef* is the learner's firm grasp
of material won by repeated instruction, and *talpiyos* is a tower where young
soldiers are trained in arms. Malbim reads *aluf* the other way, as a leader who
must teach his followers how to act.

*Alef* also names the ox (Devarim 7:13, 28:4, 28:12, 28:51; Tehillim 8:8, 50:10;
Mishlei 14:4; Yeshayahu 30:24). Malbim: a *trained* ox, one that has learned to
work the land. The Vilna Gaon reads *alafim* in Mishlei 14:4 as students, so
that "for want of oxen the trough is empty" warns the teacher that without
students he will become an empty vessel, in the spirit of Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's
"from my students I learned most of all" (Taanis 7a; Makkos 10a).

### hadrachah (הדרכה)
Malbim's contrast term. *Hora'ah*, *limmud*, and *alef* all impart new
knowledge; the *madrich* gives *hadrachah*, laying out a *derech*, a path, for
applying what the student already knows. He supplies direction, not information.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "teach" will absorb *hora'ah*, *limmud*, and *alef*. Where a
  passage sets two of them against each other, use "instruct" for *hora'ah*,
  "drill" or "teach thoroughly" for *limmud*, and "train" or "school" for
  *alef*.
- *Hora'ah* in a Mishnaic or halachic context is "ruling," not "teaching."
  *Bar hora'ah* is one fit to rule, not merely a teacher.
- Where a mussar passage cites Melachim II 12:3, keep the verb visible: "as
  Yehoyada instructed him," so that the contrast with drilling survives in
  English.
- *Hadrachah* is "guidance," never "instruction," which would pull it back into
  *hora'ah*.
- *Alef* as a noun in a pasuk is an ox; as a verb it is to train or teach. In an
  unpointed text let the context decide, and note that the Vilna Gaon's derashah
  on Mishlei 14:4 turns on holding both senses at once.
