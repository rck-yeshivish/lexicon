# Shame: bushah / klimah / cherpah (chafrah)

## Bottom line

*Bushah* is the general, milder word: **shame**. *Klimah* is the graver,
inherently disgraceful kind: **humiliation** (or **disgrace**). *Cherpah* is
shame inflicted by another's insult or reproach: **disgrace, reproach**; its
metathesised twin *chafrah* is the mortified wish to dig a hole and hide:
**mortification**. When two appear together the Torah writes the milder first
(Radak), so a doubled pair keeps the ascending order in English: "shame and
humiliation," never the reverse.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| בושה | bushah | shame | mildest; written first in a pair (Radak); can be social only, without contrition (Edel) |
| כלימה | kelimah | humiliation | graver; inflicted from outside (Malbim); the deed itself is disgraceful (Edel) |
| חרפה | cherpah | disgrace, reproach | follows another's insult (Wertheimer); disparagement rather than shame (Bedersi) |
| חפרה | chafrah | mortification | wanting to dig a hole and hide; most intense (Malbim) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why bushah always precedes klimah in a pasuk.** Two readings, and the choice
changes nothing in translation but everything in a derashah.

1. **Intensity.** Radak (to Yeshayahu 45:16 and *Sefer HaShorashim*), Rabbeinu
   Yonah (*Shaarei Teshuvah*), and Rabbi Avraham Bedersi (*Chotam Tochnit*):
   the milder word comes first, so *bushah* is the lighter shame. Bedersi makes
   this a general rule for every set of synonyms in Tanach.
2. **Sequence, not intensity.** Rabbi Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter: *bushah*
   is the shaming act that causes it, *klimah* the shame that results, so the
   order is cause then effect. He explicitly rejects the intensity reading, and
   rejects with it the blush-then-pale account of a Maskilic writer that the
   column records and dismisses.

**Which word is the most intense.** Bedersi says *klimah*. Malbim says
*chafrah*, one who would bury his head. They are not reconcilable; follow
Bedersi for the common *bushah*/*klimah* pair, and Malbim where *chafrah*
appears.

**Whether cherpah belongs to this cluster at all.** Bedersi treats it as
disparagement, a word about what others say, not about what the ashamed man
feels. Wertheimer and Malbim keep it in, as shame received from outside.

## Distinctions

### bushah (בושה)
Root ב-ו-ש. The hypernym. Radak (to Yeshayahu 45:16 and in *Sefer HaShorashim*)
notes that wherever *bushah* stands beside *klimah* it comes first, because it is
the less intense form; Rabbeinu Yonah (*Shaarei Teshuvah*) says the same. Radak
adds that *bushah* covers shame for a good reason as well as a bad one (a humble
man praised for a good deed), while *klimah* is only for something negative.
Rabbi Avraham Bedersi (*Chotam Tochnit*): *bushah* is the shame of an
expectation that failed. Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel: *bushah* can be merely social,
the thief ashamed at being seen (Yirmiyahu 2:26), with no moral contrition in
it. Malbim: *bushah* is internal shame. Targum renders it *bahat*.

### klimah (כלימה)
Root כ-ל-ם. The most intense of the three for Bedersi: shame for having done what
one ought not, or for being called out for it. Edel: *klimah* and *cherpah* name
the inherently shameful aspect of the deed itself, so a sinner who uses them
owns the wrong, not just the exposure. Malbim and Rabbi Shlomo Aharon
Wertheimer: *klimah* is shame that comes from outside, from another person
humiliating him. Rav Hirsch (to Vayikra 13:59, Tehillim 139:16) links it to
*golem*: the humiliated man feels stripped of his shape, part of his humanity
taken. Targum renders it *kissuf*; hence the Aramaic *ichsaf* and the kabbalistic
*nehama d'kisufa*, "the bread of shame."

### cherpah (חרפה) and chafrah (חפרה)
Root ח-ר-ף / ח-פ-ר. Wertheimer: *cherpah* is the shame felt after being
belittled or insulted by another; *chafrah* is wanting to dig (*chofer*) a hole
and crawl in. Malbim: *chafrah* is the most intense, burying one's head out of
sheer shame; he also separates internal *charfah* from external *cherpah*.
Bedersi reads *cherpah* as closer to disparagement than to shame (see the
column's separate piece on *cherpah* and disparagement). Targum's rendering
*chisda* led the Maggid of Kozhnitz to read *chassid* as one ashamed to think
he could achieve anything without Hashem.

Minority readings of the *bushah*/*klimah* order treat it as sequence rather
than intensity: Rabbi Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter (cause, then the shame it
produces). The column records and rejects a Maskilic blush-then-pale reading.

## Rendering guidance

- Doubled pair בושה וכלימה: "shame and humiliation." Keep the order; the Hebrew
  is climactic.
- Standalone בושה in a mussar context: "shame." Do not reach for "embarrassment"
  in a sefer; it reads light and modern.
- כלימה alone: "humiliation" when the source is another person, "disgrace" when
  the deed itself is the disgrace.
- חרפה: "disgrace" or "reproach" (KJV-style "reproach" fits a pasuk); חפרה:
  "mortified" as a verb, "mortification" as a noun.
- Trap: English "shame" can cover all three. If the passage turns on the
  difference (a list, an ascending pair, a derashah on the order), do not let
  one English word absorb two Hebrew ones.
