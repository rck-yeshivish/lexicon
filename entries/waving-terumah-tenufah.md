# Waving: terumah / tenufah

## Bottom line

Both are the ritual waving of a korban. By Rashi's definition, which is the
working one, *tenufah* is **waving** back and forth on the horizontal axis and
*terumah* is **raising and lowering**, the vertical. Outside the korban,
*terumah* is the **separated portion**: the donation to the Mishkan and the
tithe given to a Kohen, where Rashi renders it "separation," not "lifting."
The trap: English "elevation offering" or "wave offering" will collapse the
pair, and in the several pesukim that name both for the same limb the reader
must be able to see two distinct acts.

## Distinctions

### Which korban takes which
*Tenufah* is stipulated for the breast of a shelamim given to a Kohen (Vayikra
7:30, 7:34, 10:14-15; Bamidbar 6:20), the Omer (Vayikra 23:11-12), the shtei
halechem and their lambs (Vayikra 23:20), the ram of the miluim with its
menachos (Shemos 29:24, Vayikra 8:27) and its breast given to Aharon (Shemos
29:26-27, Vayikra 8:29), the Leviim at their inauguration (Bamidbar 8:11, 8:13,
8:15, 8:21), the asham and oil of the metzora (Vayikra 14:12, 14:24), the
minchah of the sotah (Bamidbar 5:25), the cooked shank of the nazir's ram
(Bamidbar 6:20), and possibly bikkurim (Mishnah *Menachos* 5:6). *Terumah* is
stipulated for the right thigh of the shelamim (Vayikra 7:32, 7:34, 10:14-15;
Bamidbar 6:20) and the thigh of the miluim ram (Shemos 29:27). Several pesukim
require both of breast and thigh together (Shemos 29:27, Vayikra 9:21, 10:15),
and *Toras Kohanim* (*Tzav, parshasa* 11) derives from the mixing that whatever
needs *tenufah* needs *terumah* and the reverse.

### The two axes
Mishnah *Menachos* 5:6 implies that one of the two is vertical and the other
horizontal. Rashi (to Shemos 29:26-27, Vayikra 7:34, 10:15): *terumah* is up
and down, *tenufah* is back and forth; Ibn Ezra (long commentary to Shemos
29:27) reads the same way. Rashi (to Shemos 29:24, following Menachos 62a)
adds the meaning: the horizontal *tenufah* toward the four directions declares
that all four are Hashem's, and the vertical *terumah* that heaven and earth are
His; *tenufah* wards off harmful winds that travel horizontally, *terumah*
harmful dews that come down from above. *Peirush HaRokeach* and Baal HaTurim
(to Vayikra 7:30) find the hint in the count: in the shelamim parashah (Vayikra
7:28-38) forms of *tenufah* appear four times (three, with an extra hei counted
as a fourth) and forms of *terumah* twice, matching four lateral directions
against two vertical ones.

Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam (to Shemos 29:24) holds that *tenufah* itself
means both back and forth and up and down, evidently because in practice
whatever requires the one requires the other; that settles the practice but not
the words.

### Why breast and thigh are named as they are
Rashi (to Vayikra 10:15) says outright that he does not know why the breast is
tied to *tenufah* and the thigh to *terumah* when both limbs undergo both.
*Peirush HaRokeach* (to Shemos 29:27, Vayikra 7:34) answers from the shape: the
breast is a broad flank, fitting the widthwise *tenufah*, while the thigh
stands upright, fitting the up-and-down *terumah*; see also Ramban to Vayikra
10:15. Rabbi Yosef Bechor Shor (to Shemos 29:24) answers that the two are
simply synonyms and the Torah alternates them to avoid repeating itself, with
no significance in which limb gets which.

Targum Onkelos complicates the picture. To Shemos 29:24 it renders *tenufah*
with an Aramaic cognate of *terumah*, that is, as lifting rather than
horizontal waving. Rabbi Yaakov Zev Lev (*Me'at Tzari* to Shemos 29:24) reads
this as Onkelos disagreeing with Rashi and defining *tenufah* as up and down;
and since Onkelos renders *terumah* of the miluim thigh (Shemos 29:27) as
"separating" rather than lifting (*Me'at Tzari* there), he is not simply
following Bechor Shor's synonymy either.

### terumah as separation
Shemos 25:2, the donations for the Mishkan: Rashi glosses *terumah* as
"separation." Rabbi Yehuda Kalats (*Miseach Ilmim* there) explains why it must
be so: no physical lifting is involved in giving a donation, and the *trei
mi'meah* reading cannot apply because no fixed amount was prescribed for the
Mishkan; so *terumah* here is simply setting a portion of one's wealth aside
for the sacred. (*Tenufah* likewise stands for a donation at Shemos 35:22.) As
the Kohen's tithe, Mishnah *Terumos* 4:3 sets one fortieth for the generous, one
fiftieth for the average, one sixtieth for the stingy, and the Zohar (*Korach*
179a), the Rambam (commentary to that Mishnah) and *Sefer HaChinuch* (mitzvah
507) parse the word itself as *trei mi'meah*, two per hundred, that is one
fiftieth. At Vayikra 23:17 Rashi glosses *Lechem Tenufah* as *Lechem Terumah*,
reading it as the loaves' raised sanctity rather than a physical wave.

### The roots
In both words the initial tav is not part of the root: ר-ו-ם and נ-ו-פ. Ibn
Janach (introduction to *Sefer HaRikmah*) and the Rambam (commentary to Mishnah
*Terumos* 1:1) note that in rabbinic Hebrew the tav of *terumah* is treated as
radical, hence *torem*.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim on ר-ו-ם, core meaning height and esteem: *ram*,
*marom*, *re'em*, *ramah* (an arcing throw upward), *mirmah* (a crooked rather
than straight way of acting), *rimah* (the worm that seems to hover above the
ground) and *rimon*. *Terumah* is lifting one item up out of the rest of one's
possessions for a higher purpose.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim on נ-ו-פ, core meaning upward movement: *l'hanif* (to
lift, especially a sickle), *l'nofef* (to wave), *nafah* (a sifter), *nof* (the
top branch), *anaf* (a form of anger), *anafah* (a bird), *niuf*
(licentiousness). His resolution of the two: *terumah* is specifically the
up-down axis, while *tenufah* is the broader term covering both axes, and the
proof is the *nafah*, a sifter worked back and forth so that the fine flour
falls down, which is both motions at once. *Meshech Chochmah* (to Shemos 29:27)
and *HaKtav VeHaKabbalah* (to Devarim 23:26) make much the same point. He
explains *anaf* as the anger that sets the body shaking, a *tenufah*-like
motion, *anafah* as the bird easily provoked or whose flight resembles a wave,
and *niuf* as motions of the same kind. This *anaf* is spelled with an alef;
Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 48:3) connects *tenufah* rather to *anaf* with an ayin,
a branch, which spreads sideways from the trunk and not upward, matching
back-and-forth motion that is not up and down.

*Nofes*, flowing honey (Tehillim 19:11, *nofes tzufim*), belongs to נ-ו-פ.
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim ties it to *nof*, since the nectar sits at the top of
the branch, or to *tenufah*, since honey was placed on coals and its scent
waved about a room (see Rashi to Mishlei 7:17). Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of
Carpentras (*Aholei Yehuda*) reads *nofes* as figuratively lifted above other
foods for its esteem, and reads *ramos* (Iyov 28:18), from *terumah*'s root, the
same way, a gem so rare it is figuratively raised above the rest. *Ramos* as
topographical heights gives Ramos Gilad and the modern place-names built on it.
Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg reads the name Remalyahu as ר-ם plus the lamed plus
three letters of the Shem; others take it from an otherwise unattested ר-מ-ל.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: *tenufah* "waving," *terumah* "raising" (or "the raised portion"),
  and say once in a note that Rashi assigns the horizontal to the first and the
  vertical to the second.
- Trap: "elevation offering" and "wave offering" each absorb both words, and a
  pasuk like Shemos 29:27 or Vayikra 10:15, which names both acts for the same
  limbs, becomes a tautology in English. Give two verbs there: "waved ... and
  raised."
- *Terumah* the tithe stays transliterated and is never "wave offering."
  *Terumah* the Mishkan donation is "the portion set aside" or simply
  "donation," following Rashi's "separation" at Shemos 25:2; do not import
  lifting into it.
- Where a passage cites Bechor Shor's synonymy, do not translate the two words
  differently in that quotation, or his point disappears. Where it cites Rashi
  or the Rokeach, do.
- *Lechem Tenufah* (Vayikra 23:17) follows Rashi as "bread of terumah," the
  loaves raised in sanctity, not "waved bread," unless the passage is about the
  physical act.
