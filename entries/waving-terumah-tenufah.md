# Waving: terumah / tenufah

## Bottom line

Both are used of the ritual waving of a korban, and whether they name two acts
is the question in dispute. Rashi (and, it seems, Ibn Ezra) makes *tenufah*
**waving** back and forth on the horizontal axis and *terumah* **raising and
lowering**, the vertical; this entry takes that split as its default. Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim makes *terumah* the up-and-down alone and *tenufah* the
broader term covering both axes; Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam has *tenufah*
itself cover both; Rabbi Yosef Bechor Shor holds the two synonyms, alternated
for elegance; and Targum Onkelos, as Rabbi Yaakov Zev Lev reads him, makes
*tenufah* up and down just like *terumah*. Outside the korban, *terumah* is the
**separated portion**: of the Mishkan donation Rashi (to Shemos 25:2) renders it
"separation," not "lifting," and as the Kohen's tithe the word is parsed *trei
mi'meah*, two per hundred. The trap: English "elevation offering" or "wave
offering" will collapse the pair, and on Rashi's reading the pesukim that name
both for the same limb describe two distinct acts.

## Distinctions

### Which korban takes which
*Tenufah* is stipulated for the breast of a shelamim given to a Kohen (Vayikra
7:30, 7:34, 10:14-15; Bamidbar 6:20), the Omer (Vayikra 23:11-12), the shtei
halechem and their lambs (Vayikra 23:20), the ram of the miluim with its
menachos (Shemos 29:24, Vayikra 8:27) and its breast given to Aharon (Shemos
29:26-27, Vayikra 8:29), the Leviim at their inauguration (Bamidbar 8:11, 8:13,
8:15, 8:21), the asham and oil of the metzora (Vayikra 14:12, 14:24), the
minchah of the sotah (Bamidbar 5:25), the cooked shank of the nazir's ram
(Bamidbar 6:20), and possibly bikkurim (Mishnah *Menachos* 5:6). *Terumah* is
stipulated for the right thigh of the shelamim (Vayikra 7:32, 7:34, 10:14-15;
Bamidbar 6:20) and the thigh of the miluim ram (Shemos 29:27). Several pesukim
require both acts of breast and thigh together (Shemos 29:27, Vayikra 9:21,
10:15), and *Toras Kohanim* (*Tzav, parshasa* 11) derives from the mixing that
whatever needs *tenufah* needs *terumah* and the reverse.

### The two axes
Mishnah *Menachos* 5:6 implies that one of the two is vertical and the other
horizontal. Rashi (to Shemos 29:26-27, Vayikra 7:34, 10:15): *terumah* is up
and down, *tenufah* is back and forth; Ibn Ezra (long commentary to Shemos
29:27) also seems to agree. Rashi (to Shemos 29:24, following Menachos 62a)
adds the meaning: the horizontal *tenufah* toward the four directions alludes
to all four being Hashem's, and the vertical *terumah* to His sovereignty over
heaven and earth; *tenufah* wards off punishments and harmful winds that travel
horizontally, *terumah* harmful dews or rains that come down from above.
*Peirush HaRokeach* and Baal HaTurim (to Vayikra 7:30) find an allusion in the
count: in the shelamim parashah (Vayikra 7:28-38) forms of *tenufah* appear
four times (technically three, with an extraneous hei counted as a fourth) and
forms of *terumah* twice, matching four lateral directions against two vertical
ones.

Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam (to Shemos 29:24) writes that *tenufah* itself
means both back and forth and up and down. This seems to be based, the column
suggests, on the rule that in practice whatever requires the one requires the
other; and it does not really help define the two words against each other.

### Why breast and thigh are named as they are
Rashi (to Vayikra 10:15), reacting to that rule, says he does not know why the
breast is tied to *tenufah* and the thigh to *terumah* when both limbs undergo
both. *Peirush HaRokeach* (to Shemos 29:27, Vayikra 7:34) attempts an answer
from the shape: the breast is a wide flank, fitting the widthwise *tenufah*,
while the thigh is upright and erect, fitting the up-and-down *terumah*; see
also Ramban to Vayikra 10:15. Another approach may be gleaned from Rabbi Yosef
Bechor Shor (to Shemos 29:24), who writes that the two are synonyms which the
Torah uses in tandem to avoid being repetitive; on his view breast takes
*tenufah* and thigh *terumah* as a matter of linguistic elegance, with no deeper
significance.

Targum Onkelos complicates the picture. Rabbi Shmuel Botnick, whose question
prompted the column, noted that to Shemos 29:24 Onkelos renders *tenufah* with
an Aramaic cognate of *terumah*, which would make it "elevating, lifting"
rather than horizontal waving. Rabbi Yaakov Zev Lev (*Me'at Tzari* to Shemos
29:24) notes that there Onkelos disagrees with Rashi, defining *tenufah* as up
and down just as he would define *terumah*. It might be tempting to say Onkelos
simply follows Bechor Shor's synonymy, but Rabbi Lev (to Shemos 29:27) points
out that Onkelos renders *terumah* of the miluim thigh as "separating," not
"lifting."

### terumah as separation
Shemos 25:2, the donations for the Mishkan: Rashi glosses *terumah* as
"separation." Rabbi Yehuda Kalats (*Miseach Ilmim* there) explains Rashi's
intent: no physical lifting is involved in giving a donation, and the *trei
mi'meah* reading used of the tithes cannot apply because no fixed amount was
prescribed for the Mishkan; so *terumah* here is simply separating a portion of
one's wealth to donate it to the sacred. (*Tenufah* likewise refers to a
donation at Shemos 35:22.) As the Kohen's tithe, Mishnah *Terumos* 4:3 sets one
fortieth for the generous, one fiftieth for the average, one sixtieth for the
stingy, and in line with the average the Zohar (*Korach* 179a), the Rambam
(commentary to that Mishnah) and *Sefer HaChinuch* (mitzvah 507) parse the word
itself as *trei mi'meah*, two per hundred, that is one fiftieth. At Vayikra
23:17 Rashi glosses *Lechem Tenufah* as *Lechem Terumah*, reading it as the
loaves' being lifted to a higher plane of sanctity rather than a physical wave.

### The roots
In both words the initial tav is not part of the root, which is ר-ו-ם and נ-ו-פ
respectively. Ibn Janach (introduction to *Sefer HaRikmah*) and the Rambam
(commentary to Mishnah *Terumos* 1:1) note that in rabbinic Hebrew the tav of
*terumah* is treated as part of the root, hence *torem*.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim on ר-ו-ם, core meaning height and esteem: *ram*,
*marom*, *re'em*, *ramah* (an arcing throw upward), *mirmah* (a crooked rather
than straight way of acting), *rimah* (the worm that seems to hover above the
ground) and *rimon* (the fruit especially prone to the *rimah*). *Terumah* is
lifting one item up out of the rest of one's possessions to give it as a tithe
or gift for a higher purpose.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim on נ-ו-פ, core meaning upward movement: *l'hanif* (to
lift, especially a sickle), *l'nofef* (to wave), *nafah* (a sifter), *nof* (the
top branch), *anaf* (a form of anger), *anafah* (a bird), *niuf*
(licentiousness). If both roots mean lifting, what separates the words? His
resolution: *terumah* is specifically the up-down axis, while *tenufah* is the
more general term covering either axis. The idea is borne out in the *nafah*, a
sifter worked back and forth so that the fine flour falls down, both motions at
once. Rabbi Meir Simchah of Dvinsk's *Meshech Chochmah* (to Shemos 29:27) and
Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg's *HaKtav VeHaKabbalah* (to Devarim 23:26) make
very similar points. He explains *anaf* as the intense anger that sets the body
shaking, a *tenufah*-like motion, *anafah* as the bird easily provoked or whose
flight resembles *tenufah*, and *niuf* as "funny movements" of the same kind.
This *anaf* is spelled with an alef, which his system can set aside from the
root; *anaf* with an ayin, a branch, he does not discuss here, since in his
system an ayin must belong to the root. Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 48:3) seems to
connect *tenufah* to that *anaf*: a branch spreads out to the sides of the trunk
and not upward, as *tenufah* is back-and-forth motion and not necessarily up
and down.

*Nofes*, flowing honey (Tehillim 19:11, *nofes tzufim*), also seems to belong
to נ-ו-פ. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim ties it to *nof*, since the nectar sits at the
top of the branch or plant, or alternatively to *tenufah*, since honey was put on
glowing coals and its scent waved about a room (see Rashi to Mishlei 7:17).
Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras, an eighteenth-century grammarian and
*dayan*, reads *nofes* in *Aholei Yehuda* as figuratively lifted above other
foods for its esteem, and reads *ramos* (Iyov 28:18), from *terumah*'s root,
the same way, a gem so rare it is figuratively raised above the rest. According
to modern linguists, the column adds, that gemstone may be black coral, mother
of pearl, or some specific sea shell.

### Names from the two roots
*Ramos* as topographical heights gives Ramos Gilad (sometimes spelled with a
silent alef after the reish) and the modern place-names built on it. The column
knows of no biblical place-name from נ-ו-פ; the nearest is Memphis, written
Moph once (Hoshea 9:6) and more often Noph (Yeshayahu 19:13; Yirmiyahu 2:16,
44:1, 46:14, 46:19; Yechezkel 30:13, 30:16), but that is more likely a Hebrew
adaptation of a foreign name. So too with personal names: ר-ו-ם gives Ram (Rus
4:19; Divrei HaYamim I 2:9, 2:25), Yirmiyahu (a yud, the ר-ם element, and three
letters of the Shem) and Yoram (two letters of the Shem and the ר-ם element),
while as far as the column knows נ-ו-פ gives none. Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg (of
the Veromemanu Foundation) suggests that Remalyahu, father of Pekach king of
Israel, is ר-ם plus the grammatical lamed plus three letters of the Shem;
others take it from an otherwise unattested root ר-מ-ל.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: *tenufah* "waving," *terumah* "raising" (or "the raised portion"),
  and say once in a note that Rashi assigns the horizontal to the first and the
  vertical to the second.
- Trap: "elevation offering" and "wave offering" each absorb both words, and a
  pasuk like Shemos 29:27 or Vayikra 10:15, which names both acts for the same
  limbs, becomes a tautology in English. Give two verbs there: "waved ... and
  raised."
- *Terumah* the tithe stays transliterated and is never "wave offering."
  *Terumah* the Mishkan donation is "the portion set aside" or simply
  "donation," following Rashi's "separation" at Shemos 25:2; do not import
  lifting into it.
- Where a passage cites Bechor Shor's synonymy, do not translate the two words
  differently in that quotation, or his point disappears. Where it cites Rashi
  or the Rokeach, do.
- *Lechem Tenufah* (Vayikra 23:17) follows Rashi as "bread of terumah," the
  loaves raised in sanctity, not "waved bread," unless the passage is about the
  physical act.
