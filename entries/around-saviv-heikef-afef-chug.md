# Around: saviv / heikef / afef / chug

## Bottom line

*Heikef* is always a full circuit: **circumference**, **circuit** (hence
*hakafos*). *Saviv* is **around** or **about**, going around a center with no
guarantee of completing the circle (Malbim, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim); for
Pappenheim even an arc, a detour or a roundabout way of speaking qualifies.
Doubled as *saviv saviv*, Malbim notes, it does mean all the way around. *Afef*
is an encircling that closes in from every side, and Malbim holds it is always
for the worse: **envelop**, **beset**. For Malbim a *chug* is a circle seen from
its center; for Radak it is what orbits another body.

## Distinctions

### saviv (סביב)
- Malbim: *saviv* is not necessarily a complete circuit, though *saviv saviv*
  is.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau: it means moving around a center in a
  curving way, and a semicircle or a mere arc qualifies. When Bnei Yisrael went
  *around* rather than through the land of the Pelishtim (Shemos 13:18) that is
  a *sivuv*, plainly not a full circle, because any roundabout movement whose
  purpose may be to avoid a point (that point being the center of the circle)
  can be called a *sivuv*. The word also works figuratively, of speaking in a
  roundabout way instead of coming to the point (Shmuel II 14:20).

Pappenheim adds that *saviv* words usually do mean full circles, and reads a
family of words that way: *siv*, the fibrous bast wrapped around a palm's
branches; [*soveh*](drunkenness-sove-shachar-sheichar.html#%D7%A1%D7%91%D7%90), a drunkard (Devarim 21:20, Mishlei 23:20, Yeshayahu 56:12),
who does his rounds of the taverns (to taste different sorts of drink, or to
keep the bartenders from recognizing him, or because he has used up his credit
in one shop and must drink at the next); *sav*, an old man (Iyov 15:10), and
*saba*, a
grandfather, who is nearing the close of the circle of his years. Modern
Hebrew's *sevivon*, a dreidel, Rabbi Klein explains, belongs here rather than
under *heikef*, since a top may stop mid-rotation and never finish its last circle.

Radak, with the Targumim, gives the Aramaic equivalent of ס-ב-ב as *sachor*; from
it a *socheirah*, a round shield (Tehillim 91:4), and a [*socher*](merchant-socher-rochel-cnaani-tagar.html#%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%97%D7%A8), a merchant, who
travels about to sell his wares.

### heikef (היקף)
Always a complete circuit. The circumference of a circle is its *heikef*, and the
seven circuits around the [*bimah*](platform-kan-duchan-bimah-basis.html#%D7%91%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%94) on Sukkos and Simchas Torah are *hakafos*
because they ought to be complete circuits. Pappenheim connects *kafah*/*kafui*, congealed or
frozen, to *heikef*: such a substance is completely closed within the bounds of
its present state and cannot shift shape as easily as a liquid does.

### afef (עפף)
"*Afafuni chevlei maves...* and I would call in the name of Hashem, please
Hashem, save my soul" (Tehillim 116:3 to 4). Malbim: the encircling of *afef*
always carries a negative sense, and the root is bound up with [*pe'ah*](corner-peah-keren-zavit-pinah.html#%D7%A4%D7%90%D7%94), a corner
(plural *pe'os*), because it is an enveloping from every corner at once.

### chug / chag (חוג / חג)
- Radak: a *chug* is what orbits around another body, as a planet does.
- Malbim: *chug* or *machug* is a circle considered from its center.

A [*chagorah*](belt-avnet-chagor-eizor.html#%D7%97%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8), a belt, is named for encircling the body.

- On *chag* as a festival, Rabbi Yom Tov Lipmann Heller: the word's original
  sense is a circle, evoking the circles of dance that mark a *yom tov* (*Sefer
  HeAruch* and Radak to Shmuel I 30:16 read similarly). On that basis he
  explains why, of the three the Torah calls a *chag*, the Mishnah's plain
  *chag* is Sukkos: it is the festival most identified with [*simchah*](joy-simchah-sasson-gilah.html#%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%97%D7%94) (Devarim
  16:15), and so with dancing.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg differs slightly: *chag* evokes the festive gait
  of the *olei regel* going up to Yerushalayim.

Chanukah is never called a *chag*, which is why the customary greeting is not
*chag sameach* but something on the order of the Yiddish *A Lichtege Chanuka*,
an illuminating Chanukah.

Rabbi Wolf Heidenheim: since *ches* and *ayin* sometimes interchange, *chag* is the same
word as *ag*, circle. Hence [*ugah*](cake-ugah-chararah-gluskos.html#%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%92%D7%94), a round food such as a cake; *agil*, an
earring; and the Mishnah's account of Choni HaMe'agel, who *ag ugah*, drew a
circle and stood inside it (Taanis 3:8).

Rabbi Klein notes that the Targumim render *mecholos*, dancing circles (Shemos
15:20, Shoftim 11:34, Tehillim 150:4), as *chingin*, so ח-נ-ג means going around
in a circle, and *chag* comes from it with the nun dropped. Rabbi Chelouche
(*Michtam LeDavid*, Tehillim p. 15) says the same, which explains the dagesh in
the gimmel; in this reading a *chag* is the cycle of going up to Yerushalayim,
going home, and going up again.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "around" swallows *saviv* and *heikef* both. Give *heikef* a word
  that means completion ("circuit," "full circle," "circumference") and keep
  "around" or "about" for *saviv*, so that a passage contrasting them still
  contrasts in English.
- *Saviv saviv* is not a stylistic doubling to be trimmed. Following Malbim it
  means "all the way around," and English should say so.
- Shemos 13:18: "led them roundabout, by way of the wilderness," not "led them
  in a circle." Pappenheim's point is that a detour is a *sivuv*.
- *Hakafos*: leave transliterated with a gloss ("the circuits around the
  *bimah*"); "processions" loses the completeness the word insists on.
- Tehillim 116:3: *afafuni* is "beset me" or "closed in on me," never a neutral
  "surrounded me," if Malbim's reading is being followed.
- *Chag*: translate as "festival," and bring in the circle sense only when
  quoting Rabbi Yom Tov Lipmann Heller or Rabbi Wolf Heidenheim, marking it as
  *derashah* on the word rather than its working meaning.
