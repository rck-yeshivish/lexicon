# Around: saviv / heikef / afef / chug

## Bottom line

*Heikef* is always a full circuit: **circumference**, **circuit** (hence
*hakafos*). *Saviv* is going around a center without any guarantee of completing
the circle, so **around**, **about**, and even a detour or a roundabout way of
speaking; doubled as *saviv saviv* it does mean all the way around (Malbim).
*Afef* is encircling that closes in on a man from every side, and Malbim holds it
is always for the worse: **envelop**, **beset**. *Chug* is a circle seen from its
center, an orbit. The trap is rendering *saviv* as "all around" where the Hebrew
concedes only an arc.

## Distinctions

### saviv (סביב)
Malbim: *saviv* is not necessarily a complete circuit, though *saviv saviv* is.
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau: it means moving around a center in a curving
way, and a semicircle or a mere arc qualifies. When Bnei Yisrael went *around*
rather than through the land of the Pelishtim (Shemos 13:18) that is a *sivuv*,
plainly not a full circle, because any roundabout movement whose purpose is to
avoid a point (that point being the center of the circle) is a *sivuv*. The word
also works figuratively, of speaking in a roundabout way instead of coming to
the point (Shmuel II 14:20).

Pappenheim adds that *saviv* words usually do mean full circles, and reads a
family of words that way: *siv*, the fibrous bast wrapped around a palm's
branches; *soveh*, a drunkard (Devarim 21:20, Mishlei 23:20, Yeshayahu 56:12),
who does his rounds of the taverns; *sav*, an old man (Iyov 15:10), and *saba*, a
grandfather, who is nearing the close of the circle of his years. Modern
Hebrew's *sevivon*, a dreidel, belongs here rather than under *heikef*, since a
top may stop mid-rotation and never finish its last circle.

Radak, with the Targumim, gives the Aramaic equivalent of ס-ב-ב as *sechor*; from
it a *socheirah*, a round shield (Tehillim 91:4), and a *socher*, a merchant, who
travels about to sell his wares.

### heikef (היקף)
Always a complete circuit. The circumference of a circle is its *heikef*, and the
seven circuits around the *bimah* on Sukkos and Simchas Torah are *hakafos*
because each must be whole. Pappenheim connects *kafah*/*kafui*, congealed or
frozen, to *heikef*: such a substance is completely closed within the bounds of
its present state and cannot shift shape as a liquid does.

### afef (עפף)
"*Afafuni chevlei maves...* and I would call in the name of Hashem, please
Hashem, save my soul" (Tehillim 116:3 to 4). Malbim: the encircling of *afef*
always carries a negative sense, and the root is bound up with *pe'ah*, a corner
(plural *pe'os*), because it is an enveloping from every corner at once.

### chug / chag (חוג / חג)
Radak: a *chug* is what orbits around another body, as a planet does. Malbim:
*chug* or *machug* is a circle considered from its center. A *chagorah*, a belt,
is named for encircling the body.

On *chag* as a festival, Rabbi Yom Tov Lipmann Heller: the word's original sense
is a circle, evoking the circles of dance that mark a *yom tov* (*Sefer HeAruch*
and Radak to Shmuel I 30:16 read similarly). On that basis he explains why, of
the three the Torah calls a *chag*, the Mishnah's plain *chag* is Sukkos: it is
the festival most identified with *simchah* (Devarim 16:15), and so with
dancing. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg differs slightly: *chag* evokes the
festive gait of the *olei regel* going up to Yerushalayim. Chanukah is never
called a *chag*, which is why the greeting is not *chag sameach*.

Rabbi Wolf Heidenheim: since *ches* and *ayin* interchange, *chag* is the same
word as *ag*, circle. Hence *ugah*, a round food such as a cake; *agil*, an
earring; and the Mishnah's account of Choni HaMe'agel, who *ag ugah*, drew a
circle and stood inside it (Taanis 3:4).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "around" swallows *saviv* and *heikef* both. Give *heikef* a word
  that means completion ("circuit," "full circle," "circumference") and keep
  "around" or "about" for *saviv*, so that a passage contrasting them still
  contrasts in English.
- *Saviv saviv* is not a stylistic doubling to be trimmed. Following Malbim it
  means "all the way around," and English should say so.
- Shemos 13:18: "led them roundabout, by way of the wilderness," not "led them
  in a circle." Pappenheim's point is that a detour is a *sivuv*.
- *Hakafos*: leave transliterated with a gloss ("the circuits around the
  *bimah*"); "processions" loses the completeness the word insists on.
- Tehillim 116:3: *afafuni* is "beset me" or "closed in on me," never a neutral
  "surrounded me," if Malbim's reading is being followed.
- *Chag*: translate as "festival," and bring in the circle sense only when
  quoting the Tosafos Yom Tov or Rabbi Mecklenburg, marking it as *derashah* on
  the word rather than its working meaning.
