# Staff and tribe: mateh / shevet / mishenes / makel / sharvit / choter

## Bottom line

*Mateh* is a **staff** of office, the stick of a man with authority, and by
extension a **tribe** (chiefly in Bamidbar and Yehoshua). *Shevet* is a **rod**
swung with force, the instrument of discipline, and equally a **tribe**.
*Mishenes* is a **cane**, leaned on. *Makel* is a **rod for striking**, the
shepherd's stick. *Sharvit* is the royal **scepter**. *Choter* is a Hebraized
form of Aramaic *chutra*, staff. The one thing not to get wrong: *mateh* and
*shevet* are both stick and tribe, and English has no word that carries both, so
the pun in a derashah has to be built rather than translated.

## Distinctions

### mateh (מטה)
Malbim (Yirmiyahu 1:11): the stick a dignitary holds, displaying the honor owed
to his station. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: a stick held by one in
authority, which is why of all the sticks in Tanach only Moshe Rabbeinu's is
called a *mateh*, his standing as prophet and king together being unmatched. He
relates the word to *mateh*, inclined, after the bend at the top by which it is
gripped, and holds that a *mateh* is always of wood. Rabbi Yehuda ibn Kuraish
narrows it further to dry wood, though the rabbinic tradition has Moshe's
*mateh* hewn from blue stone, sapphire or lapis lazuli. On the naming of the
tribe, Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (*HaKesav VeHaKabbalah*), citing Shadal,
connects *mateh* to *lematah*, below, the tribesmen who live under their chief.

### shevet (שבט)
Rashi (Sanhedrin 5a): *shevet* denotes authority and the responsibility that
comes with it; in a judicial context the *shevet* or *shotim* is the instrument
of flogging. Wertheimer: such terms first named a stick for driving animals, and
their use of people is borrowed. The Vilna Gaon distinguishes the two sticks by
how they are wielded: a *mateh* needs only a slight lift of the hand for the
blow to be felt, while a *shevet* must be wound up with force. Wertheimer adds
that a *shevet* may be wood or metal, as in the iron *shevet* of Tehillim 2:9,
where *mateh* and *makel* are wood only. Malbim (Bereishis 49:28): *shevet* does
not strictly mean stick but *branch*, the commonest thing used as a stick, and
each tribe is a branch of the family tree of Yaakov Avinu. Mecklenburg in
Shadal's name: *shevet* first named the tribe's leader, the mast around which
the tribe rallies, and came to cover the tribesmen because the leader embodies
the tribe; as sticks, *shevet* is the top of the staff and *mateh* the bottom.

### mishenes (משענת)
A walking stick, from *mishan*, to lean or rely: the cane an old man leans on.
Wertheimer: wooden, and necessarily a thick branch, since a twig will not bear a
walker's weight.

### makel (מקל)
A rod for striking, appearing in Tanach chiefly with a shepherd driving the
animals in his charge; likely related to *malkos* / *makkos*, lashes.

### sharvit (שרביט)
The royal scepter that Achashverosh holds out in Megillas Esther. Some hold
*shevet* and *sharvit* to be the same object, Tanach sometimes adding a reish
sound, as it calls Damascus both *Damesek* and *Darmesek*.

### choter (חוטר)
Probably a Hebraized form of the Aramaic *chutra*, which is what the Targumim
use for both *mateh* and *shevet*.

## The connotations

Wertheimer in the name of Rabbi Yaakov Lorberbaum of Lissa: the three words
carry different colorings. *Makel* is negative, tied to punishment; *mishenes*
is positive, supporting the elderly; *mateh* is neutral. On that basis
Wertheimer explains why the staff with which Moshe worked the wonders in
Mitzrayim and at the sea is a *mateh*: its effects were good for Klal Yisrael
and bad for the Egyptians, so the neutral word fits.

Rabbi Zev HaKohen Hoberman draws the two tribe-words together: Klal Yisrael are
*shevatim* because Hashem gave them a measure of authority and responsibility
among the nations, and *matos* (which he takes with *mishenes* and *chutra*)
because they are Yaakov Avinu's cane after his death, the support he leans on to
carry his legacy forward, following his tradition rather than standing on their
own.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "staff" or "rod" will absorb *mateh*, *shevet*, and *makel*.
  Keep "staff" for *mateh*, "rod" for *shevet*, and "stick" or "rod for
  striking" for *makel*, and do not let one carry two.
- *Mateh* as "tribe" and *mateh* as "staff" cannot be held together in one
  English word. Where a derashah turns on the double sense, translate the
  operative one and gloss the other in a bracket or a note; do not force
  "branch" to do both jobs.
- *Mateh Moshe*: "the staff of Moshe," never "Moshe's rod," which pulls it into
  the *shevet* / punishment register that Rabbi Yaakov of Lissa's reading
  expressly rules out.
- *Shevet* in Tehillim 2:9 (iron) is a rod of rule and of shattering, not a
  tribe; check the material and the verb before choosing.
- *Mishenes* is "cane" or "support," and in a metaphor ("a *mishenes* of a
  broken reed") the leaning is the whole point, so keep a verb of leaning in the
  English.
- *Sharvit* is "scepter" and only that; do not level it to "rod" even where a
  source equates it with *shevet*.
