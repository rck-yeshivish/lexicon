# Fringes: tzitzis / gedilim / petil / tallis / kruspidin

## Bottom line

*Tzitzis* are the **fringes**, named for what protrudes and is therefore seen.
*Gedilim* are the same strings named for the **braided** third that is thickened
by twisting. *Petil* is the single **thread** of *techeiles*, named for snug
twisting and, in Rav Hirsch's reading, for its separateness. *Tallis* is not the
fringes at all but the **four-cornered garment** that carries them, and it is
Rabbinic Hebrew only. *Kruspidin* is the Targum's Aramaic for *tzitzis* and
*gedilim*, a Greek loanword for the **hem or fringe** of a garment. The one
thing not to get wrong: *tzitzis* and *tallis* are not interchangeable, and
"prayer shawl" for *tallis* imports a usage the word itself does not carry.

## Distinctions

### tzitzis (ציצית)
The classical lexicographers of the triliteral school trace *tzitzis* to
צ-י-צ, a root that yields "sprouting," "peeking," "branch, wing, fin," and
*tzitz*, the Kohen Gadol's forehead plate. Rashi (to Bamidbar 15:38) connects
*tzitzis* both to the hair that juts from the side of the head (Yechezkel 8:3,
the word's only other appearance in Tanach) and to "peeking." Rashi (to
Yirmiyahu 48:9): anything that juts out from a body is a *tzitz*. Malbim (*Sefer
HaCarmel*): a *tzitz* protrudes from the main body in a way that makes it highly
visible, which is exactly what the strings do to the garment. In Mishnaic Hebrew
the shreds of foreskin left after a milah are *tzitzin* (Shabbos 19:6) for the
same reason.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Yerios Shlomo*, *Cheshek Shlomo*) traces the word to
the single letter צ, whose core sense is "exiting, outwardness"; on one path
the *tzitzis* are a highly visible ornament that draws the eye, on the other
they are what "exits" from the garment they are tied to. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos
28:36), by his phonetic method, links צ-י-צ to ש-י-ש (*sasson*, gladness
showing outwardly) and to ז-י-ז, so that *tzitz* is what protrudes from a
surface and becomes visible to all.

Rabbeinu Bachaya (*Kad HaKemach* s.v. tzitzis, and to Bamidbar 15:38) draws the
mussar from the "peeking" sense: the wearer should be mindful that Hashem sees
him.

### gedilim (גדילים)
Appears once for the mitzvah, "you shall make yourself *gedilim* on the four
corners of your garment" (Devarim 22:12), and once for a braided metal chain
(Melachim I 7:17). All the early lexicographers (Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Ibn
Parchon, Radak) put it in ג-ד-ל, the root of *gadol*. Ibn Janach defines
*gedilim* as *tzitzis*; Radak refines it to free-hanging *tzitzis*; Ibn Parchon
defines it by *petil*; Ibn Saruk by *chut*, "string."

Rav Hirsch (to Bamidbar 15:38): a braided rope is a *gedil* because braiding
makes it thicker, so it has "grown." He notes that the Rabbinic *gudal*
("thumb") names the thickest finger, not the longest. Rabbi Yosef Kimchi (*Sefer
HaGalui*) takes the other path: the strings "expand" the edge of the garment
beyond the fabric itself.

The difference from *tzitzis*: Menachos 39a requires a third of the strings'
length to be *gadil* (braided) and two thirds to hang loose like a branch
(*anaf*). Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) uses that Gemara to resolve why the Torah
sometimes says *gedilim* and sometimes *tzitzis*: one part of the string is a
branch, which is the "branch" sense of צ-י-צ, and the other is a braid. Rav
Hirsch (to Bamidbar 15:38) reads it the same way.

### petil (פתיל)
The Torah requires one *petil* of *techeiles* among the strings (Bamidbar
15:38). The root פ-ת-ל denotes snug attachment or twistedness, as in *tzamid
pasil*, the tightly sealed vessel (Bamidbar 19:15); a string is made by twisting
fibers snugly together. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 30:8, Bamidbar 15:38) reads the
root as "separateness" instead, comparing ב-ד-ל (*havdalah*), ב-ת-ל
(*besulah*), and ב-ט-ל (*batel*): the *techeiles* thread is bound into the
*tzitzis* yet stands apart from them, since it alone is dyed. The name Naftali
is tied to this root explicitly (Bereishis 30:8). Yehudah's *petil* given to
Tamar (Bereishis 38:18) is his belt or cloak according to Ibn Janach, Rabbi
Avraham Maimuni, Rashbam, and Radak; Rabbi Moshe HaDarshan (*Bereishis Rabbasi*)
and Rabbeinu Chaim Paltiel read it as his *tallis*/*tzitzis*, which Ramban
rejects. In Mishnaic Hebrew *petilah* is a lamp wick.

### tallis (טלית)
First occurs in Rabbinic Hebrew (Nedarim 3:5, Bava Metzia 1:1, Keilim 28:7, and
many more), and in context it means the four-cornered garment itself, not its
strings. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*): in rabbinic usage it is the
special four-cornered garment with *tzitzis* worn at prayer; he and Rabbi
Tanchum HaYerushalmi hold the correct plural is *taliyos*, not *talitos* and
certainly not the Yiddish *talleisim*. Etymologies: Rabbi Nosson of Rome (*Sefer
HeAruch*) cites Rav Matzliach Gaon that the garment is "placed upon" the other
clothes, relating *tallis* to *natal*, lifting; alternatively he ties it to
ט-ל-ל, "shade, covering" (Nechemiah 3:15, Daniel 4:9), which Rabbi Ernest Klein
follows, comparing *tzel*. Rabbeinu Bachaya reads *tallis* as alluding to Hashem
being lifted above all, from Daniel 7:4. Rabbi Nosson Shapiro (*Matzas Shimurin*)
gives a kabbalistic derivation from the Aramaic *talyusa*. Rashi (to Sanhedrin
44a) identifies the Rabbinic *itztala* with *tallis*.

### kruspidin (כרוספדין)
The Targum renders both *tzitzis* and *gedilim* as *kruspeda* (plural
*kruspidin*). Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Musaf HeAruch*): an Aramaic loanword
from the Greek *kraspedon*, the fringe, edge, hem, or embroidered border of a
garment, that is, the prominent extension added to a core frame. Rabbi Shaul
Goldman draws the point out: the most prominent feature of a four-cornered
garment is neither its top nor its center but the strings protruding at its
edge.

## Rendering guidance

- *Tzitzis* stays transliterated in any frum text; where an English gloss is
  wanted once, **fringes**, not "tassels."
- *Gedilim*: **braided cords**, and where the Gemara's ratio is in play, "the
  braided part." Do not render it "fringes" as well, or Radak's resolution of
  the two Torah words collapses.
- *Petil*: **thread** or **cord** (of *techeiles*), singular. Never "fringe."
- *Tallis*: **tallis**, glossed once as the four-cornered garment. Reserve
  "prayer shawl" for a translation aimed outside the frum readership, and even
  then only where nothing in the passage turns on the garment as such.
- Trap: English "fringes" will swallow *tzitzis*, *gedilim*, and *kruspidin* at
  once. When a passage sets *tzitzis* against *gedilim* (Menachos 39a and Radak
  on it), give the first as "fringes" and the second as "braided cords," and let
  *petil* stay a single thread inside them.
- Devarim 22:12 beside Bamidbar 15:38: keep the Torah's own switch of word
  visible in English. The whole derashah is that the Torah used two names for
  one mitzvah.
