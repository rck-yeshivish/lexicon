# Names of the Jewish people: Ivri / Yisrael / Yehudi

## Bottom line

*Ivri* is **Hebrew**, first applied to Avraham; *Yisrael* and *Bnei Yisrael* are
**Israel** and **the Children of Israel**, the Torah's ordinary name for the
nation; *Yehudi* is **Jew**, strictly a subject of the Kingdom of Yehudah before
it became the name of the whole people. These are not interchangeable
registers of the same word: Rabbeinu Bachya holds
*Ivri* marks a lower standing than *Yisrael*, which is why the Torah keeps
*eved Ivri* for the Jewish slave even after Sinai, by which point the term had
otherwise largely fallen out of use in Tanach.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why Avraham is called *HaIvri*** (Bereishis 14:13). *Bereishis Rabbah* 42:8
gives three views: that the whole world stood on one *ever*, one side of the
scale, and Avraham on the other, and his stature balanced it; that it is a
genealogical marker, Avraham being descended from Ever, Shem's great-grandson
(Bereishis 11:14-17); that it records his Mesopotamian origin from the far
*ever* of the Euphrates, and that he spoke the *Ivri* tongue. *Pesikta Rabbasi*
33 adds a fourth: when the whole world served idols and Avraham held apart, he
was called *Ivri* for taking the opposite side of that question. *Shemos Rabbah*
3:8 reads the name of the nation prospectively, *Ivriim* because they were to
cross over (*she'avru*) the sea.

## Distinctions

On the Los Angeles subway a friendly stranger once asked Rabbi Klein and his
wife whether they were Amish, and he answered, "Nah, we're Hebrews." But why are
Jews called Hebrews, why are they called Jews, and what does the biblical
"Israelites" mean?

### Ivri (עברי) and ever (עבר)
The first man in the Torah called an *Ivri* is Avraham, in the refugee's report
of Lot's capture (Bereishis 14:13-14). The word rests on *ever*, a side or a far
bank, and every reading in the midrashim above takes it from there, whether the
side of a scale, the far side of a river, or the far side of an argument.
Rabbeinu Bachya (to Shemos 21:6): *Ivri* connotes a lower spiritual level than
*Yisrael*, which is why a Jewish bondsman is still an *eved Ivri* after Sinai,
though the term had by then largely dropped from use. A slave lives on a lower
plane than a free man, so the older, lower name suits him.

### Yisrael (ישראל) and Bnei Yisrael (בני ישראל)
*Bnei Yisrael* appears 636 times in Tanach and *Yisrael* as a name for the
people over two thousand. It is Yaakov's second name used as a patronym, so the
nation is *Yisrael* or the sons of *Yisrael*. Through most of Tanach this, and
not *Ivri*, is what the people are called, on Rabbeinu Bachya's account because
it is the name of the higher standing. "Israelite"
and "Israeli" are not the same word in English: the second means a citizen of
the modern state.

### Yehudi (יהודי) and Yehudah (יהודה)
After Shlomo HaMelech the kingdom split: Yehudah in the south, the tribes of
Yehudah and Binyamin under the house of David, and Yisrael in the north with the
remaining ten tribes. The gentilic *Yehudi* belonged strictly to a subject of the
southern kingdom. The north fell to Ashur and its tribes were exiled to parts
unknown; well over a century later Bavel took the south and exiled its people,
and when Persia succeeded Bavel and permitted the return, the semi-independent
province was *Yehud Medinata*, the State of Yehudah. Centuries later Rome named
that territory Judea, until it rebranded it *Syria Palaestina* after the Bar
Kochba revolt. From *Yehudi* through Greek *Ioudaios*, which dropped the
h-sound, and Old French *giu*, which dropped the d-sound, comes the English
"Jew"; many other European languages kept the d. The column notes in passing
that the earliest appearance of *Ioudaios* is in the so-called Moschos
Inscription, a stele erected in honor of the Greek gods by a Jew turned Hellene
named Moschos. The name itself is Leah's: she called her fourth son Yehudah, the
forebear of the Davidic line, as an expression of *hodaah*, thanks to Hashem for
granting her a fourth son (Bereishis 29:35). Rabbi
Dr. Norman Lamm, debating a radical secular Israeli who denied the Jewishness of
the State of Israel, is reported to have answered that one who speaks of French
and Spanish and Italian nationals while denying the nationhood of the Jewish
people should know that in America there are Hebrew Nationals too, and at least
they claim their baloney is kosher.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "Jew" will absorb all three. In a Chumash context it is almost
  always wrong: the people are *Bnei Yisrael*, "the Children of Israel," and
  *Yehudi* does not yet mean what it later means.
- *Eved Ivri* is "Hebrew bondsman" or "Hebrew slave," never "Jewish slave." The
  word choice is the whole of Rabbeinu Bachya's point.
- Where a passage sets *Ivri* against *Yisrael* deliberately (Rabbeinu Bachya's
  reading), keep "Hebrew" and "Israel"
  as two distinct English words and do not smooth either to "Jew."
- Where a later sefer of Tanach uses *Yehudim* of the people at large rather than
  of subjects of the southern kingdom, "Jews" is right and "Judahites" would be
  pedantic.
- Render *ever* by what the derashah needs: "side" for the scale, "the other
  side" for the river, and let the name Ever stand as a name.
