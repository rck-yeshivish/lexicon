# Sanctuary: Mishkan / Mikdash

## Bottom line

*Mishkan* is the **Mishkan** (English "Tabernacle"), generally the portable
Sanctuary of the wilderness and of Gilgal, Shiloh, Nov and Givon. *Mikdash*, or
*Beis HaMikdash*, is the permanent **Temple** in Yerushalayim. The *Mishkan* was
temporary and the *Mikdash* permanent, and the difference shows in the building
itself: curtains and cloth for the *Mishkan*, walls and a roof for the
*Mikdash*. The Gemara (Eruvin 2a) grants that the two words are sometimes used
almost interchangeably, but Rabbi Klein holds that those are borrowed usages and
that the words are not truly synonymous.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| משכן | Mishkan | Mishkan, Tabernacle | portable and temporary; curtains, not walls; from *le'shaken*, to dwell, and *Shechinah* |
| מקדש | Mikdash | Beis HaMikdash, Temple | permanent, in Yerushalayim on Har HaMoriah; walls, a roof, larger dimensions |

## Where the authorities disagree

Eruvin 2a admits that the two words are sometimes used almost interchangeably;
Rabbi Klein holds even so that they are not truly synonymous: they name two
specific types of holy place, and the other usages are simply borrowed meanings.
Mishnah Zevachim 14:6 gives the *Mishkan* at Shiloh
quasi-*Mikdash* status: its stone walls had the properties of a permanent
edifice, unlike the *Mishkan* everywhere else, yet it was still covered with a
curtain as it had been elsewhere. Moreover, it stood there almost four hundred
years, which gave it some sort of permanence. So, he concludes, Shiloh was not a
temporary Sanctuary like the *Mishkan* elsewhere but a semi-permanent
stepping-stone between the temporary *Mishkan* and the permanent *Mikdash*.

## Distinctions

### Mishkan (משכן)
Root ש-כ-ן, the root of *le'shaken* and of *Shechinah*: the place where Hashem's
Presence dwells. Its structure was cloth: the Kodesh HaKodashim marked off by a
curtain, and ten curtains over the whole, because the building was made to be
taken down and carried. It stood mobile for the forty years in the wilderness,
then at Gilgal for the fourteen years of conquest and division of the Land, then
at Shiloh for 369 years, and after the Pelishtim destroyed the Shiloh Sanctuary,
at Nov and Givon for a combined fifty-seven years. Private altars were permitted for certain
types of offerings during the *Mishkan* era, except during the forty years in the wilderness and the
centuries at Shiloh.

### Mikdash (מקדש)
Root ק-ד-ש, holiness. The permanent structure on Har HaMoriah in Yerushalayim,
built by Shlomo HaMelech and rebuilt under Persian rule after the seventy-year
exile in Bavel; the second stood some four centuries until the Romans destroyed
it. Its Kodesh HaKodashim was marked off by a wall in the first Beis HaMikdash
(by two curtains in the second), it had a true roof rather than curtains, and its
dimensions were larger. Once it stood, private altars were forbidden for good.

### The two eras
Yoma 9a-9b gives the causes of each destruction: the Shiloh Sanctuary for
desecration of holy foodstuffs and for impropriety in intimacy, the first Beis
HaMikdash for the three cardinal sins (idolatry, impropriety in intimacy, and
murder), the second for baseless hatred among Jews. The destruction of the two
*Batei Mikdash* is mourned each year on Tishah B'Av; Rabbi Klein adds that in
some ways the destruction of Mishkan Shiloh is an equally sad catastrophe.
Zevachim 119a
reads Devarim 12:9, "for you have not yet come to the resting place or to the
inheritance," as the two stages: the resting place is the Mishkan at Shiloh, the
inheritance the Beis HaMikdash in Yerushalayim. Rabbi Avraham Wittenstein, of
Yeshivas Mir in Yerushalayim, explains that the *Mishkan* paradigm had an
advantage of its own, since it reflected the continuous manifestation of
Hashem's Presence from the revelation at Har Sinai; when the Pelishtim destroyed
Shiloh they caused a kind of disconnection from Sinai, so that the Presence in
the later Sanctuaries at Nov and Givon and in the Beis HaMikdash was not the
direct continuation of Sinai but a new sort of revelation, one that could be
achieved only through the hard work of Shmuel HaNavi and David HaMelech.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "Temple" and "Sanctuary" will each cover both words. Keep
  *Mishkan* transliterated (or "Tabernacle" in an older register) and reserve
  "Temple" or *Beis HaMikdash* for the permanent building, so a passage that
  moves from one era to the other stays legible.
- Where the two words stand together in a Gemara, do not smooth them into one
  English word on the strength of Eruvin 2a; render both and, if the passage
  turns on it, note that the usage there is loose.
- "Mishkan Shiloh" is best left as it stands, with the Mishnah's quasi-*Mikdash*
  status noted; "the Tabernacle at Shiloh" loses the point that this one had
  stone walls.
- *Shechinah* in the same passage is "the Divine Presence" or is left
  transliterated; do not render it "the Tabernacle" even though the root is
  shared.
