# Names of Yaakov: Yaakov / Yisrael / Yeshurun

## Bottom line

Yaakov, Yisrael and Yeshurun are three names for one man, and though the Torah
seems to use the first two almost interchangeably, the commentators distinguish them.
Yaakov is the man, and by the Ohr HaChaim's reading the man in a low
moment; Yisrael is the man as founder of the nation (Maharal, Rav Hirsch), an added
name rather than a replacement (Rabbeinu Bachya); Yeshurun is a rare title of praise, four times in all
Tanach, built on *yashar*, and it answers Esav's charge that Yaakov means crooked
dealing (Ramban).

## Where the authorities disagree

**When the Torah says Yaakov and when Yisrael.**
- Many commentators, among them the Maharal and Rav Hirsch: the name marks the
  frame, *Yaakov* for the individual and *Yisrael* for the man in his role as
  father of the nation.
- Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk: the two names stand for two avenues of avodah,
  *Yaakov* for *yiras Hashem* and *Yisrael* for *ahavas Hashem*.
- The Ohr HaChaim works through the pesukim systematically and finds a different
  rule: *Yaakov* wherever the Torah shows him downcast or in distress, *Yisrael*
  wherever he is at peace and content.

**What Yeshurun means.**
- Ramban (to Bamidbar 23:10, Devarim 2:10 and 7:12): *yashar*, straight, given
  precisely to counter Esav's claim (Bereishis 27:36) that *Yaakov* comes from
  ע-ק-ב in its other sense of trickery; Michah 7:20 pairs Yaakov with truth.
- A tradition preserved in *Arugas HaBosem* of Rabbi Avraham ben Ezriel,
  *Peirush HaRokeach* to the Siddur, and *Eitz Yosef* of Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of
  Bialystok reads it as *yashar v'naeh*, straight and fine, the two phrases
  matching in *gematria* when *Yeshurun* is spelled with two vavs.
- Rabbeinu Bachya: from *shur*, to gaze, since they came nearest to gazing on
  the Shechinah at Har Sinai.
- The Vilna Gaon: from *shir*, song, for the shirah at the Sea.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: from *shirah* as a name of the Torah (Devarim
  31:19), since they alone accepted it.

**Whether Yeshurun is a name of the Jewish People at all.** The Maharsha holds that
*Yeshurun* is a Name of Hashem, and ascribes the view to Rashi. Rabbi Yechezkel Landau
(*Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Orach Chaim* §126) works out what the Maharsha meant; Rabbi
Nosson Kamenetsky devoted an exhaustive essay to the question. One place where *Yeshurun*
clearly names Yaakov is the pre-Shacharis passage that calls the Jewish People "the congregation of
Your firstborn son Yaakov, whom from Your love that You loved him and from Your
happiness that You were happy with him, You called his name Yisrael and Yeshurun," whose
earliest source is *Tanna DeVei Eliyahu* (*Seder Eliyahu Rabbah* ch. 21).

Rabbi Meir Simcha of Dvinsk notes that apart from that prayer the Jews are almost never
called *Yeshurun* in the liturgy, the exception being the Yom Kippur wording, "Forgiver
of Israel and Pardoner of the Tribes of Yeshurun in all generations." He accounts for
the doubled expression by taking "Forgiver of Israel" of sins between man and Hashem,
typified by the Eigel, and "Pardoner of the Tribes of Yeshurun" of sins between man and
his fellow, typified by the brothers' sale of Yosef.

**Which name stands highest.**
- The Zohar ranks them: *Yisrael* above *Yaakov* (*Vayechi* 222a), and
  *Yeshurun* above *Yisrael* (*Vayishlach* 177b).
- The Chasam Sofer (to Yoma 73b), explaining why later breastplates read
  *shivtei Yisrael* rather than *shivtei Yeshurun*, says that *Yisrael* is
  *yashar* plus *El*, a high level reached with Divine help, while *Yeshurun*
  describes a people inherently straight, at their peak through their own
  effort.
- *Megaleh Amukos* of Rabbi Nosson Nota Shapiro maps the three onto the Arizal's
  worlds by the shape of the suffixes, angels of *Beriah* ending in *-el* and
  those of *Yetzirah* in *-on/-ron*: *Yaakov* in *Asiyah*, *Yisrael* in
  *Beriah*, *Yeshurun* in *Yetzirah*.

## Distinctions

### Yaakov (יעקב)
Bereishis 25:26: named for the *akeiv*, Esav's heel, which he held at birth. Bereishis
27:36 records Esav's counter-reading, that the name comes from ע-ק-ב in the sense of
supplanting or deceit; that slur, for the Ramban, is what the third name answers.

*Noam Elimelech* (to Shemos 19:3): *Yaakov* is the mass of the people, *Yisrael*
its men of standing.

Chasam Sofer (*Toras Moshe*, Vayechi, third piece): the name *Yaakov* speaks of
pain, the name *Yisrael* of greatness.

### Yisrael (ישראל)
Bereishis 32:29, the angel of Esav at the Yabok: he "lords" (*sar*) over angels
(*elohim*, or *el* for short) and over men, confirmed by Hashem Himself at Bereishis
35:10. Rabbeinu Bachya: the
earlier name was not withdrawn, so *Yisrael* is an addition and both remain in force.

From the Midrash (*Bereishis Rabbah* 63:3), Rabbi Yitzchak of Pira, the Rashba's
student (to Bereishis 17:5, 21:3), writes that *Yisrael* is also a name of
Yitzchak and of Avraham.

### Yeshurun (ישורון)
Devarim 32:15, 33:5 and 33:26, and Yeshayahu 44:2. Yoma 73b: the
Kohen Gadol's *choshen* bore the names of the three Avos, the twelve *shevatim*, and the
words *shivtei Yeshurun*; the Chasam Sofer there restricts that to Aharon's *choshen*,
holding that later ones read *shivtei Yisrael*.

Maharal (*Tiferes Yisrael* ch. 11): *Yeshurun* speaks of Yaakov as the perfect
mean, balancing the *chessed* of Avraham and the *gevurah* of Yitzchak.

*Chemdas HaChemdah* (to Devarim 32:15) derives *Yeshurun* from seeing, which
sharpens Moshe's rebuke: they saw all the miracles Hashem did for them and would
still choose other gods. At Devarim 33:5 and 33:26 the same work relates it to
*yashar*, straight, used of Hashem's bond with the people to hint that He is
with them only while they act uprightly.

Chasam Sofer (*Toras Moshe* to Devarim 33:4): *Yeshurun* is the highest level,
those who hold the Torah as their king, the source of wisdom and understanding;
*Yaakov* the lowest, who keep the mitzvos only as an inheritance from their
fathers; *Yisrael* the middle, sometimes loyal to the Torah and sometimes not,
who need the gathered heads of the nation to rebuke and rouse them.

## Rendering guidance

- Transliterate all three; they are personal and national names, so they take roman
  type, not italics, and never a translated gloss in the body.
- The trap is English "Israel," which absorbs *Yisrael* the man, *Yisrael* the nation
  and (in older translations) *Yeshurun*, usually Englished "Jeshurun." Keep *Yeshurun*
  distinct, or the Zohar's ranking and the Chasam Sofer on the *choshen* become
  invisible.
- Where a source turns on the etymology, gloss it in a note rather than in the text:
  *Yaakov* from *akeiv*, *Yisrael* from *sar*, *Yeshurun* from *yashar*. Rendering a
  name by its meaning ("the straight one") loses that it is a name.
- Bereishis 27:36 must show the wordplay, Esav's *vayaakveini* against the name Yaakov;
  bracket the Hebrew rather than rewriting the pasuk.
