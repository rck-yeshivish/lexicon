# Names of Yaakov: Yaakov / Yisrael / Yeshurun

## Bottom line

All three are names and none of them is translated: **Yaakov**, **Yisrael**,
**Yeshurun**, in roman type. What a translator must get right is that they are not
interchangeable. *Yaakov* is the man, and by the Ohr HaChaim's reading the man in a low
moment; *Yisrael* is the man as founder of the nation, an added name rather than a
replacement (Rabbeinu Bachya); *Yeshurun* is a rare title of praise, four times in all
Tanach, built on *yashar*, and it answers Esav's charge that *Yaakov* means crooked
dealing (Ramban). Where a passage switches names in mid-sentence, the switch is the
point; do not smooth it to one name.

## Where the authorities disagree

**When the Torah says Yaakov and when Yisrael.** Maharal and Rav Hirsch: the name marks
the frame, *Yaakov* for the individual and *Yisrael* for the man in his role as father
of the nation. Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk: the two names stand for two avenues of
avodah, *Yaakov* for *yiras Hashem* and *Yisrael* for *ahavas Hashem*. The Ohr HaChaim
works through the pesukim systematically and finds a different rule: *Yaakov* wherever
the Torah shows him downcast or in distress, *Yisrael* wherever he is at peace and
content. All three are workable lenses; the Bottom line quotes the Maharal and Rav
Hirsch's rule because it is the one that bears on how a translator handles the nation's
name, and flags the Ohr HaChaim's because it explains the switches inside a single
narrative.

**What Yeshurun means.** Ramban (to Bamidbar 23:10, Devarim 2:10 and 7:12): *yashar*,
straight, given precisely to counter Esav's claim (Bereishis 27:36) that *Yaakov* comes
from ע-ק-ב in its other sense of trickery; Michah 7:20 pairs Yaakov with truth. A
tradition preserved in *Arugas HaBosem* of Rabbi Avraham ben Ezriel, *Peirush HaRokeach*
to the Siddur, and *Eitz Yosef* of Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok reads it as *yashar
v'naeh*, straight and fine, the two phrases matching in *gematria* when *Yeshurun* is
spelled with two vavs. Rabbeinu Bachya: from *shur*, to gaze, since they came nearest to
gazing on the Shechinah at Har Sinai. The Vilna Gaon: from *shir*, song, for the shirah
at the Sea. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: from *shirah* as a name of the Torah (Devarim
31:19), since they alone accepted it. The Bottom line follows Ramban, whose reading fits
all four occurrences and is the one with a stated purpose.

**Whether Yeshurun is a name of the Jewish People at all.** The Maharsha holds that
*Yeshurun* is a Name of Hashem, and ascribes the view to Rashi. Rabbi Yechezkel Landau
(*Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Orach Chaim* §126) works out what the Maharsha meant; Rabbi
Nosson Kamenetsky devoted an essay to the question. The prevailing reading, and the one
the Bottom line takes, is the plain one of *Tanna DeVei Eliyahu* (*Seder Eliyahu Rabbah*
ch. 21), the source of the pre-Shacharis passage that says Hashem "called his name
Yisrael and Yeshurun."

**Which name stands highest.** The Zohar ranks them: *Yisrael* above *Yaakov* (*Vayechi*
222a), and *Yeshurun* above *Yisrael* (*Vayishlach* 177b). The Chasam Sofer (to Yoma
73b) gives the reason: *Yisrael* is *yashar* plus *El*, a high level reached with Divine
help, while *Yeshurun* describes a people inherently straight, at their peak through
their own effort. *Megaleh Amukos* of Rabbi Nosson Nota Shapiro maps the three onto the
Arizal's worlds by the shape of the suffixes, angels of *Beriah* ending in *-el* and
those of *Yetzirah* in *-on/-ron*: *Yaakov* in *Asiyah*, *Yisrael* in *Beriah*,
*Yeshurun* in *Yetzirah*. Note that the article presents *Yetzirah* here as higher than
*Beriah*, whereas its own listing of the four worlds puts *Beriah* above *Yetzirah*; the
discrepancy is left as it stands.

## Distinctions

### Yaakov (יעקב)
Bereishis 25:26: named for the *akeiv*, Esav's heel, which he held at birth. Bereishis
27:36 records Esav's counter-reading, that the name comes from ע-ק-ב in the sense of
supplanting or deceit; that slur is what the third name answers.

### Yisrael (ישראל)
Bereishis 32:30, the angel of Esav at the Yabok: "for you have lorded (*sarisa*) over
angels and over men," confirmed by Hashem Himself at Bereishis 35:10. Rabbeinu Bachya: the
earlier name was not withdrawn, so *Yisrael* is an addition and both remain in force, which
is why the Torah keeps using both.

### Yeshurun (ישורון)
Devarim 32:15, 33:5 and 33:26, and Yeshayahu 44:2. Yoma 73b: the
Kohen Gadol's *choshen* bore the names of the three Avos, the twelve *shevatim*, and the
words *shivtei Yeshurun*; the Chasam Sofer there restricts that to Aharon's *choshen*,
holding that later ones read *shivtei Yisrael*.

## Rendering guidance

- Transliterate all three; they are personal and national names, so they take roman
  type, not italics, and never a translated gloss in the body.
- The trap is English "Israel," which absorbs *Yisrael* the man, *Yisrael* the nation
  and (in older translations) *Yeshurun*, usually Englished "Jeshurun." Keep *Yeshurun*
  distinct, or the Zohar's ranking and the Chasam Sofer on the *choshen* become
  invisible.
- Where a source turns on the etymology, gloss it in a note rather than in the text:
  *Yaakov* from *akeiv*, *Yisrael* from *sar*, *Yeshurun* from *yashar*. Rendering a
  name by its meaning ("the straight one") loses that it is a name.
- Bereishis 27:36 must show the wordplay, Esav's *vayaakveini* against the name Yaakov;
  bracket the Hebrew rather than rewriting the pasuk.
