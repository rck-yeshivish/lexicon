# Locust: arbeh / gazam / yelek / chasil (and the kosher four)

## Bottom line

It seems to Rabbi Klein that these are species names and not merely synonyms,
though he says plainly that it is not known for sure what each one is. *Arbeh*
is both one species and the umbrella word, the **locust** swarm. *Gazam* is the
cutter, *yelek* the licker, *chasil* the finisher, and in Yoel 1:4
they arrive in a fixed order, *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yelek*, *chasil*, each eating
what the one before left. *Chagav* is also the general term for **kosher**
grasshoppers (Meiri, *Beis HaBechirah*, and Rabbi Shmuel ben Meshullam of Gerona,
*Ohel Mo'ed*). In a plague context the sense is a devouring swarm.

## Distinctions

### the set as a whole
It seems to Rabbi Klein that these words are not simply synonyms but names of
different types of grasshopper. It is not known for sure what each one is; they
are probably different forms of grasshoppers, crickets, katydids (bush crickets)
and other orthoptera. Nine words refer to grasshoppers: *arbeh*, *gazam*,
*yelek*, *chasil*, *tzfoni*, *sal'am*, *chargol*, *chagav* and *chanamal*, and
*tzlatzel* is another possible one.

### arbeh (ארבה)
The eighth *makkah* (Shemos 10:14, "before it there was never an *arbeh* like
it, and after it there will not be so"). Radak: named for its multitudes, from
*harbeh*. *Arbeh* names one particular species and also serves as the umbrella
for every kind. It heads the four permitted species of Vayikra 11:22 (*arbeh*,
*sal'am*, *chargol*, *chagav*), and the Gemara (Chullin 65a) gives its Aramaic
as *govai*, as does the Targum to Yoel.

### gazam (גזם)
Radak: from *gozez*, because it cuts the grain. First in Yoel's sequence: the
*gazam* eats, and what it leaves the *arbeh* eats (Yoel 1:4). Targum: *zachala*,
a crawling thing (compare Devarim 32:24, where the word means a snake).

### yelek (ילק)
Radak: from *melakek*, to lick, for its habit of licking and chewing at the
grass. Third in Yoel's sequence. Nachum 3:15 describes the *yelek* as one that
"spreads out and flies away," and on that Malbim builds a life cycle: born as a
kind of worm, then a cocoon, then emerging winged. Rabbi Klein adds that after
consulting Ohr Somayach's resident zoologist, Rabbi Richard Jacobs, he has been
unable to confirm that such a grasshopper exists. Targum: *parcha*, a flying
thing.

### chasil (חסיל)
Radak: the one that finishes off (*chasal*) whatever produce remains, the word
of *chasal siddur Pesach* and of a *siyum*. Last in Yoel's sequence. Targum:
*shmota*.

### chagav (חגב)
One of the four permitted species (Vayikra 11:22), and, per Meiri (*Beis
HaBechirah*) and Rabbi Shmuel ben Meshullam of Gerona (*Ohel Mo'ed*), also the
general term for all kosher grasshoppers; see Rav Chaim Kanievsky, *Karnei
Chagavim* 2. The Gemara (Chullin 63b) says there are eight hundred kinds of
*chagav*. Aramaic (Chullin 65a): *sal'am* is *rishon*, *chargol* is *nipol*,
*chagav* is *gadyan* (see also Radak to Amos 7:1).

### tzfoni (צפוני)
Yoel 2:20, God's own promise if the Jewish People repent their sins and pray:
"I will distance the *tzfoni* from you." Rashi and Radak: another word for these
pests, so called because they usually migrate from the north (*tzafon*) of Eretz
Yisrael.

### tzlatzel (צלצל)
Rashi (to Devarim 28:42 and Bava Kama 116b): another kind of grasshopper.
Rabbeinu Bachya (to Devarim 28:42) and the commentary printed under Rashi's name
to Divrei HaYamim I 13:8 tie the word to *metziltayim*, cymbals, for the din
these insects make. *Pirush HaRokeach* connects grasshoppers to *tzel*, shade,
since a swarm crossing the sky throws a shadow on the ground beneath. Against
Rashi: Rav Saadia Gaon reads *tzlatzel* as butterflies, Ralbag as worms, and
Ramban as the clamor of enemy armies, not an insect at all.

### chanamal (חנמל)
Tehillim 78:47, in the retelling of the *makkos*. Rashi: grasshoppers. Other
*meforshim*: a kind of hail.

### How many were in Mitzrayim
*Midrash HaGadol* (to Shemos 10:14): seven of the nine kinds made up the *makkah*
of *arbeh*, namely *arbeh*, *sal'am*, *chargol*, *chagav*, *gazam*, *yelek* and
*chasil*. Rabbi Menachem Tzioni, a fourteenth-century German mekubal, adds
*chanamal* for eight, each of the eight containing a hundred sub-species, eight
hundred in all; *Midrash Sechel Tov* has the same tradition with *tzlatzel* in
place of *chanamal*. The number mirrors the Gemara's eight hundred kinds of
*chagav*.

## Rendering guidance

- Yoel 1:4 is the test case. "What the *gazam* left the *arbeh* ate, and what the
  *arbeh* left the *yelek* ate, and what the *yelek* left the *chasil* ate."
  Either transliterate all four, or give four different English words (cutter
  locust, swarming locust, licking locust, finishing locust) with a note. Never
  write "locust" four times; the escalation is the *nevuah*.
- Trap: English "grasshopper" and "locust" between them absorb all nine or ten
  words. Reserve "locust" for a swarm that devours (*arbeh* in the *makkah*,
  Yoel's four) and "grasshopper" for the *kashrus* discussion of Vayikra 11:22,
  where the insect is being classed, not feared.
- Vayikra 11:22: the four names are species and should stay transliterated, with
  a gloss, because no one can pin them to modern species with confidence. Where a
  passage rules on them, say with Meiri that *chagav* doubles as the class name.
- *Arbeh* used as an umbrella (Midrash HaGadol on Shemos 10:14, where the *arbeh*
  of the plague contains seven kinds) needs "the locust plague" or "the locust
  host," not "the *arbeh* species," or the Midrash contradicts itself in English.
- *Tzlatzel* in Devarim 28:42: if the passage is quoting Rashi, "locust"; if
  Ramban, "the clash of armies." The word cannot be rendered without deciding,
  so decide out loud in a note.
