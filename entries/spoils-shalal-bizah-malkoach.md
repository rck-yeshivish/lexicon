# Spoils of war: shalal / baz (bizah) / malkoach / shevi / ad

## Bottom line

*Shalal* is the general word for **spoils** (Malbim), and when it stands alone
it can cover anything taken in battle (Rav Hirsch). Paired with the other words
it is the clothing and jewelry (Rashi) or the clothing and vessels (Radak); for
the Gra it is the valuables, which are taken first. *Baz* / *bizah* is
**plunder**: for the Gra the lesser loot taken after the valuables, akin to
*bizayon*; for Rashi movable goods other than jewelry; for Malbim property
already taken as loot. *Malkoach* is **living booty**, people and animals
(Rashi); for Radak it is the animals when *shevi* stands beside it, and every
living thing when it stands alone. *Shevi* is **captives**: human prisoners
(Radak), all the captured people (Malbim), or living people or animals (the
Gra). On *ad* the authorities differ: plunder, as the root means in Aramaic
(Rashi); ornamental battle-dress (Pappenheim); specifically jewelry and
decorative items, where *shalal* is the catch-all (Mecklenburg); or dead animals
taken as war prizes, and not ordinary booty (Malbim). The account of the war
with Midian (Bamidbar 31) uses four of these words, each for something
different. Rabbi Klein holds that the words are not truly synonymous. The
Egyptians say "I will apportion the *shalal*" (Shemos 15:9), yet what the Jews
took from them is *bizas Mitzrayim* and *bizas hayam*, and not *shalal*.

## Distinctions

### shalal (שלל)
The word of the Shirah, "I will apportion the *shalal*" (Shemos 15:9).
- Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *shalal* is clothing and jewelry.
- Radak: clothing and vessels.
- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini (1230 to 1300) and the Maharal of Prague (*Gur
  Aryeh* there) cite Rashi's division approvingly and substantiate it from other
  places where these words or their cognates appear.
- Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer objects from Divrei HaYamim II 15:11, which speaks of
  offering captured *shalal* as korbanos, implying that *shalal* can mean
  animals, unlike Rashi and Radak.
- Rav Hirsch (to Bamidbar 31:11), making a point similar to Mecklenburg's that
  *shalal* is the catch-all (below), makes it a matter of company: where no
  other words are used alongside it, *shalal* can refer to anything pilfered in
  battle, animals and humans included, because ultimately all the spoils of war
  are alike in being taken through violence.
- Malbim: *shalal* is the general term for the defeated party's ownerless
  property, while *bizah* is specifically property already taken as loot.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740 to 1814) traces *shalal* to the
two-letter ש-ל, "moving something outside of its proper place," as in *shal
na'alecha*, "remove your shoes" (Shemos 3:5). Spoils are property taken away
from its previous owners as the prize of war. Rabbi Shmuel Ibn Tibbon (1150 to
1230), translating the *Moreh Nevuchim* from Judeo-Arabic into Hebrew, needed a
pair of words for "positive" and "negative." He re-appropriated *shalal* to
coin *shelilah*/*shelili* for "negative," because plundered property is
"lacking" and "missing," and redefined a cognate of *chayav*, "obligated," as
*chiyuv*/*chiyuvi* for "positive," because an obligation is a reality that
really exists.

*Shalal* is also a stitch of sewing (*Parah* 12:9; *Moed Katan* 22b), and the
Rosh to *Parah* there ties it to *shalal*, plunder.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bamidbar 31:26): *shalal* is related
to nakedness (Michah 1:8), and so means jewellery and clothing, what leaves a
person bare when taken.

### baz, bizah (בז, בזה)
- The Vilna Gaon (*Biur HaGra* to Yeshayahu 10:6) writes that plunder typically
  occurs in stages: first the valuables are taken, and those are *shalal*; then
  even the less important items, and those are *bizah*, a word related to
  *bizayon* (disgrace, disparagement) because they are not as prized as the
  first; finally everything left is sometimes taken away, so that the enemy is
  totally bereft.
- The Gra also explains (*Biur HaGra* to Divrei HaYamim II 14:13) that *bizah*
  is the non-living booty, *shevi* specifically the living people or animals
  taken in war.
- Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *baz* is movable property other than jewelry.

- Rabbi Pappenheim puts *baz*/*bizah* in the two-letter ב-ז, "considered
  unimportant," and gives three ways it fits: the plunderers degrade what they
  take by taking it for free, as though it were not worth paying for; having
  paid nothing they handle it callously; and the victims themselves, trying to
  save their lives, seem not to care about what they leave behind.
- Menachem Ibn Saruk agrees with the two-letter root.
- Ibn Janach and Radak assign ב-ז-ז instead, which differs from ב-ז-ה, the verb
  for disparaging.

### malkoach (מלקוח) and shevi (שבי)
- Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *malkoach* is living booty, people and animals.
- Radak: *shevi* is human prisoners and *malkoach* animals, but where *malkoach*
  appears later without *shevi* beside it (Bamidbar 31:27) it covers every
  living thing taken, human and animal.
- Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bamidbar 31:26): *shevi* is related
  to ש-ב, returning, because it is the people and things taken from the enemy
  that are *not* returned. So too *shovav* (Yeshayahu 57:17) is one who has lost
  his way and longs to return to his land and family, like the captive who wants
  to go home.
- Rabbi Pappenheim puts *shevi* / *shevuyah* under the two-letter ש-ב, which
  can refer to reverting to one's natural state (as in *Shabbos*, rest): the
  captive is one whose freedom of movement his captors have curtailed, as if
  stuck in a position of rest.
- Malbim makes the same relation here as between *shalal* and *bizah*: *shevi*
  is all the captured people of the defeated side, *malkoach* specifically those
  the captors took as slaves or for other purposes.
- The root of *malkoach* is ל-ק-ח, "taking." Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein (1860
  to 1940) explains why there are two words for living booty (*malkoach*) and
  inanimate booty (*shalal*): inanimate objects are taken by hand or physical
  force, while living creatures are often taken verbally, by persuasion or
  command.

### ad (עד)
"Binyamin is a wolf that tears; in the morning he devours *ad* and in the
evening he divides *shalal*" (Bereishis 49:27).
- Rashi: *ad* here is plunder, and the root ע-ד carries that meaning in Aramaic.
  Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (1469 to 1549) points out that most times Tanach uses
  *shalal* or *bizah*, the Targumim render them with Aramaic cognates of *ad*.
  The Aramaic root also means removal (Targum Onkelos to Shemos 25:15) and even
  atonement, the removal of sin (Targum to Yeshayahu 47:11), which Rabbi Klein
  relates to booty as the removal of property from the loser's domain.
- Rabbi Pappenheim derives it instead from *adi*, "jewelry, ornament," and
  limits it to the ornamental clothes soldiers wore to battle and were stripped
  of when captured.
- Malbim: *ad* is specifically dead animals taken as war prizes.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785 to 1865) takes *ad* from *ad* in the sense
  of transferring, since booty is a change of ownership, a sense similar to the
  commoner homonym *ad*, "until, through"; he holds that *shalal* is the
  catch-all for everything taken as booty while *ad* is specifically jewelry and
  decorative items.

## Rendering guidance

- Standing alone, *shalal* is **spoils** or **the spoils of war** (Rav Hirsch);
  in a list it narrows to **clothing and valuables** (Rashi, Radak).
- *Bizah*: **plunder** or **loot**, and where the Gra's staging matters, "the
  lesser loot." *Bizas Mitzrayim* and *bizas hayam* keep the transliteration.
- *Shevi*: **captives**. *Malkoach*: **live captives** or **the captured
  livestock**, according to whether *shevi* stands beside it (Radak).
- *Ad* at Bereishis 49:27: **prey** works for the wolf image, and Rashi's
  "plunder" is close to it; where the Pappenheim or Mecklenburg reading is
  wanted, **ornaments** or **finery taken in battle**.
- Trap: "booty" and "spoils" are near-synonyms in English and will flatten the
  Midian account of Bamidbar 31 into a single word repeated. Assign one English
  word per Hebrew word for the length of the passage (spoils / plunder / live
  captives / captives) and hold to it even where it reads slightly stiff.
- Trap: for the Gra *bizah* is akin to *bizayon*, and for Rabbi Pappenheim its
  root means "considered unimportant"; Ibn Janach and Radak put it in a root
  distinct from the verb of disparaging. Where a derashah turns on the sense of
  disgrace (the Gra on Yeshayahu 10:6), do not render *bizah* with a dignified
  word.
