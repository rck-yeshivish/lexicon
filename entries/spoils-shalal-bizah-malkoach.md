# Spoils of war: shalal / baz (bizah) / malkoach / shevi / ad

## Bottom line

*Shalal* is the general word for **spoils** (Malbim; standing alone, Rav
Hirsch); paired with others it is the clothing and jewelry (Rashi) or clothing
and vessels (Radak), the valuables taken first (the Gra). *Baz* / *bizah* is
**plunder**: the lesser loot taken after the valuables, akin to *bizayon* (the
Gra); movable goods other than jewelry (Rashi); property already taken as loot
(Malbim). *Malkoach* is **living booty**, people and animals (Rashi); for Radak,
animals beside *shevi*, every living thing alone. *Shevi* is **captives**: human
prisoners (Radak), all the captured people (Malbim), or living people or animals
(the Gra). *Ad* the column leaves unsettled: plunder, as in Aramaic (Rashi);
ornamental battle-dress (Pappenheim); jewelry and decorative items, against
*shalal* the catch-all (Mecklenburg); or dead animals taken as war prizes, not
ordinary booty (Malbim). The trap: English "spoils" and "booty" cover all five,
and the Midian account (Bamidbar 31) uses four of them, each doing separate
work. The column's frame: the words are not truly synonymous, for the Egyptians
say "I will apportion the *shalal*" (Shemos 15:9), yet what the Jews took from
them is *bizas Mitzrayim* and *bizas hayam*, not *shalal*.

## Distinctions

### shalal (שלל)
The word of the Shirah, "I will apportion the *shalal*" (Shemos 15:9). Rashi (to
Bamidbar 31:11): *shalal* is clothing and jewelry. Radak: clothing and vessels.
Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini (1230 to 1300) and the Maharal of Prague (*Gur
Aryeh* there) cite Rashi's division approvingly and substantiate it from other
places where these words or their cognates appear. Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer
objects from Divrei HaYamim II 15:11, which speaks of offering captured *shalal*
as korbanos, implying that *shalal* can mean animals, unlike Rashi and Radak.
Separately, and in the column as a parallel to Mecklenburg on *ad* (below)
rather than as an answer to Sofer, Rav Hirsch (to Bamidbar 31:11) makes it a
matter of company: where no other words are used alongside it, *shalal* can
refer to anything pilfered in battle, animals and humans included, because
ultimately all the spoils of war are alike in being taken through violence.
Malbim: *shalal* is the general term for the defeated party's ownerless
property, while *bizah* is specifically property already taken as loot.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740 to 1814) traces *shalal* to the
two-letter ש-ל, "moving something outside of its proper place," as in *shal
na'alecha*, "remove your shoes" (Shemos 3:5). Spoils are property taken away
from its previous owners as the prize of war. Rabbi Shmuel Ibn Tibbon (1150 to
1230), translating the *Moreh Nevuchim* from Judeo-Arabic into Hebrew, needed a
pair of words for "positive" and "negative." He re-appropriated *shalal* to
coin *shelilah*/*shelili* for "negative," because plundered property is
"lacking" and "missing," and redefined a cognate of *chayav*, "obligated," as
*chiyuv*/*chiyuvi* for "positive," because an obligation is a reality that
really exists.

### baz, bizah (בז, בזה)
The Vilna Gaon (*Biur HaGra* to Yeshayahu 10:6) writes that plunder typically
occurs in stages: first the valuables are taken, and those are *shalal*; then
even the less important items, and those are *bizah*, a word related to
*bizayon* (disgrace, disparagement) because they are not as prized as the
first; finally everything left is sometimes taken away, so that the enemy is
totally bereft. The Gra also explains (*Biur HaGra* to Divrei HaYamim II 14:13)
that *bizah* is the non-living booty, *shevi* specifically the living people or
animals taken in war. Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *baz* is movable property other
than jewelry.

Rabbi Pappenheim puts *baz*/*bizah* in the two-letter ב-ז, "considered
unimportant," and gives three ways it fits: the plunderers degrade what they
take by taking it for free, as though it were not worth paying for; having paid
nothing they handle it callously; and the victims themselves, trying to save
their lives, seem not to care about what they leave behind. Menachem Ibn Saruk
agrees with the two-letter root; Ibn Janach and Radak assign ב-ז-ז instead,
which differs from ב-ז-ה, the verb for disparaging.

### malkoach (מלקוח) and shevi (שבי)
Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *malkoach* is living booty, people and animals.
Radak: *shevi* is human prisoners and *malkoach* animals, but where *malkoach*
appears later without *shevi* beside it (Bamidbar 31:27) it covers every living
thing taken, human and animal. Malbim makes the same relation here as between
*shalal* and *bizah*: *shevi* is all the captured people of the defeated side,
*malkoach* specifically those the captors took as slaves or for other purposes.
The root of *malkoach* is ל-ק-ח, "taking." Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein (1860 to
1940) explains why there are two words for living booty (*malkoach*) and
inanimate booty (*shalal*): inanimate objects are taken by hand or physical
force, while living creatures are often taken verbally, by persuasion or
command.

### ad (עד)
"Binyamin is a wolf that tears; in the morning he devours *ad* and in the evening
he divides *shalal*" (Bereishis 49:27). Rashi: *ad* here is plunder, and the root
ע-ד carries that meaning in Aramaic. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (1469 to 1549)
points out that most times Tanach uses *shalal* or *bizah*, the Targumim render
them with Aramaic cognates of *ad*. The Aramaic root also means removal (Targum
Onkelos to Shemos 25:15) and even atonement, the removal of sin (Targum to
Yeshayahu 47:1, as the column cites it), which the column relates to booty as
the removal of property from the loser's domain. Rabbi Pappenheim derives it
instead from *adi*, "jewelry, ornament," and limits it to the ornamental clothes
soldiers wore to battle and were stripped of when captured. Malbim: *ad* is not
ordinary booty but specifically dead animals taken as war prizes. Rabbi Yaakov
Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785 to 1865) takes *ad* from *ad* in the sense of
transferring, since booty is a change of ownership, a sense similar to the
commoner homonym *ad*, "until, through"; he holds that *shalal* is the catch-all
for everything taken as booty while *ad* is specifically jewelry and decorative
items.

## Rendering guidance

- Standing alone, *shalal* is **spoils** or **the spoils of war** (Rav Hirsch);
  in a list it narrows to **clothing and valuables** (Rashi, Radak).
- *Bizah*: **plunder** or **loot**, and where the Gra's staging matters, "the
  lesser loot." *Bizas Mitzrayim* and *bizas hayam* keep the transliteration.
- *Shevi*: **captives**. *Malkoach*: **live captives** or **the captured
  livestock**, according to whether *shevi* stands beside it (Radak).
- *Ad* at Bereishis 49:27: **prey** works for the wolf image, and Rashi's
  "plunder" is close to it; where the Pappenheim or Mecklenburg reading is
  wanted, **ornaments** or **finery taken in battle**.
- Trap: "booty" and "spoils" are near-synonyms in English and will flatten the
  Midian account of Bamidbar 31 into a single word repeated. Assign one English
  word per Hebrew word for the length of the passage (spoils / plunder / live
  captives / captives) and hold to it even where it reads slightly stiff.
- Trap: for the Gra *bizah* is akin to *bizayon*, and for Rabbi Pappenheim its
  root means "considered unimportant"; Ibn Janach and Radak put it in a root
  distinct from the verb of disparaging. Where a derashah turns on the sense of
  disgrace (the Gra on Yeshayahu 10:6), do not render *bizah* with a dignified
  word.
