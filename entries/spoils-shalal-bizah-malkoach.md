# Spoils of war: shalal / baz (bizah) / malkoach / shevi / ad

## Bottom line

*Shalal* is the general word for **spoils**, and where it is paired with others
it means the clothing, vessels, and valuables. *Baz* / *bizah* is the **lesser
loot**, what is stripped afterward, named from a root of contempt. *Malkoach* is
**live captives**, animals, and where it stands alone, people and animals both.
*Shevi* is **human captives**. *Ad* is booty in the sense of **ornaments**
stripped from the fallen. The trap: English "spoils" and "booty" will cover all
five, and Bamidbar 31:11-12 uses four of them in two pesukim, where each is
doing separate work.

## Distinctions

### shalal (שלל)
The word of the Shirah, "I will divide the *shalal*" (Shemos 15:9). Rashi (to
Bamidbar 31:11): *shalal* is clothing and jewelry. Radak: clothing and vessels.
Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini and the Maharal (*Gur Aryeh* there) endorse
Rashi's division and support it from other appearances. Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer
objects from Divrei HaYamim II 15:11, where captured *shalal* is offered as
korbanos, so *shalal* can include animals against Rashi and Radak. Rav Hirsch
(to Bamidbar 31:11) resolves it as a matter of company: standing alone, *shalal*
can mean anything taken in battle, animals and people included, since all spoils
are alike in being taken by force; it narrows only when other words stand
beside it. Malbim: *shalal* is the general term for the defeated party's
ownerless property, while *bizah* is specifically property already taken as
loot.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces *shalal* to the two-letter ש-ל, "moving
something out of its proper place," as in *shal na'alecha*, "remove your shoes"
(Shemos 3:5). Spoils are property moved off its owner as the prize of war. On
that sense Rabbi Shmuel Ibn Tibbon, translating the *Moreh Nevuchim*,
re-appropriated *shalal* to coin *shelilah*/*shelili* for "negative," since
plundered property is what is now lacking, and paired it with *chiyuv* for
"positive."

### baz, bizah (בז, בזה)
The Vilna Gaon (*Biur HaGra* to Yeshayahu 10:6) reads plunder as staged: first
the valuables are taken, and those are *shalal*; then the lesser items, and
those are *bizah*, a word akin to *bizayon* (disgrace) because they are not
prized like the first; finally whatever remains is stripped so the enemy is left
with nothing. Elsewhere (*Biur HaGra* to Divrei HaYamim II 14:13) he draws the
line differently: *bizah* is the non-living booty, *shevi* the living. Rashi (to
Bamidbar 31:11): *baz* is movable property other than jewelry.

Rabbi Pappenheim puts *baz*/*bizah* in the two-letter ב-ז, "reckoned
unimportant," and gives three ways it fits: the plunderers degrade what they
take by taking it for free, as though it were not worth paying for; having paid
nothing they handle it carelessly; and the victims themselves, fleeing for their
lives, show they no longer value what they leave behind. Ibn Saruk agrees with
the two-letter root; Ibn Janach and Radak assign ב-ז-ז instead, distinct from
ב-ז-ה, the verb of disparaging.

### malkoach (מלקוח) and shevi (שבי)
Rashi (to Bamidbar 31:11): *malkoach* is living booty, people and animals.
Radak: *shevi* is human prisoners and *malkoach* animals, but where *malkoach*
appears later without *shevi* beside it (Bamidbar 31:27) it covers every living
thing taken. Malbim makes the same relation here as between *shalal* and
*bizah*: *shevi* is all the captured people of the defeated side, *malkoach*
specifically those the captors took for themselves, as slaves or otherwise. The
root of *malkoach* is ל-ק-ח, "taking." Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein explains why
Hebrew needs separate words for living and inanimate booty: inanimate objects
are taken by hand and by force, while living creatures are often taken by word,
by command or persuasion.

### ad (עד)
"Binyamin is a wolf that tears; in the morning he devours *ad* and in the evening
he divides *shalal*" (Bereishis 49:27). Rashi: *ad* here is plunder, and the root
ע-ד carries that meaning in Aramaic. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur notes that the
Targumim usually render Hebrew *shalal* and *bizah* with Aramaic cognates of
*ad*; the Aramaic root also means removal (Targum Onkelos to Shemos 25:15) and
even atonement, the removal of sin (Targum to Yeshayahu 47:1), which suits booty
as the removal of property from the loser's domain. Rabbi Pappenheim derives it
instead from *adi*, "ornament," and limits it to the decorative battle-dress
soldiers wore and were stripped of when captured. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg
takes *ad* from *ad* in the sense of transferring, since booty is a change of
ownership, and holds that *shalal* is the catch-all while *ad* is specifically
jewelry and decorative items. Malbim reads *ad* narrowly as dead animals taken
as war prizes.

## Rendering guidance

- Standing alone, *shalal* is **spoils** or **the spoils of war**; in a list it
  narrows to **clothing and valuables**.
- *Bizah*: **plunder** or **loot**, and where the Gra's staging matters, "the
  lesser loot." *Bizas Mitzrayim* and *bizas hayam* keep the transliteration.
- *Shevi*: **captives**. *Malkoach*: **live captives** or **the captured
  livestock**, according to whether *shevi* stands beside it.
- *Ad* at Bereishis 49:27: **prey** works for the wolf image; where the
  Pappenheim or Mecklenburg reading is wanted, **ornaments** or **finery taken
  in battle**.
- Trap: "booty" and "spoils" are near-synonyms in English and will flatten
  Bamidbar 31:11-12 into a single word repeated four times. Assign one English
  word per Hebrew word for the length of the passage (spoils / plunder / live
  captives / captives) and hold to it even where it reads slightly stiff.
- Trap: *bizah* carries contempt in its root and *shalal* does not. Where a
  derashah turns on that (the Gra on Yeshayahu 10:6), do not render *bizah* with
  a dignified word.
