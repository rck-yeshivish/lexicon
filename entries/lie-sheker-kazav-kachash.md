# Falsehood: sheker / kazav / kachash

## Bottom line

*Sheker* is **falsehood** outright, false already when it was said. Whether it
must also be knowing is left open: Rabbi Edel and Mecklenburg say yes, and Rabbi
Klein offers a possible defense of Albo's broader *sheker*, which covers the
inadvertent untruth too.
*Kazav* is **a lie that time exposes**: it sounded true, and only later proves
false (the Gra: he meant it when he said it and then did not keep his word).
*Kachash* is **denial**, a false answer to a claim already standing against
him. The three are not interchangeable; the Yom Kippur confession *b'kachash
uvchazav* names two of them side by side.

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

## Distinctions

### sheker (שקר)
The Torah's own word: *midvar sheker tirchak* (Shemos 23:7), and *lo teshakru*
in the prohibition of Vayikra 19:11.

- Rabbi Yosef Albo (*Sefer HaIkkarim* 2:27) works from *emes*, truth, as the
  antonym of both *sheker* and *kazav*: truth requires agreement between the
  statement and reality, and between the mouth and the heart. *Sheker* is the
  break in the first, a statement out of joint with the way things are.
- Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel presses him: if *sheker* is any objective untruth, how
  can the Torah forbid testifying *sheker* or swearing *sheker* (Vayikra
  19:11-12), given that a man can present things only as he perceives them, and
  given that Shevuos 26a exempts one who swore falsely while thinking that what
  he said was true? To Rabbi Edel this suggests *sheker* must carry an element
  of advertent deceit.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg reaches the same place from the wording: the
  Torah writes *lo teshakru* and not *lo sechazvu*, which shows the prohibition
  covers deliberate lying and not mistaken lying (on his reading *kazav* is the
  mistaken lie, and *sheker* the purposeful or deceptive one).
- Rabbi Klein suggests, though, that Rabbi Albo may be defended by holding that
  the general definition of *sheker* does cover any objective untruth,
  inadvertent or wanton, and that the Gemara is applying a separate scriptural
  imperative, derived from Vayikra 5:4, which limits the prohibition of false
  testimony and false oaths to one who perjures knowingly. On that defense
  Albo's broad *sheker* stands.
- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 9:21, 21:23, 43:11), taking kuf and kaf as
  interchangeable, joins *sheker* to *shikur*, a drunkard: as the drunk's
  imagination throws up what has no place in reality, *sheker* is what stands
  outside the real.
- Shadal (to Shemos 20:7): *shav* is related to *nashaf*, *nashav* and *sha'af*,
  breath and air, nothingness; from there it widened to anything false, a
  statement that is only air with no substance.

### kazav (כזב)
Scripture uses it plainly for falsehood: "the remnant of Israel do no iniquity
and speak no *kazav*" (Tzefaniah 3:13).
- Albo: *kazav* is the break in the second equation, the mouth saying what the
  heart does not hold.
- The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 12:25) draws the line by time instead: *sheker* was
  a lie the whole time, *kazav* became a lie afterward. A man who says he will
  do what he never intended has spoken *sheker*; a man who meant it and then
  failed to keep his word has spoken *kazav*. Rabbeinu Yosef Bechor Shor (to
  Bamidbar 23:19) likewise reads *kozev* as one who does not keep his word.
  Rabbi Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter draws the consequence: the Torah never
  forbade *saying kazav*, because at the moment of speech there is no *kazav*
  yet; it becomes one retroactively.
- Malbim (*Yair Ohr*): *sheker* has no validity at all, while *kazav* has some
  purchase on reality, seeming true at first and only later fully exposed. In
  *Sefer HaCarmel* he adds that one utterance can sometimes be both at once:
  *sheker* from the speaker's side, who knew, and *kazav* from the hearer's
  side, who cannot yet disprove it. That is what the phrase *sheker v'chazav*
  holds together.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 7:11, 21:17) links the root כ-ז-ב to כ-ש-ף, witchcraft:
  what looks real on the surface and proves wholly untrue, and Yechezkel indeed
  uses *kazav* of witchery (13:6, 13:7, 13:9, 21:34, 22:28).

### kachash (כחש)
Usually rendered "denial," and forbidden in the same pasuk as *sheker* (Vayikra
19:11).
- Malbim (*Sefer HaCarmel*): a *sheker* meets no immediate contradiction when it
  is spoken, while a *kachash* is contradicted before it is uttered, said into
  the teeth of the other party's claim.
- Rav Hirsch (to Vayikra 5:21, 19:11, Devarim 9:7) makes it a false *reaction*
  to another's claim, and contrasts it with *ka'as*, anger (taking ches with
  ayin and shin with samech as interchangeable): *ka'as* is a real and warranted
  response to another's wrongdoing, *kachash* a manufactured response of denial
  to another's warranted claim.
- Rabbi Yonah Wilheimer sets *kazav* against *kachash* as the two shapes a lie
  can take: *kazav* says of what is not that it is (fabrication), *kachash* says
  of what is that it is not (denial). It would seem, Rabbi Klein adds, that on
  Rabbi Wilheimer's reading *sheker* is then an umbrella term over both.

Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer seems to grade all three by how soon the lie can
be caught: *sheker*, everyone knows it is false the moment it leaves the mouth;
*kachash*, plausible but not refutable outright; *kazav*, any lie whose falsity
can be discovered only later.

## Rendering guidance

- *Sheker*: **falsehood** in a pasuk (*midvar sheker tirchak*, "keep far from a
  false matter"), **a lie** in plain prose. Never "a mistake."
- *Kazav*: **a lie**, and where the passage turns on the Gra's or Malbim's
  distinction, **a lie that time exposes** or **a broken word**. In Yechezkel's
  prophecies against the false prophets, **falsehood** with the divination
  sense noted.
- *Kachash*: **denial**, or **denying**. Reserve "deceit" for *sheker*.
- Trap: English "lie" will absorb all three. The confession *al cheit shechatanu
  lefanecha b'kachash uvchazav* must not read "through lies and lies"; render
  "through denial and through falsehood."
- Second trap: *sheker v'chazav* is a real pair, not a doublet for emphasis.
  Malbim's reading gives you the two vantage points, so render it "false and
  deceptive" rather than collapsing it.
- Where Albo's frame is in play, *sheker* is **out of joint with reality** and
  *kazav* is **out of joint with the heart**; keep the two mismatches visible.
