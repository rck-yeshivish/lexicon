# Falsehood: sheker / kazav / kachash

## Bottom line

*Sheker* is **falsehood** outright, false when it was said and knowingly so.
*Kazav* is **a lie that time exposes**: it sounded true, and only later proves
false (the Gra: he meant it when he said it and then did not keep his word).
*Kachash* is **denial**, a false answer to a claim already standing against
him. The trap is rendering all three "lie"; the Yom Kippur confession *b'kachash
uvchazav* needs two different English words, and a *sheker* is never merely a
mistake.

## Distinctions

### sheker (שקר)
The Torah's own word: *midvar sheker tirchak* (Shemos 23:7), and *lo
teshakru* in the prohibition of Vayikra 19:11. Rabbi Yosef Albo (*Sefer
HaIkkarim* 2:27) works from *emes*, truth, as the antonym of both *sheker* and
*kazav*: truth requires agreement between the statement and reality, and
between the mouth and the heart. *Sheker* is the break in the first, a
statement out of joint with the way things are. Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel presses
him: if *sheker* is any objective untruth, how can the Torah forbid swearing
*sheker*, given that a man can testify only as he perceives things, and given
that Shevuos 26a exempts one who swore what he believed to be true? So *sheker*
must carry deliberate deceit. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg reaches the same
place from the wording: the Torah writes *lo teshakru* and not *lo sechazvu*,
which shows the prohibition covers deliberate lying and not mistaken lying.
Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 9:21, 21:23, 43:11), taking kuf and kaf as
interchangeable, joins *sheker* to *shikur*, a drunkard: as the drunk's
imagination throws up what has no place in reality, *sheker* is what stands
outside the real.

### kazav (כזב)
Albo: *kazav* is the break in the second equation, the mouth saying what the
heart does not hold. The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 12:25) draws the line by time
instead: *sheker* was a lie the whole time, *kazav* became a lie afterward. A
man who says he will do what he never intended has spoken *sheker*; a man who
meant it and then failed to keep his word has spoken *kazav*. Rabbeinu Yosef
Bechor Shor (to Bamidbar 23:19) likewise reads *kozev* as one who does not keep
his word. Rabbi Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter draws the consequence: the
Torah never forbade *saying kazav*, because at the moment of speech there is no
*kazav* yet; it becomes one retroactively. Malbim (*Yair Ohr*): *sheker* has no
validity at all, while *kazav* has some purchase on reality, seeming true at
first and only later fully exposed. In *Sefer HaCarmel* he adds that one
utterance can be both at once: *sheker* from the speaker's side, who knew, and
*kazav* from the hearer's side, who cannot yet disprove it. That is what the
phrase *sheker v'chazav* holds together. Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 7:11, 21:17)
links the root כ-ז-ב to כ-ש-ף, witchcraft: what looks real on the surface and
proves wholly untrue, and Yechezkel indeed uses *kazav* of witchery (13:6, 13:7,
13:9, 21:34, 22:28).

### kachash (כחש)
Usually rendered "denial," and forbidden in the same pasuk as *sheker* (Vayikra
19:11). Malbim (*Sefer HaCarmel*): a *sheker* meets no immediate contradiction
when it is spoken, while a *kachash* is contradicted before it is uttered, said
into the teeth of the other party's claim. Rav Hirsch (to Vayikra 5:21, 19:11,
Devarim 9:7) makes it a false *reaction* to another's claim, and contrasts it
with *ka'as*, anger (taking ches with ayin and shin with samech as
interchangeable): *ka'as* is a real and warranted response to another's
wrongdoing, *kachash* a manufactured response of denial to another's warranted
claim. Rabbi Yonah Wilheimer sets *kazav* against *kachash* as the two shapes a
lie can take: *kazav* says of what is not that it is (fabrication), *kachash*
says of what is that it is not (denial); on his reading *sheker* is the
umbrella over both.

Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer grades all three by how soon the lie can be
caught: *sheker*, everyone knows it is false the moment it leaves the mouth;
*kachash*, plausible but not refutable outright; *kazav*, discoverable only
later.

## Rendering guidance

- *Sheker*: **falsehood** in a pasuk (*midvar sheker tirchak*, "keep far from a
  false matter"), **a lie** in plain prose. Never "a mistake."
- *Kazav*: **a lie**, and where the passage turns on the Gra's or Malbim's
  distinction, **a lie that time exposes** or **a broken word**. In Yechezkel's
  prophecies against the false prophets, **falsehood** with the divination
  sense noted.
- *Kachash*: **denial**, or **denying**. Reserve "deceit" for *sheker*.
- Trap: English "lie" will absorb all three. The confession *al cheit shechatanu
  lefanecha b'kachash uvchazav* must not read "through lies and lies"; render
  "through denial and through falsehood."
- Second trap: *sheker v'chazav* is a real pair, not a doublet for emphasis.
  Malbim's reading gives you the two vantage points, so render it "false and
  deceptive" rather than collapsing it.
- Where Albo's frame is in play, *sheker* is **out of joint with reality** and
  *kazav* is **out of joint with the heart**; keep the two mismatches visible.
