# Angel classes: kruvim / chayos / ofanim / erelim / ishim

## Bottom line

These are not synonyms for one another; they are ranks. Rambam (*Hilchos Yesodei
HaTorah* 2:7) counts ten, from the *chayos* at the top down to the *ishim* at
the bottom: *chayos*, *ofanim*, *erelim*, *chashmalim*, *serafim*, *malachim*,
*elohim*, *bnei elohim*, *kruvim*, *ishim*. Render *kruvim* as **cherubim** and
leave the rest transliterated; the one that also does double duty is *malachim*,
which is both a rank of its own and the general word for **angels**. The trap is
translating any of the others as "angel," which collapses a hierarchy into one
word.

## Distinctions

### the lists
Rambam's ten are as above. The *Orchos Chaim* (*Seder Tefillas Shabbos
Shacharis* §2) and the *Kolbo* (§37) cite the tradition as nine, dropping
*malachim*, *elohim*, *bnei elohim*, and *ishim*, and adding *galgalim*, *irin*,
and *kadishin*. The kabbalistic *Masechta Atzilus* has ten that follow Rambam
but replace *chayos* and *elohim* with *shananim* and *tarshishim*. Rabbi Moshe
ibn Chaviv notes that angels are also called *abirim* (Tehillim 78:25; the
article prints 75:25). Rabbi Dovid Luria, in his glosses to *Pirkei d'Rabbi
Eliezer* ch. 4, understands that midrash to hold *ofanim* and *galgalim*
synonymous, and *kruvim* and *chayos* likewise.

### chayos (חיות)
The highest created rank. The anonymous commentator to Rambam brings two
explanations. They appeared to Yechezkel in the form of *chayos*, wild animals,
because in this world man stands at the peak of creation and animals do not; so
the highest of the angels appeared as animals to teach that even the highest
creature Above is as an animal beside Hashem. Alternatively they are so called
because through them the *chiyus*, the life-force, is given to all lower
creations.

### ofanim (אופנים)
Second rank; the word also means "wheel." If the Divine glory is likened to a
chariot, as in Yechezkel's visions, the *ofan* is the wheel that carries it. In
works of the school of Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms, the *ofan* is the inside
of the wheel and the *galgal* its outside.

### erelim (אראלים)
Third rank (Yeshayahu 33:7). The anonymous commentator: the word denotes
something strong or important, as the Beis HaMikdash and the Mizbeach are called
*Ariel* and *Harel* (Yechezkel 43:15). The school of the Rokeach: the *erelim*
announce Hashem's decrees to the other angels. Bar Kappara announced the passing
of Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi by saying that the *erelim* and the righteous both took
hold of the Holy Ark, the *erelim* prevailed, and the Ark was captured (Kesubos
104a), the *erelim* being those charged with carrying out the decree.

### chashmalim (חשמלים)
Fourth rank; they reveal themselves to the nevi'im in flashes of fire. The
Gemara (Chagigah 13b) reads the name as *chash* (silent) and *mal* (speaking),
because at times they speak of Hashem's glory and at times they are silent. The
school of the Rokeach reads it as *chashuv* (important, from their nearness to
the Divine glory) and *mal* (speaking).

### serafim (שרפים)
Fifth rank, named for their appearance, so awesome that one who gazes on them is
burned up (*saruf*).

### malachim (מלאכים)
Sixth rank, and also the general word for every rank, which the anonymous
commentator likens to *Taharos*, the name of a whole seder of Mishnah and also
of one masechta within it. The school of the Rokeach takes the rank as those
angels sent to this world on particular missions.

### elohim (אלהים) and bnei elohim (בני אלהים)
Seventh and eighth. *Elohim* names any entity in a position of power or
authority, and is used of an angel, a judge, a navi, and of Hashem Himself. The
*bnei elohim* are so called because they are secondary to the *elohim*.

### kruvim (כרובים)
Ninth. Many meforshim derive *kruv* from the Aramaic *ravia* (lad), found in
Targum to Bereishis 21:17 and elsewhere, with the kaf as the prefix "like." That
is the basis for the Gemara's statement (Chagigah 13b, Sukkah 5b) that the
*kruvim* had the faces of children. Whether the two on the Aron were two boys or
a boy and a girl is disputed; other sources give them the form of birds, or a
child-bird combination. The word also names the destructive angels set to guard
the way to the Eitz HaChaim (Rashi to Bereishis 3:24).

### ishim (אישים)
The lowest rank in Rambam's list, the angels who speak with human nevi'im.
Literally "men," because their level is close to that of the nevi'im they
address. Rabbi Mordechai Shlomo Movshovitz explains instead that they sometimes
act like men, as the three who came to Avraham (Bereishis 18) looked and behaved
like people.

### irin (עירין) and kadishin (קדישין)
Not on Rambam's list. The school of the Rokeach: *kadishin* are holy and set
apart in that they do not carry out decrees themselves but tell other angels
what to do; *irin* are sent to the cities (*ir*) of this world to observe men's
deeds and to give particular dreams. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg derives
*irin* instead from *eir* (awake), since they are always awake and watching what
people do.

## Rendering guidance

- Transliterate the rank names: *chayos*, *ofanim*, *erelim*, *chashmalim*,
  *serafim*, *ishim*, *irin*, *kadishin*. Gloss each once at first occurrence.
- *Kruvim* is the exception: **cherubim** is settled English for the two on the
  Aron, and reads properly in a sefer.
- *Malachim*: **angels** where the word is generic, transliterated where a
  source is listing the ten ranks and *malachim* is one of them. This is the
  place where a translator most easily loses the sense, because the same English
  word would then appear twice in one list at two different heights.
- Trap: "angel" absorbs every word in this entry. When a passage runs the ranks
  in order, transliterate the whole list rather than translating any member of
  it, and let the introduction say "ten classes of angels."
- *Elohim* as a rank of angels is not the Divine Name; render it by the class
  name and let the context carry it. Where a pasuk's *elohim* is being read as
  angels or as judges, say which, since the English cannot show it.
- On *malach* against *shaliach* and the general word for a messenger, see the
  separate entry `angel-malach-shaliach.md`.
