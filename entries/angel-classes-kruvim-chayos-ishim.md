# Angel classes: kruvim / chayos / ofanim / erelim / ishim

## Bottom line

These words name the ranks of angels. Rambam (*Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah* 2:7)
counts ten, from highest to lowest: *chayos*, *ofanim*, *erelim*, *chashmalim*,
*serafim*, *malachim*, *elohim*, *bnei elohim*, *kruvim*, *ishim*. *Kruvim* are
the **cherubim**. *Malachim* does double duty: it is a rank of its own and also
the general word for **angels** (the anonymous commentator to Rambam). Rabbi
Dovid Luria, though, understands *Pirkei d'Rabbi Eliezer* to hold *kruvim* and
*chayos* synonymous, and *ofanim* and *galgalim* likewise.

## Distinctions

### the lists
- Rambam's ten are as above.
- The *Orchos Chaim* (*Seder Tefillas Shabbos Shacharis* §2) and the *Kolbo*
  (§37) cite the tradition as nine, dropping *malachim*, *elohim*, *bnei
  elohim*, and [*ishim*](man-adam-ish-enosh-gever.html#%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9), and adding *galgalim*, *irin*, and *kadishin*.
- The kabbalistic *Masechta Atzilus* has ten that follow Rambam but replace
  *chayos* and *elohim* with *shananim* and *tarshishim*.
- Rabbi Moshe ibn Chaviv notes that angels are also called *abirim* (Tehillim
  78:25).
- Rabbi Dovid Luria, in his glosses to *Pirkei d'Rabbi Eliezer* ch. 4,
  understands that midrash to hold *ofanim* and *galgalim* synonymous, and
  *kruvim* and *chayos* likewise.

### chayos (חיות)
The highest created rank: they are not the highest of all entities, only the
highest of all *created* entities in the Upper Realms.

- The anonymous commentator to Rambam brings two explanations, the first of them
  as what "some explain": they appeared to Yechezkel in the form of *chayos*,
  wild animals, because in this world man stands at the peak of creation and
  animals do not, so the highest of the angels appeared as animals to teach the
  [*navi*](prophet-navi-roeh-chozeh.html#%D7%A0%D7%91%D7%99%D7%90) that even the highest creature Above is as an animal beside Hashem.
- Alternatively they are so called because through them the *chiyus*, the
  life-force, is given to all lower creations.

### ofanim (אופנים)
Second rank; the word also means "wheel." If the Divine glory is likened to a
chariot, as in Yechezkel's visions, the *ofan* is the wheel that carries it. In
several works ascribed to the school of Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms, the *ofan*
is the inside of the wheel and the *galgal* its outside, meaning its spokes.

### erelim (אראלים)
Third rank (Yeshayahu 33:7).

- The anonymous commentator: the word denotes something strong or important, as
  the Beis HaMikdash and the Mizbeach are called *Ariel* and *Harel* (Yechezkel
  43:15).
- The school of the Rokeach: the *erelim* tell the other angels about Hashem's
  decrees.
- Bar Kappara, a talmid of Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, is said to have announced his
  rebbi's passing by proclaiming that the *erelim* and the afflicted, meaning
  the human righteous, both took hold of the Holy Ark, the *erelim* overpowered
  the afflicted, and the Ark was captured (Kesubos 104a), the *erelim* being
  those charged with carrying out the decree.

### chashmalim (חשמלים)
Fourth rank; they reveal themselves to the nevi'im in fiery flashes of light.

- The Gemara (Chagigah 13b) reads the name as *chash* (quiet) and *mal* (speak),
  because at times they speak of Hashem's glory and at times they remain quiet.
- The school of the Rokeach reads it as a portmanteau of *chashuv* (important,
  on account of their nearness to the Divine glory) and *mal* (speak, since they
  speak of Hashem's holiness).

In Modern Hebrew *chashmal* means electricity.

### serafim (שרפים)
Fifth rank. The anonymous commentator: they are named for their appearance, which
is so awesome that one who gazes on them is automatically burned up (*saruf*).

### malachim (מלאכים)
Sixth rank, and also the general word for every rank, which the anonymous
commentator likens to *Taharos*, the name of a whole seder of Mishnah and also
of one masechta within it. The school of the Rokeach takes the rank as those
angels sent to this world on particular missions.

### elohim (אלהים) and bnei elohim (בני אלהים)
Seventh and eighth. *Elohim* names any entity in a position of power or
authority, and is used of an angel, a judge, a navi, and of Hashem Himself. The
*bnei elohim* are so called because they are secondary to the *elohim*.

### kruvim (כרובים)
Ninth. The Torah commands that two *kruvim* be made atop the Aron in the Mishkan,
facing one another. Many meforshim explain *kruv* as derived from the Aramaic
*ravia* (lad), found in Targum to Bereishis 21:17 and many other places, the kaf
at the front meaning "like" or otherwise marking a simile. That etymology is the
basis for the Gemara's assertion (Chagigah 13b, Sukkah 5b) that the *kruvim*
looked like children. Whether the two on the Aron were two boys or a boy and a
girl is subject to dispute; other sources say they were in the shape of birds, or
some sort of child-bird hybrid. The word also names the destructive angels said
to guard the path to the Eitz HaChaim (Rashi to Bereishis 3:24).

Shadal (to Shemos 25:18): *keruv* comes from *rechev*, since the keruv is
described as what Hashem rides (Tehillim 18:11; Divrei HaYamim I 28:18). To
Bereishis 3:24 he adds that the *kruvim* are a vehicle to spread Hashem's
glory, as though He were riding them to bring Himself everywhere.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 3:24) discusses whether
*keruv* means a child, a calf, a bird, or a *rechev*, a vehicle for riding.

### ishim (אישים)
The lowest rank in Rambam's list, the angels who speak with human nevi'im.

- Literally "men," because their level is close to that of the nevi'im they
  address.
- Rabbi Mordechai Shlomo Movshovitz explains instead that they sometimes act
  like men, as the three who came to Avraham (Bereishis 18) looked and behaved
  like people.
- Tanach itself calls an angel *ish* (Yechezkel 9:11), since *ish* can name a single member of any species, mankind included. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi
  Mecklenburg explains that *ish* implies a more abstract and immaterial form of
  existence, which is why it can still refer to angels or other otherworldly
  beings.

### irin (עירין) and kadishin (קדישין)
Not on Rambam's list.

- The school of the Rokeach: *kadishin* are holy and set apart in that they do
  not carry out decrees themselves but tell other angels what to do; *irin* are
  sent to the cities (*ir*) of this world to observe men's deeds and to give
  particular dreams.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg derives *irin* instead from *eir* (awake), since
  they are always awake and watching what people do.
- Ramban (to Vayikra 18:25): angels are called *irin* because they rouse,
  *mis'orerin*, the heavenly forces.
- Shadal (to Bereishis 1:1): the Aramaic *ir*, angel, comes from the Hebrew
  *tzir*, messenger, Hebrew tzadi becoming Aramaic ayin.

## Rendering guidance

- Transliterate the rank names: *chayos*, *ofanim*, *erelim*, *chashmalim*,
  *serafim*, *ishim*, *irin*, *kadishin*. Gloss each once at first occurrence.
- *Kruvim* is the exception: **cherubim** is settled English for the two on the
  Aron.
- *Malachim*: **angels** where the word is generic, transliterated where a
  source is listing the ten ranks and *malachim* is one of them. This is the
  place where a translator most easily loses the sense, because the same English
  word would then appear twice in one list at two different heights.
- Trap: "angel" absorbs every word in this entry. When a passage runs the ranks
  in order, transliterate the whole list rather than translating any member of
  it, and let the introduction say "ten classes of angels."
- Trap in the other direction: *chashmal* is the modern Hebrew word for
  electricity, so the class name wants a gloss at first occurrence lest a reader
  hear the modern sense.
- *Elohim* as a rank of angels is not the Divine Name; render it by the class
  name and let the context carry it. Where a pasuk's *elohim* is being read as
  angels or as judges, say which, since the English cannot show it.
- On *malach* against *shaliach* and the general word for a messenger, see the
  separate entry [angel](angel-malach-shaliach.html).
