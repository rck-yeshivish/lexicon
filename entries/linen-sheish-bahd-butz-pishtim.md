# Linen: sheish / bahd / butz / pishtim

## Bottom line

All four are linen. *Pishtim* (and *pishtah*, *pishtan*) is **flax**, the raw
material. *Butz* is **fine linen** as a material, on one view the Aramaic-Hebrew
rendering of the Egyptian *sheish*. *Sheish* is **fine linen** thread, six fibers to the
strand, and is the Torah's word for the Mishkan's weaving. *Bahd* is the
**single-fiber** linen thread, from *bad*, "alone," and it is the word for the
Kohen Gadol's white garments on Yom Kippur. Cotton is not in this family at all:
it did not exist in the ancient Near East, and where Rabbinic Hebrew needs it,
it says *tzemer gefen*.

## Distinctions

### pishtim (פשתים)
The flax plant and the fiber it yields. Ralbag and Abarbanel (to Shemos 25:4):
*pishtim* and *butz* name linen as a **material**, while *sheish* and *bahd* name
particular **threads**. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim narrows *pishtah* / *pishtan*
further, to the flax while it is still growing in the ground.

### sheish (שש)
Ralbag and Abarbanel: a thread of six fibers, the number being the word
(*sheish*, six); Rambam draws the same distinction in Hilchos Klei HaMikdash
8:14. *Daas Zekeinim* (to Bereishis 41:42) reports that *sheish* is an Egyptian
word for the choicest grade of linen, with *butz* as its Aramaic-Hebrew
rendering. Pappenheim reads *sheish* as combed linen strands not yet spun into
thread, and ties the word to *shayish*, marble, for its smoothness.

Two readings make *sheish* not a fabric word at all, which is why Yoma 71b has to
prove that it means linen. The Steipler Gaon (in a letter to Rabbi Moshe Mordechai
Schulsinger, *Mishmar HaLevi*): *sheish* names a **color**, whitish, exactly as
*techeiles*, *argaman* and *shni tolaas* in the Mishkan passages name colors and
not materials; tradition supplies that those three are wool, and the Gemara has to
establish that *sheish* is linen. Rabbi Dov Landau, to the same question: *sheish*
is a general term for any dignified garment, so the Gemara must show that in
these pesukim it means linen specifically.

### bahd (בד)
From *bad*, alone, as in *levad* and *hisbodedus*. Ralbag and Abarbanel: a linen
thread of a single fiber. Pappenheim: a garment made of linen, and it is called
*bad*, "alone," because in those times linen fabric was never blended with
another material, since nothing else held its dye the way linen did. Zevachim 18b
establishes that *bahd* is linen; on the Steipler's account this is needed
precisely because the word by itself only says "alone."

### butz (בוץ) and kitna (כיתנא)
*Butz* is the later Hebrew and Aramaic word for fine linen. *Kitna* is the
Talmud's Aramaic for linen (see also Targum to Shemos 9:31), and Yoma 71b and
Zevachim 18b define *sheish* and *bahd* by it. Rabbi Yaakov Zev Lev (*Me'at
Tzari* to Vayikra 16:4) separates the two Aramaic words as Pappenheim separated
the Hebrew: *kitna* is flax unprocessed and still in the ground, *butz* is linen
already worked.

### cotton (כותנה, צמר גפן)
Not a Biblical category. Rabbinic Hebrew calls it *tzemer gefen*, "vine wool";
Rambam, in his commentary to Mishnah Kilayim 7:2, renders *tzemer gefen* into
Arabic as *al-qoton*. Some take *karpas* (Esther 1:3) as a foreign word for a
cotton garment. The modern *kotna* is spelled with a kuf and a tes, and is not
the same word as *kitna* / *kesones*, which are kaf and tav; Rashi spells the
French *coton* with kuf and tes (Berachos 28b, Shabbos 21a, Shabbos 110b, Niddah
17a). The link is rather to *katan*, "small," the cotton fiber being finer than a
hair of wool; compare the thin threads called *ketini* at Shabbos 105a. So the
title's question is answered no: cotton is not a kind of linen.

## Rendering guidance

- Mishkan and *bigdei kehunah* passages: *sheish* is "fine linen," *bahd* is
  "linen" (the Yom Kippur *bigdei lavan*), *pishtim* is "flax" or "linen" by
  context. Holding these apart keeps Ralbag's material-versus-thread distinction
  visible.
- Where *sheish* is doing color work rather than fabric work (the Steipler's
  reading), do not silently write "linen." Give "fine linen" and let a note carry
  that the Gemara had to establish the material.
- *Bahd*: resist "alone" in a garment context and resist "linen" in a context of
  separateness. The same word does both, and which one the pasuk wants is a
  reading decision.
- Trap: English "linen" absorbs *sheish*, *bahd*, *butz* and *pishtim* alike. In
  a passage that lists more than one, give *pishtim* the plant word ("flax"),
  keep "fine linen" for *sheish* and *butz*, and let *bahd* stand as plain
  "linen."
- Trap: never render *kotna* or *tzemer gefen* as linen, or *kitna* as cotton.
  The two words look alike and belong to different roots.
