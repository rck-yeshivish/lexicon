# Red: adom / shani / chamutz (and the rest)

## Bottom line

*Adom* is plain **red** and the deepest of the shades. *Shani* (*tola'as shani*)
is **crimson**, the worm-dye of the Mishkan, never flattened to "red" where the
ritual color matters. *Chamutz* and *amutz* are a weaker red: **red-stained** in
Yeshayahu, **chestnut** in Zechariah. *Seruk* is **pink**, *chum* **brown**,
*chachlilei* a drinker's **bloodshot** eye; in Aramaic *sumak* is everyday red.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What *seruk* means.** Radak (to Zechariah 1:8 and *Sefer HaShorashim*) takes
*serukim* as "colorful," naming no shade; Malbim (there) says pink or light red.
Follow Malbim: the pasuk already has *adumim* beside it, and "colorful" leaves
the pair saying nothing.

**Whether *chachlilei* is red.** Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi makes it the
reddened white of the eye, from *kalil*, "crown"; Ramban (to Bereishis 49:12) ties
it to *kachol*, blue. Follow the red reading, which the Peshitta supports.

## Distinctions

### adom (אדום)
Root א-ד-ם. Nine occurrences as an adjective (Esav's lentil stew, Bereishis
25:30; the beloved's complexion, Shir HaShirim 5:10; *parah adumah*, Bamidbar
19:2), with *admoni*, "ruddy," of Esav and David (Bereishis 25:25; Shmuel I 16:12)
and *adamdam*, "reddish," six times of *tzaraas* in Vayikra 13-14. The verb "to
dye red" is mostly the Mishkan's reddened hides; the nouns include *odem*, the
choshen's red stone, and Edom.

### shani (שני) and tola'as shani (תולעת שני)
The dyed wool of the *parah adumah* (Bamidbar 19:6), of the metzora's taharah
(Vayikra 14:4, 14:49-52), and of the Mishkan throughout Shemos; also bare *shani*
(Bereishis 38:30; Yehoshua 2:18) or bare *tola*. That the shade is crimson, deep
red carrying blue or purple, is stated by Rabbi Saadia Gaon, Ibn Ezra, and Rabbi
Avraham Maimuni (to Shemos 25:4), Raavad and Rash M'Shantz (to *Toras Kohanim*),
Radak, and Meiri. The dye comes from a worm, *tola'as*.

### chamutz (חמוץ) and amutz (אמוץ)
*Chamutz* is a hapax: "why are your garments red (*adom*), your clothes like one
who treads a winepress" (Yeshayahu 63:1-2). Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, and
Radak gloss it "red," Radak from context, the color of blood or wine stains; it has
nothing to do with ח-מ-ץ, "leavened, vinegar, anger." Zechariah's *amutzim* horses
(6:3) are the same word (Ibn Janach, Radak), and since that vision also has
*adumim* horses (6:2), Radak makes *chamutz*/*amutz* the weaker red.

### The remaining terms
*Seruk*: *susim adumim serukim* (Zechariah 1:8), with *sorek* the fine red grape
(Bereishis 49:11) and Mishnaic *sarak*, red rouge (Kesubos 17a). *Chum* and
*shachum*, Lavan's flocks, a red near black, named for a heating oven, *chom*.
*Chamar* describes wine at Tehillim 75:9; Ibn
Janach and Radak say it means red there, since "winey wine" says nothing. Rashbam
and Chizkuni (to Bereishis 38:30) read the name Zerach as "red," and Shadal (to
Shemos 20:5) finds a buried sense of red in *kana*, "jealous." Yemenite exegetes
(*Midrash Chefetz* to Shemos 25:4), with Shadal, read *argaman* as a yellowish-red
against the deeper crimson of *tola'as shani*, though it usually reads "purple."
*Zehoris* renders *shani* in Targum and names the
Mishnah's thread on the *sa'ir hamishtaleach* (Yoma 4:2, 6:6), a shining red
(Rabbi Tanchum HaYerushalmi). *Gichor*, a blemish disqualifying a kohen but not a
korban (Bechoros 7:6), is "very red" (Rashi).

## Rendering guidance

- תולעת שני is "crimson wool," not "red wool": the *parah adumah* is red, the wool tied with it crimson.
- Trap: English "red" swallows *adom*, *shani*, *chamutz*, *chum*, and *sumak*.
  Zechariah 1:8 and 6:2-3 test this, each setting two side by side; "red and pink
  horses," "red horses ... chestnut horses" keep what Radak and Malbim read.
- Yeshayahu 63:1-2 pairs *chamutz* and *adom* in adjacent phrases: render
  *chamutz* "stained red" and *adom* "red," so the second is not a repetition.
- חכלילי is never plain "red." "Bloodshot," or "dark from wine," keeps the idiom.
- In a Gemara, סומק is simply "red"; זהורית is "crimson," or transliterated.
