# Kidneys: kilayos / atzeh / tuchos

## Bottom line

*Kilayos* is the plain word for **kidneys**, and in a *pasuk* about Hashem
searching a man it is his **innermost thoughts**. *Tuchos* is a poetic word for
the same organ, so render it **kidneys** with Chazal, or **innards** with Ibn
Ezra. *Atzeh* is a hapax and genuinely disputed: **kidneys** on the Gemara's
reading, **the base of the spine** on Targum Onkelos's; say which you follow.
Note that both *kilayos* and *tuchos* appear only in the plural.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What *atzeh* is** (Vayikra 3:9, the fat taken "opposite the *atzeh*"). Chullin
11a reads it as the kidneys, on the strength of Berachos 61a, that the two kidneys
give *eitzah* to the heart, one advising for good and one for bad (Koheles 10:2
putting the good counsel on the right); Rashi (to Shemos 29:22 and Vayikra 3:9)
and Rashbam follow, and the column notes that the derashah appears to rest on the
spelling עצה, which can be read *eitzah*. Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms, a late
twelfth-century Ashkenazic scholar, adds when citing this tradition (*Sodi Razi*)
that the kidneys are also the source of happiness, from *v'sa'alozna kilyosai*
(Mishlei 23:16). Against them, Targum Onkelos (to Vayikra 3:9; see *Meleches
Shlomo* to Chullin 3:1), *Sefer HeAruch*, *Sefer HaTishbi*, Ibn Janach, and Radak
read it as the end of the spine, the last two
taking the root as ע-צ-ה while granting that Chazal read it as from י-ע-צ. Ibn
Ezra says only that the sense must be gleaned from context, and rejects any link
to *eitz*, "tree," which is what Menachem Ibn Saruk implies by filing *atzeh*
under ע-צ, and what Rabbeinu Bachya, Shadal, and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim say
outright: the spine with its ribs looks like a trunk with branches. Keep both
readings; the sugya and the Targum are each authoritative in their own domain.

**Why *atzeh* would mean kidneys at all.** Rabbi Shes ben Yefes HaRofeh of Aleppo
(*Chemas HaChemdah* to Vayikra 3:17): they filter the blood and cleanse the
flesh, letting the head and heart work properly, which sharpens a man's thinking
the way good advice does. He offers as an alternative that *atzeh* is literally
marrow inside a bone, and by extension the brain in the skull and the kidneys in
the body. The column adds a suggestion of its own, that the kidney is an *atzeh*
because its work is filtering, and advice too is a filtering of good ideas from
bad. (It points the reader for the scientific side of the kidneys-and-thought
question, and its bearing on transplants, to Rabbi Natan Slifkin's monograph
"The Question of the Kidneys' Counsel.")

**What *tuchos* is.** Rosh Hashanah 26a, with Targum, Rashi, and Radak: the
kidneys. Ibn Ezra (to Iyov 38:36) differs slightly, preferring the innards
generally rather than the kidneys specifically, which the column says comes to
much the same. Others read the word as a fowl, an ibis or some such bird, or as
darkness; the column cites for this *Daas Mikra* (to Iyov 38:36), the
Brown-Driver-Briggs Hebrew lexicon, and David J. A. Clines' *Dictionary of
Classical Hebrew*. Part of the question turns on whether the *sechvi* beside it
(Iyov 38:36) is the heart or a rooster.

**Where *tuchos* comes from.** *Machberes Menachem* and Rashi (to Tehillim 51:8
and Rosh Hashanah 26a): from ט-ח, "smooth," the root also of *mitachavei*, a
target (Bereishis 21:16), since an arrow needs a clear run at its mark. Radak,
Rabbi Avraham Ibn Ramoch, and *Metzudas Zion*: from the "smearing" sense of the
same root, since the kidneys are coated in fat. *Chochmas Manoach* merges the two.

## Distinctions

### kilayos (כליות)
Thirty-one times in Tanach, fifteen of them the rule that an animal's kidneys are
burned on the mizbeiach with its fats (Vayikra 3:4, 3:10, 4:9, 8:16, 8:25, 9:10,
9:19, and parallels), and again in the Mishnah (Chullin 3:2, 4:1; Tamid 4:3). In
the abstract sense it is a man's hidden motives, which Hashem alone examines,
"testing the heart and the kidneys" (Yirmiyahu 11:20, 17:10, 20:12; Tehillim
7:10). Three derivations: Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim* 344)
links it to *kele*, imprisonment, spelled with a final aleph, since these organs
sit shut away deep inside, which fits Ibn Ezra's reading (to Tehillim 7:10) that
*kilayos* can mean the thoughts hidden in a man. Ibn Ezra elsewhere (to Shemos
23:25, Vayikra 3:4; Tehillim 16:7, 139:13, cited by Radak in *Sefer
HaShorashim*) postulates that the kidneys are the seat of libidinous desire and
the word a cognate of כ-ל-ה, "to long for" (Shmuel II 13:39); Rav Hirsch (to
Shemos 23:18 and Vayikra 3:4) says the same, that they are the deepest source of
sensuous desire and so the organs of the evil impulses, and Ramban (to Vayikra
1:9) explains on that basis why
burning them is central to atonement, since they are the instruments of the desire
that caused the sin. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo*) takes a third
path, from the biliteral כ-ל, "totality," which also gives *kol* and *kli*, a
vessel that surrounds what is put in it: the kidneys are the vessel holding
filtered waste until it is expelled. In the Gemara the singular is *kulya*.

### the wheat of Devarim 32:14
"The fat of the *kilyos* of wheat" is a distinct usage. Rashi: wheat so plump each
kernel was like a kidney, fulfilled in Shlomo's day, though Kesubos 111b takes it
of the Time to Come, when kernels will be the size of a large ox's kidneys.
*Chizkuni*: the choicest wheat, from the sense of a man's innermost wants. Radak:
simply that the kernels are kidney-shaped, as English "kidney beans" are named.

### tuchos (טוחות)
"Behold, You desire truth in the *tuchos*" (Tehillim 51:8), and "who put wisdom
in the *tuchos*, who gave understanding to the *sechvi*" (Iyov 38:36). Like
*kilayos*, it is never singular.

## Rendering guidance

- Korbanos context: כליות is "kidneys," flatly. Do not reach for "reins," the old
  English rendering the column notes is often used (it is kin to English *renal*
  and *adrenaline*); it is now unreadable.
- Where Hashem "tests the heart and the kidneys," keep "kidneys" and gloss; the
  derashos depend on the anatomy.
- Trap: English "kidneys" would absorb all three words and hide the machlokes at
  Vayikra 3:9. Render עצה as "kidneys" or "base of the spine" with a note naming
  Chullin 11a against Targum Onkelos, never silently.
- טוחות is poetic. "Inmost being" reads well in Tehillim 51:8, but a translation
  that is tracking Chazal should keep "kidneys" and gloss.
- כליות חטה (Devarim 32:14) is "kidney-fat of wheat" or "the choicest of the
  wheat," depending on whether Rashi or *Chizkuni* is being followed.
