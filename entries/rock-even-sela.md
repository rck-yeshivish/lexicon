# Rock: even / sela

## Bottom line

*Even* is the general word: **stone**, any naturally occurring one, as against
*leveinah*, a man-made brick (Malbim). *Sela* is a **crag**, a hard rock still
fixed in the ground and often a rocky height, which is why it also became the
name of a coin. Rabbi Immanuel Chai Ricci puts the difference in attachment
(*sela* joined to the ground, *even* loose enough to pick up); Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim puts it in hardness, with *even* the hypernym that includes *sela*.
The trap: English "rock" and "stone" are near-interchangeable, so a passage that
contrasts *even* and *sela* (the Mishnah at *Zevachim* 13:3) needs two clearly
different English words.

## Distinctions

### even (אבן)
Malbim: a stone formed by nature, set against *leveinah*, the brick a man makes
by mixing mortar. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: *even* is the umbrella term, the
plainest way to say rock, and *sela* and the rest fall under it; it takes on
narrower senses in construct, *avnei hasadeh*, *avnei yakar* (precious stones),
*avnei shayish*. He traces the word to the two-letter ב-נ, building, since the
ordinary use of stone is to build with. Because stone is hard, *even* was then
extended to anything in a hard, solid state, as in *avnei barad*, stones of
hail, at Yehoshua 10:11. This is the word for every stone in the Yaakov Avinu
narratives: the stones he slept on (Bereishis 28:11), the stone he set up and
poured oil on (28:18), the stone he rolled off the well (29:10), and the phrase
he coined, *Even Yisrael* (49:24).

### sela (סלע)
Rabbi Immanuel Chai Ricci: a *sela* is attached to the ground and an *even* is
detached, so that one can simply lift it. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: ordinary
rock is sand, earth and minerals compacted, and shatters under a hammer; a
*sela* is the harder kind, whitish, its minerals bound with calcite, and to
break it takes real force, so that its fragments fly apart when it goes (see
Yirmiyahu 23:29). In most of Tanach a *sela* is a hard rock jutting out of the
ground, or a rocky mountain. Its hardness made it a standard, and by Mishnaic
times *sela* is also the name of a coin. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg
(*HaKesav VeHaKabbalah*, to Shemos 4:25 and Devarim 9:9) cites Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim here and builds a derashah on it about the Luchos.

Rabbi Aharon Marcus proposes that where a root looks like ס-ל, the samech stands
in for the ayin that follows it in the alef-beis, so the root is really ע-ל, the
root of *al* and *oleh*, what is on top and what rises. On that reading a *sela*
is the rock that *comes up* out of the ground.

## Rendering guidance

- *Zevachim* 13:3, Rabbi Shimon holding one liable for offering "on an *even* or
  a *sela*": "on a stone or a crag." Two English words are required; "on a rock
  or a stone" reads as a distinction without a difference, and the halachah
  turns on there being two cases.
- אבן: "stone," in every register, and especially opposite *leveinah*: "stone
  and brick" (compare Bereishis 11:3, where the builders had to substitute).
- סלע: "crag" or "rock" where it protrudes or is a height; "*sela*" transliterated
  where it is the coin, never "coin" alone, which loses why the coin has that
  name.
- Trap: English "rock" will take *even*, *sela* and (in the companion article)
  *tzur* and *chalamish* as well. Fix one English word per Hebrew word across a
  passage and hold to it, rather than choosing by what sounds well in each line.
- אבני ברד (Yehoshua 10:11): "hailstones," but if the passage is discussing why
  hail is called stone, render "stones of hail" so the point survives.
