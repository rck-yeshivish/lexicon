# Joy: simchah / sasson / gilah / rinah / ditzah / chedvah

## Bottom line

*Simchah* is the inner, continual gladness: **joy, gladness**.
*Sasson* is joy shown outwardly, in dress, music, celebration: **rejoicing** (Malbim);
for the Vilna Gaon it is the joy attained at the end, where *simchah* begins the
pursuit. *Gilah* is a burst of joy that does not last (Malbim), or the joy of
recalling past joys (Gra): **exultation, a burst of joy**. *Rinah* is joy that breaks
into song of Hashem's praise: **joyous song**. *Ditzah* and *chedvah* are, on
many commentators' account, Aramaic forms of the word for joy: **mirth, delight**. Both
ששון ושמחה and שמחה וששון are scriptural, and Chazal darshen the order (Sukkah 48b).

## Distinctions

### simchah (שמחה)
Root ש-מ-ח. Malbim: the internal, continual gladness; its antonym is *yagon*, inner
despondency. Vilna Gaon: the beginning of the process that leads to full elation,
and in this world the more prominent, because a person first pursues *simchah* and
only then reaches the goal that is *sasson*; and, against *gilah*, a new joy while
still fresh.

### sasson (ששון)
Root ש-ו-ש. Malbim: the outward expression of inner happiness, what a person does
to show he is glad, festive clothes, music at a celebration; its antonyms are
*aveilus*, mourning, the outward expression of sadness, and *anachah*, a sigh.
Vilna Gaon: the realization of happiness, the end of the process; in the days of
Mashiach the ecstasy will *begin* with *sasson* and open onto further *simchah*
according to one's merits, which is why the pasuk of the redemption, ששון ושמחה
ישיגו (Yeshayahu 35:10, the Gemara's proof-text), puts *sasson* first, while Esther 8:16 puts *simchah*
first; on his reading each is superior in its own context, which is why the
order varies. Rabbi Klein's own resolution of the Gemara's contest (Sukkah 48b),
on Malbim's terms, is that it seems both are right and the dispute reflects a
chicken-and-egg: sometimes the inner feeling comes first and bursts outward,
sometimes the outward celebration rouses the inner mood.

### gilah (גילה)
Root ג-י-ל. Malbim: a stroke of fleeting happiness that does not continue, as
against continual *simchah*. Vilna Gaon: a nostalgic joy that recalls the joys of
the past, as against fresh *simchah*. Bereishis Rabbah 63:1 seems to understand
*gilah* as specifically the joy of a man who fathers a son.

### rinah (רינה)
Root ר-נ-ן. Machzor Vitry (Rabbi Simchah of Vitry, a talmid of Rashi): the joy
that stirs the celebrant to sing Hashem's praises.

### ditzah (דיצה) and chedvah (חדוה)
Roots ד-ו-ץ, ח-ד-ה. Many commentators: Aramaic forms of the word for joy. Their
presence in the wedding berachah signals the all-inclusive joy wished on the
couple.

## Rendering guidance

- ששון ושמחה: "rejoicing and gladness" (or "joy and gladness"); שמחה וששון:
  "gladness and rejoicing." Keep the Hebrew order; the Gemara darshens it.
- The wedding berachah's list, ששון ושמחה... גילה רינה דיצה וחדוה: give each its own
  English (rejoicing, gladness, exultation, song, mirth, delight); a translator who
  writes "joy, joy, joy" has erased the berachah.
- Standalone שמחה: "joy" or "gladness." Avoid "happiness"; it reads
  modern and flattens the inner/outer split.
- גיל / גילה standing alone: "exultation" or "a burst of joy"; beside שמחה, let it
  be the sharper, shorter word.
- Trap: English "joy" absorbs *simchah*, *sasson*, and *gilah*. "Rejoicing" (a
  doing) is the natural English for outward *sasson*, "gladness" or "joy" (a
  feeling) for inner *simchah*; hold that line when they meet.
- Trap: *yagon* is the antonym of *simchah*, not of *sasson*; *aveilus*, and
  *anachah*, "a sigh," are the antonyms of *sasson* (Malbim). A passage that
  opposes joy to mourning is speaking of outward joy.
