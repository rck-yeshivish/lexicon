# Because: baavur / biglal / bishvil

## Bottom line

Rabbi Wolf Heidenheim, Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg and the Malbim all separate
the two Biblical words the same way. *Biglal* is the backward-looking "because":
a reason that **already exists** and justifies the act, so it means **on account
of** or **because of**. *Baavur* is the forward-looking one: the reason **does
not yet exist** and is the intended outcome, so it means **in order that** or
**so that**. *Bishvil* is post-Biblical, usually equal to *biglal*, and is the
everyday word in modern speech.

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| בגלל | biglal | on account of, because of | the reason already exists and justifies the act (Heidenheim, Malbim) |
| בעבור | baavur | in order that, so that | the reason is the intended outcome, not yet in existence (Heidenheim, Malbim) |
| בשביל | bishvil | because | post-Biblical, usually equal to *biglal*; the everyday modern word |

## Distinctions

### biglal (בגלל)
The cause that is already on the ground before the action is taken. Rabbi Wolf
Heidenheim, Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg and the Malbim all read the pair this
way. English hides the difference: "I will visit my parents because I love them"
is *biglal*, a factor that exists already and calls for the visit.

### baavur (בעבור)
The cause that lies ahead: what the act is meant to bring about. "I will visit my parents because I want to build a better
relationship with them" is *baavur*, the anticipated result standing as the
reason.

This is why Shemos 9:16 says *baavur* and not *biglal* or *bishvil*. Before the
plague of hail Moshe warns Pharaoh that he truly deserves to be killed, but that
Hashem said He had let him endure *baavur zos*, to show him His strength and so
that His
Name be told throughout the earth. *Bishvil* is out of the running to begin
with, never appearing in Tanach. As for *biglal*, Pharaoh had no standing merit,
nothing
already in hand, to justify sparing him; the whole reason was what would come of
it afterward. (The Midrash, Pirkei d'Rabbi Eliezer chapters 42 and 43, follows
that outcome: Pharaoh survived *makkas bechoros* though he was a firstborn, and
survived Krias Yam Suf though the rest of his army drowned, and somehow ended up
a castaway who came in time to Ninveh, where he, or perhaps his descendants or
his reincarnation, rose to prominence and roused its sinful people to heed
Yonah's warning and do teshuvah.)

### bishvil (בשביל)
Not in Tanach at all; it enters with Chazal, appearing close to fifty times in
the Mishnah. Literally it can mean *bi-shvil*, "on the path," which may itself picture the
road running from a cause to its effect. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim observes that
*eikev* means both "ankle" and "because," and that on the strength of that double
meaning the Rabbis adopted *shvil*, the road associated with ankles, to mean
"because" as well: *bishvil*. It usually does the work of *biglal*,
though in at least one case (Bava Metzia 5:10) it carries the sense of *baavur*.
Neither *biglal* nor
*baavur* appears in the Mishnah in its own voice; *baavur* shows up once only,
inside a pasuk the Mishnah quotes. Modern Hebrew made *bishvil* the catch-all.

## Rendering guidance

- Shemos 9:16: "for this purpose have I let you stand," or "in order to show you
  My strength." Not "because of this," which would say Pharaoh had earned it.
- *Biglal* in a *pasuk* of consequence (a punishment or a reward for what was
  done): "on account of," which keeps the eye on the completed cause.
- Trap: English "because" absorbs *baavur* and *biglal* alike, and the loss is one of logic, since the two point in opposite directions in time.
  Reserve "because" for *biglal* and give *baavur* a purpose clause.
- Trap: "for the sake of" reads as either one. It is safe for *bishvil* and
  risky for the Biblical pair.
- In a Mishnaic text, *bishvil* is ordinarily "because"; check Bava Metzia 5:10
  before assuming it never carries purpose.
