# Rain: geshem / matar / yoreh / malkosh

## Bottom line

*Geshem* and *matar* both mean **rain**, and the authorities split on which is
the general word and which the specific one; render both "rain" and let the
context carry any distinction. *Yoreh* and *malkosh* are not kinds of rain but
seasons of it: *yoreh* is **the first rain**, at the start of the rainy season,
and *malkosh* **the last rain**, in early spring before the harvest. Never render
*yoreh* and *malkosh* as "early rain and late rain" without saying what they are
early and late to; the pasuk in Shema (Devarim 11:14) is promising a full year's
cycle.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Which of *geshem* and *matar* is the general term.** The article gives three
positions without naming their holders, so they are recorded here as views, not
attributed rulings.

1. *Geshem* is the general term for rain of every sort, and *matar* the specific
   one, the "rains of blessing"; a variant of this reads *matar* as heavy rain,
   which fits its use for the fire and brimstone on Sedom (Bereishis 19:24) and
   for the hail in Mitzrayim (Shemos 9:18).
2. The reverse: *matar* is the general term, and *geshem* the specific one, hard
   rain or rain of blessing.
3. The two differ not in intensity but in origin. *Geshem* is rain by the natural
   order, vapors held in the clouds until the winds release them, bound to no
   fixed time; this is why later sources borrowed the word for physical matter,
   *gashmiyus*. *Matar* is rain that Hashem brings directly at set times, and the
   times are the *yoreh* and the *malkosh* of Devarim 11:14.

The Bottom line does not choose, because no rendering follows from the choice:
the third view is the most useful to a translator, since it explains the pasuk's
sequence, but the first two are equally represented and English has one word.

## Distinctions

### geshem (גשם) and matar (מטר)
The two are near enough that every account of them has to be argued from usage
rather than from form. What is agreed: *geshem* is the more frequent word, and
*matar* is what the pasuk uses in Devarim 11:14 for rain given "in its time,"
with *yoreh* and *malkosh* named as those times. The derivative *gashmiyus*,
materiality, belongs to *geshem* alone, and grows out of the third view above,
that *geshem* is rain by the ordinary running of the world.

### yoreh (יורה) and malkosh (מלקוש)
Both are seasonal markers, and Taanis 6a explains each from its own root. *Yoreh*
(or *moreh*) carries the senses of shooting and of teaching; it is the season's
first rain, and it teaches people to reinforce their roofs and bring the produce
indoors before the heavier rains come. *Malkosh* is tied to *mekoshesh*, one who
reaps; it falls at the onset of spring, around Pesach, just before the harvest.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "rain" swallows *geshem* and *matar* whole. Where a passage sets
  them against each other, mark the contrast in the prose ("the ordinary rains,
  and the rain He gives in its season") rather than hunting for two English
  nouns.
- Devarim 11:14: "the rain of your land in its time, the first rain and the last
  rain." "Early rain and latter rain" is the older English and is defensible, but
  a note should say that these are the opening and closing rains of one season.
- *Matar* in Bereishis 19:24 and Shemos 9:18 is "rained down," a verb of sending;
  do not soften it to weather.
- *Gashmiyus* in a mussar sefer is "materiality" or "the physical"; it is a
  derivative of *geshem*, not a word about rain, and should not be glossed as
  though it were.
