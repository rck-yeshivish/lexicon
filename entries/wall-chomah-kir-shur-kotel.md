# Wall: chomah / kir / shur / cheil / chayitz / kotel

## Bottom line

*Chomah* is the **city wall**, which encircles and defends. *Kir* is the
ordinary **structural wall** of a building. *Cheil* is the **low outer wall**
and the cleared space it makes. *Chayitz* is a **partition**, and by Rabbi
Moshe Zacuto a flimsy one, where *mechitzah* covers any divider. *Shur* is the
Aramaic for *chomah*, *kotel* the Aramaic for *kir*. Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino
holds the six are simply synonyms; this reading follows Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim's root analysis. The trap is "wall" for all six, especially where
*chomah* and *cheil* stand together.

## Distinctions

### chomah (חומה)
Over 130 appearances, always a wall surrounding a city or a large and important
building. Rabbi Pappenheim (with Menachem Ibn Saruk) traces it to the
two-letter root ח-מ, heat: from it *milchamah*, the heated clash of parties, and
so *chomah*, whose purpose is to shield a city from warfare; or else because
the wall blocks the cold winds and keeps the city warm. The Raavan notes that
ח-מ-ה in Aramaic is seeing (Targum Yonasan to Shemos 14:31), and Rav Hirsch (to
Bereishis 49:22) says much the same, so *chomah* also carries the watching
sense. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (*HaKsav VeHaKabbalah*) connects it to
*cheimah*, anger, and *chamah*, sun, on the point that a wall sets a city apart
from all beyond it; Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 21:15) puts *cheimes*, a flask, in
the same family, since it encloses and protects what is inside it.

### kir (קיר)
About 74 appearances as "wall." Most grammarians take the root as ק-י-ר; some
drop the yud. Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras (*Ohalei Yehuda*) links *kir*
to *kor*/*kar*, cold, a wall that gives shade to cool off under. Rabbi
Pappenheim works from the two-letter ק-ר, the strong impact of weight or
velocity: *korah*, a beam, and *tikrah*, a ceiling, whose horizontal timbers
press down on the supports beneath. On that reading *kir* is originally the
upright beam that bears a structure's weight, and only then any wall of stone
or metal. The root also destroys: Bilaam foretells that Yisrael will *karkar*
the nations (Bamidbar 24:17), which Rashi, Ibn Ezra and Ibn Janach render
"destroy" without explaining how. Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) derives *karkar*
from *kir*, to tear down a wall and so strip a nation of its protection,
comparing *shoresh*, root, whose verb *misharesh* means to uproot; one root
thus carries both building and demolishing. Rav Hirsch (to Vayikra 19:28,
Bamidbar 24:17) and *Peh Kadosh* of Rabbi Yitzchok of Volozhin explain
similarly.

### shur (שור)
Rare in Hebrew (Bereishis 49:22; Shmuel II 22:30; Tehillim 18:30; possibly
Yirmiyahu 5:10 and Iyov 24:11), but common in the Targumim as *shura*,
rendering *chomah* (Vayikra 25:29; Yehoshua 2:15, 6:20; Eichah 2:8). Rabbi
Pappenheim traces it to ש-ר, a focal point: *shar*/*sharir*, the umbilical
cord joining a fetus to its mother (Yechezkel 16:4; Mishlei 3:8; Iyov 40:16);
*shor*, an ox, whose strength sits in its midsection; *shoresh*, the focal
point of a plant's growth; *yashar*, the shortest way to a point; *shir*, a
composition holding to one theme. Sight belongs here too, since it alone can be
aimed at one point, and with it *teshurah*, *nesher* and *sheirus*. For *shur*
as "wall" he
offers two links: a wall built in a straight line (*yashar*), as against a
*chomah* that encircles; or a tall wall used as a lookout, from *shur* as
seeing. Rabbi Ernest Klein observes that *shur* and *chomah* both carry the
double sense of wall and seeing. Rashi to Bereishis 49:22 is unsure which sense
*shur* has there, and Radak reads *shor* at Bereishis 49:6 as "wall" like the
*shur* later in the perek.

### cheil (חיל)
Rabbi Pappenheim derives it from ח-ל, circular movement and the empty space
inside a circle: the *cheil* is a low wall running outside a taller one, and
the cleared ground between them is a kind of no man's land. He brings the
Mishnah (Middos 2:3), which so calls the space inside the Har HaBayis wall.
Pesachim 86a describes *chomah* and *cheil* as *shura* and *bar-shura*, a wall
and the son of a wall; Rashi takes the *chomah* as the outer wall and the
*cheil* as a shorter wall within it, the small son beside his large father.
Radak is unsure whether the *cheil* stands outside the *chomah* or inside, and
suggests it may be a moat rather than a wall at all.

### chayitz (חיץ)
A single appearance in Tanach, Yechezkel 13:10. Ibn Janach: the yud stands in
for a second tzadi, so the root is ח-צ-צ, to partition. Rabbi Pappenheim
likewise puts it under ח-צ, dividing in two, with *chatzi*, *chatzos*, *chutz*
and *cheitz*, and takes *chayitz* as a wall dividing one domain into two,
equivalent to the Mishnaic *mechitzah* (Bava Basra 1:1). Rabbi Moshe Zacuto
(*Kol HaRamaz* to Sheviis 3:8) narrows it: *chayitz* is a flimsy partition,
*mechitzah* any partition at all.

### kotel (כותל)
Three appearances: once in Hebrew (Shir HaShirim 2:9, *omed achar kosleinu*)
and twice in the Aramaic *ktal* (Daniel 5:5; Ezra 5:8). In the Targumim it is
the standard rendering of *kir* (Vayikra 1:15, 14:37; Yehoshua 2:15).

## Rendering guidance

- *Chomah*: **city wall**, or **rampart** where the fortification is the point.
  *Chomos Yerushalayim* are the walls of the city, not the walls of a building.
- *Kir*: **wall**, plain, of a house or of the mizbeach.
- *Cheil*: **the Cheil**, transliterated, in Mikdash contexts, with a gloss the
  first time (the low outer wall and its cleared space). "Rampart" alone will
  not carry it.
- *Chayitz*: **partition**; *mechitzah*: **mechitzah** or **divider**. Where
  Rabbi Moshe Zacuto's distinction is live, *chayitz* is a **flimsy
  partition**.
- *Kotel*: **the Kosel** for the Western Wall; otherwise plain **wall**.
- Trap: English "wall" absorbs all six. Pesachim 86a's *shura uvar-shura* pairs
  two of them in three words, so render "a wall and a wall's son," keeping the
  Gemara's image, and gloss which is which.
- Second trap: *shur* and *chomah* both mean "wall" and "see," so a pasuk using
  either may not be about masonry at all; Rashi himself hesitates at Bereishis
  49:22. (The article gives Bamidbar 24:27 for *shur* as seeing; that perek has
  twenty-five pesukim, and the *ashurenu* loci are 23:9 and 24:17.)
