# Seeing: ra'ah / hibit / mashkif / tzofeh (shur, metzitz, mashgiach)

## Bottom line

*Ra'ah* is plain **seeing**, the eye doing its work, including what a man happens
upon without meaning to. *Hibit* is the deliberate **look**, and in the Midrash
the look of one positioned above what he sees. *Mashkif* is **looking out** or
**down** from a height, one who sees without being seen; in the Chumash Rashi
takes it as boding ill. *Tzofeh* is **gazing** at what is not present to the eye,
which is why a navi is a *tzofeh* and why waiting for what has not yet come is
*mitzapeh*. *Shur* is seeing from far off, *metzitz* is **peeking** through a
narrow opening, and *mashgiach* is watching that acts on what it sees. The trap
is English "look," which will absorb *hibit*, *mashkif*, and *metzitz* at once.

## Distinctions

### ra'ah (ראה)
Malbim: the physiological function of the eye, the sense of sight itself, and in
particular a sight one stumbles on without intending to behold it. The Vilna Gaon
(to Yeshayahu 5:12): *ra'ah* is seeing at the surface, what a quick glance takes
in.

### hibit (הביט)
The Vilna Gaon (there): *hibit* is seeing what a glance would not have caught, so
the verb carries deliberateness, looking in order to investigate or understand.
Bereishis Rabbah 44:12: *hibit* is the looking of one above at what is below him.
Malbim accepts both and reconciles them: sometimes *hibit* is examination, and
then the looker need not be above; sometimes it is simply seeing, and then it is
specifically from above. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg rejects the deliberateness
altogether: *hibit* is turning the head to face what one wants to see, a word
about the head rather than the eye, and the real word for deliberate looking is
*mashkif*. Midrash to Koheles 5:1 makes the difference between *ra'ah* and
*hibit* one of near and far, and records a dispute among the Sages over which is
which.

### mashkif (משקיף), hashkafah (השקפה)
Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): *mashkif* is one who sees but cannot be seen. Rashi
(to Bereishis 18:16): every *hashkafah* in the Torah is for ill, except Devarim
26:15, where it is for good. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer corrects the common
overreading: Rashi meant every instance in the Torah, not in all of Tanach.
Rashi (to *Berachos* 29a): *hishkif* is staring at something to jog one's memory.
*Sefer HaChochmah*, ascribed to Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms: *mashkif* looks
down from a high place, and *tzofeh* from a very high place, a mountain.

### tzofeh (צופה)
The gazing at what does not physically exist, an abstraction rather than a thing.
Malbim: awaiting what has not yet come is *mitzapeh* on the same logic, since the
one who waits also sees what is not there and hopes it will come to be; and
nevi'im are called *tzofim* because their visions, though not physically present,
are altogether real. Rabbi Wertheimer adds the sentry: one who stays in place
over time to watch developments as they unfold. The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 15:3)
puts the distance in this word: *tzofeh* is seeing from afar.

### shur (שור)
Malbim: seeing something far away, in time or in space. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
links it to *yashar*, straight: sight alone among the senses can be aimed, the
man setting his eyes directly opposite one view so that he sees nothing else,
where the nose, ears, and palate take in whatever reaches them all at once.

### metzitz (מציץ)
Malbim: not primarily a word for seeing at all, but a borrowed term whose
hyper-literal sense is stretching; the one who is *metzitz* cranes his neck for a
better view, as in Shir HaShirim 2:9, peering through the lattice. Rabbi
Wertheimer: looking where the field of vision is constricted, which in English is
peeking.

### mashgiach (משגיח)
Rabbi Wertheimer: unlike *mashkif*, *mashgiach* always carries a good sense.
Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: not one who merely watches but one who watches actively, and
when he sees what calls for intervention he steps in and sets it right. So too
*hashgachah*, Hashem's conduct of creation: not a spectator, but One who governs
events by His will.

## Rendering guidance

- *Ra'ah*: "see." *Hibit*: "look" or "gaze at," and where the Midrash's sense is
  in play, "look down at."
- *Mashkif*: "look out" or "peer down," and in Bereishis 18:16 and its like keep
  the ominous edge Rashi gives it ("looked down upon"). Devarim 26:15 is
  "look down from Your holy dwelling" in the good sense, and the exception should
  be visible if the passage darshens it.
- *Tzofeh*: "gaze" for the abstract seeing, "watchman" for the sentry, "seer" for
  the navi. *Mitzapeh*: "await" or "look forward to," never plain "see."
- *Metzitz*: "peek" or "peer," and keep the constriction; "look" loses the whole
  word.
- *Mashgiach*: "oversee" or "watch over," not "observe," which is passive and
  cancels Rabbi Moshe Shapiro's point. *Hashgachah*: "Divine Providence."
- Trap: a pasuk that pairs two of these (Yeshayahu 5:12 with *ra'ah* and *hibit*;
  Koheles 5:1 in the Midrash) must show the pair in English. Use "see" for one
  and "look intently" or "look down" for the other, and say in a note which
  authority you followed, since the Gaon and Rabbi Mecklenburg divide *hibit*
  differently.
