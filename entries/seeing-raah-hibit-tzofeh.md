# Seeing: ra'ah / hibit / mashkif / tzofeh (shur, metzitz, mashgiach)

## Bottom line

*Ra'ah* is plain **seeing**, the eye doing its work, including what a man happens
upon without meaning to. *Hibit* is the more deliberate **look**, though Rabbi
Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg denies that the word itself denotes deliberateness; in
the Midrash it is the look of one positioned above what he sees. *Mashkif* is **looking out** or
**down** from a height, one who sees without being seen; in the Chumash Rashi
takes it as boding ill. *Tzofeh* is **gazing** at what is not present to the eye,
which is why a navi is sometimes called a *tzofeh* and why waiting for what has not yet come is
*mitzapeh*. *Shur* is seeing from far off, *metzitz* is **peeking** through a
narrow opening, and *mashgiach* is watching that acts on what it sees.

## Distinctions

### ra'ah (ראה)
Malbim: the physiological function of the eye, the sense of sight itself, and in
particular a sight one stumbles on without intending to behold it. The Vilna Gaon
(to Yeshayahu 5:12): *ra'ah* is seeing at the surface, what a quick glance takes
in.

### hibit (הביט)
- The Vilna Gaon (there): *hibit* is seeing what a glance would not have caught.
  Accordingly the verb carries a more deliberate sense, looking in order to
  investigate or understand.
- Bereishis Rabbah 44:12: *hibit* is the looking of one above at what is below
  him.
- Malbim accepts both and reconciles them: sometimes *hibit* is examination, and
  then the looker need not be above; sometimes it is simply seeing, and then it
  is specifically from above.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg rejects the idea that *hibit* itself denotes a
  deliberate form of looking: *hibit* is turning the head to face what one wants
  to see, a word about the head rather than the eye, so it is bound up with
  deliberate looking only indirectly, and the actual word for the more
  deliberate looking is *mashkif*.
- Midrash to Koheles 5:1 makes the difference between *ra'ah* and *hibit* one of
  near and far, and records a dispute among the Sages over which is which.

### mashkif (משקיף), hashkafah (השקפה)
- Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): *mashkif* is one who sees but cannot be seen.
- Rashi (to Bereishis 18:16): wherever *mashkif* or *hashkafah* appears it
  carries a negative connotation, except Devarim 26:15, which is a positive
  looking. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer explains, correctly in Rabbi Klein's
  judgment, that Rashi did not mean every instance in all of Tanach but every
  instance in the Torah.
- Rashi (to *Berachos* 29a): *hishkif* is staring at something in the hope of
  jogging one's memory.
- *Sefer HaChochmah*, ascribed to the late twelfth-century Ashkenazic scholar
  Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms: *mashkif* looks down from a high place, and
  *tzofeh* is one situated very high up, on a tall mountain, looking downward.

### tzofeh (צופה)
The gazing at what does not physically exist, an abstraction rather than a
thing.
- Malbim: awaiting what has not yet come is *mitzapeh* on the same logic, since
  the one who waits also sees what is not there and hopes it will come to be;
  and nevi'im are sometimes called *tzofim* because their visions, though not
  physically present, are altogether real.
- Rabbi Wertheimer adds the sentry: one who stays in place over time to watch
  developments as they unfold.
- The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 15:3) puts the distance in this word: *tzofeh* is
  seeing from afar.
- Others put the far-seeing in other words instead, the Midrash to Koheles 5:1
  in *ra'ah* or *hibit* and Malbim in *shur*.

### shur (שור)
Malbim: seeing something far away, in time or in space. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
links it to *yashar*, straight: sight alone among the senses can be aimed, the
man setting his eyes directly opposite one view so that he sees nothing else,
where the nose, ears, and palate take in whatever reaches them all at once.

### metzitz (מציץ)
Malbim: not primarily a word for seeing at all, but a borrowed term whose
hyper-literal sense is stretching; the one who is *metzitz* cranes his neck for a
better view, as in Shir HaShirim 2:9, peering through the lattice. Rabbi
Wertheimer: looking where the field of vision is somewhat constricted, which in English is
peeking.

### mashgiach (משגיח)
Rabbi Wertheimer: unlike *mashkif*, *mashgiach* always carries a good sense.
Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: not one who merely watches but one who watches actively, and
when he sees what calls for intervention he does not hesitate to take action and
set it right. So too *hashgachah*, Hashem's administrative role in creation: not
a passive *deus otiosus*, a god at rest, but One who
actively affects the world's events according to His will.

## Rendering guidance

- *Ra'ah*: "see." *Hibit*: "look" or "gaze at," and where the Midrash's sense is
  in play, "look down at."
- *Mashkif*: "look out" or "peer down," and in Bereishis 18:16 and its like keep
  the ominous edge Rashi gives it ("looked down upon"). Devarim 26:15 is
  "look down from Your holy dwelling" in the good sense, and the exception should
  be visible if the passage darshens it.
- *Tzofeh*: "gaze" for the abstract seeing, "watchman" for the sentry, "seer" for
  the navi. *Mitzapeh*: "await" or "look forward to," never plain "see."
- *Metzitz*: "peek" or "peer," and keep the constriction; "look" loses the whole
  word.
- *Mashgiach*: "oversee" or "watch over," not "observe," which is passive and
  cancels Rabbi Moshe Shapiro's point. *Hashgachah*: "Divine Providence."
- Trap: a pasuk that pairs two of these (Yeshayahu 5:12 with *ra'ah* and *hibit*;
  Koheles 5:1 in the Midrash) must show the pair in English. Use "see" for one
  and "look intently" or "look down" for the other, and say in a note which
  authority you followed, since the Gaon and Rabbi Mecklenburg divide *hibit*
  differently.
