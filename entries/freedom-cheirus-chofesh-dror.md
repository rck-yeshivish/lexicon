# Freedom: cheirus / chofesh / dror

## Bottom line

*Dror* is freedom from being owned by another: **release**, "free as a bird,"
the Yovel word. *Chofesh* is freedom from the obligation to serve:
**free, released** (a *chofshi* slave owes no more labor). *Cheirus* is the
later, rabbinic word, and it is the positive one: **freedom** as a standing,
the *ben chorin*. Do not use one English word for all three where a passage
turns on the difference; and note that *cheirus* never carries the sense of
freedom in Tanach, only in Chazal.

## Distinctions

### dror (דרור)
Root ד-ר-ר. First appears at the freeing of slaves in the Yovel year (Vayikra
25:10). Rashi, following Rosh Hashanah 9b, ties it to *dar*, "dwells": the
freed man dwells in his own domain and falls under no one else's control.
The *dror* bird carries the same idea: Ibn Ezra says it sings when left to
itself and starves rather than live in a man's domain, and *Sefer HaAruch*
records the same; Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) says it nests in people's houses
unafraid of capture, so free it does not reckon with being caught (see also
Beitzah 24a). *Dror* also means "pure" of the myrrh of the anointing oil
(Shemos 30:23): Rabbeinu Yonah ibn Janach and Ramban explain that the myrrh
must be free of adulteration. The word can be shortened to *dar*, as in Esther
1:6, of the tax exemption Achashverosh granted (Megillah 12a).

### chofesh (חופש)
Root ח-פ-ש. The Tanach's other slave-freeing word (Shemos 21, Devarim 15,
Yirmiyahu 34); in modern Hebrew it has drifted to "vacation." Rabbi Shlomo
Aharon Wertheimer draws the line: *chofshi* is freedom from an obligation to
work, while *dror* is freedom from subjugation to a particular person who lords
over him. Rabbi Lord Jonathan Sacks, building on the British philosopher Isaiah
Berlin's distinction between "negative liberty" and "positive liberty," reads
*chofesh* against *chafetz* ("desire") and *chapess* ("search out"): *chofshi* is
what a slave becomes when he goes free, able to do whatever his heart desires,
which Rabbi Sacks identifies as negative liberty, the mere absence of coercion.

### cheirus (חירות)
Root ח-ר-ר. Absent from Tanach in the sense of freedom, yet the standard word
in Chazal for releasing a slave: *shichrur* is the formal act, *eved
shenishtachrar* the freed slave, *bnei chorin* the free men we act as on the
Seder night, and *Birkas HaChodesh* asks to be brought from *avdus* to
*cheirus*. Avos 6:2 finds the scriptural hook in *charus* ("engraved") on the
Luchos (Shemos 32:16), spelled there with a *tav* rather than the usual *tes*:
read not *charus* but *cheirus*, for no one is free except one occupied with
Torah. The column remarks that it seems fairly clear that if the whole purpose of
Yetzias Mitzrayim was Matan Torah, the word for the freedom that came of it
should indeed be *cheirus*, and the Yom Tov that celebrates it *Zman
Cheiruseinu*. Rabbi Sacks builds his distinction on that reading: negative
liberty may serve an individual, but a society needs law, and law that is imposed
coercively is resented and resisted, so it must be taught until it is accepted
willingly and becomes part of a man's essence. *Cheirus* is therefore the freedom
of a society that studies its law until it is engraved on the heart, so that
*charus* and *cheirus* become one. This "positive liberty" looks restrictive on
the surface and proves in fact to be what frees.

### chorim (חורים)
The *cheirus*-cognate that does appear in Tanach, thirteen times, but of
noblemen and dignitaries rather than of freedom as such (see Rashi to
Yirmiyahu 27:20). Rashi to Sotah 49a: *chorin* are people of lineage. Rabbi
Yitzchok Dov Bamberger, the Wurzburger Rav, connects *chorim* to the Aramaic
root ח-ו-ר, "white": a dignitary's name must be unstained, and in the ancient
world only the important wore white. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg instead
links *cheirus* to *chor*, "hole," the same three letters; the article records
this and expressly does not develop it. Separately, the column offers a
suggestion of its own, hedged "it seems to me": Chazal chose *cheirus* to stress
that newly freed slaves begin with a clean slate and have it in them to become
people of consequence in their own right, and on Pesach we celebrate that
potential for greatness.

*Hefker*, "ownerless," is named as conceptually related and is not distinguished
in the article.

## Rendering guidance

- *Zman Cheiruseinu*: "the Time of our Freedom." Keep *cheirus* as **freedom**
  and reserve it for the standing, not the act.
- Vayikra 25:10 (*ukrasem dror ba'aretz*): "proclaim **release** throughout the
  land." "Liberty" is serviceable but it flattens the *dar*, dwell-in-your-own-
  domain sense that Rashi supplies.
- *Chofshi*: "**free**," and where the context is labor, "free of obligation" or
  "released from service."
- Trap: English "freedom" will absorb all three, and "liberty" absorbs *dror*
  and *cheirus* together. When a source contrasts them (a derashah on
  *charus*/*cheirus*, a Yovel passage set beside *chofshi*), render *dror* as
  release, *chofshi* as free of service, and hold "freedom" for *cheirus*.
- Trap: *charus* ("engraved") and *cheirus* ("freedom") are one consonantal
  spelling in the Luchos pasuk. In a passage that turns on that pun, transliterate
  both and gloss, since no English pair reproduces it.
