# Heretic: min / apikores / kofer / meshumad (mumar)

## Bottom line

Rambam's taxonomy (*Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:6-9) is the one to translate by. *Min*
is a **sectarian**, wrong about Hashem Himself. *Apikores* is a **scoffer**,
wrong about prophecy, and in practice one who insults Torah and its scholars.
*Kofer* is a **denier**, wrong about the Torah, techiyas hameisim or Moshiach.
*Meshumad* is an **apostate**, one who has left Klal Yisrael for another faith
or is notorious for deliberate transgression. *Mumar* is the censor's tamer
replacement for *meshumad*, best rendered **convert-out**. The trap: "heretic"
absorbs all five, and Rambam's point is that each names a different denial.

## Distinctions

### min (מין)
Rambam (*Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:7) gives five: one who says there is no God running
the world, one who says there are many, one who says God has a body, one who
says God did not create everything, one who serves an intermediary. *Avodah
Zarah* 26b: the *min* transgresses to anger Hashem, the *meshumad* for pleasure.

Etymologies offered: Rabbi Yishaya of Trani ties *min* to the idol Meni
(Yeshayahu 65:11). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) makes *minim* the
followers of a Greek philosopher, as *apikorsim* follow Epicurus. Rabbi Shmuel
David Luzzatto (to Shemos 20:4) works from the plain biblical sense of *min*,
"type, species": each sectarian holds a slightly different creed, but all are
one *type*. Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia: they called themselves *maaminim*, and
Chazal cut the word down to *minim*; Rabbi J. D. Eisenstein (*Otzar Yisroel*
s.v. *min*) reports the same. Rabbi Shlomo Tzvi Shick applies it to the Tzedukim.

### apikores (אפיקורוס)
Never in Tanach; twice in the Mishnah (*Sanhedrin* 10:1, *Avos* 2:14). Rambam
(*Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:8) gives three: one who denies prophecy altogether, one
who denies the prophecy of Moshe Rabbeinu, one who denies that Hashem knows
man's deeds. In his Mishnah commentary he adds the settled usage, anyone who
denigrates the Torah or those who learn it, matching *Sanhedrin* 99b, which
defines an *apikores* as one who insults a talmid chacham or insults another in
his presence. Rambam there connects the word to *hefker*; Rabbi Eliyahu
HaBachur takes it from the name of Epicurus.

### kofer (כופר)
Root כ-פ-ר. Rambam (*Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:6) gives three *kofrim*: one who denies
the Torah, one who denies techiyas hameisim, one who denies the coming of
Moshiach; and (3:8) splits the first into denying that the Torah is from Hashem,
denying the Oral Torah's authority to explain the Written, and denying that the
Torah is unchangeable. The root's Mishnaic sense is legal denial, a litigant
denying a claim (*Shevuos* 4:1, 4:3-4, 5:1-2, 6:3); the bridge to heresy is
covering over, the denier covering the facts with something else.

### meshumad (משומד)
Rambam (*Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:9): one notorious for deliberately transgressing
any single law or the whole Torah, who has turned his back on Klal Yisrael and
attached himself to another nation and its religion, uncoerced. Rashba (Responsa
3:352) rejects "renegade" and follows *Avodah Zarah* 26b, above.

Etymologies split three ways. From ש-מ-ד, to destroy: Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur,
because such conversions happen in times of *shmad*, the destructive decrees
(see the uncensored *Rosh HaShanah* 19a); Rabbi Yair Chaim Bachrach (*Mekor
Chaim* to *Orach Chaim* 118), because the apostate has destroyed his own tie to
the nation; Rabbi Shmuel David Luzzatto (to Shemos 12:43), one who converted
under a decree of *shmad* and kept practicing once the coercion lifted. Ramban
(there) derives it from מ-ד-ע, the root Targum Onkelos uses for Yosef
recognizing his brothers (Bereishis 42:8): the *meshumad* has so estranged
himself that he no longer recognizes his people or their God, the final ayin
having dropped. Third, from ע-מ-ד, which in Syriac Aramaic means to immerse:
Rav Hai Gaon, cited by Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra (*Sefer HaIyunim VeHaDiyunim*) and
Rabbi Shlomo Ibn Parchon (*Machberes HeAruch*), reads the word as originally
*meshuamad*, one who has immersed, hence a Christian by baptism, on which
reading Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra holds Rabshakeh could not have been one. Rashbatz
(*Magen Avos*) keeps both forms, *meshuamad* for Christians and *meshumad* for
other idolaters; the *Kuzari* (3:65) uses *meshuamadim* for Christians. Rabbi
Tanchum HaYerushalmi (*HaMadrich HaMaspik*) rejects Rav Hai Gaon outright:
*meshumad* covers any apostate, which is why Chazal (*Sanhedrin* 60a) call
Rabshakeh one.

### mumar (מומר)
Root מ-ו-ר, to switch. Introduced because Christian censors found *meshumad* too
offensive; switching religions sounds milder than being destroyed. Rabbi Shmuel
David Luzzatto: *mumar* is one who changed religion willingly, against the
coerced-then-persisting *meshumad*. Chasam Sofer (*Kovetz Teshuvos* 79) inverts
the severity: *mumar* is anyone who traded the eternal Torah for something
passing, even in one mitzvah, while *meshumad* is reserved for one who rejects
the whole Torah; he adds that the printers blurred the two in practice.

## Rendering guidance

- Translating Rambam's list at *Hilchos Teshuvah* 3:6, keep four English words:
  "sectarians, scoffers, deniers and apostates." A single "heretics" destroys
  the taxonomy the halachah builds.
- מין: "sectarian." Avoid "heretic," the generic, and any historical
  identification the sources do not make.
- אפיקורוס: "scoffer" where the sense is insulting Torah and talmidei chachamim
  (*Sanhedrin* 99b, Rambam to the Mishnah); "*apikores*" transliterated where
  Rambam's three doctrinal denials are meant, since no English word covers them.
- כופר: "denier," not "unbeliever"; the root is active covering-over. In a
  Shevuos sugya it is plain "denial" of a claim, so keep heresy out.
- משומד / מומר: "apostate" and "convert-out," never one word for both. Where a
  source turns on which is graver, say which view you follow: Rabbi Shmuel David
  Luzzatto has *meshumad* the coerced-then-willing one and *mumar* the freely
  willing one; Chasam Sofer has them the other way about.
- Trap: the berachah against heretics. Where the text reads *malshinim*, render
  "informers," noting that this is widely held to be a censored form of the
  older nusach *meshumadim*, which some still say.
