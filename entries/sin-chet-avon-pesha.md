# Sin: chet / avon / pesha (aveirah)

## Bottom line

*Chet* is the general and lightest word: **sin**, and beside its neighbors the
**inadvertent sin** (*shogeg*). *Avon* is **iniquity**, the willful sin
(*meizid*). *Pesha* is the **rebellious sin**, done to defy Hashem. That is the
Gemara's ladder (Yoma 36b), and the *vidui* keeps its ascending order: "I have
sinned, I have done iniquity, I have rebelled." Rabbi Netanel Weil: the ladder
holds only when the words stand together; a lone *chet* or *avon* covers every
kind of sin and is simply "sin." *Aveirah*, the Rabbinic catch-all, is
**transgression**.

## Distinctions

### chet (חטא)
Root ח-ט-א, whose plain sense is to miss, to lack. Rashi (Melachim I 1:21)
reads Batsheva's *chataim* as "lacking": she and Shlomo would lack the royal
standing due them. The Vilna Gaon (Mishlei 1:10, 13:6) builds on that: a
*chet* is a sin of lack, the failure to perform a positive mitzvah. Rabbi
Yechezkel Landau joins this to the Gemara: the *chet* is the failure to do
teshuvah after an inadvertent sin. Rabbi Moshe Shapiro keeps the Gemara's
*shogeg* and takes Rashi's "lack" as the lesson: a *chet* is a missed goal; the
sinner falls short of the purpose for which man was made. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim: *chet* names the sinner's lack of mindfulness at the moment of the
act. Malbim, who takes all three words as deliberate sins differing in motive:
*chet* is the sin of one swayed by bodily desire, who indulges in what he knows
is wrong. Peirush HaRokeach: inadvertent, as the Gemara.

### avon (עון)
Root ע-ו-ה, crooked. Gemara: the deliberate sin, *meizid*. Pappenheim: the sin
of one who "thinks too much," reasons his way to the conclusion that the sin is
right, and acts on it. Malbim: the sin of one whose intellect has been led
astray. Peirush HaRokeach departs from the Gemara here: *avon* is the sin from
which one drew physical pleasure or some other benefit.

### pesha (פשע)
Root פ-ש-ע. Gemara: the sin of rebellion. Pappenheim: the sinner knows the act
is wholly wrong and does it anyway, to rebel against Hashem. Malbim: the same,
shameless defiance. Peirush HaRokeach: simply the willful sin (he has moved
*meizid* here, having given *avon* the sense of benefit).

### aveirah (עבירה)
Root ע-ב-ר, to pass over. Not a Biblical word. Rabbinic, once specifically for
indecency, now the everyday term for any wrongdoing. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi
Mecklenburg: the sinner "passes over" his own thoughts so as not to look at
the ugliness of the deed. The Gemara (Shabbos 40a) calls even one who breaks a
Rabbinic prohibition an *avaryan*, so the word reaches across the Rabbinic line
too.

How sharp are the categories? Rambam (Hilchos Teshuvah) codifies the *vidui*
as *chatati, aviti, pashati*, the Kohen Gadol's formula (Mishnah Yoma 4:2).
Rabbi Yosef Babad (*Minchas Chinuch*): say whichever applies. Rabbi Elazar
Menachem Mann Shach: say all three always, because no sin sits cleanly in one
box; an inadvertent sin carries traces of wantonness and rebellion, and a
rebellious one traces of inadvertence.

## Rendering guidance

- The triple, in the *vidui* or in a pasuk: "sin, iniquity, and rebellious
  sin" (or as verbs, "I have sinned, I have done iniquity, I have rebelled").
  Keep the ascending order; never collapse two of them into "sin."
- Standalone: *chet* is "sin"; *avon* is "sin" too (Weil), and "iniquity" only
  where the text sets it against *chet*; *pesha* alone is "rebellious sin" or
  "willful transgression," so the defiance stays audible.
- *Chet* is "inadvertent," not "accidental" or "a mistake": the *vidui*
  confesses it, and Rabbi Moshe Shapiro reads it as a goal missed.
- Trap: "sin" absorbs all three, and "transgression" is ambiguous between
  *pesha* and *aveirah*. Reserve "transgression" for *aveirah*; give *pesha*
  the word "rebellious."
- Malbim and Peirush HaRokeach re-sort the three (by motive; by benefit). If a
  derashah turns on their reading, follow it and say so; otherwise the Gemara's
  *shogeg* / *meizid* / *mered* ladder is the default.
