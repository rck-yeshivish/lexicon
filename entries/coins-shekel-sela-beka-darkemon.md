# Coins: shekel / sela / beka (darkemon)

## Bottom line

*Shekel* does not mean the same thing in Tanach and in Mishnah. In Tanach a
*shekel* is the full weighed coin; the Mishnah calls that coin a *sela* and uses
*shekel* for the **half-shekel** of the annual donation (Ramban to Shemos 30:13).
Render Biblical שקל **shekel** and Mishnaic סלע **sela**, never "shekel," and
check the register of a Mishnaic שקל before writing "shekel." *Beka* is **half a
shekel**, from "splitting."

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

| סלע | sela | sela | the Mishnaic name for the Biblical shekel (Yerushalmi Kiddushin 1:3); also still "rock" |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Does Onkelos hold that a *beka* is a full shekel?** He renders *beka* as
*tikla* (Bereishis 24:22, Shemos 38:26), the Aramaic of *shekel* with *shin*
turning to *tav*. Rashi (to Shemos 38:26), Rashbam (to Bereishis 24:22), Ibn
Ezra (both places), and Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) say *beka* is *half* a
shekel. Rabbi Yaakov Zev Lev (*Me'at Tzari*) dissolves the clash: they speak two
dialects. Onkelos's *shekel* is the Rabbinic one, already equal to the Biblical
half-shekel; Rashi and Ibn Ezra mean the Biblical shekel. The Bottom line follows
this, since it leaves every source saying the same amount.

**Why the piyut on Shabbos Shekalim says "a *shekel* I will bear."** Rabbi David
Schlussel of Munkatch: the *paytan* used *shekel* in the Rabbinic sense, so he
means exactly the half-shekel the mitzvah requires; Rabbi David Cohen applies the
same to the name of Maseches Shekalim. Rabbi Elazar Rokach of Amsterdam reads it
esoterically: in *at-bash* the letters of שקל come to twenty-six, the gematria of
the Shem HaMeforash, and since Hashem's Name is incomplete until Amalek is wiped
out (Rashi to Shemos 17:16), the *shekel* is halved now and will be whole in the
days of Mashiach. The Bottom line follows Rabbi Schlussel; Rabbi Elazar Rokach's
is a derush on the same fact, not a rival lexicography.

**Why the Rabbis chose *sela*.** No source states it. Rabbi Asher Gvirer of
Beitar Illit suggests it is because *sela* means a strong rock, marking the
stronger, more valuable coin. That is a suggestion, not a ruling.

## Distinctions

### shekel (שקל)
Root ש-ק-ל, "to weigh": a metal coin of a fixed weight, twenty *geirah* to the
*shekel* (Shemos 30:13). Ramban (there) explains the shift: because of the annual
half-*shekel*, people began calling the coin they actually handed over "the
*shekel*," until the word attached so firmly to the mitzvah that it lost its
full-weight sense. Rabbi Menachem Azariah of Fano says the same. Hence the
Mishnah in Shevuos 6:7 can stage a dispute in which a *shekel* is worth less
than a *sela*: the lender lent a *sela*, the lost pledge was worth a *shekel*.

### sela (סלע)
In Mishnaic Hebrew the word carries two senses, the coin (Peah 8:7, Shekalim 1:6,
Bava Metzia 4:3, Bechoros 8:2-8, and many more) and the Biblical "rock, bedrock"
(Kilayim 2:8, Shabbos 6:6, Bava Basra 2:1, and many more). That the coin is the
Torah's *shekel* is stated by Rabbi Chanina in Yerushalmi Kiddushin 1:3, "all the
*shekalim* in the Torah are *selaim*," and in Bechoros 50a, where Rava defines the
*sela d'oraisa* from Shemos 30:13. It shows in how the Mishnah cites pesukim: the
five *shekalim* of pidyon haben (Bamidbar 18:16) are five *selaim* in Bechoros,
the two *shekalim* of the asham (Vayikra 5:15) two *selaim* in Kerisos. Rashi (to Bechoros 5a), Rambam (Hilchos Arachin 4:3, Hilchos Shekalim
1:2), and *Sefer HaChinuch* (mitzvah 355) state the equation plainly; Onkelos and
Targum Yonasan render every *shekel* of the parashah *sila*, the Aramaic form.

### beka (בקע)
Twice only in Tanach: the golden nose-ring Eliezer gave Rivkah (Bereishis 24:22),
and "a *beka* per head, half a *shekel* by the holy *shekel*" in the Mishkan's
silver count (Shemos 38:26). Root ב-ק-ע, "to split," hence half.

### darkemon (דרכמון)
Four times in Tanach (Ezra 2:69; Nechemiah 7:69-71), with *adarchon* twice
(Divrei HaYamim I 29:7; Ezra 8:27) and *darkon* once in the Mishnah (Shekalim
2:1). Rambam (to Shekalim 2:1): one *darkon* is two *selaim*, four times a
half-*shekel*, so Targum Yonasan's *darkemon* for *beka* cannot be a valuation;
it is the generic word for a coin. Modern Hebrew *darkon*, "passport," is from
*derech* and unrelated. Academic identifications of these coins are left out.

## Rendering guidance

- Torah context: שקל is "shekel," חצי השקל is "half-shekel," בקע is "a beka
  (half a shekel)."
- Mishnah or Gemara context: סלע is "sela," not "shekel." A Mishnaic שקל is
  "half-shekel," or "a *shekel* (half a Biblical shekel)" where that is unclear."
- Trap: English "shekel" absorbs both שקל and סלע, which is the very error the
  sugya turns on. Where a source quotes a pasuk beside a halachah (five
  *shekalim* / five *selaim* for pidyon haben), keep both words in English.
- סלע in a Mishnah is not automatically money. Check whether the context is
  bedrock (Shabbos 6:6) before writing "coin."
