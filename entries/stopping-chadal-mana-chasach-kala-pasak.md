# Stopping: chadal / mana / chasach / kala / pasak

## Bottom line

*Chadal* is **cease**: for Rabbi Yosef Grayever it stops because it cannot go
on, and for Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim and Malbim the halt comes from the person or
thing itself, with no verdict on whether it is right (Pappenheim). *Mana* is
**withhold**, a conscious refusal (Grayever), willful and total (Rabbi Shlomo
Aharon Wertheimer), and for Pappenheim a withholding of what ought not be
withheld. *Chasach* is **hold back** or **spare**: stopping a thing before it
starts (Grayever) or interrupting one under way (Wertheimer); for Pappenheim,
followed by Malbim, a check from outside, and for Pappenheim a rightly applied
one. *Kala* stops because the goal is reached or the reason has lapsed
(Grayever, Wertheimer), or, for Malbim, is detained against its nature:
**cease**, **be held back**. *Pasak* is **break off** in the middle (Grayever).
Grayever himself allows that colloquially the words interchange, and that each
keeps its own connotation in most, not all, of its appearances.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Chasach: before or during.**
- Rabbi Yosef Grayever of Ostrow (1808 to 1898): *pasak* stops a thing in the
  middle, once begun, while *chasach* stops it from starting in the first place.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (1866 to 1935), contrary to Grayever: *chasach*
  stops in the middle something already under way. Potiphar had already begun
  handing his household over to Yosef, so keeping back his wife (Bereishis 39:9)
  was not an absolute withholding but an interruption; and *v'lo chasachta es
  bincha* (Bereishis 22:12) is said to Avraham, who had already begun giving
  over to Hashem everything he had.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (1740 to 1814) frames it differently: *chasach* stops
  an act that the laws of morality or nature say should be stopped, so that it
  is in a sense an antonym of *mana*, and he derives it from ח-ס (caring) with
  ס-ך (protection), one who stops another from a reprehensible act. Malbim
  explicitly follows him that *chasach* names an impediment from outside the
  actor.

**Mana: from within or from outside.**

- Grayever: *mana* is stopping by conscious decision, not from inability.
- Wertheimer: willful and total.
- Pappenheim: *mana* means stopping what one is supposed to be doing,
  withholding what ought not be withheld; and, like *chasach*, it indicates
  outside forces that stop a person from a course of action.

**Kala: purpose served, or force applied.**

- Grayever: it stops because the goal is reached or the reason for starting no
  longer applies. His examples are the two appearances of *vayikaleh* (Bereishis
  8:2, Shemos 36:6) and Tehillim 40:12.
- Wertheimer agrees.
- Malbim offers a slightly different take: it stops against the nature of the
  person or thing, from *kele*, a jail, forcibly detained; the nature of rain is
  to fall, so when it stopped at the Mabul's end it was as though Hashem had
  detained it (Bereishis 8:2).

**Chadal: unable, or unwilling.**
- Grayever grounds it in inability, whether from nature or from law.
- Wertheimer qualifies: in the future form with *vav hahipuch*, *v'chadal*, it
  means one who plans in advance not to act though he could, which is why it is
  used of one who deliberately fails to bring the korban Pesach (Bamidbar 9:13)
  or to help his enemy's collapsing donkey (Shemos 23:5).
- Pappenheim holds the word neutral, with no judgment of good or bad, and
  locates the cause in the person himself, without outside interference; Malbim,
  like him, contrasts *chasach* with *chadal*, whose impediment comes from the
  person himself, whether on purpose or not.
- Rabbi Yaakov Toledano (below) takes *chadal* to mean specifically a willful
  decision to stop.

**How Onkelos handles them.** The nuances seem exclusive to Hebrew, Rabbi Klein
says, and the Targumim do not keep them: Onkelos renders *chasach* as *mana*
(Bereishis 20:6, 22:16, 39:9), and both *kala* (Shemos 36:6) and *chadal*
(Bereishis 18:11, 41:49; Devarim 15:11) as *pasak*, literally to split or cut;
elsewhere he leaves *kala* (Bereishis 8:2) and *mana* (Bereishis 30:2; Bamidbar
24:11) in their Hebrew forms. Rashi at Bereishis 18:11, on Sarah's cycles having
*chadal*, follows Onkelos and glosses *pasak*.

- Rabbi Yaakov Toledano of Meknes (1690 to 1771) explains that Rashi was
  troubled: *chadal* means a person willfully deciding to stop, and blood cannot
  decide, so Rashi supplied *pasak*, which an insentient thing can do.
- Rabbi Klein offers a simpler reading, that Rashi merely gave Onkelos' word and
  was not really troubled by it.
- Rabbi Rafael Binyamin Posen (1942 to 2016) notes that Onkelos renders *chadal*
  three times in the Plague of Hail with *mana* but at Sarah with *pasak*, and
  postulates two kinds of *chadal*: of an ongoing process, rendered *pasak*
  (Sarah), and of a one-time event, rendered *mana*, as at the Tower (Bereishis
  11:8) and the hail. He notes that this consistency holds in Onkelos only, not
  in the so-called Targum Yonasan.

## Distinctions

### chadal (חדל) and mana (מנע)
Moshe to Pharaoh: *v'hakolos yechdalun*, the sounds will cease (Shemos 9:29),
and so they do (9:33), and Pharaoh sees they have ceased and hardens his heart
(9:34).

- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 11:8, Tehillim 17:14) explains that *chadal* and the
  Mishnaic *chaludah*, rust, from ח-ל-ד, the root of *cheled*, the world, both
  highlight frailty: no matter how active a man is, he will eventually wear down
  and be forced to stop, as even metals, typically the strongest materials, rust
  after prolonged exposure to the elements.
- For *mana*, Grayever's example: *mena raglecha minesivasam*, withhold your
  foot from their paths (Mishlei 1:15), a conscious decision not to keep company
  with the wicked.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim derives *mana* from the biliteral נ-ע, movement (as in
  *tenuah*), on his theory that in many roots an initial mem inverts the sense of the
  two-letter root that follows: ח-ק engrave against מחק erase, ל-ץ scorn against
  מליץ defend, ר-ד rule against מרד rebel, so *mana* is the opposite of motion.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo* s.v. נע): *chasach* and *mana* are
  both withholding from joining, transitive and taking a mem. *Chasach* is
  mostly for the good, holding back from what is unnatural or unfit (*va'echsoch
  gam anochi oscha mechato li*, Bereishis 20:6; Tehillim 19:14; Mishlei 10:19),
  and *mana* mostly for the bad. The apparent exceptions (Yirmiyahu 2:25, 31:15;
  Mishlei 13:24, 21:26) turn out on close reading to fit: weeping, once
  habitual, is held back as by *mana*, and to the father striking his child is
  unnatural, so *chasach*. In *Yerios Shlomo* he takes *chasach* as a compound
  of *chasi*, covering, and *sach*, screening.
- Wertheimer cites Shmuel II 13:13 for its willful force.

### kala (כלא)
Twice in the form *vayikaleh*: the rains ceased from the heavens at the Mabul's
end (Bereishis 8:2), and the people ceased bringing once the Mishkan had enough
(Shemos 36:6), both, for Grayever, cases where the original reason to continue
was no longer in play. Grayever also brings David: *atah Hashem lo sichla
rachamecha mimeni* (Tehillim 40:12), asking that mercy not stop though his lack
of good deeds no longer justifies it.

### the verb shabbas
Malbim discusses elsewhere how *shabbas*, "stop, rest," differs from these
terms (*Ayeles HaShachar* 461; to Vayikra 2:13 and 26:6; *Yair Ohr*).

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 22:12: "you did not hold back your son from Me." Not "withhold";
  that is *mana*'s word, and the pasuk's *chasach* is, for Wertheimer, an
  interruption rather than an absolute withholding.
- Shemos 36:6: "and the people were restrained from bringing," or "ceased
  bringing." For Grayever the reason had lapsed, not the will.
- Trap: English "stop" flattens all five, and "withhold" will quietly cover both
  *mana* and *chasach*. Keep "withhold" for *mana* only.
- A Targum quoted inside a Hebrew passage is not evidence for the Hebrew
  distinction; Onkelos uses one Aramaic word for several of these.
