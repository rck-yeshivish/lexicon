# Stopping: chadal / mana / chasach / kala / pasak

## Bottom line

*Chadal* is **cease**: it stops because it cannot go on, and the halt comes from
within. *Mana* is **withhold**, a deliberate refusal, usually of what should not
have been refused. *Chasach* is **hold back** or **spare**, a check applied from
outside. *Kala* stops because the reason has lapsed: **cease**, **be held back**.
*Pasak* is **break off** in the middle. All five take English "stop," which is
the trap; where two stand near each other, give each its own verb.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| חדל | chadal | cease | cannot continue; from within, no value judgment (Grayever, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| מנע | mana | withhold | deliberate and total; of what ought not be withheld (Wertheimer, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| חשך | chasach | hold back, spare | a check from outside (Malbim); before it starts (Grayever) or mid-course (Wertheimer) |
| כלא | kala | cease, be held back | the reason has lapsed (Grayever); or detained against its nature (Malbim) |
| פסק | pasak | break off | cuts something already begun (Grayever) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Chasach: before or during.** Rabbi Yosef Grayever: *chasach* stops a thing
from ever starting, against *pasak*, which cuts one already running. Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer says the opposite: *chasach* interrupts a process
already begun. Potiphar had handed Yosef the run of his house, so keeping back
his wife (Bereishis 39:9) was a break in a transfer under way; and *"v'lo
chasachta es bincha"* (Bereishis 22:12) speaks to Avraham, who had long been
handing over all he had. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reframes it: *chasach* stops what morality or
nature says should be stopped, the near-antonym of *mana*, and he derives it from
ח-ס (caring) with ס-כ (protection). Malbim follows him that *chasach* names an
impediment from outside the actor. The Bottom line follows Wertheimer and
Malbim, the two pesukim being decisive.

**Kala: purpose served, or force applied.** Rabbi Yosef Grayever and Rabbi
Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: it stops because the goal is reached or the cause has
lapsed. Malbim: it stops against its own nature, from *kele*, a prison; rain's
nature is to fall, so when it ceased at the Mabul's end Hashem in effect detained
it. Both fit Bereishis 8:2 and Shemos 36:6; Malbim's also fits Tehillim 40:12.

**Chadal: unable, or unwilling.** Rabbi Yosef Grayever grounds it in inability.
Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer qualifies: in the future form *v'chadal* it means
a man who plans in advance not to act though he could, which is why it is used
of one who deliberately fails to bring the korban Pesach (Bamidbar 9:13) or
passes by his enemy's collapsing donkey (Shemos 23:5). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
holds the word neutral, neither praising nor blaming, and locates the cause in
the actor rather than outside him; Malbim agrees on that last point.

**How Onkelos handles them.** He does not preserve the distinctions: *chasach*
becomes *mana* (Bereishis 20:6, 22:16, 39:9), while both *kala* (Shemos 36:6) and
*chadal* (Bereishis 18:11, 41:49; Devarim 15:11) become *pasak*, literally to
cut; elsewhere he leaves *kala* (Bereishis 8:2) and *mana* (Bereishis 30:2;
Bamidbar 24:11) in Hebrew. Rashi at Bereishis 18:11, on Sarah's cycles having
*chadal*, follows Onkelos and glosses *pasak*. Rabbi Yaakov Toledano explains
that Rashi was troubled: if *chadal* is a willful stopping, blood cannot will
anything, so Rashi supplied *pasak*, which an insentient thing can do; the
plainer reading is that Rashi simply gave Onkelos' word. Rabbi Rafael Binyamin
Posen finds a rule instead: *chadal* of an ongoing process becomes *pasak*
(Sarah), of a one-time event *mana*, as at the Tower (Bereishis 11:8) and thrice
in the Plague of Hail; the consistency holds in Onkelos alone.

## Distinctions

### chadal (חדל) and mana (מנע)
Moshe to Pharaoh: *"v'hakolos yechdalun,"* the thunder will cease (Shemos 9:29),
and so it does (9:33), and Pharaoh sees it has ceased and hardens his heart
(9:34). For *mana*: *"mena raglecha minesivasam,"* withhold your foot from their
paths (Mishlei 1:15), a decision not to keep company with the wicked. Rabbi
Shlomo Pappenheim derives it from the biliteral נ-ע, movement (as in *tenuah*),
on his rule that a prefixed mem inverts a two-letter root: ח-ק engrave against
מחק erase, ל-צ scorn against מליץ defend, ר-ד rule against מרד rebel, so *mana*
undoes motion. Wertheimer notes its willful, total force (Shmuel II 13:13).

### kala (כלא)
Twice in the form *vayikale*: the rains ceased from the heavens at the Mabul's
end (Bereishis 8:2), and the people ceased bringing once the Mishkan had enough
(Shemos 36:6), both cases where the reason to continue had lapsed. David: *"atah
Hashem lo sichla rachamecha mimeni"* (Tehillim 40:12), asking that mercy not stop
for want of merits to justify it.

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 22:12: "you did not hold back your son from Me." Not "withhold";
  that is *mana*'s word, and the pasuk's *chasach* is the softer one.
- Shemos 36:6: "and the people were restrained from bringing," or "ceased
  bringing." The reason had lapsed, not the will.
- Trap: English "stop" flattens all five, and "withhold" will quietly cover both
  *mana* and *chasach*. Keep "withhold" for *mana* only.
- A Targum quoted inside a Hebrew passage is not evidence for the Hebrew
  distinction; Onkelos uses one Aramaic word for several of these.
