# Corner: peah / keren / zavit / pinah / miktzoa / katzeh

## Bottom line

*Peah* is the broad **corner** or **side** of an area, and in most pesukim
simply a **direction** (Onkelos: *ruach*). *Keren* is a **horn**, and a corner
only where something juts out, chiefly the corners of the Altar. *Zavit* is the
inside of a corner; *pinah* is the outside of it, and by extension a
**prominence** or **cornerstone**, so *pinah* often wants "cornerstone" or
"leading man," not "corner." *Miktzoa* is the sharp **angle** where two sides
meet at a point; *katzeh* is the **edge/end**, a right-angled corner. The trap:
Onkelos renders *peah*, *pinah*, *miktzoa*, *katzeh* and *paam* all as *zavit*,
which flattens the Hebrew; a translation into English must not follow him and
print "corner" five times.

## Distinctions

### peah (פאה)
The commonest biblical word for corner, roughly eighty-five occurrences, but in
most of them it names a cardinal direction (Shemos 27:9; Bamidbar 35:5), where
Onkelos gives *ruach*; only in the four places where it means "corner" proper
(Vayikra 19:9, 19:27, 23:22) does he leave it untranslated. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim (1740 to 1814) gives two etymologies: in *Yerios Shlomo* from
PEH-HEY, "mouth," a corner being where one enters and leaves an area as the
mouth is where food enters and words leave; in *Cheshek Shlomo* from PEH-ALEPH,
"wide corner." The Moabite noblemen called *paas* (Bamidbar 24:17; Yirmiyahu
48:45) are those set apart at the corner of their society and the cornerstones
on which it rests. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785 to 1865): *peah* need not
be in a corner at all, only cut off and set apart, which is why the *peah* of a
field may be left in the middle (Mishnah *Peah* 3:1).

### keren (קרן)
Primarily an animal's **horn**, and in Tanach the horn-like protrusions at the
four corners of the Altar (Shemos 38:2; Vayikra 8:15; Yechezkel 43:20); the
Mishnah widens it to any corner. Malbim: the core sense is the horn that bulges
from the head, borrowed for anything projecting outward, so a ray of sunlight is
a *keren* and a corner is a *keren* because it juts from an otherwise flat
surface. Rabbi Mecklenburg (*HaKsav VeHaKabbalah* to Shemos 27:2, Vayikra 4:7)
says the same. Pappenheim instead traces it to KUF-REISH, "strong impact,"
which yields *korah*, *tikrah* and *kir*, weight-bearing points of impact; a
corner is where two sides collide.

### zavit (זוית)
Twice in Tanach: the hewn corners of a palace (Tehillim 144:12) and the corners
of the Altar (Zechariah 9:15). Onkelos uses it as his standard rendering for
every other corner word. Pappenheim links *zavit* to *ziv* (radiance): turning a
corner is a change of direction, and a shiny thing likewise redirects the eye
(so Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg explains him). He accordingly reads the old name of
Iyar, Ziv (Melachim I 6:1), as the month in which the sun turns its corner into
spring.

### pinah (פינה)
Twice in the Chumash, both for the corners of the Altar (Shemos 27:2, 38:2),
where Onkelos gives *zavit*, and twenty-eight more times in Tanach. Pappenheim:
*zavit* is the inner face of a corner, *pinah* the outer. Stand inside the room
and it is a *zavit*; stand in the street and the same corner is a *pinah*. Hence
a *pinah* must be built stronger than the rest, being exposed, and hence leaders
are *pinos* (Shoftim 20:2; Shmuel I 14:38), men required to be of better quality
than the rest. He derives it from PEH-NUN, "front/surface," the root of *panim*
and of *panah*, to turn and face: at a *pinah* one must turn to keep following
the perimeter; *peninim* (precious stones) he reads the same way, a gem cut so
its faces and their corners show. Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche (1920 to 2016)
traces both *peah* and *pinah* to the single letter PEH, "end/edge," and the
Rashba (responsa vol. 4 §278) presumes *pinah* and *zavit* are plain synonyms.
A separate reading (*Daas Mikra* to Nechemiah 3:24) makes the primary sense
"raised place," from *rosh pinah* (Tehillim 118:22) and *pinos u'gevohos*
(Tzefaniah 1:16); the *even pinah* stands against the *even mosad* in Yirmiyahu
51:26, foundation stone underground and corner stone at the top.

### miktzoa (מקצוע)
Root ק-צ-ע, whose verb is cutting, peeling or scraping, and which also gives
*maktzua*, the tool (Yeshayahu 44:13; *Shabbos* 123b), since one cuts with the
blade held at an angle to the surface. Pappenheim: where *pinah* covers any
angle, right, acute or obtuse, *miktzoa* is specifically the acute angle (he
concedes it is sometimes a right angle), a single sharp point where two sides
converge rather than an area; hence he reads the Mishnah's praise of monetary
law as a *miktzoa baTorah* (*Bava Basra* 10:8) as the discipline that sharpens
the mind. Rabbi Yishmael ben Chachamon holds that Mishnah's *miktzoa* is the
ordinary biblical "corner"; Rabbi Yitzchak Zev Yadler (1843 to 1917) reads it as
the cornerstone and wellspring of Torah study, monetary law being built on
logic.

### katzeh (קצה)
Root ק-צ-ה, "edge/end." Pappenheim: the corner that forms a **right angle**, and
like *peah* a spacious area at the corner rather than *miktzoa*'s single point.
He separates *katzeh* from *peah* by orientation, *katzeh* being a corner above,
below, in front or behind, and *peah* one at the side. He groups here *keitz*,
*ketzitzah*, *kayitz*, *yekitzah*, *kotz* and *katzin* (officer), which stands
to *katzeh* as *paas* stands to *peah*; the full family is set out in
`waking-yekitzah-oorah.md`.

Two rarities complete the set, both too infrequent to carry weight in a
rendering: *atik* (Yechezkel 41:15, 42:3), which Ibn Saruk gives a root of its
own, Ibn Janach and Radak trace to נ-ת-ק (uprooting), and Pappenheim to TAV-KUF
("static," a panel holding a wall in place); and *demeshek* (Amos 3:12), a
*hapax* that Radak takes as the corner of a bed and Rashi as the place name
Damesek.

## Rendering guidance

- Default renderings: *peah* "corner" when it is a corner and "side/direction"
  when the pasuk pairs it with north, south, east or west; *keren* "horn" unless
  the context is plainly a corner; *zavit* "corner" (inner); *pinah* "corner"
  in a building, "cornerstone" or "leading man" when it is figurative;
  *miktzoa* "angle"; *katzeh* "edge" or "end."
- Trap: English "corner" will happily absorb *peah*, *zavit*, *pinah*, *miktzoa*
  and *katzeh* at once. Where the passage turns on the difference (Onkelos
  levelling four Hebrew words to *zavit*, or Pappenheim's angle grades), name
  the geometry: "the sharp angle," "the outer corner," "the right-angled edge."
- *keren zavit* is the Gemara's set phrase for a corner (*Berachos* 53b;
  *Kiddushin* 66a). Render it as one thing, "a corner," not "the horn of the
  corner." Rabbi Chaim Ozer Grodzenski (reported by Rabbi Chaim Pinchas
  Scheinberg) read it as "a corner of a corner"; that is a derashah for a note,
  not a lexical claim, as the article itself says.
- Mishnaic *peah* is a halachic term of art. Leave it as *peah* and gloss it;
  "the corner of the field" is misleading, since the *peah* may sit in the
  middle (Mecklenburg on Mishnah *Peah* 3:1). The Mishnah's *rosh tor*
  (*Kilayim* 2:7, 3:3) is likewise not a generic corner but an angle intersected
  by another line.
