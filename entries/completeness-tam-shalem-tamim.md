# Completeness: tam / tamim / shalem

## Bottom line

*Shalem* is **complete**, **whole**: for Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, complete in
quantity, with nothing now lacking. Having more than enough does not disqualify
it. *Tam* / *tamim* is **whole** in quality, an exact match with nothing missing
and nothing extra (Rabbeinu Bachya; Pappenheim). Of a korban it means
**unblemished**, of a person **wholehearted** or **wholesome**, and of the Torah
**perfect**. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer takes a similar line but holds that
*tam* includes quantitative completion as well. *Shleimus* is **completeness**
or **wholeness**.

## Distinctions

### tam / tamim (תם, תמים)
Biliteral ת-ם. All three Avos are called complete with words from this root:
Hashem told Avraham that with his milah he would become *tamim*, whole
(Bereishis 17:1); Yitzchak is an *olah temimah*, an unblemished burnt-offering
(*Bereishis Rabbah* 64:3); and Yaakov is an *ish tam*, a wholesome man
(Bereishis 25:27).
- Rabbeinu Bachya (Bereishis 17:1) gives two marks. First, no deficiency and no
  superfluity: the Torah is *temimah* (Tehillim 19:8) because nothing may be
  added to it or taken from it (Devarim 13:1). Second, the inside like the
  outside: what the *tam* believes in his heart is exactly what his mouth says.
  The upshot is equivalence, as in *teomim*, twins: the *tam* is precisely equal
  to what is asked of him, neither short of it nor beyond it.
- Malbim: completeness of intention, good deeds done with no ulterior motive
  (reward, fear of punishment), and full agreement among the parts of the
  psyche, so the whole man resolves on the good with no inner dissent to be
  appeased.
- Pappenheim: the word runs across the spiritual (a tzaddik), the physical (an
  unblemished animal, Vayikra 1:3, 22:21; the redness of the parah adumah,
  Bamidbar 19:2), and the quantitative (a full measure); it also names the
  innocent, "wholesome" man whose knowledge is complete to him and who seeks
  nothing beyond it, hence the *tam* of the Haggadah, the simpleton. (Rabbi
  Klein notes in passing the *whole* inside the English *wholesome*, a semantic
  affinity with *tam*.)

The Zohar (Bamidbar 165b) ranks *tam* above *tamim*. On "be *tamim* with Hashem"
(Devarim 18:13), coming after the ban on augury, divination, sorcery and
necromancy: innocent and wholesome, not appealing to outside forms of wisdom
such as the dark arts to know the future; the Kedushat Levi does not read
*tamim* as "wholesome" here at all, but as completeness of trust in Hashem,
seeing Him as the One who always supplies whatever is lacking. Onkelos there
renders *shelim*, an Aramaic cognate of *shalem*, and Sefer HaBahir (137) uses
that to prove that *tamim* means *shalem*, which Rabbi Klein takes to suggest
that the two are synonymous, at least colloquially.

### shalem (שלם)
Pappenheim traces it to the biliteral ש-ל, removed, cast away (*shal* your
shoes, Shemos 3:5; *sheol*, *shalal*, *shalvah*, each a taking-away). *Shalem*
is the completion reached when everything removed has been returned: right
now, nothing is lacking. *Shalom* likewise: every factor needed for
well-being present. Pappenheim allows that *tam* and *shalem* may colloquially
mean the same thing, but proposes a fundamental distinction all the same:
*shalem* is quantitative completeness, *tam* qualitative. Hence *shalem* for
honest weights that are not short of their declared weight (Devarim 25:15), for
the uncut stones of the mizbeach, unchipped and unnotched (Devarim 27:6;
Yehoshua 8:31), and for the quota of sin not
yet full in Avraham's day (Bereishis 15:16, of the Emori).

*Shalem* does not exclude excess: *leshalem*, to pay, still applies to one who
paid more than the price, whereas *tamim* cannot apply without an exact match
and fails the moment anything is missing or extra.

- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer takes a similar approach but differs: *shalem*
  is exclusively quantitative, while *tam* is qualitative completion that
  *includes* the quantitative.
- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi seems to understand both words as completion, but
  *shalem* as a neutral word carrying no value judgment and *tam* as a positive
  form of completion.
- And if Rabbi Klein has understood him correctly, Rabbi Shimon Dov Ber Analak
  of Siedlce holds that *tam* is completion in one particular aspect and not
  necessarily in all, while *shalem* implies a more overall completion, as
  Yaakov came to Shechem *shalem* (Bereishis 33:18) and Chazal expound it of
  body, Torah and wealth (Shabbos 33b); *tam*, by contrast, almost exclusively
  denotes completion in the spiritual realm, not the physical, intellectual or
  financial.

The commentators are bothered by *vayitmu*, "and the days of weeping in mourning
for Moshe were finished" (Devarim 34:8), which uses a cognate of *tam* for the
closing of a fixed count of days, a quantitative measure. Rabbi Klein leaves two
answers standing.

- One may accept Rabbi Wertheimer's supposition that *tam* covers quantitative
  completion as well as qualitative, while *shalem* covers only the
  quantitative.
- Or one may answer that the completion here is not of the days but of the
  quality of the mourning: Rabbi Moshe Sofer explains that the mourning for
  Moshe was completed when Yisrael compounded the loss of their leader with the
  realization that they could have become his talmidim as Yehoshua did, had they
  not been lazy.

*Tam* also means finishing, the completion of a process (Bereishis 47:15;
Vayikra 25:29; Bamidbar 17:28; Melachim I 7:22; Tehillim 102:28). So can
*shalem* (Melachim I 7:51; Yeshayahu 60:20), *maleh* (Bereishis 29:21; Melachim
I 1:14) and *kaleh* (Bereishis 2:1, 41:53, 43:2). As Pappenheim makes clear, in
this sense all four can be synonyms, and they differ in the core meaning of
their roots. *Kaleh* he traces to the biliteral כ-ל, "all, every," which looks
past the particular items to the whole; that wider view is how it came to mean
completion. Rabbi Shimon Dov Ber Analak: when *tam* means finishing, it is the
finishing of one component of a larger whole, where *kilayon* is finishing a
thing off entirely.

Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg applies Pappenheim's distinction to *sheva
shabbasos temimos* (Vayikra 23:15): Chazal teach that the weeks are *temimos*
only when Yisrael act according to Hashem's will (Vayikra Rabbah 28:3), and
Rabbi Mecklenburg sees them reading this out of *temimos* itself, which names
qualitative completeness and so cannot be a mere count of the passage of time.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shleimus*: "completeness" (the state of lacking nothing)
  or "wholeness." Reach for "perfection" only where the Hebrew itself names
  the ultimate degree and the context rules out further growth; the
  *shalem* may still gain, the "perfect" cannot.
- *Tamim* of a korban: "unblemished." Of a person: "wholehearted" (Malbim's
  undivided intention) or "whole"; "wholesome" or "simple" for the innocent
  sense (the Haggadah's *tam*, Yaakov the *ish tam*); of the Torah: "perfect."
- When the two stand together (Onkelos equates them, so a text may pair them):
  "whole" for *tam*, "complete" for *shalem*, and let the two English words
  do the work the Hebrew does.
- Trap: "complete" absorbs both, and "perfect" absorbs both while adding a
  ceiling neither word asserts. A derashah that turns on the choice of word
  (*temimos* of the Omer, *vayitmu* of the mourning) needs the quality-versus-
  quantity line visible in English.
- *Shalem* of a person is broad (health, learning, means; Shabbos 33b); do not
  narrow it to the moral sense that belongs to *tam*.
