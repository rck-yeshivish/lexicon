# Master: adon / ribbon / rav

## Bottom line

*Adon* is the Hebrew word for a **master**, and the form *Adonai* is a Name of
Hashem. *Ribbon* is the Aramaic equivalent that Targum Onkelos uses for *adon*.
It is an honorific form of *rav*, which also means **master** and is the
Mishnah's ordinary word for a slave's owner. There are two readings of the
difference. The working assumption is that it is simply Hebrew against Aramaic.
Rabbi Baruch Aryeh HaLevi Fischer *suggests* a thematic one: *adon* is a
standing title, while *rav*/*ribbon* is used only where there is a subordinate
in the master's charge.

## Distinctions

### adon (אדון)
The usual word in Tanach for the master of a slave, with its derivatives. The
special form *Adonai* is used of Hashem, Master of the Universe, all of
Creation being His servants. Rabbi Baruch Aryeh HaLevi Fischer suggests that
*adon* is a title anyone who is a master bears, in every context, once he is a
master; it is all-encompassing. On that basis he explains that *Adonai*, built
from *adon*, is considered a Name of Hashem, the all-encompassing Master of the
Universe, while *ribbon*/*rav* is not His Name as such, but only a description
of His role toward particular elements of Creation.

Tosafos Yom Tov (to *Bava Kamma* 5:6, end): *adon* is said of a slave's master,
and *baal* is not.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 38:17): Latin *don* comes
from *adon*, and from *don* came *domnus*, *dominus*, with an added mem beside
the nun; he suggests it reached Latin through the Canaanite spoken by Phoenician
settlers in North Africa. He also proposes that *adon* may come from *dan*, as
Rav Pappenheim holds, an added aleph at the start being very common.

### ribbon (רבון)
Aramaic. Targum Onkelos renders the common noun *adon* as *ribbon* throughout.
It is the honorific form of *rav*. On Rabbi Baruch Aryeh HaLevi Fischer's
suggestion, *ribbon*/*rav* is used specifically of the bond between a master
and the protégé in his charge, a slave, a *talmid*, an apprentice, and only
under those conditions.

### rav (רב) and rebbi (רבי)
"Master." In Mishnaic Hebrew it is the standard word for the owner of a slave.
In Tanach *rav* appears only in construct, bound to another word: *rav
tabachim* (chief of the executioners, an army commander), *rav hachovel* (master
of the rope, a ship's captain), *ravei hamelech* (the king's officers). *Rebbi*
is "my *rav*," my master, "Rabbi" being its Anglicized form; for a Torah
scholar, or even a lay observant Jew, that master is his teacher in Torah, and
for a thief it is the leader of his delinquent gang. Reish Lakish, the quintessential
*baal teshuvah*, who went from highway robber to master Torah scholar,
insinuated to his teacher and brother-in-law Rabbi Yochanan (*Bava Metzia* 84a)
that Rabbi Yochanan had done him no real service in bringing him to teshuvah,
"There they called me Rabbi and here they call me Rabbi." The word names the
relation of a man to his master, and the leader of bandits is his gang's
master no less than a rebbi is his talmidim's.

### The two together in one berachah
The morning berachah (*Berachos* 60b) calls Hashem *Ribbon kol hamaasim, Adon
kol haneshamos*.

- The Vilna Gaon: the first clause speaks of His eminence in the physical,
  action-bound world, and takes the Aramaic word because Aramaic belongs to the
  outer, surface layer of existence; the second speaks of His dominion over the
  spiritual realm, and takes the Hebrew word because Hebrew, like that realm,
  reaches the essence of Creation.
- Rabbi Aharon Leib Steinman, a leader of contemporary Jewry, notes that here
  the Aramaic clause comes first, but does not elaborate on what follows from
  the observation.

## Rendering guidance

- אדון: "master." In a berachah or where the word stands for Hashem, "Master"
  capitalized; *Adonai* itself stays as it is and is not translated.
- רבון: "Master" as well, so when it stands beside *adon* something must give.
  In *Ribbon kol hamaasim, Adon kol haneshamos*, render "Master of all deeds,
  Lord of all souls," or keep *Ribbon* transliterated, so the Vilna Gaon's
  Aramaic-against-Hebrew reading is still visible.
- רב: "master" for a slave's owner; in the construct forms of Tanach, "chief"
  (*rav tabachim*, chief of the executioners) rather than "master," which reads
  oddly in English.
- רבי: "Rebbi" or "Rabbi," never "my master" in running text; but where a
  passage turns on the literal sense, as Reish Lakish's does, gloss it once.
- Trap: English "Lord" and "Master" will each absorb *adon* and *ribbon*. Any
  phrase that pairs them is making a distinction, and one English word for both
  leaves a duplication on the page.
- Trap: do not treat *rav* as a lesser *adon*. On Rabbi Baruch Aryeh HaLevi
  Fischer's reading the difference is not rank but whether a subordinate is in
  view.
