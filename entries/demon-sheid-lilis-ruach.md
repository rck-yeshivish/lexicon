# Demon: sheid / lilis / ruach

## Bottom line

*Sheid* is the **demon**, the general word: *Chazal* put it between the angel
and the man, winged and knowing the future like the one, eating and drinking and
dying like the other. *Ruach*, the word that plainly means wind or spirit, is
the **apparition** with no body and no form at all. *Lilis* is the **female
demon**, and whether that is the name of one particular demoness or the name of
a whole class of them is disputed, as is what the word means in the one pasuk
where it appears. Two of the three are separated by body: the *sheid* has one,
the *ruach* has none.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether Lilis is a name or a category.** The Kabbalistic reading makes it a
proper name: Lilis is one particular *sheidah*, the mother of all the *sheidim*,
and the *Zohar* has her as the consort of the Satan, Samael. Others hold it is a
category rather than a name, a kind of female demon.

**What the word means in Yeshayahu 34:14**, where it first appears. Three
views. Rashi: the name of a female *sheid*. Ibn Ezra: a kind of nocturnal bird.
Radak: some sort of nocturnal animal. *Niddah* 24b, which speaks of a woman who
miscarries a child in the form of a Lilis and whose ensuing discussion shows
that the child had wings, supports the first two and not the third, since on
that evidence a Lilis must be a demon or a bird. Rashi there again has it a
female demon, adding that *sheidim* have the faces of men and wings besides.

**Whether the sheid has wings.** *Chagigah* 16a counts wings among the ways
*sheidim* resemble angels. Rashi to *Sanhedrin* 109a gives the wings to the
*lilin* and not to the *sheidim*, which implies they have none. Some answer
that the *sheidim* the builders of the Tower turned into were a kind without
wings, though *sheidim* in general do have them.

**Whether the female demon has hair.** The *Zohar* (*Bereishis* 54b) and *Sefer
Chassidim* (§1155) hold that female demons have no hair, which is why Boaz knew
his visitor was a woman. On that, Rabbi Yehudah Fatiyah (1859 to 1942) reads
Rashi to *Eruvin* 100b precisely: Rashi wrote that a Lilis is a kind of *sheid*
and not a kind of *sheidah*, because only the males have hair. Others hold that
Lilis is the exception, a female demon who does have hair on her head, and
account for Boaz another way: Lilis comes to men when they are asleep, not when
they are awake.

## Distinctions

### sheid (שד)

The general word. The Gemara (*Chagigah* 16a) sets the *sheidim* between two
orders of creature: they are like the angels in three ways, in having wings, in
flying about the world, and in having access to what Heaven knows of the future,
and like men in four, in eating, in drinking, in reproducing and in dying.

Rashi (to *Sanhedrin* 109a) describes them from the other side, as having a
humanoid form and eating and drinking as a man does.

### lilis (לילית)

The female demon, and on Rashi's account at *Sanhedrin* 109a the one that takes
the shape of a man but has wings as well. Rashi to *Eruvin* 100b has it a kind
of *sheid*; there the Gemara counts among the ten curses that came upon Chavah
after the Forbidden Fruit that she was doomed to grow hair like a Lilis.

### ruach (רוח)

The word means wind, or spirit, and that is very nearly the ruling: Rashi (to
*Sanhedrin* 109a) marks the *ruchin* off from the other two as having no body
and no form. Where the *sheid* eats and drinks and the Lilis takes a shape, the
*ruach* is the one with nothing to take hold of.

*Sanhedrin* 109a is where the three stand together. Among the punishments of
those who built the Tower, some were turned into *kofim*, monkeys, and others
into *ruchin*, *sheidim* and *lilin*.

## Rendering guidance

Where the three appear in one list, as they do at *Sanhedrin* 109a, they need
three different English words, and the distinction Rashi draws there is the one
to keep: "demon" for *sheid*, "apparition" or "spirit" for *ruach*, and for
*lilin* a word that carries the female sense, since that is what separates them
from the *sheidim*. "Spirit" for *ruach* is safe here but ambiguous in a book
that also uses it for *ruach* as soul or wind; where the context is this one,
"apparition" is the less ambiguous choice.

*Lilis* resists translation, and where the dispute is live a transliteration
with the sense in a note serves better than picking one of the three readings of
Yeshayahu 34:14. A rendering that settles it as "screech owl" or "night
creature" takes Ibn Ezra's or Radak's side against Rashi's without saying so.
