# Spring: Gichon / Shiloach / gachon

## Bottom line

*Gichon* and *Shiloach* are two names for one body of water, the spring by
Yerushalayim (Rashi, Radak, Targum). Render both as proper names, **Gichon**
and **Shiloach**, never as a common noun like "spring" or "aqueduct"; where a
passage needs a gloss, "the Gichon spring." The trap is the *other* Gichon: the
river of Bereishis 2:13 that circles Kush is, for Rashi and Rabbi Avraham
Maimuni, a different river entirely that merely shares the name, so do not let
one English "Gichon" flatten the two. *Gachon* (Bereishis 3:14) is not a place
at all but the serpent's **belly**, from the same biliteral stock.

## Distinctions

### Gichon (גיחון)
Root ג-ו-ח. Ibn Janach and Radak (each in his *Sefer HaShorashim*) take the
core sense as "exiting": the same root yields the verb for a baby coming forth
from the womb (Iyov 38:8, Michah 4:10, Tehillim 22:10) and for a current
leaving its source. Radak (to Bereishis 2:13) therefore explains the river's
name from its going forth from its fount and splitting into tributaries that
"exit" the parent river. Radak cites his father Rabbi Yosef Kimchi that the
Gichon of Bereishis is the Nile, whose waters go out over the banks to water
the land; on that reading the Yerushalayim spring earns the same name because
its waters likewise go out into channels to irrigate the gardens.

Two rivers or one: Rashi (to Kerisus 5b, Berachos 10b, Melachim I 1:33,
Yeshayahu 8:6) insists the Gichon of the coronation and of Chizkiyahu is a
small spring near Yerushalayim, not the great Gichon of Eden. Ibn Ezra (to
Shemos 2:11), following Rav Saadia Gaon, identifies them; Ramah is cited by
*HaKosev* (to *Ein Yaakov*, Horayos 12a) the same way. Rabbi Avraham Maimuni
sides with Rashi and adds that the spring resembled the great river in some way
now lost to us, and so was named for it.

### Shiloach (שילוח)
Root ש-ל-ח. The name the Mishnah prefers for the stream near the Beis HaMikdash
(*Sukkah* 4:10; see Rashi to Sukkah 48a), though the Mishnah keeps "Gichon"
when it retells Chizkiyahu's blocking of the waters (*Pesachim* 4:9). In Tanach
the phrase *Mei HaShiloach* occurs once, Yeshayahu 8:6; the *Shelach* pool of
Nechemiah 3:15 is a near relation. Targum (to Melachim I 1:33 and to Yeshayahu
8:6) renders both Gichon and *Mei Shiloach* as *Shilucha*, and Rashi adduces
exactly that Targum as proof that the two names name one water. The practical
upshot is halachic: a *get* written in Yerushalayim names the Shiloach. Rabbi
Moshe Ibn Chaviv (*Get Pashut*, Even HaEzer 128:37) explains that "Shiloach" is
used to forestall confusion with the great Gichon of Eden. Rabbi Yoel
Teitelbaum of Satmar (*Divrei Yoel*, Even HaEzer 125) objects that a river with
two names requires both in the *get* (Rama, Even HaEzer 128:3), and answers
instead that since no one today calls it Gichon, that name has been
halachically uprooted and Shiloach is its only name.

### gachon (גחון)
The serpent's belly, in the curse "upon your belly shall you go" (Bereishis
3:14). Rav Hirsch (there) ties *gachon* to crawling, progress made not in a
straight line but by squirming along a twisted path, and suggests that the
twisting course of the water is what gave the Gichon its name. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim reaches the same result from the biliteral ג-ח shared by *gachon*
and *Gichon*. Both connect this to the birth sense of the root: forcing through
a narrow opening takes bending and crawling, as a snake crawls.

### negichah (נגיחה)
Rabbi Pappenheim derives "goring" from the same biliteral ג-ח: a horned animal
cannot gore without bending its body and neck so the horns reach the mark.
Rashi (to Bereishis 2:13) had already linked the name Gichon to *negichah*, the
river's flow resembling the outward jab of a goring ox.

## Rendering guidance

- Keep both names as proper names. "Spring," "brook," "pool," and "aqueduct"
  are all glosses, not translations, and each of them has been used to argue
  that the two names are two places; do not smuggle that claim into a
  rendering.
- Where a text says Gichon and the context is Shlomo's coronation (Melachim I
  1:33, 1:38, 1:45), Chizkiyahu's diversion (Divrei HaYamim II 32:30), or
  Menashe's wall (Divrei HaYamim II 33:14), add "the Gichon spring by
  Yerushalayim" on first mention if the reader might otherwise hear Eden.
- Trap: English "Gihon" absorbs both rivers. If the passage turns on Rashi's
  distinction, or on Ibn Ezra's identification, name the two explicitly (the
  Gichon of Eden, the Gichon of Yerushalayim) rather than trusting context.
- גחון in Bereishis 3:14 is "belly," never "Gichon." Only where a source is
  building the wordplay (Rav Hirsch, Rabbi Pappenheim) should the English keep
  both in view, and then by a note, not by the rendering.
- Talmudic idiom: Kerisus 5b derives from these pesukim that a king is anointed
  at a spring, so that his kingship should flow on without interruption. Render
  *maayan* there as "wellspring"; the point is the continuous current.
