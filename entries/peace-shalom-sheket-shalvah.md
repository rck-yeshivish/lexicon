# Peace: shalom / shalvah / sheket / shaanan

## Bottom line

*Shalom* is the general word: **peace**, the absence of war and, at root, wholeness
with nothing lacking (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim). *Shalvah* is peace felt within, or a
pocket of peace inside a larger discord: **tranquility, ease** (Malbim, Pappenheim),
with a shadow of complacency (Bedersi, Rav Hirsch). *Sheket* is the cessation of
motion and outward disturbance: **quiet, stillness, calm**; whether it ranks above
*shalom* (Bedersi) or below it as a mere ceasefire (Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer)
is disputed, and both readings stay live. *Shaanan* is the
fullest rest, free even of the busyness of prosperity: **serenity** (Bedersi,
Pappenheim).

## Distinctions

The Latin *pax* is the progenitor of the
English "peace" and may also be an ancestor of the Mishnaic Hebrew *piyus*,
"appeasement"; Rome had its *pax Romana*, and the Jewish People their own under
Shlomo HaMelech, whose very name is a cognate of *shalom*.

### shalom (שלום)
Root ש-ל-ם.

- Pappenheim takes it back to ש-ל, removed or taken away (שַׁל נעליך, Shemos
  3:5; *sheol*, *shalal*): *shalem* is that from which nothing removed is still
  missing, complete, and *shalom* is having every good needed for prosperity
  with nothing lacking. Elsewhere he defines it as the absence of friction among
  multiple parties; Hashem is *Adon HaShalom* (Maariv of Shabbos) and *Melech
  SheHaShalom Shelo* (Shir HaShirim Rabbah 3:14) because He has no conflicting
  parts and is at peace with Himself.
- Bedersi (Divrei HaYamim I 22:9): the opposite of war, the cessation of
  hostilities.
- Wertheimer: *shalom* (with *shalvah*) is total peace and harmony.
- Malbim: coming to terms with something outside oneself, though it can borrow
  the sense of inner peace.

### shalvah (שלוה)

- Root ש-ל-ה, from Pappenheim's ש-ל: the state in which every disturbance has
  been removed; hence *moshel* and *memshalah*, the rulers whose task is to keep
  it.
- Bedersi ranks it with *shalom*.
- Wertheimer groups the two as total peace.

Why do both of them pair the words? Rabbi Klein suggests that the answer may lie
in their shared etymological root, the biliteral ש-ל.

- Pappenheim, after entertaining synonymy, concludes that *shalom* is the more
  all-encompassing state, which can show itself in all sorts of harmonious
  relationships, while *shalvah* is one peaceful relationship within a wider
  discord.
- Malbim: *shalvah* is inner peace, *shalom* external.

The verb *shalu* sometimes means to be at ease (Rashi and Ibn Ezra to Eichah
1:5) and sometimes to sin unwittingly (Melachim II 4:28, Shmuel II 6:7, Divrei
HaYamim II 29:11; the Targumim typically render *shogeg* as *shaluta*, and see
Daniel 6:5, Rashi to Rus 2:16 and Ramban to Bereishis 38:5). How do the two
meanings converge in one word?

- Bedersi: the complacency of *shalvah* easily breeds indolence, which leaves a
  man not careful enough to avoid sin.
- Rav Hirsch (Bereishis 8:1, Vayikra 5:4) similarly reads the double meaning as
  an allusion to the possible negative side of tranquility, since a man can
  become content with his present spiritual stature, stop striving for greater
  perfection, smugly keep to his tried and tested ways, and so start down the
  slope toward sin.

### sheket (שקט)
Root ש-ק-ט.
- Bedersi: the opposite of movement, the end of all harried toil, and therefore
  more than *shalom*: "stillness" beside "peace."
- Wertheimer, without citing Bedersi, disagrees with that ranking: *sheket* is
  only the absence of outward conflict, with disagreement possibly still in the
  background, a ceasefire while true *shalom* stays elusive.
- Pappenheim: not "quiet" so much as patience and forbearance, a person no
  outside stimulus can work into a frenzy; he remains calm and serene.
- Ibn Janach and Radak seem to define it as abeyance, a calming down after
  turbulence.

### shaanan (שאנן) and shalanan (שלאנן)
- Malbim: related to *shaon*, boisterous din, and its exact opposite, quiet
  because the din is gone (Yirmiyahu 30:10, 48:11; Mishlei 1:33).
- Pappenheim: from ש-א, or possibly even the monoliteral ש, something uniform or
  level in which no differences among its parts are apparent, a root that runs
  to both extremes, yielding *shoah* and *shayis* for total destruction and
  *shaanan* for total tranquility.
- Bedersi posits that *shaanan* implies a still more complete rest than *sheket*
  does, and Pappenheim seems to echo him: unlike *shalom* and *shalvah*, which
  are peace, *shaanan* is calmness and serenity, since one may be in a state of
  complete *shalom* yet still be harried tending the products of his prosperity,
  while *shaanan* precludes that busyness and means one need not interact with
  others at all.

*Shalanan* (Iyov 21:23, a *hapax legomenon*): Ibn Janach, the same thing as
*shaanan* despite the extra lamed; Radak and Pappenheim, a composite word made
of *shalvah* and *shaanan*.

### Shabbos (שבת)
One more peace-word begins with shin: Shabbos
itself, the epitome of rest and quiet in This World and a microcosm of the final
peace awaiting us in the World to Come. Hence the Shabbos afternoon prayer, which
calls the day's rest a rest of *shalom*, *shalvah*, *hashket* (*sheket*) and
*betach*, though many versions of the siddur omit *shalvah* from it. Bereishis
Rabbah 10:9 teaches that by resting on Shabbos, Hashem created *shaanan*, *nachas*
(a cognate of *menuchah*, rest), *shalvah* and *hashket*, and Rashi in *Sefer
HaPardes* finds the allusion in Yeshayahu 32:18, which uses forms of all of them.

## Rendering guidance

- Shabbos Minchah, מנוחת שלום ושלוה והשקט ובטח: "a rest of peace, tranquility,
  quiet, and security"; four words, four renderings. Bereishis Rabbah 10:9 lists
  שאנן, נחת, שלוה, השקט as what Shabbos rest created, and Rashi (*Sefer HaPardes*)
  finds them in Yeshayahu 32:18; a translator who collapses them loses the Midrash.
- שלום ושקט (Divrei HaYamim I 22:9, of Shlomo's reign): "peace and quiet"; the
  English idiom happens to be exact, and it keeps the two words apart.
- Standalone שלוה in mussar: "tranquility" or "ease." When the passage itself warns
  against it (the *shalu* double sense), "complacent ease" is fair; otherwise do not
  smuggle the warning in.
- שאנן: "serene," "untroubled"; as a noun, "serenity."
- Trap: English "peace" absorbs *shalom* and *shalvah*; "quiet" absorbs *sheket* and
  *shaanan*; "calm" absorbs *sheket*, *shalvah*, and *shaanan*. Where two meet, hold
  *shalom* to "peace" (parties), *shalvah* to "tranquility" (the self), *sheket* to
  "quiet" (the scene), *shaanan* to "serenity" (the fullest).
- Trap: *sheket* is not automatically the deeper word. Bedersi and Wertheimer rank it
  in opposite directions; in a passage that contrasts it with *shalom*, follow the
  author's own argument rather than a fixed scale.
