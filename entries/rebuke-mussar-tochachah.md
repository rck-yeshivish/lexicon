# Rebuke: mussar / tochachah

## Bottom line

*Tochachah* is **reproof** (or **rebuke**): spoken, argued, proving to the
other that he did wrong, about his past deeds, and possible between equals.
*Mussar* is **discipline** (or **chastisement**): from a superior to a
subordinate, by word or by blow, aimed at his future conduct; the Vilna Gaon
ties it to *yissurin*, afflictions.

## Distinctions

The column frames the whole discussion with the mitzvah of rebuke (Vayikra
19:17) and with a dictum the column says Edmund Burke (1729-1797), the father of
contemporary conservatism, purportedly said: that the triumph of evil needs
nothing more than that good men do nothing. The Torah, the column writes, takes
this Burkian approach to the responsibility of the righteous and implores us to
reprimand the wicked for their sins. Hebrew collates the notion of rebuking
sinners in two words, *tochachah* and *mussar*.

### tochachah (תוכחה)
Root י-כ-ח. Vilna Gaon: rebuke that comes from the mouth. Hashem's spoken
warning of what will befall Yisrael if they abandon the Torah is called the
*Tochachah*, and there are two (Vayikra 26:14-43, read before Shavuos; Devarim
28:15-68, read before Rosh Hashanah). Malbim sets out three differences, the
who, the what, and the why: *tochachah* may pass even between equals; it is
exclusively verbal, admonishing by logically proving that the other has done
wrong; and it looks backward, castigating for past deeds (the Gaon also has
this third point). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740-1814): kin to
*nochach*, opposite, in ideology and in physical location alike, since by
offering his criticism the rebuker opposes the rebuked ideologically and
possibly physically too, if the two meet face to face. Nonetheless, Pappenheim
argues that the most effective way of rebuking is not direct confrontation but
to question the other's actions "innocently" and spur a debate about whether
they are right; hopefully the ensuing discussion will let the sinner realize the
folly of his ways more powerfully than a litany of his misdeeds would. For that
reason *tochachah* is also related to *hochachah*, proof, and *vikuach*, debate.
The column contrasts this with sermonizing about fire and brimstone in the vein
of Jonathan Edwards's Puritan tract *Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God*: such
a tract may indeed arouse emotive feelings of repentance through elaborate
descriptions of the hellish fires of purgatory, but those emotions are merely
fleeting bursts of contrition unless backed by a logical and intellectual
acceptance to do only good. *Tochachah* seeks to prove the vanity of sin
logically and to provide fodder for a rational acceptance to do only good.

### mussar (מוסר)
Root י-ס-ר. Vilna Gaon: rebuke that comes from the other limbs, not the mouth:
a parent disciplining a child with a smack, Hashem teaching a man by
afflicting him; hence the kinship with *yissurin*. Malbim: given by a person
of authority to a subordinate; carried out by corporal punishment or by a
tongue-lashing; and forward-looking, inculcating right behavior for the
future. Pappenheim: kin to *lehasir*, to remove or separate, either because
*mussar* removes the offender from the path of sin he was entrenched in, or
because it marks the line between right and wrong; or else kin to *assur*,
bound (the everyday "forbidden"), because the rebuker shows the other that he
is bound to certain expectations. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg likewise:
*mussar* is tying, since it gives a man the power to tie down his *yetzer*
and hold his desires in check.

## Rendering guidance

- The pair מוסר ותוכחת (as in Mishlei, e.g. 5:12): "discipline and reproof."
  Two words, and the order of the Hebrew.
- *Tochachah* standalone: "reproof" where the argument is the point, "rebuke"
  where the register is blunt. The two Torah passages keep their proper name,
  "the Tochachah."
- *Mussar* standalone in the sense of chastisement: "discipline,"
  "chastisement," or "correction" (Malbim's future-facing sense); of Hashem's
  afflictions, "chastening." In the sense of the ethical discipline, "moral
  instruction," or the transliteration.
- Trap: "rebuke" absorbs both words, and "discipline" in English also names a
  field of study, which happens to match *mussar*'s second sense but blurs
  the first. When a passage turns on the Gaon's mouth-versus-limbs line or
  Malbim's past-versus-future line, the English must show which word is
  which.
- Where a derashah draws on the kinship to *yissurin* (the Gaon), render
  *yissurin* "afflictions" or "sufferings" and keep *mussar* as "discipline,"
  so the two stay visibly related.
