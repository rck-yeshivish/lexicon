# Thumb: bohen / gudal / agudal / zikfa / ravravta / ilyon

## Bottom line

*Bohen* is the Torah's word for the **thumb** and the **big toe**; *gudal* and
*agudal*, which may be used interchangeably, are its apparent synonyms in later
Hebrew. Rashi equates *bohen* with *gudal* in one place, and elsewhere cites Toras
Kohanim that the *bohen* is the **middle joint** of the *gudal*. *Zikfa*, *ravravta*
and *ilyon* are Aramaic for the thumb, *ilyon* the Targumim's word for *bohen*.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Is the *bohen* the whole thumb or its middle joint?** Rashi (to Vayikra 14:14)
writes that *bohen* is *gudal*; Rashi (to Shemos 29:20) cites Toras Kohanim (to
Vayikra 8:23) that it is the middle joint of the *gudal* (see also Rashi and Tosafos
to Kesubos 5b, and Rambam's commentary to Mishnah Shabbos 8:1). Two resolutions:
- Some say *bohen* is the top part of the thumb, reaching to the middle joint, and
  *gudal* the entire thumb: somewhat synonymous, but *bohen* names a part of it.
- Others say *bohen* literally means *gudal*, but in its ritual context it is
  expounded as a particular part of the *gudal*, from its juxtaposition with the
  blood placed on the middle of the ear: as that is the middle, so is this.

**Which finger is the *gudal*, and why is it so called?** Most commentators take
it as the thumb; Rabbeinu Bachya (to Vayikra 8:23) cites an anonymous view that it
is the little finger, related to *gedilim*, fringes (Devarim 22:12). As the thumb:
- Rabbi Shimshon of Shantz (to Oholos 13:1) and Rabbeinu Bachya (to Vayikra 8:23):
  from ג-ד-ל, the root of *gadol*, big, as the thumb is generally the thickest.
- Abarbanel (to Shemos 29:20): the two words mark different features of the thumb.
  *Gudal*: the greatest and most important finger, since without opposable thumbs
  no one could grasp; it lets the other fingers work, figuratively holding them together.

## Distinctions

### bohen (בהן)
The Torah's word for the right thumb and big toe that receive blood and oil in
purifying the *metzora* (Vayikra 14:14, 14:17, 14:25, 14:28) and blood in
consecrating Aharon and his sons (Shemos 29:20; Vayikra 8:23). Abarbanel: it is
spelled like *bahen*, "on them," for the thumb's special role over the other
fingers, and was borrowed from the thumb for the similar big toe.

### gudal (גודל) and agudal (אגודל)
Neither is in Tanach; both are frequent in rabbinic writings. *Gudal* appears twice
in the Mishnah (Oholos 13:1, Niddah 8:1), *agudal* once (Yoma 2:1, where some
versions read *gudal*). Haughty, conspicuous women walked "heel beside *gudal*"
(Shabbos 62b, Yoma 9b), in small steps, one heel never passing the other foot's big
toe. Hogras ben Levi, criticized in the Mishnah (Yoma 3:11) for not teaching his art
of song, put his *gudal* in his mouth according to the Gemara (Yoma 38b) and his
*agudal* according to Shir HaShirim Rabbah (to Shir HaShirim 3:6); from this and
other sources the two may be used interchangeably. Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein (1860
to 1940) explains the doublet as another case of an aleph added at the start of a
Hebrew word, as *ezroa* (Iyov 31:22) beside *zeroa*, arm.

### zikfa (זקפא)
One who enters a town and fears the evil eye should take each thumb, *zikfa*, in the
other hand and declare that he is of the seed of Yosef (Berachos 55b); Rashi
explains *zikfa* as *gudal*, and in the parallel in Pesachim 110a as *agudal*. The
root ז-ק-ף means standing, as the thumb stands out from the side of the hand.

### ravravta (רברבתא)
In Pesachim 109a a certain finger is named three ways: *etzba*, the generic finger,
in the printed Gemara; *gudal* in some manuscripts; *ravravta* in Rashi's version
(the word is also in Eruvin 10b), which Rashi explains as *gudal*. It seems, Rabbi
Klein suggests, to be a literal Aramaic translation of *gudal*: the Targumim render
*gadol* as *rabbah*, which shares its root with *ravravta*.

### ilyon (אליון)
The Targumim do not render *bohen* as *ravravta* or *zikfa*; they tend to render
it *ilyon* or *alyon*, *ilyona* or *alyona* (see Gittin 69a and Rashi there).

- Linguists relate it to *alyah*, the fat of an animal's tail, the thumb being
  fatter and having more cartilage than the other fingers.
- Midrash Tanchuma (Ki Savo 4) reads Hashem's promise to set Yisrael *elyon*,
  high above the nations (Devarim 26:19, 28:1), as making them like the *ilyon*,
  higher than the four fingers: acting properly, they will rule over the Four
  Kingdoms (Bavel, Persia and Madai, Yavan, Rome); if not, the Four Kingdoms
  will subjugate them.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "thumb" absorbs *bohen* and *gudal*; where Toras Kohanim reads *bohen* as
  the middle joint of the *gudal*, "thumb" for both collapses the point.
- In Midrash Tanchuma's play on *elyon* and *ilyon*, give both, or the pun is lost.
