# Bed: mitah / eres (arisah)

## Bottom line

*Mitah* is the ordinary **bed**, close to thirty appearances in Tanach, from a
root meaning to incline or stretch out, since a man inclines his body to lie
down. *Eres* is the rarer word, nine appearances, and every explanation of it
makes it the more particular one: a fitted or special bed, an iron-framed one, a
crib, a bed of woven or cross-fastened planks. The trap is that English "bed"
covers both and Targum renders both by *arsa*. Ramchal holds they are genuine
synonyms paired for poetic effect; most of the others do not, so where a passage
puts them side by side, differentiate.

## Distinctions

### mitah (מטה)
The common word. The root is disputed: Menachem ibn Saruk gives mem-tes; Radak
(*Sefer HaShorashim*) gives mem-tes-tes or נ-ט-ה; Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
(*Yerios Shlomo*, *Cheishek Shlomo*) gives tes-hei; Rabbi Aharon Marcus gives the
single letter tes, which he reads as movement to the side, gathering under it
*setiyah* (deviation), *natah* (to incline), *hitah* (turn aside), *tata*
(broom), *tach* (plastering), and *taah* (erred). Every one of these yields the
same sense: to incline or stretch out, which is what a man does with his body to
lie down. Malbim, in one place: *mitah* is any piece of furniture one lies or
reclines on, a couch included, while *eres* is specifically what one sleeps on.
Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: *mitah* is the wooden-framed bed. In Rabbinic
usage *mitah* can also mean a bier or coffin.

### eres (ערש / ערס)
Nine appearances in Tanach, the famous one being Og's iron *eres* at Devarim
3:11. Targum renders both Hebrew words by the cognate *arsa*, which suggests
*eres* is an Aramaic word taken into Hebrew; the Hebrew spells it with a sin and
the Aramaic with a samech, letters that regularly interchange. Readings:

- Rashbam (to Devarim 3:11): Og's *eres* was his crib. The point of its being
  iron is that Og as an infant was already so strong that an ordinary crib would
  have broken, and an infant, less aware of his own movements, needs the sturdier
  bed more than a grown man does. Modern Hebrew follows this, *eres* a cradle and
  *arisah* a crib.
- Rabbi Nosson of Rome (*Sefer HeAruch*): the root ע-ר-ש/ע-ר-ס denotes mixture,
  which is why *arisah*, dough (Bamidbar 15:21), the Biblical equivalent of the
  Mishnaic *isah*, shares it: dough is ingredients kneaded together. Rabbi Yaakov
  Tzvi Mecklenburg and Rav Hirsch add that *arisah* is sometimes conjugated as a
  verb (Berachos 37b), which shows the root to be an act of mixing or mutual
  joining; so an *eres* is a bed whose sides are fastened to each other by strips
  or bars running through, or built of woven or layered planks.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: the difference is material, not structure.
  *Eres* is the metal-framed bed, *mitah* the wooden one.
- Malbim: *mitah* is generic, *eres* is a custom-fitted bed, whether for the sick
  (Tehillim 41:4), for a child (Og's case), or for a pampered woman (Mishlei
  7:16). Talmudic Aramaic *arsa* can also mean a coffin (Kesubos 103a), which
  sits well with his reading, though *mitah* too is used that way.

### the mussar and kabbalistic readings
Rabbi Aviad Sar-Shalom Basilea (*Emunas Chachamim*): *mitah* is the word for the
bed of husband and wife, and so stands for the right balance of influencer and
receiver, the man giving and the woman receiving; it is therefore the word
evocative of *tzaddikim*, who keep that balance by knowing themselves to be
receivers before Hashem. *Eres* is the bed of the wicked, which is why it is
Og's word. Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Kaidanover (*Kav HaYashar*) likewise ties *eres* to
evil, playing it against its homophone *eres* with an aleph, venom, an allusion
to the snake in Gan Eden. On these readings *mitah* is the bed of the righteous
and *eres* the bed of the wicked.

The Torah's use of *arisah* for the dough of *challah* (Bamidbar 15:21) has been
read by some as a deliberate echo of *eres*: as one consecrates his dough and
sets it aside for the Kohen, so must a man consecrate his bed, either by giving
himself to Hashem's service the moment he wakes, before he rises, or by keeping
what is done in that bed holy and pure.

## Rendering guidance

- מטה: "bed," or "couch" where the source is Malbim's broader sense.
- ערש: "bed" in plain prose; "bedstead" where the frame is the point (Og's iron
  *eres*, Devarim 3:11); "crib" or "cradle" where Rashbam's reading is being
  followed.
- Trap: "bed" absorbs both, and so does Targum's *arsa*. If a source is
  darshening the choice of word (Og's *eres* against a *tzaddik*'s *mitah*), the
  translation has to keep the Hebrew visible or the derashah is lost.
- Where the two stand together in one verse, take Ramchal's option only if the
  context is plainly poetic parallelism; otherwise render *mitah* "bed" and
  *eres* "couch" or "bedstead" so the pair does not collapse.
- עריסה of *challah* is "dough," not "bed"; keep the connection for a note, not
  for the body text.
