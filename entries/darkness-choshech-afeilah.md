# Darkness: choshech / afeilah

## Bottom line

*Choshech* is plain **darkness**, the dark of an ordinary night. *Afeilah* is
**thick darkness**, and every authority who separates the two makes it the
heavier word (Radak). Where they stand together, as in *choshech afeilah* at
Makkas Choshech (Shemos 10:22), render the pair rather than repeat one word: "a
thick darkness". The trap is writing "darkness" twice and losing the fact that
the Torah escalated.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| חושך | choshech | darkness | the normal dark of night, moon and stars still shining (Malbim); spelled like chosech, "lacks" (Mecklenburg) |
| אפילה | afeilah | thick darkness | darker than choshech (Radak); no light at all in the sky (Malbim), or a covering that blocks light (Ibn Parchon, Edel) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Is darkness a thing or the absence of a thing?** The oldest question in the
cluster, and it decides how *bara choshech* in Yeshayahu 45:7 is heard.

1. **Absence of light.** Rav Saadia Gaon, Rambam, and others: darkness has no
   existence of its own; the word names a lack. Hashem is said to "create"
   darkness the way a man who blows out a candle is said to have darkened the
   room, or by creating the clouds that block the light. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi
   Mecklenburg finds a hint for Rambam in the spelling: *choshech* and
   *chosech*, "lacks", differ only in the dot over the shin.
2. **A created entity.** Chizkuni, Radak, the Yaavetz, the Vilna Gaon, and
   others: Yeshayahu 45:7 says Hashem formed light and *created* darkness, and
   what is created is something.
3. **Both, by context.** Ramban follows Rambam that *choshech* ordinarily means
   the lack of light, but holds that the darkness of the Ninth Plague was a
   real, palpable mist that not only blocked light but added darkness; the
   Midrash gives it the thickness of a *dinar*. His reason for splitting them
   is likely that this passage alone adds *afeilah*.

The Bottom line does not adjudicate the metaphysics, which does not change the
English. It matters for a *derashah*, and Ramban's position is the one that
explains why Makkas Choshech is described as it is.

**What makes afeilah heavier.** Malbim: a quantitative difference, *choshech*
is night with moon and stars, *afeilah* is when they too give no light. Rabbi
Shlomo Ibn Parchon: *afeilah* means "covered", a qualitative difference. Rabbi
Yehuda Leib Edel follows that line: *choshech* is the mere lack of light,
*afeilah* is a tangible thing standing between the light and the eye, which for
him were dark clouds that filtered the daylight so that it reached Yisrael and
not the Mitzrim. Rabbi Baruch HaLevi Epstein, apparently on Ibn Parchon's
reading, takes the covering literally: cataracts grew over the Mitzrim's eyes.
Malbim's account is the safer default for translation; the covering readings
are what to cite when a passage treats the darkness as an object.

## Distinctions

### choshech (חושך)
The unmarked word. Rashi (to Shemos 10:22): the "materializing" of the darkness
means that this plague was not merely a prolonged night but darker than night.
That comment already assumes that ordinary *choshech* is the night dark, which
is what Malbim then spells out.

### afeilah (אפילה)
Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): *afeilah* is darker than *choshech*. Rabbi Shlomo
Aharon Wertheimer hears the same weight in the Haggadah's *me'afeilah l'or
gadol*: the opposite set against *afeilah* is not light but a *great* light,
since plain light is the opposite of *choshech*. That is a useful test for the
word's force in any pasuk.

## Rendering guidance

- Shemos 10:21 to 23: *choshech* alone is "darkness"; *choshech afeilah* is "a
  thick darkness" or "a darkness of gloom", never "darkness, darkness".
- Trap: English "darkness" absorbs both. Where a passage pairs them or builds
  on the difference, reserve "darkness" for *choshech* and give *afeilah* an
  adjective: thick, utter, palpable.
- *Me'afeilah l'or gadol* in the Haggadah: "from thick darkness to a great
  light." Keeping "great" is not decoration; Wertheimer's point rests on it.
- Yeshayahu 45:7, *yotzer ohr u'vorei choshech*: "Who forms light and creates
  darkness." Do not soften "creates" to "brings about"; the machlokes above
  lives in that verb.
