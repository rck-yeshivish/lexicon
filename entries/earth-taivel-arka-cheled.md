# Earth: taivel / arka / cheled (charavah, yabashah)

## Bottom line

Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 1, ch. 37) counts seven words for the earth:
*eretz*, *adamah*, *arka*, *charavah*, *yabashah*, *taivel*, *cheled*. Of these,
*taivel* is **the inhabited world**, the settled parts as against *eretz* for
the whole (Malbim); *arka* is **earth**, a hapax in the one Aramaic pasuk of Yirmiyahu;
*cheled* is **the transient world**, the earth under time; *charavah* and
*yabashah* are **dry land**. *Taivel* is not *tevel*, abomination: same
letters, different nikud, one root. And *karka* is not on this list at all.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What *taivel* is against *eretz*.**

- Where the two stand together (Tehillim 90:2, 24:1), *Metzudas Tzion*: *eretz*
  is the uninhabited parts and *taivel* the inhabited.
- Malbim similarly: *eretz* is the globe entire, *taivel* only what is settled.
- Rashi (to Tehillim 24:1): *eretz* is Eretz Yisrael and *taivel* the rest of
  the planet.

**Why *taivel* is so called.** Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 1) gives two answers,
both from the root BET-LAMED as Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reads it, a thing losing
its own standing by being mixed into something else or by wearing away.

- First, the world is *metubal*, spiced, with everything, the root of Mishnaic
  *tavlin*; version 2 has it spiced with Torah, so the world earns the name
  because Torah is mixed into its running.
- Second, its way is that things enter it and do not come out, which Rabbi Klein
  explains of people buried in the ground who do not emerge from the grave, at
  least until techiyas hameisim reverses it, so that the ground is where bodies
  decay.

**Whether *arka* is Hebrew or Aramaic.**

- Rabbi Shmuel Yaffe-Ashkenazi: the word is Hebrew even though the pasuk around
  it is Aramaic, since the Aramaic of *eretz* is *ara*, TZADI becoming AYIN as
  it often does, and that form appears in the same verse. How Hebrew *eretz*
  would yield *arka* is unclear; Rabbi Klein offers only that perhaps the TZADI
  became a KUF because the two letters adjoin in the aleph-beis.
- *Midrash Mishlei* ch. 8 instead reads *arka* as the land that fled from before
  Hashem when He came to give the Torah at Sinai. Rabbi Klein says plainly that
  he is not sure what this means, and adds that the basis for the derashah seems
  to be the resemblance between Hebrew *arka* with an ALEPH and the Aramaic
  *arka* spelled with an AYIN, which means "to flee."

## Distinctions

### taivel (תבל) and tevel (תבל)
Same letters, different vocalization, one root. Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon (Mahari
Tatz): *tevel*, abomination, is at core "mixing," either because one who does
such things is usually himself mixed up or because he mixes up the order of nature.
*Taivel*, the populated world, is a mixture the other way, many kinds of people,
plants and animals in one space.

### arka (ארקא)
Once in Tanach, at Yirmiyahu 10:11, the only pasuk in that sefer written in
Aramaic: Yirmiyahu's letter to Yehoyachin and the exiles taken to Bavel, telling
them what to answer Babylonian pressure to serve idols.

### cheled (חלד) and chedel (חדל)
*Cheled* (Tehillim 49:2) and *chedel* (Yeshayahu 38:11) seem to be metathesized
forms of one word (see Ibn Ezra to Tehillim 39:6).
- Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) makes both roots synonyms of *zman*, time,
  presumably because the word bears on the ephemeral, temporary nature of This
  World and those who live in it.
- Yerushalmi Shabbos 14:1: "dwellers of *cheled*" likens people to the weasel,
  *chuldah*, which drags food from place to place and leaves it without knowing
  whom it will feed, as a man leaves things behind without knowing who will
  enjoy them (compare Taanis 23a).
- Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche: CHES-LAMED, beginning, with LAMED-DALET, birth,
  since a man knows the day of his birth and not of his death.
- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 11:8, Tehillim 17:14) ties in *chadal*, to cease, and
  *chaludah*, rust: metal wears out, and so does a man.

### charavah (חרבה) and yabashah (יבשה)
Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2): both mean dry land, the earth being so called for
absorbing the Mabul's waters (*Midrash Mishlei*: for absorbing Hevel's blood).

### The list itself, and karka outside it
Version 2 of Avos d'Rabbi Nasan adds three further words for the land, *reishis*,
*gai* and *sadeh*, which the standard edition found too farfetched; and *Vayikra
Rabbah* 29:11 gives a different list of seven altogether. Rabbi Avraham Bedersi
sets *karka* outside the whole discussion: in Biblical Hebrew it does not mean
"land" but the floor underneath a building or a sea, though he admits that in
Rabbinic Hebrew it came to be almost synonymous with *eretz* and *adamah*.

### The four and the seasons
Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel (Bereishis Rabbah 13:12) ties four of the names to
the seasons: *eretz* to spring, when the land brings forth (*ratz*) its produce;
*taivel* to summer, when the produce rots; *adamah* to autumn, when the ground
breaks into clods; *arka* to winter, when the land is empty (*reik*).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "earth" and "world" take *eretz*, *taivel*, and *cheled* alike.
  Give *taivel* "the inhabited world" and *cheled* "the fleeting world" wherever
  a pasuk pairs one of them with *eretz*.
- Tehillim 24:1: "To Hashem belongs the land and its fullness, the inhabited
  world and those who dwell in it." Both halves as "the earth" flattens it.
- *Tevel* as abomination (Vayikra 18:23) is never "world"; check the nikud.
- *Karka* in a Gemara is "ground," but in a pasuk it is the floor of a building
  or of the sea. Bedersi's caution is worth keeping.
