# Earth: taivel / arka / cheled (charavah, yabashah)

## Bottom line

Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 1, ch. 37) counts seven words for the earth:
*eretz*, *adamah*, *arka*, *charavah*, *yabashah*, *taivel*, *cheled*. Of these,
*taivel* is **the inhabited world**, the settled parts as against *eretz* for
the whole; *arka* is **earth**, a hapax in the one Aramaic pasuk of Yirmiyahu;
*cheled* is **the transient world**, the earth under time; *charavah* and
*yabashah* are **dry land**. Never confuse *taivel* with *tevel*, abomination:
same letters, different nikud, one root. And *karka* is not on this list at all.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What *taivel* is against *eretz*.** Where the two stand together (Tehillim
90:2, 24:1), *Metzudas Tziyon*: *eretz* is the uninhabited parts and *taivel*
the inhabited. Malbim: *eretz* is the globe entire, *taivel* only what is
settled. Rashi (to Tehillim 24:1): *eretz* is Eretz Yisrael and *taivel* the
rest of the planet. The Bottom line follows Malbim and *Metzudas Tziyon*,
Rashi's reading being local to his pesukim.

**Why *taivel* is so called.** Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 1) gives two answers,
both from the root BET-LAMED as Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reads it, a thing losing
its own standing by being mixed into something else or by wearing away. First,
the world is *metubal*, spiced, with everything, the root of Mishnaic *tavlin*;
version 2 has it spiced with Torah, so the world earns the name because Torah is
mixed into its running. Second, its way is that things enter it and do not come
out, the ground where bodies decay, until techiyas hameisim reverses it.

**Whether *arka* is Hebrew or Aramaic.** Rabbi Shmuel Yaffe-Ashkenazi: the word
is Hebrew even though the pasuk around it is Aramaic, since the Aramaic of
*eretz* is *ara*, TZADI becoming AYIN as it often does, and that form appears in
the same verse. How Hebrew *eretz* would yield *arka* is unclear; the article
offers only that TZADI and KUF adjoin in the alef-beis. *Midrash Mishlei* ch. 8
instead reads *arka* as the land that fled from before Hashem at Matan Torah, on
the strength of the Aramaic *arak*, to flee, spelled with an AYIN.

## Distinctions

### taivel (תבל) and tevel (תבל)
Same letters, different vocalization, one root. Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon (Mahari
Tatz): *tevel*, abomination, is at core "mixing," either because one who does
such things is himself mixed up or because he mixes up the order of nature.
*Taivel*, the populated world, is a mixture the other way, many kinds of people,
plants and animals in one space.

### arka (ארקא)
Once in Tanach, at Yirmiyahu 10:11, the only pasuk in that sefer written in
Aramaic: Yirmiyahu's letter to Yehoyachin and the exiles taken to Bavel, telling
them what to answer Babylonian pressure to serve idols.

### cheled (חלד) and chedel (חדל)
Ibn Ezra (to Tehillim 39:6) treats *cheled* (Tehillim 49:2) and *chedel*
(Yeshayahu 38:11) as metathesized forms of one word, and Radak makes both roots
synonyms of *zman*, time. Yerushalmi Shabbos 14:1: "dwellers of *cheled*" likens
people to the weasel, *chuldah*, which drags food from place to place and leaves
it without knowing whom it will feed, as a man leaves things behind without
knowing who will enjoy them (compare Taanis 23a). Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche:
CHET-LAMED, beginning, with LAMED-DALET, birth, since a man knows the day of his
birth and not of his death. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 11:8, Tehillim 17:14) ties
in *chadal*, to cease, and *chaludah*, rust: metal wears out, and so does a man.

### charavah (חרבה) and yabashah (יבשה)
Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2): both mean dry land, the earth being so called for
absorbing the Mabul's waters (*Midrash Mishlei*: for absorbing Hevel's blood).

### The four and the seasons
Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel (Bereishis Rabbah 13:12) ties the first four names to
the seasons: *eretz* to spring, when the land brings forth (*ratz*) its produce;
*taivel* to summer, when the produce rots; *adamah* to autumn, when the ground
breaks into clods; *arka* to winter, when the land is empty (*reik*).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "earth" and "world" take *eretz*, *taivel*, and *cheled* alike.
  Give *taivel* "the inhabited world" and *cheled* "the fleeting world" wherever
  a pasuk pairs one of them with *eretz*.
- Tehillim 24:1: "To Hashem belongs the land and its fullness, the inhabited
  world and those who dwell in it." Both halves as "the earth" flattens it.
- *Tevel* as abomination (Vayikra 18:23) is never "world"; check the nikud.
- *Karka* in a Gemara is "ground," but in a pasuk it is the floor of a building
  or of the sea. Bedersi's caution is worth keeping.
