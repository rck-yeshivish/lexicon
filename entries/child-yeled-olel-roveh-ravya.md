# Child: yeled / olel / roveh / elem (and Aramaic ravya, talya)

## Bottom line

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim calls *yeled* the general word for a **child**, and
writes that it does not necessarily denote any specific age. *Olel* is the
**toddler** who has been weaned and now gets himself up to look for food.
*Roveh* is a **youngster**, the stage *Midrash Tadshe* places between *naar* and
*elem*. *Elem* is the **young man** who has nearly reached his full height. In
Aramaic, *ravya* and *talya* both mean **boy** or **lad**, and the Targum is not
consistent about which Hebrew word each renders. For *naar* and *almah*
themselves, see [youth](youth-naar-almah-elem.html).

| טליא | talya | lad (Aramaic) | Targum for yeled and naar; from tleh, a kid goat |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether naar and elem are one stage or two.**

- Ibn Janach and Radak (each in his *Sefer HaShorashim*): *naarah* and *almah*
  mean the same thing.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's two roots also seem, on the surface, to land both
  words at the same point in life.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg, summarizing Pappenheim, grants that the stage
  is the same but splits the focus: *elem* names the showing of physical
  maturity, *naar* the coming of intellectual and spiritual maturity.

**The order of the stages.**

- Pappenheim's account runs *olel*, growing through childhood, then *elem*.
- *Midrash Tadshe* (ch. 6) instead sets *elem* after *naar* and inserts *roveh*
  between them, listing the stages of life as *yeled*, *naar*, *roveh*, *elem*,
  *ish*, *sav*, *zaken*. In the Midrash's list *yeled* is the earliest stage.

**How old a yeled is.**
- Pappenheim: not necessarily any specific age, and Yishmael is called a *yeled*
  at sixteen (Bereishis 21:15 to 16).
- Rashi (to Erachin 18b) on the Mishnah's *yeled* (*Erachin* 4:4): anyone from
  twenty to sixty, as against *zaken*, over sixty.
- Rabbi Eliyahu HaKohen of Izmir cites anonymous "sages of astrology" who put
  *yeled* at two to five, *naar* at five to eight, and *bachur* at eight to
  eighteen.

**The Aramaic correspondences.** They do not line up. Onkelos usually renders
*yeled* as *ravya* (Bereishis 21:8, 21:16; Shemos 2:6; see also Sukkah 5b), the
feminine being *rivah/riviysa* (see Rashi to Sanhedrin 58b), yet at the single
appearance of *yaldah* in Chumash (Bereishis 34:4) he writes *ulemta*, the
Aramaic of *almah*, rather than *rivah*. *Ulema* and *ulemta* are his normal words for *naar* and
*naarah*, but he renders *naar* as *ravya* at Bereishis 37:2, Bamidbar 30:17, and
Rus 2:5 to 6; and Targum renders *bachur* with cognates of *revi/ravya* at Rus
3:10 and Eichah 5:13, but with *ulema* at Koheles 11:9. Rashi
translates *rivah/riviysa* as *naarah* (Shabbos 127b, Kesubos 62b, Sanhedrin
109b) but at Sotah 26a
defines *roveh* as *bachur* and as *yeled*. Rabbi Yaakov Kamenetsky already
noted some of these inconsistencies and offered a resolution of his own.

## Distinctions

### yeled (ילד)
Pappenheim traces it to the biliteral ל-ד, birth: hence *yalad* and *yaldah*
for giving birth, *toldos* for what birth produces, *valad* for the womb where
birth begins, and *yeled* for any child born. Since the word says only "born",
it fixes no age. Rabbi Meir Simcha of Dvinsk: Reuven deliberately called the
seventeen-year-old Yosef a *yeled* (Bereishis 42:22) to press that he had not
yet reached twenty and was therefore not yet liable to punishment in the
Heavenly Court.

### olel (עולל) and elem (עלם)

- Both from Pappenheim's biliteral ע-ל, "on top", the root behind *al*, *elyon*,
  *l'maalah*, *oleh*, *aleh* (a leaf, which grows on the branch), *ol* (a yoke,
  placed on the animal), *me'il* (a tunic worn over other clothes), and *na'al*
  (a shoe worn on the foot). An *olel* is the child already weaned from his
  mother's milk who now gets himself up to find or ask for food; he grows
  through childhood until he is an *elem*, grown up to nearly his full size.
- According to many commentators, *elem* and *almah* are related instead to
  *eilum* and *ne'elam*, hidden; Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 13:15) explains that
  an *elem* is a young *naar* who has not yet matured, so that his potential
  remains hidden and unrealized.

### naar (נער), for the contrast
Pappenheim's root here is ע-ר, revealing: *va'te'ar* for emptying a jug and so
revealing its bottom (Bereishis 24:20), *ohr* (skin), *ervah* (nakedness), *taar*
(a razor, which reveals the skin), *ar* (an enemy who shows his enmity openly),
and *eir* (awake, since a sleeper's powers are hidden and waking reveals them). A
*naar* is the adolescent whose powers suddenly show, as though he had just woken
from the sleep of childhood; Pappenheim adds that the word can carry the youthful
irresponsibility of one who shakes off (*l'na'er*) his obligations.

### ravya (רביא) and talya (טליא)
Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein, having found no source that explicitly treats their
origins, offers what he calls the straightforward etymologies: *ravya*, *reva*
and their cognates are clearly derived from the two-letter ר-ב, to grow in
Aramaic (see Targum to
Bereishis 25:27), so a boy or girl is one still growing; *talya* and *talyasa*
seem to him related to *tleh*, a young goat, the same doubling that gives
English "kid" both senses, ostensibly because both run around wildly. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 30:32)
writes that *tleh* itself refers to a young human as well as a young lamb, just as English "kid" means both a young goat and a young child. Targum
in some places uses *tali/talya* for *yeled* and for *naar* (Megillah 5b; *Dikdukei Sofrim* to
Yevamos 114a), and Rashi (to Megillah 5b) likewise defines *tali* as *naar*. On
the name Avtalyon, the Rashbatz and Bartenura (to *Avos* 1:10) write that it
alludes to that Sage's role as head of the Sanhedrin: the Gemara (Gittin 36a)
has the court functioning as father of orphans, and the name can be read as *av*
(father of) plus *talyon* (children).

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "child" and "boy" will absorb *yeled*, *olel*, *naar*, and *elem*
  together. Reserve "child" for *yeled*, "toddler" for *olel*, "youth" or "lad"
  for *naar*, and "young man" for *elem*.
- Where a source lists the stages in order (*Midrash Tadshe* ch. 6), keep them
  distinct and ascending: child, youth, youngster, young man, man, elder, old
  man. A repeated "young man" destroys the list.
- Do not import a halachic age into a translated pasuk: *yeled* says "born", not
  "under twenty". Where a drashah wants the age (Rabbi Meir Simcha on Bereishis
  42:22), put it in a note. With Aramaic, translate the sense the Hebrew carries,
  not the mapping; Onkelos' *ulemta* at Bereishis 34:4 renders *yaldah* and should
  still read "girl".
