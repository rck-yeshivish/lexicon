# Underworld: Gehinnom / sheol / shachat / avadon

## Bottom line

*Gehinnom* is a place name pressed into service for the afterlife and should
stay transliterated, never "hell" and never "purgatory," both of which carry
another religion's doctrine. *Sheol* is literally **the grave** (Ibn Ezra), and
only by derashah the soul's place of judgment (Rashi). *Shachat* is a **pit**,
with "destruction" audible in it; *avadon* is **loss** or ruin. Eruvin 19a
lists seven Biblical names for Gehinnom, and the list is a list of *places
within* it (Maharsha), not seven synonyms, so a translation that flattens them
into "hell" seven times destroys the sugya.

## Distinctions

### The seven of Eruvin 19a
Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's list: *sheol*, *avadon*, *be'er shachat*, *bor
shaon*, *tit hayaven*, *tzalmaves*, and *eretz hatachtis*. Maharsha: the seven
names answer to seven distinct places within Gehinnom (see Sotah 10b). *Midrash
Konen* (in *Otzar Midrashim* p. 256) assigns them: Korach and his company in
*sheol*; the lost souls of the wicked in *avadon*; robbers, thieves, and those
who hold back a worker's wages in *be'er shachat*; those who transgressed in
matters of intimacy in *tit hayaven*; slanderers in *tzalmaves*; those who
quarrel with talmidei chachamim in *eretz tachtis*.

### sheol (שאול)
Close to seventy occurrences. Ibn Ezra (to Bereishis 37:35) holds the literal
sense is grave, and faults the Vulgate for rendering it *infernus* there. Rashi
(there): the plain sense is grave, but by derashah it is where the soul is
judged. Malbim: a deep pit from which there is no climbing out, which suits
both the grave and Gehinnom. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: root ש-ל, something cast
away or negated, and death is a plane of existence away from the living. Rabbi
Tzvi Matisyahu Abrahams (*Root Connections in the Torah* p. 274) reads it from
*she'eilah*, the question hanging over a man as he is lowered in.

### avadon (אבדון)
Tehillim 88:12. Either the body's ruin and rotting after death, or that souls
are lost (*avad*) there for a time.

### shachat (שחת), be'er shachat (באר שחת)
Tehillim 16:10, 55:24. In many pesukim *shachat* means simply a pit. Rabbi
Avraham Bedersi HaPenini: a pit dug to trap wild animals, and the wicked
likewise set traps for the righteous; he also hears *hashchasah*, destruction,
since the body decays in the grave. Rabbi Pappenheim: root ש-ח, bending, since
one stuck in so cramped a place must bend; from the same root *hishtachavah*
(bowing, bending one's posture) and *mashach* (anointing, since oil softens a
hard thing and makes it pliable).

### bor shaon (בור שאון)
Tehillim 40:3, "the pit of tumult." Ibn Janach and others hold *shaon* (uproar)
and *sha'anan* (quiet) are one root, a case of a Hebrew root carrying a sense
and its opposite; on that reading *bor shaon* is the Pit of Silence, where one
can no longer complain or speak at all. Rashi (to Yeshayahu 9:4) and Radak
(*Sefer HaShorashim*) put *shaon* with *shoah*, devastation, which lines it up
with *avadon* and *shachat*.

### tit hayaven (טיט היון)
Tehillim 40:3, the clinging mire. Gehinnom holds a man as quicksand does, and
the dead lie in the grave without moving. Rabbi Pappenheim: *hayaven* from י-ן,
trickery, the root of *onaah* (profiting by cheating another) and of *yayin*
(wine, which deceives by tasting good and then taking a man's mind); quicksand
deceives likewise, looking like ground a man can walk on. Rav Hirsch (to
Bereishis 9:20) and Rabbi Aharon Marcus (*Keses HaSofer* to Bereishis 10:2) say
much the same.

### tzalmaves (צלמות)
Tehillim 107:10, Iyov 10:21. "Shadow of death"; the connection needs no
argument.

### eretz hatachtis (ארץ תחתית) or eretz chittis (ארץ חתית)
The Gemara finds no pasuk for *eretz hatachtis* and falls back on a received
tradition. *Sefer Russiana* and Rabbi Yitzchak Abuhab's *Menoras HaMaor* have
*eretz chittis*, the land of the Hittites, and Tosafos prefer that girsa: since
*eretz tachtis* does occur in Tanach (Yechezkel 31 in several places; see also
Devarim 32:22), the Gemara would not have needed a tradition, whereas *eretz
chittis* occurs nowhere, and the sugya then runs properly.

### Variant lists
*Midrash Hallel* (*Otzar Midrashim* p. 134) drops *bor shaon* and *eretz
hatachtis* and adds *gehinnom* and *tziyah* (waste). *Midrash Din HaKever* (p.
94) has *be'er shaon* for *bor shaon*, *bor hatachtis* for *eretz hatachtis*,
and *chatzar maves* for *tzalmaves*. The Targumic Tosefta (start of Yechezkel)
has *dumah* for *bor shaon* (in kabbalistic sources *Dumah* is the angel set
over Gehinnom), *arka* for *eretz hatachtis* (Aramaic for earth, see Yirmiyahu
10:11), and *gehinnom* for *tzalmaves*.

### gehinnom (גיהנום) and tophet (תפת)
Neither names the netherworld in Tanach. *Gei ben Hinnom* and *Tophes* are
places south of Yerushalayim where the Baal was served, child sacrifice
included (Yirmiyahu 19). Both names were then borrowed for the sinner's
afterlife. The Gemara asks why Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi left them out and
answers that they are not names of the place or of quarters within it but
allusions to what lands a man there: *gehinnom*, the valley (*gei*) into which
those who occupy themselves with what is *chinam*, pointless, descend (Rashi:
sexual impropriety); *tophes*, the place into which fall those whom the yetzer
hara talks over (*mefateh*).

### alukah (עלוקה)
Tosafos cite sources reading *alukah*, the leech of Mishlei 30:15, as another
name for Gehinnom, though they also weigh its being a name for Shlomo HaMelech.
Maharal: as a leech draws out blood, Gehinnom draws out the soul. Rabbi Chaim
of Volozhin (*Nefesh HaChaim* 1:12): a leech draws out bad blood and then dies,
and so Gehinnom cleanses a man of his sins until what the sin made is gone.

## Rendering guidance

- *Gehinnom* stays transliterated. "Hell" imports eternal damnation,
  "purgatory" imports a Catholic doctrine, and Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin's leech
  is precisely the point neither word carries.
- *Sheol*: "the grave" as the default, on Ibn Ezra. Only where a source is
  reading it as the place of the soul should the English shift, and then say
  so.
- Trap: English "pit" will absorb *shachat*, *bor shaon*, and *tit hayaven* at
  once. In Eruvin 19a and in the *Midrash Konen* list, keep all seven distinct,
  transliterated with a gloss: "*be'er shachat*, the well of the pit," and so
  on. The moment two of them read alike the count of seven stops meaning
  anything.
- *Tzalmaves*: "the shadow of death," not "deep darkness."
- *Tophes* stays a proper name in Yirmiyahu and transliterated in the Gemara's
  derashah, since the derashah works off the sound of the word.
