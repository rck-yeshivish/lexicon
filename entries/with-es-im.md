# With: es (itto) / im (imo)

## Bottom line

*Es*, where it means "with," and *im* both mean **with**, and Tanach uses now
one and now the other. On both views the difference is whether the two parties
stand as equals. The Vilna Gaon and
Malbim: *im* is "with" in the full sense, two equal and cooperative parties,
while *es* does not connote equality: one party is dominant and active, the
other passive. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim and Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg take
the opposite approach: *im* is circumstantial, they merely happened to be
together, while *es* is the stronger, more deliberate and more equal "with." The
two schools read the same pesukim in opposite ways (Lot's *itto* and *imo*),
though on Bilam Rabbi Mecklenburg's reading mirrors the Gra's.

## Distinctions

### es (את) as a preposition
*Es* is by far the commonest word in Tanach, roughly twelve thousand
appearances, and usually cannot be translated at all: it marks the object of a
verb, working more like punctuation than a word with meaning, and Rabbi Nachman
Marcuson likens it to an arrow pointing the reader to what the verb acts on. As
Ibn Janach and Radak explain, it sometimes does carry meaning: "to" (Vayikra
13:49; Bamidbar 13:17), "from" (Bereishis 44:4; Bamidbar 35:26), "next to"
(Bereishis 40:4, 44:24), "instead of" (Vayikra 22:17; Shmuel II 12:6), "on top
of" (Melachim I 9:25), and "with." Suffixed it gives *itto*, *itti*, *ittah*.
Rashi (to Bereishis 37:18) simply glosses *itto* as *imo*.

### im (עם)
Ayin-mem, "with," suffixed as *imo*, *imi*, *imah*.

### the Vilna Gaon and Malbim: im is the stronger
The Gra (Rabbi Eliyahu Kramer of Vilna, 1720 to 1797) reads the Bilam
narrative on this. Hashem first forbade him to go *imahem* (Bamidbar 22:12),
then told him to rise and go *itam* (22:20), and the Torah reports that he went
*im* Balak's men, whereupon Hashem was angered (22:21-22). The Gra asks why
Hashem seemingly changed His mind, and, having allowed Bilam to go, why He was
angry. His answer: *im* is "with" in the fullest sense, the joining of two
completely equal and cooperative parties; *es* also means "with" but carries no
such equality and congruence. So Bilam was barred from joining Balak's effort
to curse the people (*im*), permitted only to go physically with the men
(*es*), and when he went *im*, with them in the fullest sense, he violated the
directive. The Gra and Malbim both derive this from *es*'s ordinary grammatical
use of marking an object. Rabbi Klein illustrates with *achalta es halechem*,
where the eater and the bread are not equal; likewise *es* in the sense of
"with" marks an unequal pair, one dominant and active and the other passive.
Malbim applies it to Lot: he came to Eretz Yisrael *itto* with Avraham
(Bereishis 12:4), deferentially following his lead, and at the parting
he is *imo* (13:14), having asserted his independence and tried to show that he
was an equal.

### Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim and Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: es is the stronger
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim of Breslau (1740 to 1814), in *Cheshek Shlomo*, and
Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785 to 1865), in *HaKsav VeHaKabbalah*, take
the opposite approach: *es* denotes a stronger and more equal "with" than *im*.
In Rabbi Pappenheim's words, *im* is "with" circumstantially, two who happen to
be together, and *es* is "with" deliberately and absolutely. Rabbi Mecklenburg
applies it in several places. The same Lot verses: the switch from *itto* to
*imo* is, he explains, the basis for the *Zohar*'s contention (Bereishis 78b)
that Lot first attached himself to Avraham to learn from him and become his
equal, but never reached that goal. Eliezer: Besuel washed the feet of the men
who were *itto* with Eliezer (Bereishis 24:32), and at the banquet Eliezer and
the men *imo* ate and drank (24:54); Rabbi Mecklenburg postulates that the
switch reflects the family's attitude, which at first took Eliezer and his men
for equals, until Eliezer took charge, explained his mission, and emerged as the
leader. Yaakov's burial: Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his house and the
elders of Mitzrayim went up *itto* with Yosef (Bereishis 50:7), as equals or
near-equals, while the chariots and horsemen, far lower in rank, went up *imo*
(50:9). Curiously, in his commentary on Bilam Rabbi Mecklenburg's approach
mirrors the Gra's rather than his own general rule.

## Rendering guidance

- Ordinary prose, either word: "with." No English preposition marks the
  distinction, and forcing one ("together with" against "alongside") reads
  strained if the source is not making the point.
- Where a source is darshening the switch, supply the sense in a bracket or a
  note rather than in the preposition: on the Gra's reading of Bilam, "with
  them [*imahem*, as full partners]" against "with them [*itam*, merely
  accompanying]."
- Trap: "with" absorbs both words completely, so a derashah built on the change
  from *itto* to *imo* simply vanishes in translation unless the Hebrew is kept
  visible. Where the pair turns up in one passage (Bereishis 12:4 and 13:14;
  24:32 and 24:54; 50:7 and 50:9), quote both Hebrew forms.
- Second trap: most appearances of את are the object marker and carry no meaning
  at all. Before reading "with" into one, check that the word is not simply
  pointing at the object.
- Where the source reads the two as opposites, say which school it belongs to,
  since the Gra and Malbim put the equality in *im* and Rabbi Pappenheim and
  Rabbi Mecklenburg put it in *es*.
