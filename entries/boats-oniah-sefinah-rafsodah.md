# Boats: oniah / sefinah / rafsodah (and the smaller craft)

## Bottom line

*Oniah* is the general Biblical word, **ship**; *sefinah* is the ordinary
Mishnaic word for the same thing, **ship** or **boat**, and appears only once in
Tanach. *Rafsodah* and *dovrah* are both a **raft** of tied beams; *avarah* a
**ferry**; *dugit* a small **fishing skiff**; *tzi* a **large ship**, usually a
warship. In a pasuk *sefinah* is a rarity, in a Mishnah the default word.

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

## Where the authorities disagree

**What *oniyos eiveh* means (Iyov 9:26).** Rashi: *eivah* is the name of a strong
river, so these are boats for heavy currents. Ibn Ezra: a place name, so boats
sturdy enough to reach Eiveh, or else a commodity they carry. Ralbag: especially
fast boats. Nothing settles it; render by what the argument needs, footnote the
rest.

**Where *sefinah* comes from.** Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim gives four derivations
pulling in different directions: from *saf*, a vessel it resembles in shape
(Shemos 12:22); from *safah*, "edge," since it berths along the shore; from its
being less seaworthy than an *oniah*, so it stays near shore; or from
*tzafun*/*safun*, "hidden," since goods are packed away in it (Devarim 33:19).
Only the third has a translation consequence, and it is a suggestion, not a
ruling; the Bottom line keeps *sefinah* and *oniah* equivalent.

**Whether a *dugit* is sold with the ship it is tied to.** Sumchos (*Tosefta*
Bava Basra 4:1): yes, since the mother ship tows it. Yerushalmi Bava Basra 5:1
and *Tosefta* Succah 3:2 imply not, calling the small boat *iskupah*.

## Distinctions

### oniah (אניה)
First in Yaakov's berachah to Zevulun, who will settle the coast and live from
sea trade (Bereishis 49:13). The general term, taking modifiers for type: *oni
Tarshish*, long-haul ships (Melachim I 10:22; Yeshayahu 2:16); *oniyos socher*,
merchant ships (Mishlei 31:14); *oni shayit*, either rowboats, since *shotim* are
paddle-rods (Radak), or boats that "swim" on the surface (Rashi). Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim traces it to the biliteral א-נ, "where," since a ship goes where the
water pushes it, so a watcher asks where it is bound; Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis
16:8) says only that the *oniah* brings a person to a "where."

### sefinah (ספינה)
Once in Tanach, of Yonah's ship (Yonah 1:5), and thereafter the ordinary word,
across the Mishnah (Berachos 4:1, Shabbos 9:2, Bava Basra 5:1, Keilim 15:1,
Ohalos 8:1, and many more). It takes modifiers as *oniah* does: *sefinah
Alexandris*, the deep-sea ships out of Alexandria (Rambam to Keilim 15:1);
*sefinas haYarden*, the ferry across the Yarden (Shabbos 83b); *sefinos shel
piraton*, pirate ships (Shemos Rabbah 17:5). Why Yonah's vessel is called both
words in one perek is an open question.

### rafsodah (רפסודה) and dovrah (דוברה)
Rashi and Radak: both are seagoing rafts, beams lashed together (*dovros*,
Melachim I 5:23; *rafsodos*, Divrei HaYamim II 3:15); in later Hebrew *rafsodah*
is a footstool (*Midrash Tanchuma*, Bereishis 5).

### the smaller and Aramaic craft
*Avarah*, the ferry (Shmuel II 19:19), from *l'avor*, appears in the Gemara as
*ma'abara* or *mavra* (Shabbos 32a, Bava Kama 116a). *Siros dugah* are fishing
boats (Rashi and Radak to Amos 4:2), the Mishnaic equivalent being *dugit* (Bava
Basra 73a). *Tzi* and *tzim* are the large ships (Rashi to Bamidbar 24:24,
Yeshayahu 23:13), which Malbim takes as warships. In Aramaic the Targumim render
*oniah* sometimes *ilfa* (Yonah 1:3) and sometimes *sefinta* (Bereishis 49:13),
and Rashi glosses *ilfa* by both words (Avodah Zarah 10b; Eruvin 53b). *Arva*, a small boat, is
spelled with an *ayin* or an *aleph* (*Eitz Chaim*, following Rashi to Shabbos
103b); *borni* and *zuzi* (Rashi, Shabbos 19b, citing the Geonim) are further
Aramaic boat words.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: אניה "ship," ספינה "ship" in a Mishnah and "boat" where a smaller
  vessel is meant. Do not manufacture a size difference the sources do not fix.
- Trap: English "boat" covers *oniah*, *sefinah*, *dugit*, and *arva* at once.
  Where a sugya turns on two together, as in whether the *dugit* goes with the
  sale of the *sefinah*, keep "ship" for the large and "skiff" for the small.
- צי is "warship" only where Malbim is being followed; otherwise "great ship."
- Aramaic in a Gemara: אילפא is "ship," ערבא "small boat"; do not import the
  Hebrew distinctions into them.
