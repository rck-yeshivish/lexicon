# Love: ahavah / chibah / rechim / agav

## Bottom line

*Ahavah* is the general word and the widest: **love**; in its true sense the love
that gives itself to the beloved (Rav Hirsch, Rabbi Eliyahu Lopian) and embraces the
whole person, faults included (Rabbi Yosef Zechariah Stern). *Chibah* is love held
close and inward, love of what is dear in someone: **fondness, affection**; *chaviv*,
**dear**. *Rechim* is the Targum's Aramaic for *ahavah* (in Hebrew the root is
mercy, save Tehillim 18:2): **love**. *Agav* is the counterfeit: **lust**, never
love. The one thing not to get wrong: *chibah* is not a synonym to flatten into
"love"; beside *ahavah* it is the narrower, inner, selective word.

## Distinctions

### ahavah (אהבה)
Root א-ה-ב for the classical lexicographers (Menachem, Chayyuj, Ibn Janach, Ibn
Parchon, Radak). Rav Hirsch (Bereishis 22:2, 37:4; Vayikra 19:18; Devarim 6:5)
reads the core as ה-ב, *hav*, to give: *ahavah* is giving oneself over to another
and striving to bring him as close as can be. Rabbi Eliyahu Lopian (*Lev Eliyahu*
vol. 1 p. 110) teaches the same from the mussar side: most people take love for the
pleasant feeling one has in the beloved's company, which is self-love through
another ("I love fish"); true love is what rouses a person to give to and provide
for the beloved. So Yaakov's seven years were "like a few days in his love for her"
(Bereishis 29:20): every day of work was giving to her. Rav Dessler (*Michtav
MeEliyahu* vol. 1 pp. 32-36) says likewise. *Orchos Tzadikim* (Shaar HaAhavah): one
brings himself to love another by helping him and spending on him (Rabbi Avraham
Erlanger: the basis of Rav Hirsch's reading). Rabbi Yehoshua Steinberg adds
Kiddushin 30b, which ties *vaheiv* (Bamidbar 21:14) to *ahavah*. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim (*Cheshek Shlomo*) weighs *hav* and rejects it, reading the root as
א-ב, "primary cause," as in *av* and *avah* (to desire): *ahavah* is the awakening
of desire in a positive direction, its antonym *eivah* the same desire turned
negative. Rabbi Yitzchak of Zeldin (*Shoresh Yesha*) offers both: *hav* as the
smitten lover's "give, give" (Mishlei 30:15, the leech), or a metathesis of *avah*.
*Sefer HaBris* (Rabbi Pinchas Eliyahu Horowitz): love is the expansion of the soul
and its joy in what pleases it, desired above all else. Rabbi Meir Ibn Gabbai:
*ahavah* and *echad* are both thirteen in gematria; love is the longing of two to
become one. Plural *ahavim* (Mishlei 7:18, Hoshea 8:9) means expressions of love.

### chibah (חיבה) and chaviv (חביב)
Root ח-ב-ב. Pappenheim (*Yerios Shlomo*, *Cheshek Shlomo*): from ח-ב, hidden (as in
*mischabeh*); one who is *chaviv* is embedded in the depths of the heart, as if
hidden within the one who cherishes him. Rabbi Benzion Chaim Lubetzky (*Midrash
Safah Echas*): *chibah* is love kept inside and not necessarily expressed
outwardly, unlike *ahavah*, which tends to outward expression. Rabbi Yosef Zechariah
Stern (via Rabbi Yehuda Leib Maimon-Fishman): *chibah* is love of a person's
positive aspects only; *ahavah* embraces the whole person, shortcomings included.
Hence the Mishnah (Berachos 9:5) derives from ואהבת that one blesses over the bad as
over the good, and ואהבת לרעך means loving every Jew with his faults as one loves
oneself with one's own; Rabbi Stern therefore called himself an *ohev Tzion*, not a
*chovev Tzion*. Shadal (Devarim 33:3) ties *chibah* to *chubi*, bosom (Iyov 31:33),
what one carries close.

### rechim (רחים)
Root ר-ח-ם. In Hebrew nearly always mercy or womb; the one exception is ארחמך ה'
(Tehillim 18:2), which Ibn Janach, Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Radak, and Ibn Ramoch all
render "I will love You." In the Targumim *rechim* is the standard rendering of
*ahavah*; hence *dechilu u'rechimu*, fear and love, in the *LeShem Yichud*. Rabbi
Yitzchak Avineri: love is the root of mercy, for one has mercy only on whom he
loves. Rav Hirsch (Shemos 13:2): the root's core is a mother's protectiveness.

### agav (עגב)
Root ע-ג-ב. Ten occurrences, all in Yechezkel 23 and 33 except Yirmiyahu 4:30: a
negative love, lust, used of Israel's lusting after idolatry. Plural *agavim* is
expressions of lust, lewd songs (Yechezkel 33:31-32). Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel: *agav*
is marked by outward professions of love, lewd gestures and songs, the opposite of
Lubetzky's inward *chibah*. Rabbi Yitzchak of Zeldin links the *ugav* (an instrument
of sensuous tone) and *agavos* (Berachos 24a) to it; Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche
derives all three from a half-oval shape.

## Rendering guidance

- Standalone אהבה: "love." Do not upgrade to "true love" or "selfless love" unless
  the passage is making Rav Hirsch's or Rabbi Lopian's point.
- חיבה alone: "fondness" or "affection"; חביב: "dear." חבה יתרה (Avos 3:14): "a
  special fondness," not "extra love."
- אהבה and חיבה together: "love and fondness" (or "love and affection"), keeping
  *ahavah* as the wider word and *chibah* as the inner, selective one.
- דחילו ורחימו: "fear and love"; *rechimu* is simply Aramaic *ahavah*.
- עגב, עגבים: "lust," "lewd love"; never bare "love." Yechezkel 23 turns on the
  contrast.
- Trap: English "love" absorbs *ahavah*, *chibah*, and *rechim*, and is misapplied
  to *agav*. Where two stand side by side, or a derashah turns on ואהבת against a
  *chibah* cognate, assign each its own word.
- Trap: "beloved" absorbs *ahuv* and *chaviv*. When both appear, *ahuv* is "beloved"
  and *chaviv* is "dear." And ארחמך (Tehillim 18:2) is "I will love You," not "I
  will have mercy on You."
