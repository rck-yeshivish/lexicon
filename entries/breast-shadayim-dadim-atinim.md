# Breast: shadayim / dadim / atinim (ziz)

## Bottom line

*Shadayim* (singular *shad* or *shod*) is the standard Biblical word:
**breasts**, twenty-four times in Tanach, only once in Chumash (Bereishis
49:25). *Dadim* is the rarer Biblical pair-word, and by the Mishnah's Hebrew it
plainly means **breasts** too, including breast-shaped **spigots**. *Atinim* is a
hapax in Iyov 21:24, understood as **breasts** or, of an animal, **udders**.
*Ziz* (Yeshayahu 66:11) is disputed: Radak reads it as *ziv*, **radiance**, while
others read it as **breast**. Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) lists the
first three as Biblical synonyms; do not assume *dadim* always matches
*shadayim*, since Malbim holds they name different parts of the body.

## Distinctions

### shadayim (שדים), shad / shod (שד)
Every classical lexicographer (Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Shlomo Ibn
Parchon, Radak) traces it to the two-letter root ש-ד. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
takes the core of ש-ד as abnormality, a departure from the ordinary: the mammary
glands secrete nourishing milk, unlike any other limb. He derives from the same
root *shoded* (to plunder, taking or using something outside its normal way),
*sheid* (a demon, which disrupts the natural order), and *shidah/shidos* (Koheles
2:8), nimble dancers who move like *sheidim*. In Bereishis 49:25 Yaakov blesses
Yosef with "blessings of *shadayim* and *rechem*," which reads plainly as breasts
and womb; Rashi instead ties *shadayim* there to Aramaic *shadi*, to shoot or
cast, making it the father's contribution rather than the mother's. Most
occurrences are in Shir HaShirim, and *Shaddai*, written on the outside of a
mezuzah, is associated with *shadayim*, Hashem nurturing and sustaining creation.

### l'shad (לשד)
Bamidbar 11:8 describes the taste of the man as *l'shad hashamen*, a hapax whose
apparent root ל-ש-ד occurs nowhere else. *Yoma* 75a gives two readings that take
the lammed as part of the root: from *shadayim*, since a nursing infant tastes
many flavors in the milk, so the man held many tastes; or from *sheid*, which
shifts its shape and color, as the man shifted its taste. Rashi (to Bamidbar
11:8) asks what the first reading has to do with oil and inclines to Dunash, who
renders *l'shad* a loaf. *Sifrei* (Behaaloscha 89) and Tosefta (*Sotah* 4:1)
expand the nursing comparison: the man was Yisrael's sole sustenance as milk is an
infant's; nursing all day never harms a baby, and neither did the man; and as a
toddler suffers at weaning, so were Yisrael pained when it ceased.

### dadim (דדים), dad (דד)
Four times in Tanach: Yechezkel 23:3, 23:8, 23:21, and Mishlei 5:19. Menachem Ibn
Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) and Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) say *dadim* means
exactly what *shadayim* means, from the two-letter root ד-ד. Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim traces it to the single letter dalet, whose sense he takes as
separation and protrusion: the breasts protrude and stand apart from the body.
He puts in the same family *dud* (pot) and *dudaim*, shaped like *dadim*; *dod*
and *dodah*, branching off the family line, with *dod* (a beloved) and *yedid*;
*niddah*, separated from her husband; *nadan/nedunya*, a dowry set apart from
one's property; *nadan*, a sheath; and *yad*, the hand separating out from the
body's sides.

Against the identification, Malbim (*Sefer HaCarmel*) holds that in Biblical
Hebrew *shadayim* and *dadim* are different body parts: *shadayim* the upper
breasts from which she nurses, *dadim* the lower parts, the place of maidenhood.
Rabbeinu Chananel, quoted by Rabbi Menachem ben Shimon of Posquieres (to
Yechezkel 23:3), reads *dadim* similarly. In Rabbinic Hebrew, however, *dadim*
unambiguously means breasts: *Eruvin* 54b on Mishlei 5:19, that as an infant finds
milk each time he nuzzles the *dadim*, a talmid chacham finds new taste each time
he returns to his learning; *Shabbos* 53b, the widower whose *dadim* opened so he
could nurse his child; Ben Katin's twelve *dadim*, breast-like spigots, on the
kiyor (*Yoma* 3:10); and *Negaim* 6:7-8, *Niddah* 5:8, *Keilim* 8:11. Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim*) keeps them synonymous in
sense but separate in origin: *shadayim* from pouring, *dadim* an infant's own
sound for his mother's breast. Rabbi Shaul Goldman suggests they are one word,
the shin shifting to tav as Hebrew does in Aramaic, and tav to dalet.

### atinim (עטינים)
A hapax: the wicked man dies at ease, "his *atinim* full of milk" (Iyov
21:23-25). The Ralbag and others read it as breasts, that is, the man's stores are
full and he need not scavenge. Rashi (there) points to the Mishnaic cognate
*maatan*, the vat in which olives are gathered so that they give off their oil
(*Maasros* 4:3, *Taharos* 9:1): the olive is made to act like a breast and give
off liquid. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to ע-ט, insatiability: *vaya'at* (Shmuel I
14:33, 15:19), scrounging after spoil; *ayit*, the bird always after more; *eit*,
a writing instrument never done writing; *me'at*, the little that justifies
reaching for more. The udder is never at rest, always filling and being emptied.
Targum to Iyov 21:24 renders *atinim* *bizoi*, and Rabbi Natan of Rome (*Sefer
HeAruch*) explains the Rabbis' *bizaya* in "woe to the breasts that nursed this
one" (Yerushalmi *Kilayim* 1:7, Bereishis Rabbah 5:9) as that same Greek word.

### ziz (זיז)
Yeshayahu 66:11, "you shall nurse and be satisfied from the *shod* of her
consolations, and suckle and delight from the *ziz* of her honor." Radak reads
*ziz* as a form of *ziv*, glow, and the zemer *Shimru Shabsosai* paraphrases the
pasuk with some nusachos reading *ziv*. *Daas Mikra* reads *ziz* as breast, and
Malbim (there) as any protrusion, which would connect it to *mezuzah*, what
stands out at the side of the doorway.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shadayim*, *dadim*, and *atinim* alone: "breasts" for the first two, "breasts"
  or, where the subject is livestock, "udders" for *atinim*.
- Yechezkel 23:3 pairs *shadayim* and *dadim* in one pasuk, and English "breasts"
  cannot carry both. Follow the Machberes and Radak and render the couplet with
  two words ("their breasts were pressed, their maiden bosom kneaded"), or follow
  Malbim and render *dadim* the lower parts; say which reading you took.
- *Ziz* in Yeshayahu 66:11: "radiance" follows Radak, "breast" follows *Daas
  Mikra*, "protrusion" follows Malbim. The pasuk already has *shod* for breast,
  which is what makes Radak's reading avoid a flat repetition.
- *L'shad hashamen* (Bamidbar 11:8): "the richness of oil" reads well, but where
  the derashah of *Yoma* 75a is in play, keep the Hebrew and gloss both readings.
- *Dadim* of the kiyor (*Yoma* 3:10): "spigots," not "breasts"; the shape is the
  reason for the word, not the meaning in context.
- Trap: a discussion turning on Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's roots (ש-ד as
  abnormality, ד as protrusion, ע-ט as insatiability) cannot survive translation
  of the derived words alone. Keep the Hebrew and translate the sense.
