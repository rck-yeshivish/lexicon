# Breast: shadayim / dadim / atinim (ziz)

## Bottom line

*Shadayim* (singular *shad* or *shod*) is the standard Biblical word for
**breasts**. It occurs twenty-four times in Tanach, and only once in Chumash
(Bereishis 49:25). *Dadim* is rarer in Tanach; in Rabbinic Hebrew it plainly
means **breasts**, and the Mishnah also uses it of breast-shaped **spigots**.
*Atinim*, a hapax in Iyov 21:24, is understood by the Ralbag and others as
**breasts**; in Modern Hebrew it is the **udders** of a milk-producing animal or
a human **nipple**. *Ziz* (Yeshayahu 66:11) is disputed: Radak reads it as
*ziv*, **radiance**, while Shadal and Gesenius read it as **breast**. Rabbi
Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) lists *shadayim*, *dadim* and *atinim* as
Biblical synonyms, though Malbim holds that *dadim* and *shadayim* name
different parts of the body.

## Distinctions

### shadayim (שדים), shad / shod (שד)
Every classical lexicographer (Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Shlomo Ibn
Parchon, Radak) traces it to the two-letter root ש-ד. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
takes the core of ש-ד as abnormality, a departure from the ordinary: the mammary
glands secrete nourishing milk, unlike any other limb. He derives from the same
root *shoded* (to plunder, taking or using something outside its normal way),
*sheid* (a demon, which disrupts the natural order), and *shidah/shidos*
(Koheles 2:8), nimble dancers who move like *sheidim*.

- In Bereishis 49:25 Yaakov blesses Yosef with "blessings of *shadayim* and
  *rechem*," which on a simple reading is breasts and womb as symbols of
  fertility; archeologists know that many fertility-goddess idols of the ancient
  world deliberately exaggerated those features for the same reason.
- Others, Rashi among them, tie *shadayim* there to Aramaic *shadi*, to shoot or
  cast, making it the father's contribution rather than the mother's.
- Rabbi Yaakov Chaim Sofer points out that *Peirush HaRokeach* explains
  *shadayim* in that pasuk as clouds that empty out water, as nursing breasts
  empty out milk, so that *shadayim* can also mean "clouds."

The largest share of the word's appearances are in Shir HaShirim, and *Shaddai*,
written on the outside of a mezuzah, is sometimes associated with *shadayim*,
Hashem nurturing and sustaining creation.

Two names may carry the word. Pseudo-Rasag (to Daniel 1:7) explains Shadrach,
the Babylonian name given to Chananyah, as a portmanteau of *shad* and *rach*,
"soft," declaring him a refined and delicate person. And Shdeiohr, father of the
prince of Reuven (Bamidbar 1:5, 2:10, 7:30, 7:35, 10:18), can be read "breasts
of light," or "my breasts are light," or "Shaddai is light."

### l'shad (לשד)
Bamidbar 11:8 describes the taste of the man as *l'shad hashamen*, a hapax whose
apparent root ל-ש-ד occurs nowhere else.

- *Yoma* 75a gives two readings that take the lamed as part of the root: from
  *shadayim*, since a nursing infant tastes many flavors in the milk, so the man
  held many tastes; or from *sheid*, which shifts its shape and color, as the
  man shifted its taste.
- Rashi (to Bamidbar 11:8) asks what the first reading has to do with oil and
  inclines to Dunash, who renders *l'shad* a loaf.
- *Sifrei* (Behaaloscha 89) and Tosefta (*Sotah* 4:1) expand the nursing
  comparison: the man was Yisrael's main sustenance as milk is an infant's; or,
  nursing all day never harms a baby, and neither would the man; or, as a
  toddler suffers at weaning, so were Yisrael pained when it ceased.

### dadim (דדים), dad (דד)
Four times in Tanach: Yechezkel 23:3, 23:8, 23:21, and Mishlei 5:19.

- Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) and Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) say
  *dadim* means exactly what *shadayim* means, from the two-letter root ד-ד.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to the single letter dalet, whose sense he
  takes as separation and protrusion: the breasts protrude and stand apart from
  the body. He puts in the same family *dud* (pot) and *dudaim*, shaped like
  *dadim*; *dod* and *dodah*, branching off the family line, with *dod* (a
  beloved) and *yedid*; *niddah*, separated from her husband; *nadan/nedunya*, a
  dowry set apart from one's property; *nadan*, a sheath; and *yad*, the hand
  separating out from the body's sides.

- Against the identification, Malbim (*Sefer HaCarmel*) holds that in Biblical
  Hebrew *shadayim* and *dadim* are different body parts: *shadayim* the upper
  breasts from which she nurses, *dadim* the lower parts, the place of
  maidenhood. Rabbeinu Chananel, quoted by Rabbi Menachem ben Shimon of
  Posquieres (to Yechezkel 23:3), reads *dadim* similarly.
- In Rabbinic Hebrew, however, *dadim* unambiguously means breasts: *Eruvin* 54b
  on Mishlei 5:19, that as an infant finds milk each time he nuzzles the
  *dadim*, a talmid chacham finds new taste each time he returns to his
  learning; *Shabbos* 53b, the widower whose *dadim* opened so he could nurse
  his child; Ben Katin's twelve *dadim*, breast-like spigots, on the kiyor
  (*Yoma* 3:10); and *Negaim* 6:7-8 on tzaraas, *Niddah* 5:8 on a girl's
  development, *Bechoros* 7:5 on blemishes, *Sotah* 1:6 on the sotah ceremony,
  and *Keilim* 8:11.
- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim*) keeps them synonymous in
  sense but separate in origin: *shadayim* from pouring, *dadim* an infant's own
  sound for his mother's breast.
- Rabbi Shaul Goldman suggests, as an alternative, that they may be two forms of
  one etymon, the shin shifting to tav as often happens moving from Hebrew to
  Aramaic, and that tav to dalet, the second dalet standing in both.

Several names look to carry *dad*. Eldad and Meidad (Bamidbar 11:26-27) seem to
be *El* and *mei* plus *dad*; so too Elidad, prince of Binyamin at the entry to
the land (Bamidbar 34:21). And the foreign kings Hadad of Edom and Ben-Hadad of
Aram bear a name that literally means "the breast."

### atinim (עטינים)
A hapax: the wicked man dies at ease, "his *atinim* full of milk" (Iyov
21:23-25).
- The Ralbag and other commentators read it as breasts, that is, the man's
  stores are full and he need not scavenge. In Modern Hebrew *atinim* (singular
  *atin*) is the udders of a milk-producing animal, or a human nipple.
- Other explanations in Biblical scholarship include "pail," "vessel," "trough,"
  "watering place," "side/thigh," even "body" (see David J. A. Clines'
  dictionary of classical Hebrew).
- Rashi (there) points to the Mishnaic cognate *maatan*, the vat in which olives
  are gathered so that they give off their oil (*Maasros* 4:3, *Bava Metzia*
  5:7, *Taharos* 9:1 and 9:9), and the act of placing them in it (*Menachos*
  8:4): the olive is made to act like a breast and give off liquid.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim traces it to ע-ט, insatiability and stinginess:
  *vaya'at* (Shmuel I 14:33, 15:19), scrounging after spoil; *ayit*, the bird
  always after more; *eit*, a writing instrument never done writing; *me'at*,
  the little that justifies reaching for more; and *atah*, wrapping, a lazy way
  of putting on clothes, stingy in effort. The udder is never at rest, always
  filling and being emptied.
- Targum to Iyov 21:24 renders *atinim* *bizoi*, and Rabbi Natan of Rome (*Sefer
  HeAruch*) explains the Rabbis' *bizaya* in "woe to the breasts that nursed
  this one" (Yerushalmi *Kilayim* 1:7, Bereishis Rabbah 5:9) as that same Greek
  word. The Greek βυζίου (*byziou*) is a cousin of the English "bosom," from
  Proto-Germanic *bosmaz*, both said to go back to the Proto-Indo-European root
  *bhew*-, "swelling," "bending," "curving."

### ziz (זיז)
Yeshayahu 66:11, "you shall nurse and be satisfied from the *shod* of her
consolations, and suckle and delight from the *ziz* of her honor."
- Radak reads *ziz* as a form of *ziv*, glow, and the zemer *Shimru Shabsosai*
  paraphrases the pasuk with some nusachos reading *ziv*.
- Shadal and Gesenius explain *ziz* as breast, as does *Daas Mikra* there; they
  associate the Biblical *ziz* with the Italian *zizza* and the German *zitze*,
  both of them the teat of a female mammal. Linguists derive those from the
  theoretical Proto-Germanic *titt*, and that from the Proto-Indo-European
  *tata*, "father," "parent," or "nipple"; Dr. Chaim Tawil, in his *An Akkadian
  Lexical Companion for Biblical Hebrew*, notes an Akkadian *zizu* that also
  means breast, so that, Rabbi Klein says, it is quite possible the
  Indo-European words are cognate with their Semitic counterparts.
- Mitchell First argues that *ziz* refers to any protrusion (see Malbim there),
  which leads him to speculate that this word for a breast, which protrudes from
  the bosom, and *mezuzah*, which protrudes at the side of the doorway, may be
  related.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shadayim*, *dadim*, and *atinim* alone: "breasts" for the first two, "breasts"
  or, where the subject is livestock, "udders" for *atinim*.
- Yechezkel 23:3 pairs *shadayim* and *dadim* in one pasuk, and English "breasts"
  cannot carry both. Follow the Machberes and Radak and render the couplet with
  two words ("their breasts were pressed, their maiden bosom kneaded"), or follow
  Malbim and render *dadim* the lower parts; say which reading you took.
- *Ziz* in Yeshayahu 66:11: "radiance" follows Radak, "breast" follows Shadal,
  Gesenius and *Daas Mikra*, "protrusion" follows Mitchell First reading Malbim.
  The pasuk already has *shod* for breast,
  which is what makes Radak's reading avoid a flat repetition.
- *L'shad hashamen* (Bamidbar 11:8): "the richness of oil" reads well, but where
  the derashah of *Yoma* 75a is in play, keep the Hebrew and gloss both readings.
- *Dadim* of the kiyor (*Yoma* 3:10): "spigots," not "breasts"; the shape is the
  reason for the word, not the meaning in context.
- Trap: a discussion turning on Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's roots (ש-ד as
  abnormality, ד as protrusion, ע-ט as insatiability) cannot survive translation
  of the derived words alone. Keep the Hebrew and translate the sense.
