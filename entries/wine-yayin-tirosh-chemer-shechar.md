# Wine: yayin / tirosh / chemer / shechar

## Bottom line

*Yayin* is **wine**, the default word. *Tirosh* is **new wine**, on the weight
of the authorities unfermented or barely fermented juice; do not render it
"wine" where a pasuk pairs it with *dagan* or sets it against *yayin*. *Chemer*
is **wine** as well, the ordinary Aramaic word, read by most as "the red one."
*Shechar* is **strong drink**, though the Targumim narrow it for the Nazir.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| יין | yayin | wine | the default; aged wine on the older tradition (Rashi, Ramban, Radak) |
| תירוש | tirosh | new wine, grape juice | at its beginning, sweet and weak (Pappenheim); pre-fermentation juice (Rav Hirsch) |
| חמר | chemer | wine, red wine | "red" turned noun (Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra, Radak); standard in Aramaic |
| שכר | shechar | strong drink | any intoxicant (Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra); beer or mead in Chazal (Bedersi) |
| עסיס | assis | pressed juice | named from the pressing (Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether yayin and tirosh differ at all.** *Machberes Menachem* defines *yayin*
as *tirosh*; Rabbi Yosef Kimchi (*Sefer HaGalui*) objects that this adds nothing
and is wrong, and Yoma 76b treats them as two words naming two properties.

**How they differ.** (1) **Age**: Ramban (to Devarim 14:22), Radak (*Sefer
HaShorashim*), *Tosafos Rid* to Yoma 76b, and Rashi (to Yoma 76b, Menachos 86b),
that *yayin* is old wine and *tirosh* new, wine earning the name *yayin* only
forty days in; Rav Hirsch (to Devarim 32:14) and Rabbi Dr. Yehuda Felix make
*tirosh* juice before fermentation. (2) **Sweetness and hour**: the Vilna Gaon
(to Hoshea 4:11), that *yayin* was drunk in the morning and *tirosh* was the
sweet wine taken after lunch, the navi's rebuke being that they sinned around
the clock. The Bottom line follows the first, the majority and older tradition.
Radak adds that excess *tirosh* is the worse of the two, intoxicating faster.

**What shechar is.** Targum Onkelos and Targum Yonasan render *yayin v'shechar*
(Bamidbar 6:3) as new and old wine, and the *Sifrei* (Naso §23) makes both one
drink; Rabbeinu Bachaya reverses the assignment. Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra holds it
literally means any intoxicating drink, as the Targumim render it for the kohen
in Vayikra 10:9, the Rabbis narrowing it for the Nazir only because Tanach
restricts him to grape products (Bamidbar 6:4). Ibn Ezra keeps the wide sense
even for the Nazir; Rambam (*Hilchos Nezirus* 5:1) has it as a drink with wine
mixed in. Bedersi: in rabbinic usage it is a drink of figs, pomegranates,
raisins or dates, not wine (Pesachim 42b).

**What chemer means.** Some grammarians, per Rabbi Moshe Ibn Ezra, held it a
word known only by tradition. He, Rabbi Yehuda Ibn Balaam, and Radak (to
Yeshayahu 27:2, Tehillim 75:9) argue it means "red," an adjective that became a
noun; Rabbi Aharon Marcus shifts the color to brown. Others, Rav Hirsch among
them, read ח-מ-ר as fermentation, which yields *chamirah*, sourdough, as well.

## Distinctions

### yayin (יין)
Yoma 76b: *yayin* alludes to *yelalah*, the wailing wine brings to the world;
Rashi there links it to *taaniyah va'aniyah* (Yeshayahu 29:2). Menachem Ibn
Saruk, Ibn Janach and Radak take the root as י-י-ן; Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
takes it as the biliteral י-נ, deception, the root of *onaah*, since wine is
sweet at the start and robs the drinker of his thinking after.

### tirosh (תירוש)
Under forty appearances, usually paired with *dagan*. Yoma 76b expounds it from
*rosh* and *rash*: one who merits becomes a head, one who does not a pauper
(Rabbeinu Elyakim, he drinks away his money; Rabbi Yaakov Emden, his body grows
weak). Rabbi Pappenheim derives it from ר-ש, head and so beginning, hence wine
at its start; Ibn Janach and Radak class it under י-ר-ש while conceding it has
nothing to do with inheriting, and Rav Hirsch reads it as wine driven out of the
grape by force.

### chemer (חמר, חמרא)
Devarim 32:14; Yeshayahu 27:2; Tehillim 75:9. Rashi to Devarim 32:14: *chemer*
is the Aramaic for *yayin*, standard in the Gemara, the Targumim and biblical
Aramaic (Daniel 5:1-23); *chamar medinah* is whatever drink holds that place
locally. The same letters spell *chamor*, donkey, which Eruvin 53b turns into a
joke on a Galilean's blurred gutturals; the Maharal ties it to *chomer*.

## Rendering guidance

- יין: "wine," always. Do not upgrade it to "old wine" unless the passage
  contrasts it with *tirosh*; put the forty-day tradition in a note.
- תירוש: "new wine" is safe on both readings; "grape juice" only where the
  sefer follows Rav Hirsch, and say so.
- Trap: "wine" for both words destroys Hoshea 4:11 and every *dagan v'tirosh*
  pairing. Where both stand together, render "wine and new wine."
- שכר: "strong drink" in a pasuk (Vayikra 10:9); "beer" only in Chazal.
- עסיס is "pressed juice"; *mel'ayah* (Shemos 22:28) is wine for Targum Yonasan
  and Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino, grain for Rashbam and Bechor Shor, so follow the
  meforshim the sefer is quoting rather than defaulting to "wine."
