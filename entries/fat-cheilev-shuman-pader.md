# Fat: cheilev / shuman / pader / tarba

## Bottom line

*Cheilev* is **fat**, and in halachic contexts specifically the **forbidden
fat**: the hard visceral globs offered on the mizbeach and barred from eating.
*Shuman* is post-biblical and names the **permitted fat**, the soft fat attached
to the meat. *Pader* is the **fatty membrane** dividing the innards, or on
another view the organ cluster of trachea, lungs, heart, and liver. *Tarba* is
the Aramaic standing in for *cheilev*. The one thing not to get wrong: *cheilev*
also means **the choicest part** of a non-animal thing, and in that borrowed
sense has nothing to do with fat at all.

## Distinctions

### cheilev (חלב)
About ninety occurrences in Tanach, most of them in the opening chapters of
Vayikra. It is fat in the plain sense, of animals and of people, as when Ehud
ben Gera's blade disappeared into Eglon's belly (Shoftim 3:22; see also Tehillim
73:7). Rashi (Bereishis 45:18; Tehillim 147:14): the word is also borrowed for
whatever is choicest and richest, even where no animal fat is involved. So
Pharaoh's promise that Yosef's brothers would eat the *cheilev* of the land
(Bereishis 45:18), and the Levi'im separating *terumas maaser* from the
*cheilev* of the produce (Bamidbar 18:29-32).

*Cheilev* also covers parts of a korban that are not fat at all but share its
law. The tail of a sheep holds no visceral fat, yet the Torah speaks of its fat
(Vayikra 3:9) because it goes on the mizbeach with the *cheilev* proper (Ramban
and Rabbeinu Bachya there; Rambam, *Hilchos Maachalos Assuros* 7:5). Likewise
*chelvei hatamid* in the version of *Shekalim* 8:8 cited at Sukkah 54b: since
the *tamid* is an olah, every limb is burned, and Rashi explains that they are
all called *cheilev* because they share *cheilev*'s law, burned on the mizbeach
and forbidden to eat (see Tosafos there and the standard text of the Mishnah).

The root ches-lamed-beis also gives *chalav*, milk. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
(*Cheshek Shlomo*): *cheilev* is from *chalav* because it accrues in hard white
globs, milk-colored. Rav Hirsch (Bereishis 45:18) joins *cheilev*, *chalav*, and
*chalaf*, exchanged or set aside for another use: milk is the nourishment
separated out of the mother's body to sustain another, and *cheilev* is the
energy the body stores aside for future use rather than present need.

### shuman (שומן)
Not in Tanach at all; it enters with the Mishnah (*Kerisus* 4:1). Rabbi Shlomo
Aharon Wertheimer ties it to the biblical root shin-mem-nun, oily, familiar from
*shemen*. Rashi (Chullin 45b): *cheilev* is the general Hebrew word covering
both forbidden and permitted fats, and *shuman* was coined by Chazal for the
permitted fats specifically, so that the two could be told apart. Ramban and
Rabbeinu Bachya (Vayikra 3:9) sharpen it anatomically: *cheilev* is the visceral
fat, the free globs not attached to muscle, which the Torah forbids, while
*shuman* is the fat bound up with the muscle, the marbling in a kosher cut,
which is permitted. Pappenheim: *shuman* is from *shemen* because it is the more
pourable, oil-like fat.

### tarba (תרבא)
Targumic Aramaic for *cheilev* (Onkelos to Shemos 23:18, 29:13; Vayikra 3:3,
7:24, 8:26), with the exception of Hevel's korban, where Onkelos renders it with
a form of *shuman* / *shemen* (Bereishis 4:4; so too Targum to Tehillim 73:7).
Rashi regularly defines the Gemara's *tarba* as *cheilev* (Shabbos 133b, 136b;
Avodah Zarah 28b; Chullin 93a; Bechoros 30a), though once as *shuman* (Bava
Metzia 83b). Chizkuni (Vayikra 3:3) and *Tirgem Avraham*: *tarba* is from the
Aramaic *rav*, big or growing, since the more one eats the more fat accrues,
which in turn goes with the Hebrew reish-beis-(hey) of multiplying.

### pader (פדר)
Three occurrences, all in Vayikra and all in korbanos lists beside limbs such as
the head (Vayikra 1:8, 1:12, 8:20). Onkelos always renders it *tarba*, which
points to identity with *cheilev*, and Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes
Menachem*), Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*), Rashi (Tamid 31a), Ibn Ezra and Rashbam
(Vayikra 1:8) all simply define *pader* as *cheilev*. Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino
(*Ohel Moed*) makes the argument explicit from Onkelos rendering both words the
same way. Chullin 27b explains the pairing with the head: since the place of
slaughter would ooze blood unbecomingly on the mizbeach, it was covered with the
*pader*; the Mainz commentary attributed to Rabbeinu Gershom takes this as the
internal fats, again equating *pader* with *cheilev*.

Against this, Ramban (Vayikra 1:8): *pader* is not a general word for fat but
one specific fat, the thin membrane separating the internal organs from one
another, and the word comes by metathesis from peh-reish-dalet, to separate. Rav
Hirsch (Vayikra 1:8; Shemos 13:2) builds a family on peh-dalet-reish with
peh-tes-reish (*peter*, opening) and peh-tav-reish (*pisaron*, the
interpretation of a dream), all of them revealing, removing, or separating: the
firstborn opens the womb and so reveals the mother's motherhood realized; an
interpreter separates a dream's true meaning from the rest; and *pader* is fat
set apart from the body for the korban, lying over the entrails like a covering.
He also floats a link to beis-tav-reish, cutting. Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche
takes the core as peh-dalet, equivalent to beis-dalet, alone or apart. Rabbi
Shaul Goldman notes that Onkelos does not in fact render *pader* by *tarba*
alone but by *prisusa d'tarba*, a spread of fat, so that *pader* implies fat
that spreads over and covers, not a generic word for fat.

A fourth view drops the fat sense entirely: Rabbi Saadia Gaon (Vayikra 1:8), Ibn
Janach (*Sefer HaShorashim*), Ibn Parchon (*Machberes HeAruch*), and Rabbi
Eliyahu HaBachur (*Nimukim* to Radak's *Sefer HaShorashim*) take *pader* as the
attached structure of trachea, lungs, heart, and liver.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "fat" will absorb *cheilev*, *shuman*, and *pader*. In a korbanos or
  kashrus passage, render *cheilev* "the forbidden fat" (or leave it
  transliterated and gloss it once), *shuman* "permitted fat," and *pader* by
  the reading you are following, "fatty membrane" or "suet."
- *Cheilev* in a non-animal context is "the choicest" or "the richest," never
  "the fat": *v'achaltem es cheilev ha'aretz* is "you shall eat the fat of the
  land" only as an English idiom, and where the idiom does not carry, write "the
  best of the land."
- Where a sugya sets *cheilev* against *shuman*, the contrast is the whole
  point, so do not translate both "fat" and rely on the reader.
- *Chelvei hatamid* is not "the fats of the tamid" in the literal sense; on
  Rashi's reading it means the portions burned on the mizbeach. Translate the
  law, not the anatomy, and note the variant texts.
- *Pader* is contested. If the passage does not depend on it, "suet" is a safe
  neutral; if it does, name the view (Ramban's membrane, or Rabbi Saadia Gaon's
  organ cluster) rather than smoothing it to "fat."
