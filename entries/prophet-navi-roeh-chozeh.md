# Prophet: navi / roeh / chozeh

## Bottom line

All three are words for a **prophet**, but they name different sides of the office.
*Navi* is the prophet as **mouth**, the one who brings and speaks Hashem's word,
and in Shemos 7:1 it means plainly **spokesman**. *Roeh* is the **seer**, the
earlier kind, sought out by individuals, whose vision was sharp. *Chozeh* is the
later **visionary**, the Aramaic word for a seer, and the Vilna Gaon takes the
switch to Aramaic as a mark of dimmer clarity.

## Distinctions

### navi (נביא)
Two accounts of the root.

- Rashi (to Shemos 7:1 and Nechemiah 6:7) and Rashbam (to Bereishis 20:7) tie
  *navi* to *niv*, as in *niv sefasayim* (Yeshayahu 57:19), the fruit of the
  lips; a *navi* is one whose lips bear fruit. Rashbam defines the man Hashem
  calls a *navi* as one who is regularly with Him, speaks His words, and whose
  words He loves and whose *tefillos* He hears. Radak says the same at Shmuel I
  9:9.
- Against this, Ibn Ezra (to Shemos 7:1) rules the derivation untenable: *navi*
  and its cognates never appear in Tanach without an aleph, which suggests that
  the aleph belongs to the root, נ-ב-א. Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak, each
  in his *Sefer HaShorashim*, take the same root; Rabbi Klein notes the tension
  in Radak's own two comments and does not resolve it.
- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 20:7) also holds נ-ב-א, and reads it off the
  neighboring נ-ב-ע, "to flow from, to become the source of": a *navi* is the
  source from which Hashem's word issues, the vehicle through which His spirit
  speaks to man.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim takes the biliteral ב-א, "come, bring": the *navi*'s
  job is to bring Hashem's word to its intended audience, and in the case of
  Moshe and Aharon, Aharon was Moshe's *navi* because his role was that of a
  spokesman, to bring Moshe's words to Paroh.

In 2018
archaeologists found a seal inscribed with the name Yishaya followed by the
letters נ-ב-י. Could it be the seal of Yeshayahu HaNavi? It could be, Rabbi Klein
suggests, that this depends on the root of *navi*: if the *aleph* belongs to the
root, then the word after Yishaya on a seal without an *aleph* is not *navi* but
something else, such as "Nobite"; if the *aleph* is not part of the root, then it
is possible the seal really was the prophet's.

*Bereishis Rabbah* (44:6), on *hayah dvar Hashem el Avram bamachazeh*: prophecy
is called by ten names, among them *chazon*, *hatafah*, *dibbur*, *amirah*,
*tzivui*, *masa*, *mashal*, *melitzah* and *chidah*. Which is the harshest?
Rabbi Eliezer says *chazon*, Rabbi Yochanan says *dibbur*, and the Rabbanan say
*masa*.

### roeh (רואה)
Shmuel I 9:9 states outright that what a later generation called a *navi* an
earlier generation called a *roeh*. Malbim: the earlier prophet was called a
*roeh* because his chief function was to see by *ruach hakodesh* and use that to
help individuals uncover what was hidden from them, whether a lost object or a
fault in themselves. Shaul, when his father's donkeys went missing, went to
Shmuel to find them or to learn what sin had caused the loss (Shmuel I 9). A
*roeh* was consulted; people came to him.

Chasam Sofer (*Toras Moshe* to Devarim 12:13): a *roeh* only saw visions from
Hashem and was not charged to spread them, while a *navi* is a prophet Hashem
sends to preach to the people. From this he explains Devarim 12:13 as meaning
that only a *navi* may suspend the prohibition of offering outside the Mikdash
for the moment, as Eliyahu did on Mount Carmel, and a *roeh* may not.

### navi against roeh as a historical shift
Malbim continues: when the nation declined, a different kind of prophet
appeared. The *navi* was Hashem's messenger sent to rebuke the nation for its
sins and turn it back, generally addressing the public rather than the individual, and he
was feared. Where the *roeh* was sought out, the *navi* was one people tended to run from.

### chozeh (חוזה)
The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 22:12 and Yeshayahu 1:1): *navi* names the prophet's
mouth (*niv*), while *roeh* and *chozeh* name his seeing. Within the seeing
there are two ranks. *Roeh* is the Hebrew word for one who sees and belongs to
the earliest prophets, whose visions were sharp; *chozeh* is the Aramaic word
for one who sees, and later prophets bear the Aramaic term because Hebrew is
considered the more spiritually attuned language, so the Aramaic marks a vision less
clearly grasped. Accordingly the earlier prophet's vision is a *mareh* and the
later prophet's a *chazon* or *machazeh*. *Machazeh* denotes a
lower level of prophecy; Rabbi Aharon Marcus points out that this
is the type Avraham Avinu experienced before his *milah* (Bereishis 15:1) and,
later, the heathen prophet Bilaam (Bamidbar 24:4, 24:16).

### navi as spokesman (Shemos 7:1)
Hashem tells Moshe that Aharon "will be your *navi*," which does not seem to mean
Moshe's prophet but something else. Targum Onkelos and Rasag (both to Shemos
7:1): Aharon was to serve as Moshe's *meturgaman*, his interpreter, translator
and spokesman before Paroh. The root of
*meturgaman*/*targum* seems to be ר-ג-ם, which has no other cognates in Hebrew
but in Ugaritic means "saying," so that the Hebrew word for translating from one
language into another is itself derived from the word for "saying" in a different
language. *Avos d'Rabbi Nasan* ch. 34 is where the three words are listed
together.

## Rendering guidance

- נביא: "prophet." In Shemos 7:1 it is "spokesman," and the English must say so;
  "Aharon will be your prophet" is unreadable.
- רואה: "seer." Reserve "seer" for this word alone.
- חוזה: "visionary," or "seer" with a qualifier where the pasuk needs the
  parallel. Shmuel I 9:9 and passages that pair the words demand two distinct
  English words.
- מראה: "vision" (the clearer kind). מחזה / חזון: "vision" too, so where a
  source is grading them, render *mareh* "sight" and *machazeh* "vision," or
  gloss the *machazeh* once as the dimmer grade.
- Trap: English "prophet" will absorb all three, and "vision" will absorb
  *mareh* and *machazeh*. Where the passage is the Vilna Gaon's grading, or
  Shmuel I 9:9's historical note, one English word for two Hebrew ones erases
  the point being made.
- Trap: do not turn the *navi*/*roeh* difference into a grading. Malbim's account
  is one of function and of the generation being served. The grading of clarity
  is the Vilna Gaon's, and it runs between *roeh* and *chozeh*,
  not between *navi* and *roeh*.
