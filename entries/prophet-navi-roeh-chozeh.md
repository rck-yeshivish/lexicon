# Prophet: navi / roeh / chozeh

## Bottom line

All three are rendered **prophet**, but they name different sides of the office.
*Navi* is the prophet as **mouth**, the one who brings and speaks Hashem's word,
and in Shemos 7:1 it means plainly **spokesman**. *Roeh* is the **seer**, the
earlier kind, sought out by individuals, whose vision was sharp. *Chozeh* is the
later **visionary**, the Aramaic word for a seer, and the Vilna Gaon takes the
switch to Aramaic as a mark of dimmer clarity. Do not render *roeh* and *chozeh*
identically where a passage sets them side by side.

## Distinctions

### navi (נביא)
Two accounts of the root. Rashi (to Shemos 7:1 and Nechemiah 6:7) and Rashbam
(to Bereishis 20:7) tie *navi* to *niv*, as in *niv sefasayim* (Yeshayahu
57:19), the fruit of the lips; a *navi* is one whose lips bear fruit. Rashbam
defines the man Hashem calls a *navi* as one who is regularly with Him, speaks
His words, and whose words He loves and whose *tefillos* He hears. Radak says
the same at Shmuel I 9:9.

Against this, Ibn Ezra (to Shemos 7:1) rules the derivation untenable: *navi*
and its cognates never appear in Tanach without an aleph, so the aleph belongs
to the root, נ-ב-א. Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak, each in his *Sefer
HaShorashim*, take the same root; the article notes the tension in Radak's own
two comments and does not resolve it. Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 20:7) also holds
נ-ב-א, and reads it off the neighboring נ-ב-ע, "to flow from, to become the
source of": a *navi* is the source from which Hashem's word issues, the vehicle
through which His spirit speaks to man. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim takes the
biliteral ב-א, "come, bring": the *navi*'s job is to bring Hashem's word to its
intended audience.

### roeh (רואה)
Shmuel I 9:9 states outright that what a later generation called a *navi* an
earlier generation called a *roeh*. Malbim: the earlier prophet was called a
*roeh* because his chief function was to see by *ruach hakodesh* and use that to
help individuals uncover what was hidden from them, whether a lost object or a
fault in themselves. Shaul, when his father's donkeys went missing, went to
Shmuel to find them or to learn what sin had caused the loss (Shmuel I 9). A
*roeh* was consulted; people came to him.

### navi against roeh as a historical shift
Malbim continues: when the nation declined, a different kind of prophet
appeared. The *navi* was Hashem's messenger sent to rebuke the nation for its
sins and turn it back, addressing the public rather than the individual, and he
was feared. Where the *roeh* was sought out, the *navi* was run from.

### chozeh (חוזה)
The Vilna Gaon (to Mishlei 22:12 and Yeshayahu 1:1): *navi* names the prophet's
mouth (*niv*), while *roeh* and *chozeh* name his seeing. Within the seeing
there are two ranks. *Roeh* is the Hebrew word for one who sees and belongs to
the earliest prophets, whose visions were sharp; *chozeh* is the Aramaic word
for one who sees, and later prophets bear the Aramaic term because Hebrew is
the more spiritually attuned language, so the Aramaic marks a vision less
clearly grasped. Accordingly the earlier prophet's vision is a *mareh* and the
later prophet's a *chazon* or *machazeh*. Rabbi Aharon Marcus: *machazeh*
denotes the lower grade of prophecy, the kind Avraham Avinu had before his
*milah* (Bereishis 15:1) and the kind Bilaam had (Bamidbar 24:4, 24:16).

### navi as spokesman (Shemos 7:1)
Hashem tells Moshe that Aharon "will be your *navi*," which cannot mean Moshe's
prophet. Targum Onkelos and Rasag (both to Shemos 7:1): Aharon was to serve as
Moshe's *meturgaman*, his interpreter and spokesman before Paroh. This fits
Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's ב-א exactly: Aharon's role was to bring Moshe's words
to their hearer. *Avos d'Rabbi Nasan* ch. 34 is where the three words are
listed together.

## Rendering guidance

- נביא: "prophet." In Shemos 7:1 it is "spokesman," and the English must say so;
  "Aharon will be your prophet" is unreadable.
- רואה: "seer." Reserve "seer" for this word alone.
- חוזה: "visionary," or "seer" with a qualifier where the pasuk needs the
  parallel. Shmuel I 9:9 and passages that pair the words demand two distinct
  English words.
- מראה: "vision" (the clearer kind). מחזה / חזון: "vision" too, so where a
  source is grading them, render *mareh* "sight" and *machazeh* "vision," or
  gloss the *machazeh* once as the dimmer grade.
- Trap: English "prophet" will absorb all three, and "vision" will absorb
  *mareh* and *machazeh*. Where the passage is the Vilna Gaon's grading, or
  Shmuel I 9:9's historical note, one English word for two Hebrew ones erases
  the point being made.
- Trap: do not read the *navi*/*roeh* difference as one of rank. Malbim makes it
  a difference of function and of the generation being served, not of stature.
