# Gift: minchah / matanah / shai / teshurah / eshkar / doron

## Bottom line

*Minchah* is a gift of **homage or tribute**: for Rav Hirsch it serves the
giver, who is showing his dependence on the receiver. *Matanah* is a gift that
serves the **receiver's need**, given with nothing coming back (Wertheimer); it
is the word for *matanos la'evyonim*. Rabbi Mecklenburg holds it inappropriate
for an offering to Hashem, and Rabbi Klein explains why: Hashem has no needs to
fill. *Shai* is a **weighty gift**, on Bedersi's reading substantial in itself
and on Wertheimer's weighty in the giver's eyes though not in the receiver's; in
Tanach it is always given to Hashem. *Teshurah* is the **showpiece gift**
brought when one comes before a great man. *Eshkar* is obscure and disputed.
*Doron* is the Greek word for "gift" that the Targum uses. *Minchah* is not "meal-offering," it is "gift," and only the sacrificial
context narrows it (Rabbi Mecklenburg).

## Distinctions

### minchah (מנחה)
The commonest of the six, over two hundred appearances, and the word the Torah
uses four times for Yaakov's tribute of five hundred and fifty animals to Esav
(Bereishis 32:14, 32:19-22).
- Rav Hirsch: a *minchah* is a gift of homage, by which the giver shows his
  subservience; it serves the giver's interest in demonstrating his dependence.
- Moshe Halbertal (*On Sacrifice*, p. 10): *minchah* always means one of lower
  standing placing something before a superior, while *matanah* is an exchange
  between equals.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer takes it differently, as a gift that gains the
  giver atonement.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: a *minchah* can be an outright tax or tribute
  paid by a subject to his master, as the subjugated Moavim paid David (Shmuel
  II 8:2) and as Hoshea, the last king of Yisrael, paid his Assyrian overlord
  (Melachim II 17:3).
- Radak and others root it in *nach*/*nachah*, "placed, rested," since the gift
  is meant to settle and placate the recipient.
- Shadal (to Bereishis 4:3): *minchah* is from נ-ו-ח, a gift meant to appease a
  superior in case one sinned against him unawares.

Rabbi Mecklenburg presses further: the more submission a gift shows, the more it
deserves the name *minchah*. Hence *Tefillas Minchah*, said as the sun is on its
way down, bowing, as it were, before Hashem; and hence the poor man's offering,
the *Korban Minchah*, in which the giver shows plainly that he is handing over
the little he has.

### matanah (מתנה)
Some twenty appearances.

- Wertheimer: *matan* and *matanah* are formed from the verb of giving itself,
  and the word fixes on the giving. English *gift* and *give* likewise share an
  etymology, on the interchange of the f and v sounds. A *matanah* is given
  where the recipient needs something and the giver gains nothing from it beyond
  having filled that need, which is why it builds friendship and camaraderie,
  and why it is mandated on Purim, *matanos la'evyonim* (Esther 9:22).
- Rabbi Mecklenburg writes that it is inappropriate to use *matanah* of an
  offering to Hashem and leaves the reason unstated; Rabbi Klein supplies what
  seems to him the obvious reason, that a *matanah* fills a need and Hashem,
  being complete, has none.

### shai (שי)
Three appearances: Yeshayahu 18:7, Tehillim 76:12, Tehillim 68:30.

- Rabbi Avraham Bedersi HaPenini: *shai* is *yesh* ("there is") reversed,
  because a *shai* is a gift of real substance.
- Wertheimer: it is a gift the giver holds to be significant while for the
  receiver it is nothing special, which is why every appearance of *shai* in
  Tanach is a gift to Hashem and the word never occurs in any other setting.

### teshurah (תשורה)
A *hapax legomenon*, Shmuel I 9:7.

- *Midrash Shocher Tov* (Tehillim 87) expounds it as the sort of tribute that
  people look at and sing praises over. Wertheimer reads the Midrash as
  splitting the root two ways: *shur*, a kind of seeing, and *shir*, a song. A
  *teshurah* is the most honorable and eye-catching present one can bring.
- Malbim narrows the setting: it is the gift presented on greeting an eminent
  figure, a kind of *minchah*, given when one comes to a navi or a holy man for
  a berachah or a prophecy. Its purpose is to settle the receiver's body so that
  he can rise past his physical limits and let his spiritual influence flow back
  to the giver, so that giver and receiver trade places. This is why Yitzchak
  asked Esav for a meal before blessing him (Bereishis 27).

### eshkar (אשכר)
Twice in Tanach (Tehillim 72:10, Yechezkel 27:15) and obscure.
- Radak is unsure whether the root is all four letters or only the last three
  (ש-כ-ר, "beer" or "drunk").
- Rabbi Avraham ben Chaim Ibn Ramoch: a *minchah* is gold, silver, and precious
  stones while an *eshkar* is choice fruits, which, Rabbi Klein suggests, seems
  to mean inedible against edible, and which might explain the link to
  *sheichar*.
- Wertheimer: an *eshkar* is a gift whose worth is aesthetic rather than
  monetary or useful, a gift that "bribes" the recipient, so to speak, into
  overrating it, leaving him as though intoxicated (*shikur*) and unable to
  weigh its real value.
- Malbim relates *eshkar* to *sachar*, "reward" or "payment," the shin shifting
  to a sin.

### doron (דורון)
It does not appear in Tanach at all. Targum Yonasan renders the *minchah* Yaakov
sent Esav as *doron*. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) notes that
*doron* is not Aramaic but Greek for "gift." It is close in sense to the name
Theodore, "God's gift" in Greek, which is the parallel to the Hebrew name
Yonasan, "Hashem's gift."

## Rendering guidance

- *Minchah* outside the korbanos: **tribute** where submission is the point
  (Shmuel II 8:2, Melachim II 17:3), **gift** where it is Yaakov to Esav. Inside
  the korbanos: **meal-offering**, but that is the context speaking, not the
  word, per Rabbi Mecklenburg's note.
- *Matanah*: **gift**, plain. *Matanos la'evyonim* stays transliterated.
- *Shai*: **tribute** in the pesukim where it is brought to Hashem, and never
  "little present"; on Bedersi's reading the word means the opposite, and even
  on Wertheimer's the smallness is only in the receiver's eyes.
- *Teshurah*: **presentation gift**, or "a gift of greeting" where Malbim's
  setting is in view.
- *Eshkar*: leave it transliterated and gloss the machlokes; there is no settled
  English.
- Trap: English "gift" will absorb *minchah*, *matanah*, *shai*, and *teshurah*
  at a stroke, and the whole distinction is who the gift serves. If a passage
  contrasts them, hold the axis: tribute (serves the giver) against gift (serves
  the receiver).
- Trap: "offering" reads as sacrificial in English. Do not use it for a
  *minchah* sent between men.
