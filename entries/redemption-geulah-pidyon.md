# Redemption: geulah / pidyon (pedus)

## Bottom line

*Pidyon* (verb *podeh*) is redemption by exchange: **ransom**, **buying back**,
the fact of being freed. *Geulah* (verb *goel*) is redemption by right of
kinship and by force, with no payment: **redemption**, **deliverance**, and it
carries the sense of being raised up, not merely let out. Several
explanations run in the same direction (the Chatam Sofer, Rabbi Menachem Mendel
of Kosov, Malbim), each making *geulah* the fuller term and *pidyon* the bare
one; the Vilna Gaon sets exchange against sheer force, and Rabbi Chaim of Brisk
and Rabbi Shmuel Tuvia Stern cut the difference on other axes.

## Distinctions

### pidyon (פדיון), pedus (פדות), podeh (פודה)
Root פ-ד-ה. The Vilna Gaon, on the two parallel redemptions of Yetzias
Mitzrayim: the *pidyon* of the night of Pesach was an exchange, Hashem trading
the Jewish firstborn for the Egyptian firstborn, saving the former and
annihilating the latter; so *pidyon* is salvation that comes through a swap.
Rabbi Chaim of Brisk, in the classical yeshivish *cheftza-gavra* terms: *pidyon*
looks at the object redeemed and marks a change in the object's own status.
Chatam Sofer: *pidyon* is negative redemption, simply being saved from the
calamity at hand. Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Kosov: temporary relief from what
ails one. Malbim: *pidyon* is the bare fact of salvation. Rabbi Shmuel Tuvia
Stern: *pidyon* takes something holy and releases it by transferring the
holiness onto something else (the redemption of hekdesh); alternatively,
*pidyon* is case-by-case redemption of the individual. (Rabbi Stern was the
Rabbi of Miami Beach.)

A note on spelling: *podeh* is
generally spelled with a heh, but in one place (Iyov 33:24) Scripture replaces
the heh with an ayin (פדע). Rashi there and Ramban (to Shemos 21:14) explain
that ayin and heh are interchangeable letters, so it is the same word. Ibn
Janach, Menachem ibn Saruk, and Radak, however, have separate entries for the
heh form and the ayin form in their lexicons of Hebrew roots, implying that they
understand the latter to be a separate word; and to this effect Malbim writes
that *podeh* with an ayin *may* be related to a similar-sounding Arabic word
meaning "removal."

### geulah (גאולה), goel (גואל)
Root ג-א-ל. The Vilna Gaon: the *geulah* of Yetzias Mitzrayim was rescue by sheer
force, with no remuneration of any kind. Rabbi Chaim of Brisk: *geulah* looks at
the owner and marks a change of ownership. Chatam Sofer: positive redemption,
not only saved from something bad but raised above one's circumstances and made
greater. Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Kosov: comprehensive, everlasting
deliverance. Malbim: *geulah* is redemption born of closeness between redeemer
and redeemed, and that closeness is the impetus for the redeemer's feeling of
responsibility to save. The same closeness lies behind *goel hadam*: when a
man's relative is murdered he holds a status that may, under certain
circumstances, perhaps allow avenging the death, and Bamidbar 35:19 calls him
*goel hadam*, "redeemer of blood," where *goel* alludes to the avenger's kinship
with his murdered brethren. Rabbi Shmuel Tuvia Stern:
*geulah* takes what is
profane and shakes off its non-holy associations so it may enter the domain of
the holy; alternatively, *geulah* is mass redemption of the nation at large.

The end of Vayikra treats
redemption in the legal sense: the right, or even the commandment, of the
impoverished and their extended family to redeem property sold out of
desperation and bring it back to the family, whether a field, a house of
residence, or a person sold as a slave (Vayikra 25:25-55), and the halachic
mechanisms for redeeming consecrated animals, houses, or fields (Vayikra
27:9-34). The verb there is sometimes *goel* and sometimes *podeh*: what is
the difference between them?

## Rendering guidance

- פדיון with a price in view (*pidyon haben*, *pidyon shevuyim*, redeeming
  hekdesh or a field): "redemption" is conventional, but where the passage
  turns on the exchange, prefer "ransom" or "buying back," or gloss "(a
  redemption by exchange)."
- גאולה: "redemption" is the settled English; "deliverance" where the sense is
  rescue by force. When *geulah* is contrasted with *pidyon*, let the English
  show the lift (Chatam Sofer): "not merely rescued but redeemed."
- גואל as the kinsman (*goel hadam*, the *goel* of Rus): "redeemer" keeps the
  kinship sense the column reads into the word, following Malbim's closeness
  principle; do not write "avenger" alone. (The *goel* of Rus is our example;
  the column cites only *goel hadam*.)
- Trap: English "redemption" absorbs both words. In a passage that names them
  together (the Vilna Gaon's two redemptions of Pesach night, or a derashah on
  why one pasuk says *podeh* and another *goel*), keep both Hebrew words
  visible and render *pidyon* as "ransom/exchange" and *geulah* as
  "redemption."
- The Aramaic *purkan* belongs to this field as well; see the salvation entry
  ([salvation](salvation-yeshuah-teshuah-hatzalah-purkan.html)) for that cluster. The
  column does not discuss *purkan*, so nothing here is a ruling on it.
