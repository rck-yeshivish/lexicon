# Path: derech / orach

## Bottom line

*Derech* is the **way** or **road**: broad, public, open to everyone.
*Orach* is the **path**: short, narrow, less commonly traveled (Vilna Gaon), the
road that reaches the outlying towns and villages (Malbim). *Derech* and *orach*
are not interchangeable. Pappenheim dissents: for him they can name one road,
*derech* for its length, *orach* for its width. Six more words fill out the
cluster. *Rechov* is the **street** inside a town, where *derech* is the road
between towns; *netiv* is the **narrow path**, shorter or narrower than a
*derech*, and on the Zohar's approach a private one open only to select
individuals; *mesilah* is the **paved road**; *mishol* the **footpath**, for a
man on foot; *maagal* the **circular path**, from *igul*, a circle; *shvil* the
**downhill path**, from *shafel*, low. Of these six, only the Zohar's reading of
*netiv* is attributed to anyone; the rest are given anonymously.

## Distinctions

Elul generally brings an uptick in the study of mussar, and several famous
mussar sefarim take road words for their titles: *Orchos Tzaddikim*, "Ways of
the Righteous"; *Mesillas Yesharim*, "Paths of the Just"; *Derech Hashem*, "Way
of God"; *Nesivos Shalom*, "The Lanes of Peace."

### derech (דרך)
Root ד-ר-ך, related to [*dorech*](trampling-romes-dorech-boss.html#%D7%93%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%9B), to tread, which also means to tread the
winepress (Yeshayahu 63:2); Rashi (Devarim 33:29): to tread on one's enemies, to
subdue them underfoot.
- Vilna Gaon: a *derech* is wide and open to the public.
- Malbim: both words are public, but the *derech* runs from major city to major
  city, province to province.
- The Zohar (Vayakhel 215a, Kedoshim 88a): the road to which everyone has
  access.
- Pappenheim: it can be the same road as the *orach*, viewed for its great
  length.
- Rabbi Moshe Shapiro, from Rashi's treading: the *derech* is the path of life,
  strewn with trials that a man is expected to tread down and overcome; only so
  does he go forward on it.
- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim*), reading the word by the
  ground walked on and ignoring the initial dalet: *derech* is related to *rach*,
  soft, a soft parcel of ground that a man can simply walk onto, and by treading
  it he creates a path for others to follow.

### orach (אורח)
Root א-ר-ח; Pappenheim derives it from the two letters ר-ח, width (*revach*,
*rachav*).
- Vilna Gaon: short, narrow, not as commonly used.
- Malbim: the road that reaches the outlying towns and villages.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, after Malbim, suggests that because an *orach*
  breaks off the main road to lead more directly to a less-traveled destination,
  the tzaddik is said to guard the *orach* (Mishlei 2:20), being exact in the details as well as the general route.
- The Zohar: an *orach* is a brand new road no one has trodden, which is why the
  righteous are tied to it: they bring novel Torah into the world and reveal
  Hashem's presence where it had not yet been revealed.
- Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Shlez on Dan (Bereishis 49:17), reading Yaakov as foreseeing
  the two idols destined to be set up at the city of Dan: Michah's idol was the
  snake on the *derech*, a passive discouragement from the pilgrimage to Shiloh
  and Yerushalayim, whereas Yeravam's calf, which barred the pilgrimage by
  decree, made the way a narrow *orach* with a serpent on it.
- Radak: *oreach*, a guest, arrives by the road he traveled; Pappenheim adds
  *aruchah*, the banquet given for a guest.

### rechov (רחוב)
The street of a settled place. Some explain that a *derech* is specifically an
intercity road and a *rechov* specifically an intracity one.

### netiv (נתיב)
Two distinctions, both drawn against *derech*.

- First, others explain that a *derech* denotes the longer and/or wider road and
  a [*netiv*](paths-netiv-mesilah-shvil-mishol.html#%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%91) the shorter and/or narrower one.
- Second, an approach found in the Zohar: a *derech* is open to the public,
  while a *netiv* is for private use and is open only to select individuals.

### mesilah (מסילה)
A paved road: a *mesilah* is said to refer specifically to a paved path.

### mishol (משעול)
A pedestrian footpath.

### maagal (מעגל)
Explained as a circular path, derived from *igul*, a circle.

### shvil (שביל)
Explained as related to the word *shafel*, low, and so a path that has a steep
incline, one that goes downwards.

## Rendering guidance

- The pair (Bereishis 49:17, "a snake on the *derech*, a serpent on the
  *orach*"): "a snake on the road, a viper on the path." Two different nouns.
- *Derech* in the moral sense (the way of Hashem, his ways, *derech eretz*):
  "way." "Road" is for the literal sense; "path" belongs to *orach* and to
  *netiv*.
- Where *derech* and *rechov* stand together, keep the scale visible: "road"
  for what runs between towns, "street" for what runs inside one. That is the
  only distinction drawn between the two.
- *Mesilah*, *mishol*, *maagal* and *shvil* each carry one added fact and
  should not be flattened to "path": "paved road," "footpath," "circular
  path," and, where the derashah from *shafel* is in play, "the path that goes
  down." Plain "path" for any of them loses the very thing that named it.
- *Orchos* in a plural of conduct (*orchos tzaddikim*): "paths." The received
  English titles ("Ways of the Righteous," "Paths of the Just") are titles and
  stay as they are; Rabbi Klein uses them so.
- Trap: "path" absorbs both words in English, and "way" drifts to "manner"
  and loses the road altogether. When a pasuk or a derashah sets the two side
  by side (Dan; Mishlei's *derech* and *orach* parallels), the English must
  keep the broad word and the narrow word apart.
- Where a derashah reads *derech* from *dorech* (Rabbi Moshe Shapiro), keep
  "tread" visible: "the way one treads."
