# Path: derech / orach

## Bottom line

*Derech* is the **way** or **road**: broad, public, open to everyone, the
general term, and the one that extends to conduct ("the way of Hashem").
*Orach* is the **path**: short, narrow, seldom traveled, branching off the
road toward a particular place (Vilna Gaon; Malbim). Keep "way"/"road" for
*derech* and "path" for *orach* and never swap them; English treats the two as
interchangeable, the Hebrew does not. Pappenheim's dissent (one road, *derech*
its length, *orach* its width) is recorded below; the Gaon and Malbim carry
the rendering. *Nativ* and *mesillah* are not treated here (the article
promised them for a sequel).

## Distinctions

### derech (דרך)
Root ד-ר-ך, from *dorech*, to tread, which also means to tread the winepress
(Yeshayahu 63:2); Rashi (Devarim 33:29): to tread on one's enemies, to subdue
them underfoot. Vilna Gaon: a *derech* is wide and open to the public. Malbim:
both words are public, but the *derech* runs from major city to major city,
province to province. The Zohar (Vayakhel 215a, Kedoshim 88a): the road to
which everyone has access. Pappenheim: the same road as the *orach*, viewed
for its great length. Rabbi Moshe Shapiro, from Rashi's treading: the *derech*
is the path of life, strewn with trials that a man is expected to tread down
and overcome; only so does he go forward on it.

### orach (אורח)
Root א-ר-ח; Pappenheim derives it from the two letters ר-ח, width (*revach*,
*rachav*). Vilna Gaon: short, narrow, not commonly used. Malbim: the road that
reaches the outlying towns and villages. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer, after
Malbim: because an *orach* breaks off the main road to lead more directly to a
less-traveled destination, the tzaddik is said to guard the *orach* (Mishlei
2:20), being exact in the details and not only the general route. The Zohar:
an *orach* is a brand new road no one has trodden, which is why the righteous
are tied to it: they bring novel Torah into the world and reveal Hashem's
presence where it had not yet been revealed. Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Shlez on Dan
(Bereishis 49:17): Michah's idol was the snake on the *derech*, a passive
discouragement from the pilgrimage to Shiloh and Yerushalayim, whereas
Yeravam's calf, which barred the pilgrimage by decree, made the way a narrow
*orach* with a serpent on it. Radak: *oreach*, a guest, arrives by the road
he traveled; Pappenheim adds *aruchah*, the banquet given for a guest.

## Rendering guidance

- The pair (Bereishis 49:17, "a snake on the *derech*, a serpent on the
  *orach*"): "a snake on the road, a viper on the path." Two different nouns.
- *Derech* in the moral sense (the way of Hashem, his ways, *derech eretz*):
  "way." "Road" is for the literal sense; "path" belongs to *orach* (and, when
  the sequel is ingested, to *nativ*).
- *Orchos* in a plural of conduct (*orchos tzaddikim*, *orchos chaim*):
  "paths." The received English titles ("Ways of the Righteous," "Paths of the
  Just") are titles and stay as they are.
- Trap: "path" absorbs both words in English, and "way" drifts to "manner"
  and loses the road altogether. When a pasuk or a derashah sets the two side
  by side (Dan; Mishlei's *derech* and *orach* parallels), the English must
  keep the broad word and the narrow word apart.
- Where a derashah reads *derech* from *dorech* (Rabbi Moshe Shapiro), keep
  "tread" visible: "the way one treads."
