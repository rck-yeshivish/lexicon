# Eleven: achad-asar / ashtei-asar

## Bottom line

Both mean eleven, and both stand in the opening pesukim of Devarim: "eleven
(*achad asar*) days from Chorev... in the eleventh (*ashtei asar*) month"
(Devarim 1:2-3). Render *achad asar* **eleven** and *ashtei asar* **eleventh**
where the context allows, because the working distinction is that *achad asar*
is eleven as a number of its own and *ashtei asar* is eleven as an outgrowth of
ten. The tell that settles most cases: *ashtei asar* never takes the definite
article, and every other number does.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| אחד עשר | achad asar | eleven | an independent number; takes *ha-* (Rabbi Meir Mintzberg) |
| עשתי עשר | ashtei asar | eleventh | eleven as a swelling of ten; never takes *ha-* (Rabbi Meir Mintzberg) |
| עשתי | ashtei | the disputed element | "upon twelve" (Ibn Janach), "born of ten" (Ibn Ezra), "bloated" (Mintzberg), or unknown (Radak) |

Counting the feminine forms there are four words: *achad asar* and *achat
esreh*, *ashtei asar* and *ashtei esreh*. *Ashtei asar* and *ashtei esreh*
appear nineteen times in Tanach, *achad asar* and *achat esreh* thirteen.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What ashtei means.**

1. **Upon twelve.** Rabbi Yonah Ibn Janach: *ashtei asar* contracts *al*,
   "upon," with *shteim asar*, twelve, eleven being the number that sits just
   under twelve and so is reckoned from it.
2. **Born of ten.** Ibn Ezra, rejecting Ibn Janach twice over. First on sense:
   if anything, twelve is "upon" eleven and not the reverse. Second on form: on
   Ibn Janach's account the masculine should be *ashnei asar*, since the
   masculine twelve is *shneim asar*, yet there is no such word; only *ashtei
   asar* and *ashtei esreh* exist. Ibn Ezra's own reading takes *ashtei* as a
   cognate of *ashtonosav*, "his thoughts" (Tehillim 146:4): as a plan is born
   out of thought, eleven is born out of ten, so *ashtei asar* is what issues
   from ten. He notes that a great secret lies behind the connection of ten to
   eleven and leaves it there.
3. **A bloated ten.** Rabbi Meir Mintzberg: *eshes* denotes an overly distended
   body (Rashi to Shir HaShirim 5:14 and Yirmiyahu 5:28; Yoma 34b; Menachos
   28a), so *ashtei asar* is a swollen ten.
4. **Unknown.** Radak rejects both Ibn Janach and Ibn Ezra and concludes that
   the derivation is beyond us.

The Bottom line does not rest on any of these, since all four agree on the
number. It does note that Ibn Janach ties eleven to twelve and Ibn Ezra ties it
to ten, which matters for a derashah on the number and not for a translation.
Mintzberg's reading has the advantage of explaining the grammar, and the Bottom
line follows him and the Rokeach on usage. The article's appeal to a Ugaritic
funerary inscription is omitted here under house rule 9; the article itself
concedes that it does not account for all the pesukim.

**When each form is used.** *Peirush HaRokeach*: where eleven is invoked with
all the numbers before it included, the text says *achad asar*; where only the
eleventh member of a set is meant and the rest are set aside, it says *ashtei
asar*. The column reads this, tentatively, as the difference between the ordinal
eleventh and the cardinal eleven, on the model of *rishon* against *echad* and
*revi'i* against *arba*. Rabbi Meir Mintzberg reaches the same place from the
grammar: *ashtei asar* never carries the definite article *ha-*, though every
other number does, *achad asar* and *achat esreh* among them, because it names
no number with standing of its own, only ten carried one further. He suggests
this is what the Rokeach meant by an independent against a dependent eleven.

## Distinctions

### achad asar (אחד עשר) / achat esreh (אחת עשרה)
Literally "one ten," built transparently from *echad*. Eleven counted as a
number: eleven days from Chorev (Devarim 1:2), where the days before it are
counted too. Takes the definite article normally.

### ashtei asar (עשתי עשר) / ashtei esreh (עשתי עשרה)
The opaque form, and the more common one in Tanach. Used of the eleventh month
(Devarim 1:3), the eleventh in a series where the others are not in view.
Never prefixed with *ha-*.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: אחד עשר = "eleven," עשתי עשר = "eleventh," and check the context
  before overriding, since the correlation is strong but the article concedes it
  does not hold everywhere.
- Trap: English "eleven" will absorb both, and in Devarim 1:2-3 that is exactly
  the loss, since the two forms stand two pesukim apart and the shift is the
  only signal the text gives. Render the first "eleven days" and the second "the
  eleventh month," and the Hebrew's own distinction comes through in English
  without a note.
- Where a derashah turns on *ashtei*, do not translate the element at all; give
  the Hebrew and the reading being used, since Ibn Janach's "upon twelve" and
  Ibn Ezra's "born of ten" point in opposite directions and the choice is the
  derashah.
- In a list of ordinals, keep *ashtei asar* in the ordinal series ("the tenth,
  the eleventh, the twelfth") rather than letting it fall back to a cardinal.
