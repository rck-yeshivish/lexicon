# Egypt: Mitzrayim / Patros / Rahav (Matzor, Kaftor, eretz Cham)

## Bottom line

*Mitzrayim* is **Egypt**, and also the name of the man from whom the Egyptians
descend. *Patros* is properly **Upper Egypt**, or the Patrusim who came from
Mitzrayim. The *paytanim* use it for Egypt as a whole, as they use *Ludim*,
*Anamim*, and *Lehavim*, and Rabbi Klein concludes that they seem to do so by
poetic license, not because the words are synonyms. *Rahav*, a word meaning
haughty, is a name for Egypt and for its heavenly minister. *Eretz Cham*, the
Land of Cham, is Tehillim's name for Egypt.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why Mitzrayim has a plural or dual ending.**
- Radak (to Bereishis 10:13): unknown, perhaps something in the circumstances of
  his birth, and once Cham's son bore it he named his own sons the same way.
- *Peirush HaRokeach* and Rabbi Yehuda HaChassid (there): each of those nations
  came from a pair of twins whose families intermarried, which accounts for the
  sons' names but does not quite explain Mitzrayim's own.
- *Bereishis Rabbah* 37:5 notes that the sons' names all carry a *yam* ending;
  Maharzu, that they settled along the coast, and Ramban (to 10:13) places them
  all in Egypt's vicinity.
- Rabbeinu Bachya (to 10:13): the yud-mem hints that these family-nations were
  destined to drown at Krias Yam Suf.
- Many sources read the name as a portmanteau of *meitzar*, a narrow place,
  border or strait, and *yam*, sea, each explaining the connection its own way;
  the earliest Rabbi Klein found is Rabbi Chaim Vital, that Egypt places a limit
  on the Upper Seas, since its fecundity hinged on the Nile and it was not as
  reliant on rain as other countries, a state of affairs that could, chas
  v'shalom, lead people to forget Hashem in their pursuit of a crop, no longer
  feeling the need to look upward for rain (see Bereishis 13:10, Devarim 11:10).
- *Sodei Semuchim*, ascribed to Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms: it is the
  inflection Hebrew uses for paired things, and Egypt earned it as a nation
  assembled out of several of Cham's lines, like Yerushalayim, two cities
  joined.
- Ibn Ezra (to 10:13) reads the whole list as names of countries, not of men.

**Whether "Mitzrayim" at the Yam Suf is the man.** *Midrash Sechel Tov* (to
Shemos 14:30): "Yisrael saw Mitzrayim dead on the seashore" means Grandfather
Mitzrayim himself, revived to watch his wretched descendants destroyed; some
*baalei Tosafos* had a tradition extending this to Shemos 14:10 and 14:25
(*Ateres Zekeinim* and *Hadar Zekeinim* to 14:10, *Sefer Chassidim* §607,
*Siddur Rokeach* pp. 213 and 222, *Peirush HaRokeach* to 14:30).

**Who the Luvim are.** The Luvim come up four times in Tanach: countless of them
march with Shishak against Yerushalayim (Divrei HaYamim II 12:3, in Rechavam's
reign), again as soldiers a few generations later in Asa's day (16:8), and at
Nachum 3:9 and Daniel 11:43.
- Rid and pseudo-Rasag (to Daniel 11:43), and Tosafos with *Tosafos Chitzoniyos*
  (in *Shitah Mekubetzes* to Bechoros 5b), state plainly that the Luvim are the
  Lehavim, probably because hei and vav interchange.
- Rabbeinu Tam (cited by Tosafos to Shabbos 51b, and see Rabbeinu Nissim Gaon to
  Shabbos 90b): the word simply means Egyptians.
- Rashi: Luvim donkeys were of especially high quality (Bechoros 5b), and they
  came from Luv, the land of the Luvim (Shabbos 51b, and see Rabbeinu Chananel
  there).
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus and Dr. Alexander Kohut postulate that Luv, the homeland
  of the Luvim and Lehavim, is Libya, west of modern Egypt.
- Rabbi Klein offers as an alternative of his own that Lehavim/Luvim may be
  another term for the Nubians, who lived just to Egypt's south, lamed and nun
  being often interchangeable.

## Distinctions

### Mitzrayim (מצרים)
First a person: a son of Cham (Bereishis 10:6, Divrei HaYamim I 1:8), father of
Ludim, Anamim, Lehavim, Naftuchim, Patrusim, and Kasluchim, the last two of whom
begot the Plishtim and Kaftorim (Bereishis 10:13 to 14). Then the land. Rabbi
Elazar Rokeach of Worms adds a sign: the pasuk of Makkas Bechoros (Shemos 12:23)
holds every letter of the aleph-beis but tes, whose value is nine, for the nine
families struck.

### Patros (פתרוס), Ludim (לודים), Anamim (ענמים), Lehavim (להבים)
*Patros* appears seven times, five for the land of Egypt (Yeshayahu 11:11,
Yirmiyahu 44:1 and 44:15, Yechezkel 29:14 and 30:14) and twice for the Patrusim
(Bereishis 10:14, Divrei HaYamim I 1:12). Rabbi Aharon Marcus: in Egyptian, the
land of the south, Upper Egypt, since the Nile flows northward, near the border
with Nubia and Ethiopia. The Hungarian maskil Solomon Löwisohn and Shadal (to
Bereishis 10:14) add that Patros, Pathyris in Greek, was a major city in Upper
Egypt, and that the region took its name from the city, as in Greek times the
region took the name Thebias from the city of Thebes while *Aegyptus* meant Lower
Egypt in particular. Patros, then, is closely associated with Mitzrayim but not
quite synonymous with it. It seems, Rabbi Klein concludes, that Yanai's *Makkas
Bechorei Pasros* uses it for Egypt at large as a poetic way of naming Mitzrayim
by a more obscure term; he reached that conclusion after consulting Professor
Shulamit Elitzur, the world's expert in *piyyutim*, who confirmed that there is
probably no deeper meaning in these substitutions. HaKallir does the same with
*Lud* in the weekday Sukkos piyyut, and *Yom L'Yabashah* calls the Egyptians
*Anamim*, as do many less popular piyyutim. The Ludim themselves appear five
times (Bereishis 10:13, Yechezkel 27:10 and 30:5, Divrei HaYamim I 1:11,
Yirmiyahu 46:9). Rashi (to 10:13, and possibly to Yeshayahu 13:8) presumes a
connection between *Lehavim* and the root LAMED-HEI-BES, flame or blade, saying
that their faces resembled a *lahav* without explaining what that means. Ibn
Gabirol's *Azharos* reads "I desired you and loved, I redeemed you from Lahav",
and in some versions "from Rahav".

### Rahav (רהב), Matzor (מצור), and eretz Cham (ארץ חם)
*Rahav* as a name for Egypt: Rashi to Bereishis 40:23, Chizkuni to 43:32, Radak
to Tehillim 87:4; in *divrei Chazal* Rahav is the *sar* of Egypt and of the sea
(Bava Basra 74b, *Bamidbar Rabbah* 18:22, Zohar *Terumah* 170b, Zohar Chadash
*Bishalach* 38b). The word means confident, strong, haughty, an apt description
of the Egyptian mentality, and Rabbi Aharon Marcus surmises that it is a
permutation of *Lahav*, lamed and reish interchanging. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
(*Cheshek Shlomo*) relates *rahav*, haughtiness, to the biliteral root of *rav*
and *harbeh*, which he defines as quantitative increase: the conceited person has
more self-pride than normal. *Matzor* is, for Radak
(*Sefer HaShorashim*, and to Yeshayahu), a singular form of the name that Tanach
sometimes uses (Melachim II 19:24, Yeshayahu 19:6, 37:25); in the same vein, the
Arabic for Mitzrayim is Misr, which seems to be simply the singular form. Other
*meforshim* read *matzor* there as the ordinary noun for narrow places. *Eretz Cham* is Tehillim's name
(105:23, 105:27, 106:22), with *admas bnei Cham* in the weekday Maariv and
*malchus eglah* in *Maoz Tzur* (see also Yirmiyahu 46:20); *Kaftor* (Amos 9:7,
Yirmiyahu 47:4, Tzefaniah 2:5) is the Plishtim's homeland, a related place and
not Egypt, which Shadal (to Bereishis 10:14) takes to be either Cyprus or Crete,
also known as Kandia.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 6:10): Egypt was called
*Chamia* or *Chemia*, related to the Arabic *alchemy*, the source of
*chemistry*.

### Where the English word "Egypt" comes from
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus claims that "copt" is a corruption of the Hebrew *Kaftor*,
  and that from that corruption came the Greek *Aíguptos* and the Latin
  *Aegyptus*, the eventual etymons of English "Egypt."
- Others argue that those antecedents derive instead from the native Egyptian
  phrase meaning "temple of the soul of Ptah," Ptah being the deity of the
  Egyptian city of Memphis, whose name the Greeks applied to the region as a
  whole.

Two early forms of the word turn up in Chazal: a language called Gipti (Shabbos
115a to 115b, Megillah 18a), which seems to be Coptic; and the parsing of
*totafos* (Devarim 6:8, 11:18) as *tot*, two in Kapti, plus *pat*, two in Afriki
(Sanhedrin 4b, Zevachim 37b, Menachos 34b), where the consensus takes Kapti for
Coptic and Afriki for Phrygian. The Romani were once thought to have come from
Egypt, hence "Gyptians" and eventually "Gypsies," though anthropologists now
place their origin in South Asia.

## Rendering guidance

- In a piyyut, keep the poet's word and gloss it: "the firstborn of Patros" with
  a note that Patros is Egypt. The same for *Lud*, *Anamim*, *Lahav*, and
  *Rahav*; flattening them deletes the poem's device.
- Trap: English "Egypt" will absorb *Mitzrayim*, *Patros*, *Matzor*, and *Rahav*
  at once. Reserve "Egypt" for *Mitzrayim* and transliterate the rest.
- *Mitzrayim* in Shemos 14 may be the nation or the man; translate "the
  Egyptians" and put *Midrash Sechel Tov* in a note. In a frum text keep
  *Mitzrayim*, *Mitzrim*, and *Mitzri* transliterated for the people.
