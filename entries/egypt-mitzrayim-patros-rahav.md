# Egypt: Mitzrayim / Patros / Rahav (Matzor, Kaftor, eretz Cham)

## Bottom line

*Mitzrayim* is **Egypt**, and also the name of the man from whom the Egyptians
descend. *Patros* is properly **Upper Egypt** or the Patrusim who came from
Mitzrayim; the *paytanim* use it, and *Ludim*, *Anamim*, and *Lehavim*, for Egypt
as a whole by poetic license, not because the words are synonyms. *Rahav* is
Egypt named for its haughtiness and the name of its heavenly minister; *eretz
Cham* is the liturgical name. The trap is flattening all of these to "Egypt" in a
piyyut, where the obscure name is the poet's whole point.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| מצרים | Mitzrayim | Egypt | the standard name, 680 times; a person before it was a land (Bereishis 10:6) |
| מצור | Matzor | Egypt, singular | Radak's singular form of the name; others read it as "narrow places" |
| פתרוס | Patros | Upper Egypt | southern Egypt (Rabbi Aharon Marcus), or the Patrusim; used loosely for Egypt in piyyut |
| רהב | Rahav | Egypt, the haughty | Rashi, Chizkuni, Radak; also the angelic minister of Egypt and the sea |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Why Mitzrayim has a plural or dual ending.** Six accounts, no reconciliation.
Radak (to Bereishis 10:13): unknown, perhaps something in the circumstances of
his birth, and once Cham's son bore it he named his own sons the same way.
*Peirush HaRokeach* and Rabbi Yehuda HaChassid (there): each of those nations
came from a pair of twins whose families intermarried, which explains the sons'
names but not Mitzrayim's own. *Bereishis Rabbah* 37:5 notes that the sons'
names all carry a *yam* ending; Maharzu, that they settled along the coast, and
Ramban (to 10:13) places them all in Egypt's vicinity. Rabbeinu Bachaya (to
10:13): the yod-mem hints that these family-nations were destined to drown at
Krias Yam Suf. Rabbi Chaim Vital, earliest of the portmanteau readings:
*meitzar* plus *yam*, since Egypt sets a limit on the Upper Seas, its
fruitfulness coming from the Nile and not from rain (see Devarim 11:10).
*Sodei Semuchim*, ascribed to Rabbi Elazar Rokeach of Worms: it is the
inflection Hebrew uses for paired things, and Egypt earned it as a nation
assembled out of several of Cham's lines, like Yerushalayim, two cities joined.
Ibn Ezra (to 10:13) reads the whole list as names of countries, not of men.

**Whether "Mitzrayim" at the Yam Suf is the man.** *Midrash Sechel Tov* (to
Shemos 14:30): "Yisrael saw Mitzrayim dead on the seashore" means Grandfather
Mitzrayim himself, revived to watch his descendants destroyed; some *baalei
Tosafos* extend this to Shemos 14:10 and 14:25 (*Ateres Zekeinim* and *Hadar
Zekeinim* there, *Sefer Chassidim* §607). The plain reading takes it collectively.

**Who the Luvim are.** Rid and pseudo-Rasag (to Daniel 11:43), Tosafos and
*Tosafos Chitzoniyos* (in *Shitah Mekubetzes* to Bechoros 5b): the Luvim are the
Lehavim, hei and vav interchanging. Rabbeinu Tam (Tosafos to Shabbos 51b): the
word simply means Egyptians. Rashi: Luvim donkeys were of high quality (Bechoros
5b), and they came from Luv (Shabbos 51b).

## Distinctions

### Mitzrayim (מצרים)
First a person: a son of Cham (Bereishis 10:6, Divrei HaYamim I 1:8), father of
Ludim, Anamim, Lehavim, Naftuchim, Patrusim, and Kasluchim, the last two of whom
begot the Plishtim and Kaftorim (Bereishis 10:13 to 14). Then the land. Rabbi
Elazar Rokeach of Worms adds a sign: the pasuk of Makkas Bechoros (Shemos 12:23)
holds every letter of the alef-beis but tes, whose value is nine, for the nine
families struck.

### Patros (פתרוס), Ludim (לודים), Anamim (ענמים), Lehavim (להבים)
*Patros* appears seven times, five for the land of Egypt (Yeshayahu 11:11,
Yirmiyahu 44:1 and 44:15, Yechezkel 29:14 and 30:14) and twice for the Patrusim
(Bereishis 10:14, Divrei HaYamim I 1:12). Rabbi Aharon Marcus: in Egyptian, the
land of the south, Upper Egypt, since the Nile flows northward. Yanai's *Makkas
Bechorei Pasros* uses it for Egypt at large, which Rabbi Klein, finding no deeper
reason, treats as poetic latitude; HaKallir does the same with *Lud*, and *Yom
L'Yabashah* calls the Egyptians *Anamim*. Rashi (to 10:13) connects *Lehavim* to
*lahav*, a flame or blade; Ibn Gabirol's *Azharos* reads "I redeemed you from
Lahav", and in some versions "from Rahav".

### Rahav (רהב), Matzor (מצור), and eretz Cham (ארץ חם)
*Rahav* as a name for Egypt: Rashi to Bereishis 40:23, Chizkuni to 43:32, Radak
to Tehillim 87:4; in *divrei Chazal* Rahav is the *sar* of Egypt and of the sea
(Bava Basra 74b, *Bamidbar Rabbah* 18:22). The word means confident, strong,
haughty, which suits the Egyptian character, and Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads it as
a permutation of *Lahav*. *Matzor* is Radak's singular of the name (*Sefer
HaShorashim*, and to Melachim II 19:24, Yeshayahu 19:6, 37:25), which other
*meforshim* read as the ordinary noun for straits. *Eretz Cham* is Tehillim's
name (105:23, 105:27, 106:22), with *admas bnei Cham* in the weekday Maariv and
*malchus eglah* in *Maoz Tzur*; *Kaftor* (Amos 9:7, Yirmiyahu 47:4) is the
Plishtim's homeland, a related place and not Egypt.

## Rendering guidance

- In a piyyut, keep the poet's word and gloss it: "the firstborn of Patros" with
  a note that Patros is Egypt. The same for *Lud*, *Anamim*, *Lahav*, and
  *Rahav*; flattening them deletes the poem's device.
- Trap: English "Egypt" will absorb *Mitzrayim*, *Patros*, *Matzor*, and *Rahav*
  at once. Reserve "Egypt" for *Mitzrayim* and transliterate the rest.
- *Mitzrayim* in Shemos 14 may be the nation or the man; translate "the
  Egyptians" and put *Midrash Sechel Tov* in a note. In a frum text keep
  *Mitzrayim*, *Mitzrim*, and *Mitzri* transliterated for the people.
