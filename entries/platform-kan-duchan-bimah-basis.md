# Platform: kan / duchan / bimah / basis / itztaba

## Bottom line

*Kan* is the Biblical word: a **base**, **stand** or **pedestal**, and by
extension a man's **post**. *Duchan* is Mishnaic, the **platform** the Leviim
sang from and, in the Gemara, the platform the Kohanim bless from (hence the
Yiddish *duchenen*); it is a noun, not a verb. *Bimah* is the **stage** or
**dais**, and *basis* the **base** of a vessel, both Mishnaic. *Itztaba* is a
raised **platform** or **colonnade** in the Beis HaMikdash. The trap is
translating *duchan* as an action; a Kohen goes up *to* the *duchan*.

## Distinctions

### kan (כן, sometimes kein)
The base, foundation, stand or pedestal on which something rests. It is the
word for the base of the *kiyor* in the Mishkan (Shemos 30:18, 30:28, 31:9,
35:16, 38:8, 39:39, 40:11; Vayikra 8:11, which the article prints as 8:111),
and it serves abstractly for a man's position: Yosef told the butler he would
be restored *al kanecha*, to your post (Bereishis 40:13, fulfilled at 41:13).
In this sense the word appears seventeen times in Tanach, on Even Shoshan's
concordance. In the Mishnah it is the golden *kan* on which the Kohen Gadol set
the vessel of blood on Yom Kippur (Yoma 5:3-4). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Cheshek
Shlomo*) traces it to the two-letter root כ-נ, base or basis, physical or
abstract, and gathers under it *kein* (yes) and *nachon* (correct), which
affirm that a thing has a valid footing, and *tochnis* (plan) and *hachanah*
(preparation), the footing of any serious undertaking. He derives *kohen* from
the same root, the one charged with seeing that the preparations for the avodah
are in order, and *kavanah*, the mental preparation before a task.

### duchan (דוכן)
Not in Tanach. In the Mishnah it is three times the platform the Leviim stood
on for the shirah: six Leviim at least on the *duchan* (Arachin 2:6); its
location on the cubit-high step between the Ezras Kohanim and the Ezras
Yisrael, itself three steps of half a cubit each (Middos 2:6); an ancestor who
stood on the *duchan* as proof of unblemished lineage (Kiddushin 4:5). The
Gemara keeps that sense (Taanis 29a and Arachin 11b, where the Leviim were
still singing on the *duchan* when the Babylonians broke in; also Yoma 15b,
Sukkah 51a, Megillah 24b, Sotah 48a, Arachin 11a) and adds a second: the
platform the Kohanim stand on for Birkas Kohanim, even outside the Mikdash
(Shabbos 118b, Rosh Hashanah 28b and 31b, Sotah 38b and 40a, Menachos 44a,
Kiddushin 71a). Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) already had to say
that *duchan* is a noun and not a verb. Rabbi Yaakov Emden (*Ezer Ohr*, his
glosses to *Sefer HaTishbi*): *duchan* is a cognate of the Aramaic *duchta*,
place, a designated place for Kohanim and Leviim to stand. Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch
Ferber (*Kerem Tzvi* to Bamidbar 6:23) gives two other readings: a compound of
a Greek *du*, double, with the Hebrew *kan*, since the platform is twice the
width of the steps up to it; or from the root ד-ו-כ, to crush or flatten, since
those standing on so small a platform must press together. Rabbi Baruch HaLevi
Epstein (*Degel HaTorah* §11) reports that a Rosh Yeshiva was once called a
*Reish Duchana*, from his lectern; Rabbi Menachem Kasher (*Torah Shleimah* vol.
26 p. 289) corrects him, since at the one place the term appears (Bava Basra
21a) Rashi explains it as a teacher's assistant who drilled the children in
what the melamed had taught.

### bimah (בימה)
Not in Tanach either. The Mishnah has it twice: the *bimah* built for the king
to read from at Hakhel (Sotah 7:8), and the ruling that a Jew may not build a
*bimah* for idolaters (Avodah Zarah 1:7). It is the ordinary word for the
platform in the middle of the shul on which the Torah is read. Rashi (Sukkah
51b, Avodah Zarah 16a, Sotah 41a) glosses it into Old French as *almemar*, the
form German Jewry still uses (see also Rambam's commentary to Keilim 2:3).

### basis (בסיס)
A loanword, in the Mishnah once, for the base of a candelabrum (Keilim 11:7).
Its halachic career is larger: *basis l'davar ha'assur*, a permitted object
serving as the base for a forbidden one and thereby becoming forbidden itself
(Shabbos 47a, 117a, 120b, 125b, 142a; Pesachim 83a; Chullin 125).

### itztaba (אצטבא)
Four times in the Mishnah, three of them in the Mikdash: the two loaves set on
the *itztaba* to mark the times for stopping and burning chametz on Erev Pesach
(Pesachim 1:5); the parochet spread on the roof of the *itztaba* to dry after
tevilah, so the people could see it (Shekalim 8:4); the lulavim placed on the
roof of the *itztaba* before Shabbos (Sukkah 4:4). The fourth is the *itztaba*
of a bathhouse (Niddah 9:3). Rashi (to Taanis 29a and Arachin 11b) defines
*duchan* by *itztaba*, taking the two as near-synonyms; Rabbi Ernest Klein
gives it a wider range, colonnade, portico, balcony, shelf, or a bench fixed to
a wall.

## Rendering guidance

- *Kan*: **base** or **stand** for an object, **post** or **position** for a
  person. Bereishis 40:13 is "restore you to your post," not "to your base."
- *Duchan*: **platform**, or leave it transliterated where the Levi'im's
  *duchan* or Birkas Kohanim is the subject. Never render *duchenen* as
  "to duchan"; write **to go up for Birkas Kohanim**.
- *Bimah*: **bimah** in a shul, **platform** or **dais** in the Mishnah's
  Hakhel and avodah zarah cases.
- *Basis*: **base**, and in halachah leave *basis l'davar ha'assur* standing.
- *Itztaba*: **platform** in the Mikdash contexts; do not import "colonnade"
  unless the passage describes columns.
- Trap: English "platform" absorbs *duchan*, *bimah* and *itztaba*, and "base"
  absorbs *kan* and *basis*. Rashi's gloss of *duchan* as *itztaba* is a
  statement about two words, so keep both visible: "*duchan*, that is, an
  *itztaba*, a raised platform."
- Second trap: *bimah* and *bamah* are not the same word and must not be
  rendered alike. A *bamah* is a high place of worship; a *bimah* is furniture.
