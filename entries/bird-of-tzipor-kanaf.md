# Bird: of / tzipor / kanaf

## Bottom line

*Of* is the broad word, **fowl** or anything that flies. *Tzipor* is the
narrower **bird**, and what narrows it is disputed: every bird as against
insects (Radak), only a kosher bird (Sifri and Rambam), or the small chirping
songbird that greets the morning (Ramban). The Ramban names the *tzipor* for
*tzafra*, the morning; Rabbi Aharon Marcus (*Keses HaSofer*) runs it the other
way, *tzipor* from its chirping and *tzafra* named for the birds. *Kanaf* is literally **winged
creature**, the wing itself doing the naming, and it reaches to grasshoppers and
other winged insects. All three stand in one pasuk, Bereishis 7:14, where Rashi
reads *kol kanaf* as including what *kol tzipor* had not covered.

## Where the authorities disagree

**What narrows tzipor.**

- Radak: any bird, as against insects.
- The Sifri and Rambam: only a kosher bird.
- Ramban: the small songbird that chirps at morning.
- Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok joins the last two: the weaker,
  non-predatory bird that trusts another to feed it.

**Which way the name runs.**

- Ramban (to Vayikra 14:4): *tziporim* are named for *tzafra*, the morning, when
  they rouse and chirp.
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus (*Keses HaSofer* to Bereishis 7:14): the reverse. *Tzipor*
  is named for the *tziftzuf*, chirping, of most kinds, and the morning is
  called *tzafra* for them.

## Distinctions

### of (עוף)
Root ע-ו-ף, which also gives the verb "flies."

- Radak (to Bereishis): the general term for any flying creature, with *tzipor*
  and *kanaf* as classes under it.
- Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok adds a character to it, on the model of the
  Gemara's own split between *behemah gasah* and *behemah dakah*: an *of* is a
  bird with strong wings, even if it is physically small, typically a predator,
  restless because it depends on nothing but itself and is always hunting its
  next meal.
- Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 38:17): *of* is the
  source of Latin *avis*, and he discusses the interchange of vav and peh.

### tzipor (ציפור)
Root צ-פ-ר. Three readings, and the Tosafos Yom Tov notes the dispute:
1. Radak (commentary to Bereishis and *Sefer HaShorashim*): any type of bird.
   The Gemara seems to suggest as much, and Chullin 139b gives it some Talmudic
   precedent.
2. Sifri, followed by Rambam in his commentary to the Mishnah: *tzipor* is
   specifically a kosher bird, while *of* covers all birds. This, Rambam explains, is how the
   Mishnah (Chullin 12:2) knows that *shiluach hakein* (Devarim 22:6-7) applies
   only to a kosher bird, since the pasuk there says *tzipor*.
3. Ramban (to Vayikra 14:4), who also rejects Radak but narrows the word
   differently: small birds that wake in the morning and chirp, the word coming
   from Aramaic *tzafra*, "morning."

Rabbi Chanoch Zundel joins the last two. A *tzipor* is the flimsier, weaker
bird, generally not a predator, content with whatever man or nature hands it; because it
trusts another to feed it, it can sleep at night and wake to sing in the morning.
That is exactly Ramban's dawn-chirper, and it is exactly the Mishnah's kashrus
criterion: the Torah simply lists the non-kosher birds (Vayikra 11:13-19), and it
is the Mishnah (Chullin 3:6) that reveals the criterion, that any predatory bird
is non-kosher. So Ramban's *tzipor* and Rambam's kosher *tzipor* turn out to
describe the same creature.
The *mussar* he draws: one who trusts that Hashem provides can rest; one who
believes only in his own hands is never at ease.

Rabbi Aharon Marcus (*Keses HaSofer* to Bereishis 7:14): *tzipor* is named for
the *tziftzuf*, chirping, of most kinds, and the morning is called *tzafra* in
Aramaic for them, since they rouse the dawn with chirping. This seems the
reverse of the Ramban (to Vayikra 14:4), for whom *tziporim* are named for
*tzafra*.

### kanaf (כנף)
Root כ-נ-ף. Literally "wing," and used for a bird only by way of its wings, so
its focus is the wing rather than the creature.

- Rashi to Bereishis 7:14: the clause *kol kanaf* comes to include every winged
  thing, grasshoppers among them, which *kol tzipor* had not covered.
- Radak makes it the class term for winged insects: grasshoppers, wasps, flies.

Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 1:21 and 7:14, Vayikra 19:11), on the interchange of
kaf with gimmel and of peh with beis, compares *kanaf* with *ganav*, thief: both
involve hiding or concealing, since a wing covers part of the bird's body just
as a thief steals in a hidden way; alternatively, the wing lets the bird soar so
high that it is hidden from plain sight, as the thief steals when nobody is
looking.

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 7:14, כל עוף למינהו כל צפור כל כנף: keep the three distinct.
  "Every *of* after its kind, every bird, every winged thing" carries Rashi's
  inclusion; "every bird... every bird... every bird" does not.
- In a *shiluach hakein* passage, *tzipor* is load-bearing for the halachah.
  Render it "bird" and gloss the Sifri and Rambam reading, or write "kosher
  bird" outright if the passage is deriving the din.
- Where the sense is the dawn songbird (Ramban), "songbird" or "small bird"
  beats plain "bird," especially in a poetic or *mussar* passage that leans on
  the bird's trust and its morning song.
- Trap: English "bird" will absorb both *of* and *tzipor*. Keep "fowl" for *of*
  in a Torah-legal setting (*of tahor*, *of tamei*), and reserve "bird" for
  *tzipor*. For *kanaf*, "winged creature" is worth the extra word, since the
  whole point is that it is not restricted to birds.
