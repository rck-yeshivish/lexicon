# Bird: of / tzipor / kanaf

## Bottom line

*Of* is the broad word, **fowl** or anything that flies. *Tzipor* is the
narrower **bird**, and what narrows it is disputed: every bird as against
insects (Radak), only a kosher bird (Sifri and Rambam), or the small chirping
songbird that greets the morning (Ramban). *Kanaf* is literally **winged
creature**, the wing itself doing the naming, and it reaches to grasshoppers and
other winged insects. The trap is Bereishis 7:14, where all three stand in one
pasuk: an English rendering that writes "bird" three times destroys the very
inclusion Rashi reads there.

## Distinctions

### of (עוף)
Root ע-ו-ף, which also gives the verb "flies." Radak (to Bereishis): the general
term for any flying creature, with *tzipor* and *kanaf* as classes under it.
Rabbi Chanoch Zundel of Bialystok adds a character to it: an *of* is a bird with
strong wings, typically a predator, restless because it depends on nothing but
itself and is always hunting its next meal.

### tzipor (ציפור)
Root צ-פ-ר. Three readings, and the Tosafos Yom Tov notes the dispute:
1. Radak (commentary to Bereishis and *Sefer HaShorashim*), with support in
   Chullin 139b: any bird at all, kosher or not.
2. Sifri, followed by Rambam in his commentary to the Mishnah: specifically a
   kosher bird. This is how the Mishnah (Chullin 12:2) knows that *shiluach
   hakein* (Devarim 22:6 and 22:7) applies only to a kosher bird, since the pasuk
   there says *tzipor*.
3. Ramban (to Vayikra 14:4): small birds that wake at dawn and chirp, the word
   coming from Aramaic *tzafra*, "morning."

Rabbi Chanoch Zundel joins the last two. A *tzipor* is the flimsier, weaker
bird, not a predator, content with whatever man or nature hands it; because it
trusts another to feed it, it can sleep at night and wake to sing in the morning.
That is exactly Ramban's dawn-chirper, and it is exactly the Mishnah's kashrus
criterion, since Chullin 3:6 disqualifies every predatory bird. So Ramban's
*tzipor* and Rambam's kosher *tzipor* turn out to describe the same creature.
The *mussar* he draws: one who trusts that Hashem provides can rest; one who
believes only in his own hands is never at ease.

### kanaf (כנף)
Root כ-נ-ף. Literally "wing," and used for a bird only by way of its wings, so
its focus is the wing rather than the creature. Rashi to Bereishis 7:14: the
clause *kol kanaf* comes to include every winged thing, grasshoppers among them,
which *kol tzipor* had not covered. Radak makes it the class term for winged
insects: grasshoppers, wasps, flies.

## Rendering guidance

- Bereishis 7:14, כל עוף למינהו כל צפור כל כנף: keep the three distinct.
  "Every *of* after its kind, every bird, every winged thing" carries Rashi's
  inclusion; "every bird... every bird... every bird" does not.
- In a *shiluach hakein* passage, *tzipor* is load-bearing for the halachah.
  Render it "bird" and gloss the Sifri and Rambam reading, or write "kosher
  bird" outright if the passage is deriving the din.
- Where the sense is the dawn songbird (Ramban), "songbird" or "small bird"
  beats plain "bird," especially in a poetic or *mussar* passage that leans on
  the bird's trust and its morning song.
- Trap: English "bird" will absorb both *of* and *tzipor*. Keep "fowl" for *of*
  in a Torah-legal setting (*of tahor*, *of tamei*), and reserve "bird" for
  *tzipor*. For *kanaf*, "winged creature" is worth the extra word, since the
  whole point is that it is not restricted to birds.
