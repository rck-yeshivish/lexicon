# Outer garment: aderes / glima / itztala / tachrich

## Bottom line

*Aderes* is the biblical word: a **mantle**, a large outer garment marking the
wearer's stature. *Glima* is its Aramaic counterpart in the Gemara, an
**unfitted robe**, a flat sheet of cloth rather than a tailored garment.
*Itztala* is a **ceremonial cloak**, an outer garment tied to office and rank.
*Tachrich* is a **wrap**, and in the plural *tachrichin* the burial shrouds.
Rashi glosses all three of the first as *tallis*, so the trap is treating them
as identical: *glima* stresses the garment's shapelessness, *itztala* the
wearer's standing.

## Distinctions

### aderes (אדרת)
Eleven occurrences in Tanach. Eisav is born "ruddy, entirely like a hairy
*aderes*" (Bereishis 25:25). Achan steals a Babylonian *aderes* from the
consecrated spoil of Yericho (Yehoshua 7:21, 7:24). It is above all Eliyahu
HaNavi's garment: he hides his face in it at Chorev (Melachim I 19:13), he is
known as a hairy man on its account (Melachim II 1:8; the article prints 2:8),
he splits the Yarden with it (Melachim II 2:8), and Elisha takes up the *aderes*
that fell from him as the sign of the succession (Melachim II 2:13-14).

Rashi (Zechariah 13:4) and Ibn Janach and Radak in their respective *Sifrei
HaShorashim* define *aderes* as a *tallis*, a shawl. Radak ties it to the root
aleph-dalet-reish, *adir*, greatness and power: the *aderes* displays its
wearer's importance. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim treats the opening aleph and
closing tav as accretions and traces the word to the two-letter element
dalet-reish, freedom from restraint, whose family he gives as *dror*
(emancipation), *dardar* (a thorn growing wild on ownerless ground), *dirah* (a
home, where a man may act as he wishes), *dor* (a generation, the span of one's
residence in this world), *medurah* (a bonfire spreading unchecked), *doher* (a
horse's neighing when let out to roam), *neder* (an obligation freely
self-imposed), and *adir* (unrestrained power). He offers two ways to fit the
*aderes* into this: either it hangs loosely on the body, unlike fitted clothing,
or only the *adirim* wear it.

Targum Onkelos renders *aderes* at Bereishis 25:25 as *gelem*, cognate to
*glima*; Targum Yonasan at Yehoshua 7:21 and 7:24 renders it *itztala*.

### glima (גלימא)
Frequent in the Gemara for an outer garment, often formal. It stands in the
*kesubah*, where the husband pledges even "the *glima* on my shoulders," and it
names the gold coat of the Rishon LeTzion today (on which garments qualify, see
Rabbi Yosef Chaim of Baghdad, *Torah Lishmah* 327). The root is the biblical
gimmel-lamed-mem, which occurs three times (Melachim II 2:8; Tehillim 139:16;
Yechezkel 27:24) in the sense of wrapping or of unformed matter, and which gives
*golem*, the Mishnah's opposite of the *chacham* (*Avos* 5:7), a man not yet
formed.

Shabbos 77b expounds *glima* as "made like a *golem*." Rashi there: a garment
not cut to the shape of the limbs but essentially a flat sheet covering the
whole body, unfitted and therefore shapeless beside tailored clothing. Rabbi
Nosson of Rome (*Sefer HeAruch*) reads it the other way: a man wrapped up in a
*glima* is fixed in place and unable to move freely, and so resembles a *golem*.

Rashi's own glosses vary. Once he defines *glima* as *aderes* (Shabbos 10a),
once as *sudar* (Bava Metzia 47a), once as *tachrichin*, where the sugya
concerns a corpse's clothing (Shabbos 150b), but in the great majority of places
as *tallis* (Shabbos 110b, 138b; Eruvin 94a, 96b, 102b; Kesubos 79b; Bava Kama
119b; Bava Metzia 28a, 114b; Bava Basra 128a; Sanhedrin 7a; Chullin 127a).

Rabbi David Golumb (*Targumna* to Bereishis 49:26) and Rav Hirsch (Vayikra
13:59; Tehillim 139:16) connect *glima* to *klimah*, humiliation, through the
interchange of gimmel and kaf: as a *golem* lacks shape, so the humiliated man
feels stripped of his form, part of his humanity taken from him. Rabbi Golumb
notes the parallel to Chazal's reading of *levush* as *lo bushah*, no shame.

### itztala (איצטלא)
An outer garment worn as a mark of status. Targum renders Achan's Babylonian
*aderes* by it, and Rav (Sanhedrin 44a) specifies that what Achan coveted was a
silk *itztala*; Rashi there (and at Bechoros 34b) glosses it *tallis*. It
appears in the Mishnah at *Gittin* 6:5 as something a husband might demand as
the condition of a *get*. In the Gemara it is nearly always a status garment: a
silk *itztala* written into a woman's *kesubah* (Yevamos 66b; Bava Metzia 17a),
and the rule that where one litigant stands in rags and the other in a costly
*itztala*, the court tells the wealthy one either to dress like his opponent or
to clothe his opponent like himself (Shevuos 31a). Hence the idiom *itztala
d'rabbanan*, the cloak of the sages, standing for the honor of rabbinic
authority.

*Sefer HeAruch* notes that the word is also spelled with a samech, *istala*; so
Targum to Esther 7:6, where Esther charges Haman with wanting to wear the king's
*istala*, and Targum to Bereishis 45:22, where Yosef's gifts to his brothers are
*istalvan*. Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Meturgeman*) already noted the
inconsistency, which follows from the word being a loanword rather than native.

### tachrich (תכריך)
Mordechai is dressed in a *tachrich* of *butz* and *argaman* (Esther 8:15). The
root kaf-reish-kaf means to wrap or surround, and gives Rabbinic *krach*, a
walled city, and Modern Hebrew *karich*, a wrap. Targum to Esther 8:15 renders
*tachrich* as *glima*, another garment one wraps around oneself. In Rabbinic
Hebrew the plural *tachrichin* is the standing term for burial shrouds.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: "cloak" or "robe" will absorb *aderes*, *glima*, and *itztala* at once,
  and Rashi's *tallis* gloss makes the collapse tempting. Where a passage
  contrasts them, use "mantle" for *aderes*, "robe" for *glima*, and "cloak of
  office" or "ceremonial cloak" for *itztala*.
- *Aderes* in the Eliyahu narratives is "mantle," and English already carries
  the transfer of authority in "assume the mantle," which matches Elisha taking
  it up at Melachim II 2:13-14. Do not weaken it to "coat."
- *Itztala d'rabbanan* is figurative. Render it "the mantle of the sages" or
  leave it transliterated and gloss it; "the sages' robe" reads as a literal
  garment and loses the point.
- *Tachrich* singular is "wrap" (Esther 8:15); *tachrichin* is "shrouds." Never
  carry the shroud sense back into the Esther pasuk.
- Where *glima* and *golem* appear in the same sugya (Shabbos 77b), keep the
  wordplay: "a *glima* is made like a *golem*," with a gloss on the unformed
  sense, rather than translating both away.
- *Aderes* rendered *tallis* by the *rishonim* is a definition, not a
  translation for a modern reader; writing "prayer shawl" for Eliyahu's *aderes*
  is an anachronism.
