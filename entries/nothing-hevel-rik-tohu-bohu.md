# Nothing: hevel / rik / tohu / bohu (efes, blimah, meumah, klum)

## Bottom line

*Hevel* is **futility** or **vanity**: a thing that really exists but yields no
benefit, and whose apparent worth proves illusory. *Rik* is **emptiness** or
**for naught**: no substance at all, and in practice the word for labor that
produces nothing. *Efes* is the absolute **there is none besides**. *Tohu* and
*bohu* are the pre-Creation pair, best kept as **formless and void** with the
commentary carrying the reading you follow, since the *rishonim* divide sharply
over which is matter and which is form. *Meumah* and *klum* are **anything** in a
negative construction, not "nothing" as a standalone noun. Bedersi and Shadal put
*hevel* below *rik* in intensity; Malbim reverses them. Follow the majority
(Bedersi and Shadal) unless the passage is Malbim's own.

## Distinctions

### hevel (הבל)
Rabbi Avraham Bedersi (*Chosam Tochnis*) works from word order: where two
near-synonyms stand together Tanach lists them from weaker to stronger, and the
one place *hevel* and *rik* are adjacent (Yeshayahu 30:7; cf. 49:4) has *hevel*
first, as does *Aleinu*. So *hevel* is a thing that is (*yesh*) but has no
*to'eles*, while *rik* does not even exist. Shadal: *hevel* is specifically what
looks advantageous on the surface and is finally shown to be worthless, as beauty
is *hevel* (Mishlei 31:30). On that reading Shadal explains the name Hevel
(Bereishis 4:2): Adam thought him a founder of the righteous, and he was killed
before fathering a child; Shadal infers that the name was given in retrospect,
which is why Tanach does not report Chavah naming him as it does for Kayin
(4:1) and Sheis (4:25). Malbim inverts the pair: *hevel* is total nothingness of
no value whatever, and *rik* is what carries surface value until one looks
deeper.

### rik (ריק)
Shadal: *rik* is toiling hard for a result that comes to nothing, as in the
*tochachah*, "you shall sow your seed *larik*, for your enemies will eat it"
(Vayikra 26:16). It repeatedly stands beside *yegiah*, toil (Yeshayahu 49:4,
65:23; Iyov 39:16), and that pairing carries into *Uva LeTzion*, that we not
toil *larik*. Targum Onkelos renders *bohu* of Bereishis 1:2 as *reikanya*, an
Aramaic form of *reik*.

### efes (אפס)
*Aleinu*: "our King is true, *efes zulaso*," there is nothing besides Him. The
series names *efes* among the words for "nothing" but does not set it against
*hevel* and *rik*; do not manufacture a distinction it does not draw.

### tohu (תהו)
Twenty appearances in Tanach, eleven of them in Yeshayahu; only twice in Chumash,
Bereishis 1:2 and Devarim 32:10, where Rashi has the Jews trusting Hashem into a
place of *tohu*, the wilderness. Yeshayahu: Hashem did not create the world for
*tohu* but to be settled (45:18), and idolatry is *tohu* (41:29, 44:9, 59:4).
Readings: Rashi and *Sefer HaBahir* 2 read *tohu* as astonishment, what a man
would feel seeing sheer nothingness; hence *toheh al harishonos* (Kiddushin 40b),
regretting one's good deeds, and the Aramaic *tahi bah* that introduces a
bewildered question (Berachos 38b, Eruvin 66a, and often). Ramban (to Bereishis
1:1), following the *Zohar* (Bereishis 16a) and Rabbi Avraham bar Chiyya HaNasi
(*Higayon HaNefesh*): *tohu* is matter (*chomer*), *bohu* is form (*tzurah*); a
name attaches only to what has both, so *tohu*, matter without form, cannot be
named, and one encountering it could only wonder (*toheh*) what to call it.
Ralbag (to 1:2) reverses this, *tohu* as form and *bohu* as matter, and Abarbanel
rejects Ralbag. Rabbi Nissim of Marseilles reads both words of the pre-Creation
moment when all was concentrated at the center: *tohu* is the man frozen in place
by wondering, before Hashem set the world expanding. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
takes the hei and vav as additions and the root as the single letter tav: an
outline whose inner essence is not yet filled in, which fits the Gemara's *tohu*
as the green line circumscribing the world (Chagigah 12a, on Yeshayahu 34:11).
Shadal: *tohu* is pointless and brings no benefit. Rabbi Avraham bar Chiyya
derives *tehom*, the sea depths, from *tohu*, a shapeless disordered region.

### bohu (בהו)
Only twice more in Tanach beyond Bereishis 1:2 (Yeshayahu 34:11; Yirmiyahu 4:23).
Targum Onkelos: *reikanya*, empty. Rashi likewise reads it with *reik*. *Sefer
HaBahir* 2: *bohu* has some minimal tangibility and is not wholly abstract.
Rabbi Nissim of Marseilles: a contraction of *bo* and *hu*, everything still
inside the center. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: root the single letter beis,
"inside," so *bohu* is an outer structure whose interior still has to be filled
in, matching the Gemara's *bohu* as the wet stones from which water issues
(Chagigah 12a). Shadal: *bohu* is worse than pointless, it is actually bad.
Compare *Sefer HaBahir* 11-12, which links *tohu* with evil and *bohu* with
peace, and 135 and 163, where *tohu*'s evil is that it makes a man wonder and
second-guess himself into sin. Targum Yonasan and Targum Neofiti simply
Aramaicise both as *tahya* and *bahya*. Rabbi Moshe Botarel cites one Rabbi Levi
ben Shlomo of Lunel for *tohu* as unshaped matter destined for shape and *bohu*
as shape, and for *tohu* as absence against *bohu* as presence; the column itself
cautions that Botarel is known to fabricate citations.

### blimah (בלימה)
A single appearance: Hashem "hangs the earth on *blimah*" (Iyov 26:7). Rabbi
Saadia Gaon, Ibn Ezra, Rabbi Moshe Kimchi (there) and Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*)
read it as *bli* plus *mah*, without anything, and it is sometimes written as two
words; Rabbi Avraham bar Chiyya (*Higayon HaNefesh*) makes it synonymous with
*tohu*. Rabbi Shabsai Donnolo (to *Sefer Yetzirah*): *bli* plus *meumah*, that
Hashem alone built without tools or materials. Derash: Chazal (Chullin 89a) take
it as one word from ב-ל-ם, to hold back (cognate at Tehillim 32:9), so the world
hangs on the merit of one who holds back from answering an insult; see Rabbeinu
Bachya to Devarim 33:27. *Sefer Yetzirah* calls the ten *sefiros* *blimah*,
either because they are nothing independent of Hashem or as a warning to hold
back from teaching esoterics to the uninitiated (Ramban to *Sefer Yetzirah*).

### meumah (מאומה) and klum (כלום)
*Meumah* means both "nothing" and "anything," because Hebrew permits the double
negative that English does not. Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras (*Ohalei
Yehudah*): a contraction of *mah u'mah*. Rabbi Ernest Klein cites that and
rejects it on technical grounds. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the root is the
biliteral mem-mem, the smallest amount, so *meumah* as "nothing" is really "not
even something" (Bereishis 30:31, 39:23; I Melachim 10:21), the equivalent of
Rabbinic *mah shehu* and *kol shehu*. *Klum* is the Mishnaic counterpart and
behaves the same way. Rabbi Binyamin Mussafia (*Musaf He'Aruch*): a contraction
of *kol me'um* (Daniel 1:4), cited approvingly by Rabbi Klein; Rabbi Yair Chaim
Bachrach (*Mar Kashisha*) disagrees without offering a replacement.

## Rendering guidance

- הבל: "futility," or "vanity" in Koheles where the register is fixed. Not
  "nothing," which loses Bedersi's point that the thing does exist.
- לריק / ריק of effort: "for naught," "in vain." ריק of a vessel or a place:
  "empty."
- תהו ובהו: "formless and void" is the safe pair. If the passage follows Ramban,
  say so in the commentary ("unformed matter and form") rather than forcing it
  into the body text, since Ralbag holds the reverse.
- Trap: English "nothing" will swallow *hevel*, *rik*, *efes*, *tohu* and *klum*
  at once. Where two of them stand in one phrase (*hevel varik* in *Aleinu*),
  render them apart: "futility and emptiness," in that order, since the order is
  Bedersi's whole evidence.
- Second trap, grammatical: לא ... מאומה and לא ... כלום are single negatives in
  Hebrew. Render "he did not have anything" or "he had nothing," never "he did
  not have nothing."
- Where a source darshens *blimah* as holding back (Chullin 89a), do not
  translate it "nothingness" in that line; the derashah needs "restraint."
