# Teeth: shein / mesalos (malteos) / kaka / nivi / gallal / chit

## Bottom line

*Shein* is the ordinary word for a **tooth**, and by extension **ivory** and the
"teeth" of a tool. *Mesalos* and *malteos* are one word with the lamed and tav
switched, and how they differ from *shein* is answered three ways: they are the
**molars**, the thick teeth at the back, against *shein* the front incisors
(Rabbi Tedeschi; Rashi, Radak and the *Biur HaGra* likewise make them the thick
teeth deeper in the mouth); they are the **fangs** protruding from a wild
animal's mouth (Ibn Parchon); or *shein* is the general term and they the
specific one, whether fangs, tusks or molars. *Kaka* is the Targum's Aramaic for
*shein*, *nivi* its Aramaic for *mesalos*, and Rabbi Klein finds the difference
between the Hebrew pair evident from that split. *Gallal* is "tooth" only as the
Gemara reads it, in context the tort category of tooth damage. *Chit*, in
Rabbinic literature only in one Mishnah and its Sifra parallel, is disputed: the
**gums** (Rashi) or the **teeth** themselves (the Mainz commentary ascribed to
Rabbeinu Gershom, and the *Aruch*). Do not render *mesalos* simply "teeth."

## Distinctions

### shein (שן)
Fifty-five occurrences in Tanach, mostly a tooth in the mouth of a man or an
animal. Extended to things made of **ivory**: the Ivory House (Melachim I 22:39,
Amos 3:15), Ivory Palace (Tehillim 45:9), Ivory Tower (Shir HaShirim 7:5), ivory
thrones (Melachim I 10:18, Divrei HaYamim II 9:17), ivory beds (Amos 6:4). In
the Mishnah, a person's or animal's tooth (Shabbos 6:5 and often), the tort
category of damage like that done by an animal eating with its tooth (Bava
Kamma 1:4, 2:2, 2:5), and the sharp protruding "teeth" of a tool: a sickle
(Chullin 1:2), a hoe, a fine-writer, a saw, a lock or key, a comb (Keilim 13:2-8
and elsewhere). The idiom is rich: an obviously pregnant woman has "her stomach
between her teeth" (Rosh Hashanah 2:8); "whitening one's teeth to his friend," a
smile, is better than giving him milk (Kesubos 111b); one in a sticky situation
has "placed his finger between his teeth" (Kesubos 71a); and the Haggadah bids
us "stun the teeth" of the wicked son.

Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, and Radak trace *shein* to the two-letter ש-ן
without explaining what else that string yields. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim gives
the two-letter root the sense "double," and gathers under it *shnayim* ("two"),
*shanah* ("repeat"), *shinun* ("sharpening," a repeated rubbing), *shoneh*
("change," since what is repeated inevitably changes), *sheinah* ("sleep," when
the mind goes over the day's thoughts a second time), *shanah* ("year"), and
*yashan* ("old"); *shein* belongs because most people grow two sets of teeth.
*Shoresh Yesha* argues that *shein* is more closely related to *shinun*:
chewing, like sharpening, is a repetitive motion, meant to break the food down.
Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche (1920 to 2016) links ש-ן to ס-נ (*sneh*,
thornbush), the thorns on a branch resembling sharp teeth.

### mesalos (מתלעות) and malteos (מלתעות)
Both always plural: *mesalos* at Iyov 29:17, Yoel 1:6, and Mishlei 30:14;
*malteos* at Tehillim 58:7. The commentators agree the two mean the same thing,
with the lamed and tav metathesized, and Menachem, Ibn Janach, and Radak
accordingly assign ת-ל-ע and ל-ת-ע with no difference in sense noted.

Rabbi Pappenheim disagrees with the classical lexicographers: the tav is not
part of the core, and both words go back to the two-letter ל-ע, "the organ of
swallowing," that is, the throat (*loe*, once in Tanach, Mishlei 23:2). The
teeth are named for their nearness to it. He puts *tola'as* ("worm") in the same
family, since worms consume their food as though swallowing without chewing,
and parses *bala* ("swallow") as ב-א ("enter") fused to ל-ע.

Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi Ashkenazi (1821 to 1898, *Otzar Nirdafim*): *shein* is
one of the eight incisors at the front, used for the first biting and cutting,
while *mesalos*/*malteos* are the ten molars at the back, used for grinding just
before swallowing. Rabbi Klein remarks that with Rabbi Pappenheim's throat root
this dovetails nicely, and that it is in consonance with Rashi (to Yoel 1:6 and
Mishlei 30:14), Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*), and the *Biur HaGra* (to Mishlei
31:9), who all explain the words as thick teeth set deeper in the mouth than a
*shein*. Rabbi Tedeschi further parses *mesalos* as a fusion of ת-ל
("hanging," a tooth hangs onto the gums) with ל-ע ("swallowing"), the shared
lamed written once. He notes an affinity, by metathesis and a tav-tet exchange,
with ל-ע-ט, to guzzle or gulp, as in *haliteini* (Bereishis 25:30, as his
teacher Shadal notes there) and the Mishnah's *malitin*, force-feeding (Shabbos
24:3), and relates these roots as well to ל-ע-ס, to chew, in Rabbinic Hebrew
(Shabbos 19:2, Pesachim 2:7, Niddah 9:7).

Rabbi Shlomo Ibn Parchon (twelfth century, *Machberes HaAruch*) instead makes
*mesalos*/*malteos* especially long teeth protruding from a wild animal's mouth,
that is, fangs. A third way of differentiating, given without an author: *shein*
is the general term for teeth of every kind, and *mesalos*/*malteos* the
specific one, whether fangs, tusks, or molars.

### kaka (ככא) and nivi (ניבי)
The Targumim consistently render *shein* into Aramaic as *kaka* (e.g. Tehillim
3:8, 35:16, 58:7; Iyov 19:20, 29:17) or Aramaicize it as *shina* and otherwise
leave it untranslated (e.g. Shemos 21:24; Mishlei 10:26, 30:14; Yoel 1:6; Amos
4:6), but they render *mesalos*/*malteos* as *nivi* (Mishlei 30:14, Iyov 29:17,
Yoel 1:6, Tehillim 58:7). Rabbi Klein finds the difference between the Hebrew
words evident from the split. In the Gemara *kaka* is a tooth (Pesachim 113a,
Yoma 84a), and when Rava told Bar Hedya that he dreamed his *shein* and *kaka*
all fell out (Berachos 56a), Rashi explains *kaka* there as the inner teeth,
molars, implying *shein* is the outer ones. Tosafos (to Avodah Zarah 28a,
Gittin 69a) dispute the notion that *kaka* could mean the gums and read it as
large teeth, again molars. In a borrowed sense *kaka* is also the "teeth" of a
wooden key (Shabbos 89b).

*Nivi* in the Gemara is not readily understood: on Chullin 59a, where an
animal's kosher status is read from its *shein* and *nivi*, Rashi gives two
explanations, either two teeth that stand out from the rest (possibly, Rabbi
Klein wonders, fangs) or the indentations in the gums where teeth would have
been expected. Tosafos there favor the first; the Shulchan Aruch (Yoreh Deah
79:1) cites both (see also Rashi to Shabbos 63b and Bava Kamma 83a). (The
Biblical Hebrew *niv* of Yeshayahu 57:19 and Malachi 1:12 is associated with the
mouth too, but means speech.)

### gallal (גלל)
Achiyah HaShiloni foretold the destruction of the House of Yeravam, that they
would be consumed "as the *gallal* consumes until it is wholly gone" (Melachim I
14:10), and Bava Kamma 2b takes *gallal* as "tooth," in context any damage like
that done by the tooth. Three explanations of the connection: Rabbeinu Chananel
(cited by Tosafos there) reads *gallal* as something rock hard (Ezra 5:8, 6:5),
and teeth are hard; Rashi (to Bava Kamma 3a) ties it to *gilui*, revealing, a
body part sometimes shown and sometimes hidden; Rashi alternatively takes
*gallal* in its plain sense of dung, teeth being among the body's tools for
turning food into refuse.

### chit (חיט)
The Mishnah (Bechoros 6:4) and the Sifra (*Emor*, *parshasa* 7:12) count as a
blemish in a *bechor* an outer *chit* that is deficient or cut, or an inner
*chit* wholly uprooted, but not an inner *chit* merely deficient (Bechoros
6:12). Rashi (to Bechoros 35a): the gums, the outer *chit* being where the
incisors belong and the inner where the molars belong. The Mainz commentary
ascribed to Rabbeinu Gershom (there) reads *chit* as the teeth themselves,
incisors and molars respectively, and Rabbi Nosson of Rome (1035 to 1106) adopts
that reading in *Sefer HaAruch*. The Rambam (to Bechoros 6:4) and others have a
different girsa, *chut*, with a vav.

Until very recently *chit* was attested nowhere in Tanach or Rabbinic literature
save that one Mishnah and its Midrash. In 2022 a group of archaeologists led by
Dr. Daniel Vainstub of Ben-Gurion University reported in the *Jerusalem Journal
of Archaeology* an ivory comb from Lachish with a Canaanite inscription
reconstructed as "May this *chit* root out the lice of the hair and the beard."
Rabbi Klein explains that the comb is called *chit* both because it is ivory, an
elephant's "tooth," and because a comb has "teeth," and takes the find as
evidence that Rabbinic Hebrew preserved ancient Semitic words that predate the
Bible yet do not appear in it.

On the etymology: Dr. Alexander Kohut (*HaAruch HaShaleim*) derives *chit* from
ח-ט-ט, to dig or peck, the tooth digging into food and cutting it into smaller
digestible pieces. Rabbi Klein offers his own guess, that *chit* is related to
*chitah*, wheat, whose spikelets protrude from the stalk somewhat as teeth
protrude from the gums.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shein*: **tooth**, or **ivory** where the object is made of it. In the tort
  sugyos leave it transliterated, *shein*, since it names a category.
- *Mesalos* / *malteos*: **molars** following Rabbi Tedeschi, with Rashi, Radak
  and the Gra; **fangs** where the subject is a beast and Ibn Parchon's reading
  fits. Do not silently write "teeth."
- *Kaka* and *nivi* stay transliterated in a Gemara passage, glossed once from
  the Hebrew they translate; for *nivi* on Chullin 59a say which of Rashi's two
  explanations you follow.
- *Chit*: **gums** or **teeth**, whichever the posek in front of you follows;
  say which.
- Trap: English "teeth" covers *shein*, *mesalos*, *malteos*, *kaka*, *nivi*,
  and *gallal* alike. Wherever two of them stand in one pasuk or one sugya
  (Mishlei 30:14; Berachos 56a; Chullin 59a), the passage is drawing the line
  English erases. Give the general word "teeth" and the specific one "molars,"
  or transliterate the second.
