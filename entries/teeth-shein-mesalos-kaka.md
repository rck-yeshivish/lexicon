# Teeth: shein / mesalos (malteos) / kaka / nivi / gallal / chit

## Bottom line

*Shein* is the general word for a **tooth**, and by extension **ivory** and the
"teeth" of a tool. *Mesalos* and *malteos* (the same word with the lamed and tav
switched) are the **back teeth**, the thick grinding teeth near the throat, or
on a second reading the **fangs** of a wild animal; either way they are the
specific term against the general one. *Kaka* is the Targum's Aramaic for
*shein*, *nivi* its Aramaic for *mesalos*, and the split proves the Hebrew pair
is not two names for one thing. *Gallal* is "tooth" only as the Gemara's name
for a category of damage. *chit* is Mishnaic and disputed: the **gums** (Rashi)
or the **teeth** themselves (Rabbeinu Gershom). Do not render *mesalos* simply
"teeth"; the word is chosen against *shein* every time it appears.

## Distinctions

### shein (שן)
Fifty-five occurrences in Tanach, mostly a tooth in the mouth of a man or an
animal. Extended senses: **ivory**, in the Ivory House (Melachim I 22:39, Amos
3:15), Ivory Palace (Tehillim 45:9), ivory throne (Melachim I 10:18); in the
Mishnah, a tooth (Shabbos 6:5 and often), the tort category of damage done by an
animal eating (Bava Kamma 1:4), and the sharp protruding "teeth" of a sickle, a
hoe, a saw, a key, a comb (Chullin 1:2, Keilim 13:2-8). The idiom is rich:
"whitening one's teeth to his friend," a smile, is better than giving him milk
(Kesubos 111b).

Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, and Radak trace *shein* to the two-letter ש-ן without
explaining what else that string yields. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim gives the
two-letter root the sense "double," and gathers under it *shnayim* ("two"),
*shanah* ("repeat"), *shinun* ("sharpening," a repeated rubbing), *shoneh*
("change"), *sheinah* ("sleep"), *shanah* ("year"), and *yashan* ("old"); *shein*
belongs because most people grow two sets of teeth. *Shoresh Yesha* prefers to
tie *shein* to *shinun* directly: chewing, like sharpening, is a repeated motion
that wears something down. Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche links ש-ן to ס-נ
(*sneh*, thornbush), the thorns on a branch resembling sharp teeth.

### mesalos (מתלעות) and malteos (מלתעות)
Both always plural: *mesalos* at Iyov 29:17, Yoel 1:6, and Mishlei 30:14;
*malteos* at Tehillim 58:7. The commentators agree the two are one word with the
lamed and tav metathesized, and Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, and Radak accordingly
assign ת-ל-ע and ל-ת-ע with no difference in sense.

Rabbi Pappenheim dissents from the lexicographers: the tav is not part of the
core, and both words go back to the two-letter ל-ע, "the organ of swallowing,"
that is, the throat (*loe*, Mishlei 23:2). The teeth are named for their
nearness to it. He puts *tola'as* ("worm") in the same family, since worms
swallow rather than chew, and parses *bala* ("swallow") as ב-א ("enter") fused
to ל-ע.

Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim*) makes the distinction
explicit: *shein* is one of the eight incisors at the front, used for biting and
cutting, while *mesalos*/*malteos* are the ten molars at the back, used for
grinding just before swallowing, which fits Rabbi Pappenheim's root exactly. He
further parses *mesalos* as a fusion of ת-ל ("hanging," a tooth hangs in the
gums) with ל-ע ("swallowing"). Rashi (to Yoel 1:6 and Mishlei 30:14), Radak
(*Sefer HaShorashim*), and the *Biur HaGra* (to Mishlei 31:9) all define them as
thick teeth set deeper in the mouth than a *shein*. Ibn Parchon (*Machberes
HeAruch*) instead makes them the long teeth protruding from a wild animal's
mouth, that is, fangs. A third resolution: *shein* is the general term for teeth
of every kind, and *mesalos*/*malteos* the specific one, whether fangs, tusks,
or molars.

### kaka (ככא) and nivi (ניבי)
The Targumim render *shein* into Aramaic as *kaka* (Tehillim 3:8, 35:16, Iyov
19:20) or leave it as *shina*, but they render *mesalos*/*malteos* as *nivi*
(Mishlei 30:14, Iyov 29:17, Yoel 1:6, Tehillim 58:7). The consistent split is
itself evidence that the Hebrew words differ. In the Gemara *kaka* is a tooth
(Pesachim 113a, Yoma 84a), and when Rava dreamed his *shein* and *kaka* all fell
out (Berachos 56a), Rashi explains *kaka* as the inner teeth, molars, implying
*shein* is the outer ones. Tosafos (to Avodah Zarah 28a, Gittin 69a) reject the
suggestion that *kaka* means gums and read it as large teeth.

*Nivi* in the Gemara is less settled: on Chullin 59a, where an animal's kosher
status is read from its *shein* and *nivi*, Rashi gives two explanations, either
two teeth that stand out from the rest or the indentations in the gums where
teeth should have been. Tosafos there prefer the first; the Shulchan Aruch
(Yoreh Deah 79:1) cites both. (The Biblical Hebrew *niv* of Yeshayahu 57:19 and
Malachi 1:12 is speech, a different word.)

### gallal (גלל)
Achiyah HaShiloni foretold the end of the House of Yeravam, that they would be
consumed "as the *gallal* consumes until it is wholly gone" (Melachim I 14:10),
and Bava Kamma 2b takes *gallal* as "tooth," meaning the tort category. Three
explanations: Rabbeinu Chananel (cited by Tosafos) reads *gallal* as something
rock hard (Ezra 5:8, 6:5), and teeth are hard; Rashi (to Bava Kamma 3a) ties it
to *gilui*, revealing, a body part sometimes shown and sometimes hidden; Rashi
alternatively takes *gallal* in its plain sense of dung, teeth being the body's
instrument for turning food into refuse.

### chit (חיט)
The Mishnah (Bechoros 6:4) and the Sifra (*Emor*, *parshasa* 7:12) count as a
blemish an outer *chit* that is defective or cut, or an inner *chit* wholly
uprooted, but not an inner *chit* merely defective (Bechoros 6:12). Rashi (to
Bechoros 35a): the gums, the outer *chit* being where the incisors belong and
the inner where the molars belong. The Mainz commentary ascribed to Rabbeinu
Gershom (there) and Rabbi Nosson of Rome (*Sefer HeAruch*) read *chit* as the
teeth themselves. Rambam (to Bechoros 6:4) had a different girsa reading *chut*,
with a vav.

## Rendering guidance

- *Shein*: **tooth**, or **ivory** where the object is made of it. In the tort
  sugyos leave it transliterated, *shein*, since it names a category.
- *Mesalos* / *malteos*: **molars** following Rashi, Radak, the Gra, and Rabbi
  Tedeschi; **fangs** where the subject is a beast and Ibn Parchon's reading
  fits. Do not silently write "teeth."
- *Kaka* and *nivi* stay transliterated in a Gemara passage, glossed once from
  the Hebrew they translate.
- *Chit*: **gums** or **teeth**, whichever the posek in front of you follows;
  say which.
- Trap: English "teeth" covers *shein*, *mesalos*, *malteos*, *kaka*, *nivi*,
  and *gallal* alike. Wherever two of them stand in one pasuk or one sugya
  (Mishlei 30:14, "whose teeth are swords and whose *mesalos* are knives";
  Berachos 56a; Chullin 59a), the passage is drawing exactly the line English
  erases. Give the general word "teeth" and the specific one "molars," or
  transliterate the second.
