# Worm: rimah / toleah (tola'as)

## Bottom line

Hebrew has two words for the worms of the grave, *rimah* and *toleah*, which
always stand in that order (*Avos* 3:1 and the Gemara; Yeshayahu 14:11; Iyov
25:6). Rabbi Shaul Goldman explains that *rimah* is **maggots** or larvae, and
more broadly decay from within; *toleah* (commonly *tola'as*) is the **worm**
that eats, destruction from without. Rabbi Klein reads the fixed order as
chronological, and the Netziv and the Vilna Gaon make *rimah* the first,
smaller stage of infestation and *toleah* the later, larger worm; in a similar
vein Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi makes *rimah* a cluster of larvae still in
the egg and *toleah* one grown worm. Others draw the line elsewhere: worms of
fruit and cheese against worms of the innards and the ground (Rabbi
Tedeschi-Ashkenazi's alternative); formed, or seemingly formed, from within
against coming from outside (Rav Hirsch); not yet moved from where they were
generated against able to crawl, or worms of rot against worms as such
(Malbim); creatures that do not eat against creatures that eat as if with teeth
(*Midrash Sechel Tov*).

## Distinctions

### rimah (רימה)
Seven occurrences in Tanach, five of them in Iyov (7:5, 17:14, 21:26, 24:20,
25:6), plus Shemos 16:24, where the manna kept for Shabbos had no *rimah*, and
Yeshayahu 14:11; a few pesukim earlier the leftover manna *vayarum*, "became
infested," and bred *tola'im* (Shemos 16:20). The pair *rimah v'toleah* recurs
in the Gemara (Berachos 24b; Nedarim 9b; Bava Basra 17a, on the seven whose
bodies were not given to *rimah* and *toleah*; Bava Metzia 83b) and the Midrash
(*Shemos Rabbah* 20, *Vayikra Rabbah* 18, *Devarim Rabbah* 11, *Koheles Rabbah*
5 and 12), though curiously never in the Yerushalmi. Ibn Janach, Radak (each
in *Sefer HaShorashim*) and Rabbi Shlomo Ibn Parchon (*Machberes HeAruch*)
trace it to ר-מ-ם; Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) to the biliteral
ר-מ. Either way the root family (with ר-ו-ם) covers lifting, exalting,
separating, removing, producing, and throwing.

On which of those senses yields "maggot," the authorities split.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Yerios Shlomo*, *Cheshek Shlomo*), expanding on
  Menachem: from "throwing" upward in an arc, because these creatures have no
  legs to jump with and instead gather themselves to fling their bodies in an
  arc onto the spot they aim at; he further suggests that perhaps *rimon*, the
  pomegranate, is named from *rimah*, that fruit being so prone to infestation.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 16:20): from "lifting," since food lying on the ground
  looks lifeless, and once worms move over it the thing seems raised into
  animation; he stresses that he is describing what the eye sees, with no
  bearing on whether such worms arise by spontaneous generation or from eggs, a
  controversy of his time.
- Rabbi Klein suggests that swarming worms "ascend" upon the rotting item, an
  explanation somewhat implied by Malbim in *Sefer HaCarmel*.
- Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras (*Aholei Yehudah*) offers two: in line with
  the science of his times, worms are generated from excess fat when rotting
  food grows hot and putrid, so ר-מ-ם is somehow related to ח-מ-ם, heat (he does
  not explain the reish and ches interchange); or the creature is so slight that
  its body barely lifts off the ground.
- Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche (*Michtam L'David Luach HaShorashim* to Tehillim
  24:4; *Ohr Chadash Luach HaShorashim* to Shemos 16:20): a root can carry a
  thing and its exact opposite, so ר-מ gives the exalted *ram* and the lowest of
  the low, *rimah*, with *mirmah* and *ramai* in between, seeming exalted and
  being the reverse.
- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi suggests relating *rimah* to ר-ב, through the
  interchange of mem and beis, for the proliferation of larvae at the onset of
  infestation.

### toleah (תולעה), tola (תולע), tola'as (תולעת)
*Toleah* twice (Yeshayahu 14:11, Iyov 25:6), *tola* twice (Eichah 4:5, Yeshayahu
1:18), *tola'as* close to forty times.
- On *tola'as Yaakov* (Yeshayahu 41:14), Radak explains the comparison by Klal
  Yisrael's weakened state in exile, while *Tanchuma* (Beshalach 9) says that as
  a worm fells a cedar with its mouth, so Klal Yisrael fells nations with its
  mouth, through tefillah.
- Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach, Radak and Ibn Parchon all take the root as ת-ל-ע, which
  also yields *mesalos*, "teeth" (Iyov 29:17; Yoel 1:6; Mishlei 30:14). The link
  to teeth, Rabbi Klein notes, is not insignificant, since the Midrash makes the
  worm's mouth its outstanding feature, and *Midrash Sechel Tov* (to Shemos
  16:24) states the difference that way: *rimah* do not eat or drink, while
  *toleah* eat as though they had teeth.
- Rabbi Pappenheim treats the tav as extraneous and the core root as ל-ע, "the
  organ of swallowing": *loa*, "throat," occurs once (Mishlei 23:2), teeth
  belong to the same apparatus, and *bala*, "swallow," he parses as ב-א
  ("enter") plus ל-ע. The worm, he argues, earns the name because it bolts its
  food as though swallowing without chewing.
- Rav Hirsch also ties *toleah* to *loa*, the worm being defined by its eating.
- Rabbi Tedeschi-Ashkenazi suggests tying it to *loa* by the long tubular shape.

### Tola, the name
Tola is also a name, borne by a son of Yissachar (Bereishis 46:13; Bamidbar
26:23; Divrei HaYamim I 7:1-2) and a later judge of that tribe (Shoftim 10:1).
- *Midrash Sechel Tov* (to Bereishis 46:13) ties it to Yissachar's Torah study
  (Divrei HaYamim I 12:33): its bearer would bask in Torah like a worm consuming
  it, much as English speaks of a bookworm.
- *Yalkut Midrashei Teiman* (to Bamidbar 26:23): as a worm penetrates a fruit,
  the family of Tola went deep into the halachah.
- Alfred Jones (*The Proper Names of the Old Testament Scriptures Expounded and
  Illustrated*, London, 1856, p. 360) conjectures the child was so named for his
  poor, weak and scarlet appearance at birth, after *tola'as shani*.

### The difference, stated
- Rabbi Shaul Goldman: *rimah* is specifically larvae and maggots, not actual
  worms, and in the broader sense always refers to internal decay; *toleah* is
  the worm as a creature that eats, an external force of destruction. Rabbi
  Klein suggests that several of the sources below may be adduced in support.
- Word order: wherever the two stand together, *rimah* comes first (Yeshayahu
  14:11; Iyov 25:6), as in the rabbinic phrase, which Rabbi Klein takes to
  imply that *rimah* precedes *toleah* in time.
- Netziv (*Birkas HaNetziv* to *Mechilta* on Shemos 16:20; *Ha'Emek She'eilah*
  56:3): *rimah* are the small ones that appear before the thing has properly
  rotted, *toleah* the long crawling ones that come after it has spoiled. Hence
  Shemos 16:20 uses the verb *vayarum* first and the noun *tola'im* after.
- Vilna Gaon, cited by Rabbi Binyamin Rivlin (*Giviyay Giviya HaKesef* to
  Shemos 16:20): infestation usually begins small (*rimah*) and grows large
  (*toleah*); so Shemos 16:24 says there was no *rimah* in the manna, meaning it did not
  reach even the first stage, and all the more so not the second. Rabbi Shlomo
  Aharon Wertheimer (*Beiurei Shemos Nirdafim*) finds support in Rashbam to
  Shemos 16:20.
- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim* 482): *rimah* is larvae
  still in the egg, and though it looks singular it names a cluster; *toleah* is
  one fully formed worm living on its own. Alternatively, *rimah* infests fruit
  and cheese, *toleah* is found in the innards or in the ground. Rabbi
  Wertheimer infers from *Avos* 2:7 that *rimah* specially infests fatty flesh.
  Rabbi Klein later found that Rabbi Tedeschi-Ashkenazi is probably drawing on
  Rabbi Eliyahu Bachur's glosses to Radak's *Sefer HaShorashim*: *rimah* for
  maggots in flesh and cheese, *toleah* for worms.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 16:20): *rimah* form from within the rotting thing, or
  at least appear to; *toleah* plainly come from outside to consume it.
- Malbim (to Yeshayahu 14:11): *rimah* have not yet left the place where they
  were generated, *toleah* can already move about. Elsewhere (*Yair Ohr*, on
  *adam* versus *enosh*) he has *rimah* as worms infesting something rotting and
  *toleah* as worms as such.

## Rendering guidance

- Fixed phrase רימה ותולעה (*Avos* 3:1, Berachos 24b, Nedarim 9b, Bava Basra
  17a, Bava Metzia 83b, and the midrashim): "maggots and worms." Keep the
  order, which Rabbi Klein, the Netziv and the Vilna Gaon read as a sequence.
- Trap: English "worms" absorbs both. If the passage turns on the pair (the
  manna at Shemos 16:20 and 16:24, or the seven whose bodies were untouched),
  the two English words must stay two.
- *Tola'as* alone is usually "worm"; *tola'as shani* is the scarlet dye, which
  Rabbi Klein glosses "red."
- *Rimah* names the creatures, larvae or maggots; Rabbi Goldman's broader sense,
  internal decay, belongs in a note rather than in place of "maggots."
- מתלעות is "teeth," never "worms," though the lexicographers give both one
  root; keep the connection to a note.
