# Tired: ayef / yaef / yagea

## Bottom line

*Ayef* is **tired**, a little more frequent in Tanach than *yaef* and the word
Modern Hebrew kept. *Yaef* is the same three letters in a different order. Some
explain the two as one word by metathesis, and the Rid compares *kesev* and
*keves*. Rabbi Shimon Schwab reads them as two degrees of tiredness: *ayef* is
tired but with some energy left, and *yaef* is **exhausted**, with nothing left,
which for him is why the morning berachah says *ha'nosen la'yaef koach*. *Yagea*
is **worn out**, tiredness from a different cause. For Malbim it comes from
(over)exertion, where *ayef* is the regular tiredness of one whose energy is
naturally low; for Rabbi Yosef of Saragossa it comes from the speed of one's
actions, where *ayef* comes from the persistence of an action.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether ayef and yaef differ at all.**

- Some explain that they do not: the two are one word by metathesis, the ayin
  and yud trading places. Rabbi Yeshayah of Trani, the Rid, compares the case to
  another well-known pair of metathetical synonyms, [*kesev*](sheep-and-goats-seh-keves-ayil-eiz.html#%D7%9B%D7%A9%D7%91) and *keves*, both
  "lamb" in Tanach.
- Rabbi Shimon Schwab says they differ in degree, and reads the morning berachah
  accordingly: on the surface its wording simply follows Yeshayahu's, but he
  sees more in its use of *yaef* rather than *ayef*, chosen for maximum effect,
  that Hashem strengthens even the completely exhausted.

**What sets yagea apart from ayef.**

- Rabbi Yosef of Saragossa (d. 1420, a student of the Ran) addresses *ayef/yaef*
  against *yagea*: all mean tired in the same way, but from different causes,
  *ayef* from repeatedly performing certain movements, the persistence of the
  action, and *yagea* from the speed of one's actions, having overexerted
  oneself.
- Malbim also sees two causes, but different ones: *ayef* is the regular
  tiredness that one with naturally low energy experiences, *yagea* the
  tiredness that results from (over)exerting oneself.

## Distinctions

### ayef (עיף)
The more frequent of the pair in Tanach (a few more occurrences than *yaef*) and
the surviving word in Modern Hebrew.
- Wertheimer: fatigue severe enough to bring a man to faint; from that comes its
  borrowed sense "thirsty" (Iyov 22:7, Tehillim 63:2), which Radak notes in
  *Sefer HaShorashim*, dehydration being what generally makes tired people
  faint.
- For Schwab it is the lighter of *ayef* and *yaef*;
- for Malbim it is the ordinary tiredness of one whose energy is naturally low;
- for Rabbi Yosef of Saragossa, tiredness from persistent, repeated action.

### yaef (יעף)
Yeshayahu 40:29, in the haftarah of Lech Lecha: "the Giver of strength to the
tired (*yaef*), and to those without energy, an abundance of might." The first
part is paraphrased in the morning berachah *ha'nosen la'yaef koach*, and is
commonly appended to the end of works of Torah literature as the author's way of
saying that his ability to endure weariness while writing came from Above.

- Some treat the form as *ayef* transposed (the Rid compares *kesev/keves*).
- Schwab treats the choice of *yaef* in the berachah as meaningful.

### yagea (יגע)
Three occurrences only: Devarim 25:18 (*ve'atah ayef veyagea*), Shmuel
II 17:2, and Koheles 1:8 (there in the plural, *yegei'im*). The word is related to the
root of the verb *yaga*, "he toiled."

## Rendering guidance

- The berachah *ha'nosen la'yaef koach*: **"Who gives strength to the exhausted."**
  "Tired" there flattens exactly the word Schwab says the berachah chose on purpose.
  Schwab's comment is on the berachah's word choice; Rabbi Klein himself translates the
  pasuk, Yeshayahu 40:29, "to the tired (*yaef*)." On the metathesis view the two words
  are one and "tired" is not a loss.
- Devarim 25:18 puts *ayef* and *yagea* side by side. Render them separately: **"weary
  and worn out"**, or **"faint and exhausted from toil"**. One English word covering
  both is the standard failure here.
- Keep **thirsty** available for a standalone *ayef* where the context is water (Iyov
  22:7, Tehillim 63:2), and note the fatigue sense rather than losing it.
- *Yagea* as an adjective is **worn out**; the related noun *yegiah* is **toil**, and
  should not be translated as tiredness.
