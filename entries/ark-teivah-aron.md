# Ark: teivah / aron

## Bottom line

Both words are rendered **ark** in English, and in Scripture they do not overlap:
*teivah* is Noach's ark and the basket of the infant Moshe, *aron* is the Ark of
the Covenant and Yosef's coffin. Where a distinction is needed, render *teivah*
as **ark** or **basket** (on the two proposals offered below, a floating vessel
or a container for a living person) and *aron* as **ark**, **chest**, or
**coffin**. The one thing not to get wrong:
in Rabbinic Hebrew the two words are used almost interchangeably, so a Mishnaic
*teivah* may well be an *aron*.

## Distinctions

### teivah (תיבה)
In Tanach the word appears in two contexts: the ark Noach built to house
his family through the Mabul (Bereishis 6 to 9), and the basket in which
Moshe's mother laid him on the Nile (Shemos 2:3). Two readings are proposed from
that usage, and both are floated as possibilities rather than settled. By
*where*: one may argue that a *teivah* is a container kept afloat on water, which
an *aron* need not be. By *what*: alternatively, one may suppose that a *teivah*
holds a living person, while an *aron* covers other kinds of storage, even
inanimate contents. Rabbi Aharon Marcus (1843 to 1916) points out that *teivah*
is related to *bayis* (house) because the two contain the same letters; the
implication drawn from this is that a *teivah* is in some ways like a person's
home, which supports the second reading.

### aron (ארון)
Also two contexts in Tanach: the *Aron HaBris*, the gold-plated wooden box
holding the Luchos and a Sefer Torah, and the coffin in which Yosef's body was
placed (Bereishis 50:26). Neither floats and neither holds a living person, which
is what makes the two proposals above possible in the first place.

### The rabbinic usage that unsettles both readings
The Mishnah (Taanis 2:1) has the *teivah* carried out to the city square on a
public fast day, and another Mishnah (Megillah 3:1) rules that the proceeds of a
sold shul may buy only a *teivah* or something holier than a *teivah*. The
commentators discuss whether that *teivah* is the shul's Holy Ark, which houses
the Sifrei Torah, or the table on which a Sefer Torah is laid to be read, known
as the *bimah*; either way it is neither afloat nor holding a living person, which
defeats the definitions offered above. The Talmud
Yerushalmi (Shekalim 6:1) goes further: the *Aron HaBris* was built of three
*teivos*, two of gold and one of wood nested so that every exposed surface was
gold. There a *teivah* is a component of an *aron*.

Modern Hebrew draws a clean line, though historically the distinction may not be
well grounded: *teivah* is a storage chest, *aron* a closet.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "ark" covers both, and in a passage that turns on the difference
  (a derashah pairing Noach's *teivah* with the *Aron*, say) one English word
  hides it. Render one of them by its other sense: "Noach's ark" beside "the Ark
  of the Covenant" works only if the context already separates them; otherwise
  give *teivah* as "ark" and *aron* as "the Aron."
- Shemos 2:3: "basket," not "ark," unless the passage is drawing the deliberate
  parallel to Noach, in which case keep "ark" in both and say so.
- Bereishis 50:26: "coffin," never "ark."
- In a Mishnaic or Talmudic quotation, do not import the Biblical distinction. A
  *teivah* in Taanis 2:1 is a shul fixture; translate it as "the Ark" (or leave
  it as *teivah* and gloss) and note the machlokes rather than deciding it.
