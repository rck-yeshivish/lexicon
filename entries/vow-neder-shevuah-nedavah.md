# Vow: neder / shevuah / nedavah

## Bottom line

*Neder* is a **vow**: it works on an **object**, giving it a forbidden status
on the model of hekdesh. *Shevuah* is an **oath**: it works on the **person**,
binding him to do or not do, and it ordinarily invokes Hashem's name. They are
not two grades of one thing, and the Gemara (Nedarim 2b) treats them as two
distinct instruments, each with its own masechta. A *nedavah* is a subclass of
*neder*, a **freewill offering** that both undertakes the korban and designates
the animal. The trap is the common English "promise" and "oath," which make the
pair look like a weaker and a stronger version of the same act.

## Distinctions

### neder (נדר)
A verbal declaration that shifts the halachic status of a thing. Its focus is
the object: the *neder* forbids benefit from an item or from a person, or
forbids a food, by making that thing something like consecrated property, and
so forbidden to him. In this form it is called a *konam*, a deliberately
altered form of *korban*, since the mechanism copies the making of an object
into property of the Beis HaMikdash. A man who takes a *neder* not to smoke has
in effect said that benefit from cigarettes shall be forbidden to him as the
use of a consecrated animal is forbidden to him. The same word covers the
pledge to bring a korban or a donation to the Beis HaMikdash. The masechta is
*Nedarim*, in Seder Nashim.

### nedavah (נדבה)
A type of *neder* which is one statement doing two jobs: undertaking to bring
an offering, sometimes only by implication, and designating the specific animal
as that offering. One who points to his ram and says that this ram shall be an
olah has both obligated himself in an olah and consecrated that ram.

### shevuah (שבועה)
An oral statement, ordinarily invoking Hashem's name, whose focus is the
speaker. It can be positive or negative and can face the past or the future: I
swear I will do, I swear I will not do, I swear I did, I swear I did not. Beis
din can also impose two further kinds in litigation, one on a litigant to back
his claim and one on a subpoenaed witness who says he has nothing to testify.
Every valid *shevuah* must be kept or must be true.

The prohibited class is the *shevuas shav* of the third of the Aseres HaDibros,
taking Hashem's name in vain, of four kinds: swearing to what is plainly false
(that a marble pillar is gold), swearing to what is self-evident (that the
heavens are the heavens), swearing to transgress (that he will not put on
tefillin), and swearing to the impossible (that he will not sleep for three
days). The masechta is *Shevuos*, in Seder Nezikin.

### The dividing line
Both are oral pronouncements with legal force under the Torah, both are covered
by the charge not to profane one's word (Bamidbar 30:3). The difference is
where each lands: the *neder* on a specific object, forbidding it or benefit
from it; the *shevuah* on the person, obligating him to an act or its
avoidance. Shlomo HaMelech's "death and life are in the power of the tongue"
(Mishlei 18:21) is the measure of what speech can create. Because of that
weight it is the custom to annul *nedarim* and *shevuos* before Rosh Hashanah
and Yom Kippur.

## Rendering guidance

- *Neder*: "vow." *Shevuah*: "oath." Fix these and keep them fixed; do not use
  "promise" for either in a halachic text, since a promise binds nothing.
- Trap: English "oath" will absorb both, and "vow" is worse, since in ordinary
  English a vow is personal (a marriage vow), which is precisely the *shevuah*
  side. Where a passage sets the two against each other (Nedarim 2b and the
  opening sugyos of both masechtos), keep the Hebrew and gloss on first use.
- *Nedavah*: "freewill offering" in korbanos contexts; do not render it "gift,"
  which drops the consecration.
- *Konam* stays transliterated with a gloss at first use, since the point of
  the word is that it is a substitute form of *korban*; "prohibition formula"
  loses it entirely.
- Bamidbar 30:3 sets both terms in one pasuk. Render "if a man vows a *neder*
  to Hashem or swears a *shevuah*"; a rendering that gives "makes a vow or
  swears an oath" without the Hebrew leaves the reader unable to follow the
  sugya that follows.
