# Excision: karet / ariri

## Bottom line

*Karet* is **excision**: the sinner himself is cut off, whether from his years,
from his soul's place in the World of Souls, or from the Upper Source to which
his soul is joined. *Ariri* is **childless**, the line ending rather than the
man. Whether they are one punishment or two is a *machlokes*. Rashi holds that
every *karet* can carry *ariri* with it; Riva holds that *ariri* is a separate
penalty, attaching only to the forbidden marriages.

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

| כרת | karet | excision | the man himself cut off; in body, soul or both (Ramban) |

## Distinctions

### karet (כרת)
Root כ-ר-ת. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (*Yerios Shlomo*, in a treatment of more
than fifteen Hebrew words for cutting): *karet* is the general word for cutting,
whether or not the cut actually severs. So a [*get*](document-gett-shtar.html#%D7%92%D7%98) is a *sefer krisus* (Devarim
24:1), a document of cutting, since marriage joins two into one and ending it
tears one body in two. A covenant, too, is *karat*, "cut," from the ancient rite
of cutting animals in half and having the parties to the agreement walk between
the pieces, which is why the Biblical verb for entering a covenant is *karet* at
all. Some linguists link this to the English idiom "to cut a
deal." In *Cheshek Shlomo* Rabbi Pappenheim
takes the two-letter root כ-ר as a kind of digging, which breaks up the earth and
parts one clod from another, and cutting does the same. *Karet* also carries
plain destruction, and above all the Divine punishment of spiritual excision, on
which the Mishnah (Kerisos 1:1) counts thirty-six prohibitions.

What is severed, and from what:
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg: cut off from the rest of the World to Come, in
  which all souls have their eternal reunion with Hashem. The man punished is
  isolated from that joy and does not join the others.
- Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin (*Nefesh HaChaim* 1:18): cut off from the soul's Upper
  Source. The soul's lifeline is a sort of umbilical cord from creature to
  Creator; with *karet*, nine tenths of that cord is cut, and it is never
  completely severed.
- Ramban and Rabbeinu Bachya (to Vayikra 18:29) divide it in three. *Karet* of
  the body: for one whose merits outweigh his sins but who transgressed a
  *karet* prohibition; he dies early, but his soul enters the World of Souls and
  returns at Techiyas HaMeisim. *Karet* of the soul: for one whose sins outweigh
  his merits; he may live long and comfortably, and the punishment falls only
  after death, barring his soul from the World of Souls. Both together: for
  *avodah zarah*, where the Torah writes the doubled *hikares tikares* (Bamidbar
  15:31), an early death and a punished soul after it.

The age at which "early death" is measured is itself disputed.
- Yerushalmi Bikkurim 2:1: before fifty, derived from Bamidbar 4:18, where Moshe
  and Aharon are warned not to let the tribe of Kehas be "cut off" through
  gazing at the uncovered vessels, together with Bamidbar 8:25, that the Levi'im
  served only to fifty.
- Moed Katan 28a: even a death between fifty and sixty is *karet*; Rav Yosef
  made a [*seudah*](meal-seudah-mishteh-mesibah.html#%D7%A1%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%93%D7%94) on turning sixty, no longer liable, and even past sixty a
  sudden death can be *karet*.
- Ibn Ezra (Bereishis 17:14): death before fifty two, or else the death of one's
  descendants, so that his memory is wiped out entirely and his legacy is what
  gets cut off.

### ariri (ערירי)
Root ע-ר-ר. Four appearances in Tanach. Avraham, questioning the promise of the
land: *v'anochi holech ariri*, "I go childless" (Bereishis 15:2). Yechonyah,
next to last king of Yehudah, cursed to remain *ariri* (Yirmiyahu 22:30; by
tradition he later did teshuvah and the decree was lifted, and some sources have
Mashiach descending from him). And twice as *aririm*, the penalty for the
forbidden marriages of Vayikra 20:20 and 20:21.

- Rashi (to Shabbos 25b), like Ibn Ezra, holds that just as *karet* can bring a
  man's own early death, it can also reach his children; every *karet* therefore
  potentially includes *ariri*.
- Riva, a Tosafist (probably Rabbi Yitzchak ben Asher HaLevi, a talmid of
  Rashi), disputes this: *karet* does not touch the sinner's children at all,
  and *ariri* is a separate punishment attaching only to the forbidden marriages
  (Tosafos to Yevamos 2a).

Even Rashi grants that grown children who do not follow their father in his sin
are not affected by his *karet* (Tosafos Yeshanim to Yevamos 2a).

Shadal (to Bereishis 15:2): *ariri* is bare, without descendants.

Rabbi Shlomo Astruc (to Bereishis 15:1): Avraham's *holech ariri* (15:2), with
an ayin, is related to [*arur*](curse-arur-kelalah-allah-kavah.html#%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A8), cursed, with an aleph, alluding to Eliezer's
cursed descent, which unfitted him to be Avraham's heir.

## Rendering guidance

- Keep *karet* transliterated in any halachic passage; it is a term of art with
  thirty six *aveiros* attached to it. Where an English word is wanted, "excision"
  or "being cut off," with the gloss "a Divine punishment" at first use.
- *Ariri*: "childless." Bereishis 15:2 is "I go childless," and Vayikra 20:20 is
  "they shall be childless," not "they shall be cut off," which would import
  *karet* into a pasuk that did not say it.
- The doubled *hikares tikares* (Bamidbar 15:31) is load bearing for Ramban and
  Rabbeinu Bachya. Render the doubling ("he shall surely be cut off") rather than
  smoothing it, since two *kareisos* are being derived from it.
- *Karat bris* is "made a covenant" in idiomatic English, but where a passage is
  working the cutting sense (Pappenheim, or the *bris bein habesarim*), write
  "cut a covenant" and let the image stand.
- Trap: English "cut off" serves for *karet*, for *ariri*, and for *sefer
  krisus* alike. Fix "cut off" to *karet*, "childless" to *ariri*, and "bill of
  severance" or plain *get* to *sefer krisus*.
- Trap: "extirpation" and "excommunication" both drift into range. The second is
  simply wrong; *karet* is not *cherem*.
