# Almond: luz / shaked

## Bottom line

*Shaked* is the Hebrew word for the almond, and its root ש-ק-ד seems to be the
one meaning watchful and diligent, which is the whole point of the *nevuah* in
Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12: render it **almond**, and keep the wordplay with
**vigilant** visible. *Luz* means the same thing in Arabic,
according to Rabbi Saadia Gaon as Radak and Ibn Ezra cite him, and the column
reaches Aramaic as its own hedged upshot, so **almond**
again, though a substantial set of *Rishonim* read *luz* as **hazel** and Targum
Onkelos never once renders *shaked* as *luz*. The article's own upshot is a
hedged one: it seems that *luz* is an Aramaic word and *shaked* a genuinely
Hebrew one, in which case the two mean exactly the same thing and differ only by
language. The trap is deciding either word's English without checking which
*Rishon* the passage is following.

## Distinctions

### shaked (שקד)
The plain Hebrew word: *shekeidim* among the gifts Yaakov sent down to Mitzrayim
(Bereishis 43:11), the *shekeidim* that Aharon's staff produced (Bamidbar
17:23), and the almond-shaped cups *meshukadim* on the Menorah (Shemos 25:33 to
34, 37:19 to 20; the article gives the second range as 37:19 to 29).

The word seems to belong to the root ש-ק-ד, which carries watchfulness,
vigilance, diligence, persistence. Grammarians as early as Ibn Janach
(990-1055) and Radak (1160-1235) intimated a connection between the two senses,
explaining that the almond tree is especially zealous in bringing out its
blossom faster than other trees, though *Machberes Menachem* does not seem to
draw the connection. The column adds a second explanation from the botanist Dr.
Joshua D. Klein of the Volcani Center, that *shaked* suits the almond because it
tends to stay on the tree rather than drop when ripe as fleshy fruits do, so
that the nut is "diligent" in hanging on longer than most fruits.

The link may even predate the *medakdekim*, as it seems to be found in Hashem's
pun to Yirmiyahu: "What do you see, Yirmiyahu? And I said, I see a *makel
shaked*. And Hashem said to me, you have seen well, for *shoked ani al devari
la'asoso*" (Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12). Rashi: just as the *shaked* is overly
attentive to bear fruit before every other tree, so is Hashem attentive to carry
out what He told the *navi*. The Yerushalmi (Taanis 4:5) paraphrases the *shaked*
of that pasuk with *luz*.

In Modern Hebrew *shaked* also has an anatomical sense, as *luz* does, but not
the mythical *luz* bone: it means the tonsils. Even-Shoshan's dictionary calls
that neologism a folk usage based on the resemblance between a tonsil's shape
and an almond's. *Shkadiyah*, the almond *tree* as against *shaked* the nut,
seems to have been coined by the poet Levin Kipnis (1894-1990) in his well-known
Tu B'Shvat song.

English "almond" sounds Arabic because of the *al-* prefix, but the Arabic for
almond is *luz*, as in Aramaic. The column traces the word's ancestor instead to
the Latin *amygdala*, seemingly, through various intermediate forms; some argue
the *al-* came about by contamination from the last syllable of the Latin
etymon, others that the Latin became Spanish *almendra* under Arabic influence,
*al-* being the Spanish definite article, and reached English through Spanish.

### luz (לוז)
Yaakov set *luz* rods in the animals' troughs (Bereishis 30:37). Targum Onkelos
leaves *luz* untranslated. Radak and Ibn Ezra cite Rabbi Saadia Gaon that *luz*
there means almond, as it does in Arabic; Ibn Ezra adds that Hebrew, Aramaic and
Arabic are one family of languages, so a word may carry across.

Targum Yonasan and Targum Neofiti support the equation from the other side: they
render the *shekeidim* of Bereishis 43:11 as *meshach diluzin*, almond oil or
butter, and the *shekeidim* of Bamidbar 17:23 as *luzin*.

On that reading, the column says, it seems *luz* is Aramaic and *shaked* Hebrew,
and the two mean exactly the same thing. Dr. Gary Rensburg of Rutgers University
adds that the Torah nonetheless uses the Aramaic *luz* in the story of how Lavan
the Aramean tried to swindle Yaakov because the story happened in Aram, the home
of Aramaic, so that an Aramaic word in an Aramean setting lends the account
authenticity. Rabbi Klein offers an alternative of his own, marked as a thought
rather than a ruling: perhaps in Hebrew *shaked* is the almond fruit or nut
itself and names the tree only in a borrowed sense, while *luz* names the tree
directly; Avraham Even-Shoshan's dictionary seems to follow that approach.

Not everyone equates them. Another version of Rabbi Saadia Gaon's commentary has
*luz* as the silverleaf poplar; Menachem Ibn Saruk writes that the *luz* is
related to the cedar; and there are many opinions that take *luz* as hazel
(Rashi and Tosafos to Bechoros 8a, Rashi to Bereishis 30:38 and to Shoftim 1:26).
Targum Onkelos seems to follow one of these, since he renders *shaked*
consistently as *segd* (Bereishis 43:11, Bamidbar 17:23, and see the Targum to
Koheles 12:5) and never as *luz*; *segd* means almond in Syriac and Ethiopic and
seems itself to derive from the Hebrew *shaked* by the interchange of gimmel and
kuf.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the biliteral ל-ז means sideward deviation, whether
physical movement to the side or moral swerving from the straight path. The
*luz* tree fits because its shoots branch off the trunk very early, unlike other
trees. The demonstrative *halaz*, "that one," comes from the same root, its
antecedent being someone far off, removed from here. Midrash *Sechel Tov* reads
*halaz* homiletically the other way, connecting it to the *luz* tree's outward
majesty, where it is used of Yitzchak (Bereishis 24:65), Yosef (Bereishis
37:19), Zecharyah (Zecharyah 2:8), and Eretz Yisrael (Yechezkel 36:35). The
column adds that the almond's aesthetic quality may also be what is reflected in
the almond-like decorations fixed (*meshukadim*) to the Menorah.

Solomon Löwisohn (1789-1821), whom the column calls a Hungarian Maskilic
scholar, derived Lusitania, the Roman name for Portugal, from the Aramaic *luz*,
on the ground that the Phoenicians reaching the Iberian peninsula found almond
trees in abundance; he adduces Portuguese place-names such as Calmende, the
house of almonds, and Castelmende, the castle of almonds, both tied to
Spanish and Portuguese *almendron*, almond. The column notes that this etymology
for Lusitania is not widely accepted nowadays.

### Luz the city
Luz was renamed Beis El, the site of Yaakov's dream (Bereishis 28:19); the
Canaanite who showed the tribe of Yosef the hidden way in later founded a second
Luz in the land of the Hittites (Shoftim 1:23 to 26). *Bereishis Rabbah* 69:8,
about which the column notes it remains unclear whether it means the Luz in Eretz
Yisrael or the other one: its people were not mixed up when Sancheriv conquered
the area, it was not destroyed by Nevuchadnetzar, and the *malach hamaves* has no
dominion there. Rabbi Abba bar Kahana: one who enters it blossoms with *mitzvos*
and good deeds like a *luz*. Chazal there also say that as an almond has no
opening, so the city was sealed; Rabbi Simon adds that a *luz* tree stood at its
entrance, and Rabbi Pinchas bar Chamah that the way in was a cave whose mouth a
*luz* tree concealed. Rabbi J. D. Eisenstein (1854-1956) claimed the Luz of this
midrash is the Tibetan city of Lhasa, whose people are reputed to be long-lived
and so could be said to elude the *malach hamaves*. The *luz* bone, said to be
the kernel from which a person is revived at *techiyas hameisim* and nurtured
only through *melaveh malkah* (*Mateh Moshe* 513), belongs to the same word,
though the column sets it aside as a discussion for another time.

## Rendering guidance

- Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12: the pun is the message. "A branch of an almond tree...
  for I am watchful over My word to perform it," with a note that *shaked* and
  *shoked* are the same letters. An English that renders *shoked* as "diligent"
  in one clause and "hasten" in the other kills it.
- Trap: English "almond" would absorb *luz* and *shaked* both, which is fine for
  the Targum Yonasan and wrong for Targum Onkelos, who keeps them apart. In a
  passage that quotes Onkelos, or Rashi to Bereishis 30:38, render *luz* as
  "hazel" and say which *Rishon* is being followed.
- Bereishis 30:37: leave the identification open where the *sefer* does. "*Luz*
  rods" with a gloss beats a confident "almond rods" when Onkelos declined to
  translate the word at all.
- Shemos 25:33: *meshukadim* is "shaped like almond blossoms," not "hastened."
  Keep the root's second sense out of the Menorah's description.
- *Halaz*: "that one" or "yonder"; do not import the almond sense unless quoting
  *Sechel Tov*, and then mark it as *derush*.
