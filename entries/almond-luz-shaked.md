# Almond: luz / shaked

## Bottom line

*Shaked* is the Hebrew word for the almond, and its root ש-ק-ד means watchful
and diligent, which is the whole point of the *nevuah* in Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12:
render it **almond**, and keep the wordplay with **vigilant** visible. *Luz* is
the Aramaic (and Arabic) word for the same thing according to Rabbi Saadia Gaon
as Radak and Ibn Ezra cite him, so **almond** again, though a substantial set of
*Rishonim* read *luz* as **hazel** and Targum Onkelos never once renders *shaked*
as *luz*. The trap is deciding either word's English without checking which
*Rishon* the passage is following.

## Distinctions

### shaked (שקד)
The plain Hebrew word: *shekeidim* among the gifts Yaakov sent down to Mitzrayim
(Bereishis 43:11), the *shekeidim* that Aharon's staff produced (Bamidbar
17:23), and the almond-shaped cups *meshukadim* on the Menorah (Shemos 25:33 to
34, 37:19 to 29).

Its root ש-ק-ד carries watchfulness, vigilance, diligence, persistence. Ibn
Janach and Radak already tie the two senses together: the almond tree is
uncommonly eager to bring out its blossom before the other trees. *Machberes
Menachem* does not appear to link them.

The link may be older than the *medakdekim*. Hashem's pun to Yirmiyahu: "What do
you see, Yirmiyahu? And I said, I see a *makel shaked*. And Hashem said to me,
you have seen well, for *shoked ani al devari la'asoso*" (Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12).
Rashi: just as the *shaked* is intent on bearing fruit before every other tree,
so is Hashem intent on carrying out what He told the *navi*. The Yerushalmi
(Taanis 4:5) paraphrases the *shaked* of that pasuk with *luz*.

### luz (לוז)
Yaakov set *luz* rods in the animals' troughs (Bereishis 30:37). Targum Onkelos
leaves *luz* untranslated. Radak and Ibn Ezra cite Rabbi Saadia Gaon that *luz*
there means almond, as it does in Arabic; Ibn Ezra adds that Hebrew, Aramaic and
Arabic are one family of languages, so a word may carry across.

Targum Yonasan and Targum Neofiti support the equation from the other side: they
render the *shekeidim* of Bereishis 43:11 as *meshach diluzin*, almond oil, and
the *shekeidim* of Bamidbar 17:23 as *luzin*.

Not everyone equates them. Another version of Rabbi Saadia Gaon's commentary has
*luz* as the silverleaf poplar; Menachem Ibn Saruk relates *luz* to the cedar;
and a wide body of opinion takes *luz* as hazel (Rashi and Tosafos to Bechoros
8a, Rashi to Bereishis 30:38 and to Shoftim 1:26). Targum Onkelos evidently
holds one of these, since he renders *shaked* consistently as *shigd* (Bereishis
43:11, Bamidbar 17:23, and see the Targum to Koheles 12:5) and never as *luz*.

Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim: the biliteral ל-ז means sideward deviation, whether
physical movement to the side or moral swerving from the straight path. The
*luz* tree fits because its shoots branch off the trunk very early, unlike other
trees. The demonstrative *halaz*, "that one," comes from the same root, its
antecedent being someone far off, removed from here. Midrash *Sechel Tov* reads
*halaz* homiletically the other way, connecting it to the *luz* tree's outward
majesty, where it is used of Yitzchak (Bereishis 24:65), Yosef (Bereishis
37:19), Zecharyah (Zecharyah 2:8), and Eretz Yisrael (Yechezkel 36:35).

### Luz the city
Luz was renamed Beis El, the site of Yaakov's dream (Bereishis 28:19); the
Canaanite who showed the tribe of Yosef the hidden way in later founded a second
Luz in the land of the Hittites (Shoftim 1:23 to 26). *Bereishis Rabbah* 69:8:
its people were not exiled by Sancheriv, it was not destroyed by Nevuchadnetzar,
and the *malach hamaves* has no dominion there. Rabbi Abba bar Kahana: one who
enters it blossoms with *mitzvos* and good deeds like a *luz*. Chazal there also
say that as an almond has no opening, so the city was sealed; Rabbi Simon adds
that a *luz* tree stood at its entrance, and Rabbi Pinchas bar Chamah that the
way in was a cave whose mouth a *luz* tree concealed. Rabbi J. D. Eisenstein
identified this Luz with Lhasa. The *luz* bone, the kernel from which a person is
revived at *techiyas hameisim*, sustained by *melaveh malkah* (*Mateh Moshe*
513), belongs to the same word.

## Rendering guidance

- Yirmiyahu 1:11 to 12: the pun is the message. "A branch of an almond tree...
  for I am watchful over My word to perform it," with a note that *shaked* and
  *shoked* are the same letters. An English that renders *shoked* as "diligent"
  in one clause and "hasten" in the other kills it.
- Trap: English "almond" would absorb *luz* and *shaked* both, which is fine for
  the Targum Yonasan and wrong for Targum Onkelos, who keeps them apart. In a
  passage that quotes Onkelos, or Rashi to Bereishis 30:38, render *luz* as
  "hazel" and say which *Rishon* is being followed.
- Bereishis 30:37: leave the identification open where the *sefer* does. "*Luz*
  rods" with a gloss beats a confident "almond rods" when Onkelos declined to
  translate the word at all.
- Shemos 25:33: *meshukadim* is "shaped like almond blossoms," not "hastened."
  Keep the root's second sense out of the Menorah's description.
- *Halaz*: "that one" or "yonder"; do not import the almond sense unless quoting
  *Sechel Tov*, and then mark it as *derush*.
