# Dig: karah / chafirah / chatzivah

## Bottom line

*Karah* is the opening of a dig, the preparatory or partial digging that does
not finish the work: **dig** in the sense of **begin to dig, hollow out**.
*Chafirah* (verb *chofer*) is digging carried to completion and depth, down
to the water: **dig out, dig through**; in the abstract, **search out**.
*Chatzivah* is hewing rock with a tool: **hew, quarry** (Malbim; Rabbi Yosef
Kara instead makes it digging a round pit). The one thing not to get wrong: a
verse that uses both verbs of one pit (Tehillim 7:16; Bereishis 26:25 against
26:32; Bamidbar 21:18) is not repeating itself, and *karah* is the *earlier,
lesser* act (Malbim, Mecklenburg, Netziv, Rav Hirsch), so never render the pair
as "dug and dug" or reverse their weight.

## Distinctions

### karah (כרה)
Root כ-ר-ה; fifteen Biblical occurrences in the sense of digging. Malbim: the
first stage of digging a pit, which is why "he dug (*karah*) a pit and dug it
out (*chafirah*)" (Tehillim 7:16) runs in that order (so too Ibn Ezra, Ibn
Ramoch, Meiri there), and why Yitzchak's servants first *karah* a well
(Bereishis 26:25) and only later report the well they *chafirah* and found
water (26:32; the same reading in Mecklenburg, HaKsav VeHaKabbalah, and
Netziv, HaEmek Davar). Hence the Torah's *karah* in the law of the pit (Shemos
21:33): even an unfinished pit, under ten *tefachim*, brings liability for
damage (Malbim, with Bava Kamma 5:5 for the exception of death). Netziv: Yaakov
"dug (*karah*) for myself" a grave (Bereishis 50:5) because he had only
marked out a plot, not dug it; *chafirah* would have said the absurd. Rav
Hirsch (to Bereishis 26:25, 49:5): preparatory digging that does not finish
the project, akin to *kara* (calling), which summons in preparation for
something larger; to Shemos 21:33, the arrangements before the actual dig.
Pappenheim: *karah* is the core of the biliteral כ-ר, "digging," from which
he derives *kur* (a furnace dug into the ground, with *kiyor*, *kikar*,
*kirah*), *kar* (a field ringed by dug ditches, with *kor*, *ikar*, *kerem*),
*kares* (cutting apart, as digging parts the earth), and *hakarah*,
recognition, which is digging into one's mind to reach a conclusion before all
the facts are in (whence *nochri*, one unrecognised, and *mechirah*, the sale
that makes parties recognise one another).

### chafirah (חפירה)
Root ח-פ-ר; twenty-three Biblical occurrences as digging, and the Mishnah's
standard verb for making a *bor* (Sheviis 3:10, Bava Kamma 5:5, Bava Basra
2:12). Malbim: the completion of the dig. Rav Hirsch: digging so deep that
one reaches the earth's depths and brings up the spring water; from that
depth the word's second sense, to scout, spy, or investigate, retrieving what
is hidden from view (Modern Hebrew *chafran*, a busybody). Mecklenburg: in the
sense of digging for information *chafirah* carries a negative color, hunting
for something discreditable, and may be kin to *cherpah*, disgrace (see the
shame entry). Rabbi Shaul Bruch: the Song of the Well (Bamidbar 21:18) puts
*chafirah* ("dug by the princes") before *karah* ("dug by the nobles of the
people") on purpose; it is not chronological but qualitative, the finished
well credited to Moshe and Aharon (Taanis 9a), the people's merits able only to
begin the digging.

### chatzivah (חציבה)
Root ח-צ-ב. Malbim: not digging at all but quarrying, excavating with a hammer
that chisels rock or hard ground. Rabbi Yosef Kara (to Yeshayahu 5:2): a
synonym of *karah* and *chofer* that names the digging of a round pit, on the
model of the Rabbinic *chatzav*, a round jug.

## Rendering guidance

- *Karah* alone: "dug," but where the halachah or the narrative turns on
  incompleteness (Shemos 21:33, Bereishis 50:5) let the English show it:
  "began to dig," "hollowed out," "prepared" for the grave.
- *Chafirah* alone: "dug," "dug out"; of a well, "dug through to water." In
  the abstract, "search out," "probe," "spy out," and hear Mecklenburg's
  warning that it can shade into digging up dirt on someone.
- The pair in one verse: Tehillim 7:16 "he dug a pit and dug it out";
  Bamidbar 21:18 "the princes dug it out, the nobles of the people hollowed
  it," keeping Rabbi Bruch's reversed order and its weight.
- *Chatzivah*: "hew" or "quarry" (Malbim); if the text is about the shape of
  the hole rather than the rock, Rabbi Yosef Kara's "round pit" is the
  minority.
- Trap: "dig" absorbs *karah* and *chafirah*, and "search" or "investigate"
  could absorb abstract *chafirah* along with unrelated verbs of inquiry.
  Where two digging verbs appear together, one English verb per Hebrew verb.
- *Hakarah* (recognition) is a Pappenheim derivative, not a member of this
  cluster; render it "recognition" and do not import the digging image unless
  the text itself draws on Pappenheim.
