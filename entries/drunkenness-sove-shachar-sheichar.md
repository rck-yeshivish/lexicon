# Drunkenness: sove / shachar (sheichar)

## Bottom line

*Sove* is **swilling**, drinking intoxicants to excess. For Malbim it names the
drinking itself, whether or not the drinker actually got drunk, though Menachem
Ibn Saruk defines it simply as drunkenness. *Shachar* is **getting drunk**, the
state achieved, and the noun *sheichar* is the **strong drink** that produces
it. In the *ben sorer u'moreh* pasuk, *zollel* is gluttony, the meat half of the
pair, and *sove* is swilling, the drink half.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| סבא | sove | swilling | drinking to excess, whether or not he gets drunk (Malbim) |
| סבא | sava | wine, drink | the drink itself; wine (Targum, Rashi, Kara), elsewhere in Rashi any drink |
| שכר | shachar | getting drunk | actually drunk, the state achieved (Malbim); the head as if dancing (Marcus) |
| שכר | sheichar | strong drink | the intoxicating drink; aged wine against *yayin* (Onkelos, Rashi); later beer or mead |

## Distinctions

### sove (סבא)
Root ס-ב-א, appearing at least eight times in Tanach for getting drunk or for
the drink that does it.
- Menachem Ibn Saruk (*Machberes Menachem*) defines *sove* simply as *shichrus*,
  drunkenness.
- Rabbi Shlomo of Urbino (*Ohel Moed*) counts it as a fourth synonym for
  drinking.
- Malbim (*Yair Ohr*): *sove* is drinking things that could intoxicate, whether
  or not intoxication follows, while *shichrus* is having actually become drunk.

- On the root, Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reduces it to the two-letter ס-ב of
  *sivuv*, going around: the drunkard goes around from store to store, tavern to
  tavern, wherever the drink is. (He adds that they do so to reach better wine
  and be able to say *HaTov V'HaMeitiv* over the upgrade, see Shulchan Aruch
  Orach Chaim 175:2; Rabbi Moshe Tzuriel notes in his glosses to *Yerios Shlomo*
  that this last was a sarcastic witticism, drunkards in reality caring nothing
  for berachos.)
- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi (*Otzar Nirdafim*) takes the same ס-ב
  differently: people sitting *around* a table drinking together, which would
  make *sove* kin to [*mesibah*](meal-seudah-mishteh-mesibah.html#%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%91%D7%94).
- Rabbi Yehudah Aryeh of Carpentras (*Aholei Yehudah*), Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis
  24:25 and Devarim 21:19), Rabbi Shimon Yehuda Leib Goldblit (*Leshon
  Chachamim*) and Rabbi Dr. Asher Weiser (*Mikra V'Lashon*) all tie ס-ב-א to its
  homonym שׂ-ב-ע, *seviah*, satisfaction: the *sove* keeps drinking past the
  point of being full. Rav Hirsch also suggests a connection between ס-ב-א and
  צ-מ-א, on the interchangeability of *samech* with *tzadi* and of *beis* with
  *mem*.

### sava (סבא, as a noun)
The same root as a noun for drink.

- Targum, Rashi (to *Bava Basra* 15b) and Rabbi Yosef Kara (to Yeshayahu 1:22,
  "your *sava* is diluted with water") take *sava* as wine; so does Rashi to
  Hoshea 4:18.
- Elsewhere Rashi seems to treat it as a general term for drinks (Yeshayahu
  1:22, Nachum 1:10; and *savyuta*, wine merchants, at *Avodah Zarah* 71a).
- Rabbi Yosef Nechemias (to Mishlei 23:20): because drunkards are called
  *sovim*, the word *sava* came to refer to wine itself.
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer explains the Yeshayahu charge sharply: a man
  drunk enough to be a *sava* is no longer sensitive to the taste of what he is
  drinking, so unsavory sellers serve him wine diluted with water at the full
  price of the unadulterated article, and he never realizes he has been cheated.

Sva, or Seba, is one of the sons of Kush (Bereishis 10:7; Divrei HaYamim I 1:9),
and his descendants the Sabeans appear later alongside the Kushites (Yeshayahu
43:3, 45:14).

- On the *savaim* of Yechezkel 23:42, Radak in his commentary reads that Sabean
  people, while in *Sefer HaShorashim* he writes that they were drunkards.
- Targum Yonasan and Rashi there read merchants who go around, taking *savaim*
  as a cognate of *sivuv*.

### shachar / sheichar (שכר)
Root ש-כ-ר. The verb of getting drunk (Noach, *vayishkar*, Bereishis 9:21)
occurs nineteen times in Tanach on Even-Shoshan's biblical concordance; the noun
*shikaron*, drunkenness, appears in three places (Yirmiyahu 13:13; Yechezkel
23:33 and 39:19), and is also the name of a town in Yehudah's territory
(Yehoshua 15:11); post-biblical *shikur*, a drunkard, is the etymon of the
Yiddish *shikker*. The noun *sheichar*, the beverage that renders one drunk,
appears twenty-three times.

- Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi: strictly it is any intoxicating drink, but in
  Tanach it sometimes means strong wine in particular.
- Targum Onkelos and Rashi to Bamidbar 6:3 read it as aged wine against *yayin*,
  new wine; Rashi to Bamidbar 28:7 as intoxicating wine, and at *Zevachim* 91a
  he ties *sheichar* to *seviah*, satisfaction. It is as though the libation
  poured down the altar's pipes were satisfying the thirst of those hollow
  tubes.

But sometimes *sheichar* in Tanach could refer to other intoxicating drinks:
Vayikra 10:9 forbids drinking *sheichar* before entering the Mikdash, and there
is ample reason to hold that one may be liable even for other intoxicating
substances besides wine (*Nazir* 4a, *Kerisus* 13b, Ramban to Vayikra 10:9). In
Rabbinic Hebrew *sheichar* is what we call beer or mead (*Pesachim* 3:1).

- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 13:12) makes a comment Rabbi Klein calls somewhat
  cryptic, connecting *shachar* with *sheger*, *shir* and [*sheker*](lie-sheker-kazav-kachash.html#%D7%A9%D7%A7%D7%A8) on the
  interchangeability of *kaf*, *gimmel*, *yud* and *kuf*: all of them name
  something born from oneself, and Rav Hirsch gives no further detail. Dr.
  Lawrence Resnick (*1,000 Words*, which elucidates Rav Hirsch's word
  connections) spells it out as a production coming from within, mentally or
  physically: *sheger* is one's live offspring, the handiwork of one's own
  reproductive system; *shir* an enthusiastic expression of one's own mental
  vision; *shechar* the drunken state in which the imagination forms a reality
  of its own making; and *sheker* a falsehood consciously presented as true.
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus instead proposes that ש-כ-ר derives from the biliteral
  כ-ר(-כ-ר), dancing, with the initial *shin* peripheral to the stem, as in
  David *mecharker* before the Aron (Shmuel II 6:14, 6:16): getting drunk brings
  on a dizziness as though the head were dancing around.

## Rendering guidance

- זולל וסובא (Devarim 21:20): "a glutton and a swiller," or "gorging on meat and
  swilling wine." The Mishnah (*Sanhedrin* 8:2) fixes *zollel* to meat and *sove*
  to wine, so the pair must stay two words in English.
- Trap: "drunkard" for *sove*. It states the result, and by Malbim *sove* names
  the drinking without asserting the result; reserve "drunk / drunkenness" for
  ש-כ-ר. Where a passage contrasts them, "he swilled" against "he became drunk."
- Note the halachic layer: *Sanhedrin* 71a and *Sifrei Ki Seitzei* 219 concede
  that the wine reading of *sove* has no proof from the pasuk in Devarim, only a
  hint from Mishlei 23:20, and Rambam (*Hilchos Mamrim* 7:1) grounds it in
  tradition. So *sove* on its own is best rendered "swilling" or "drinking
  himself drunk," not "drinking wine."
- שכר as a noun in a pasuk: "strong drink" (Vayikra 10:9, Bamidbar 6:3) rather
  than "beer"; save "beer" for the Rabbinic usage.
- Do not render both סבא and שכר as "drink." One is the excess, the other the
  substance or the state.
