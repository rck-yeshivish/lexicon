# Forever: olam / netzach / selah / va'ed

## Bottom line

*Olam* is a very long but bounded span: **for all time**, and in a legal context
often "until the Yovel," not literally forever. *Netzach*, *selah*, and *va'ed*
are, on the Maharsha's reading of Eruvin 54a, the truly unbounded words:
**forever**, **everlasting**, **for all eternity**. That reading is not the only
one: ibn Saruk and Rabbi Yosef Kimchi treat all four as flat synonyms, and Rabbi
Avraham ben Ezriel of Vienna limits the Gemara's finding to the verses where
these words stand beside *olam*. The trap is *le'olam*: Rashi to Shemos 21:6 caps
it at the fifty-year Yovel cycle, and the Maharsha builds on that, so a
translator who writes "forever" for *le'olam* in a halachic pasuk has overshot. When *olam* stands beside one of the other three, the second word
is doing work; do not collapse the pair into one English "forever."

## Distinctions

### olam (עולם)
Root ע-ל-ם. Rashi to Shemos 21:6: the pierced bondsman serves *le'olam*, meaning
only to the Yovel, not without limit. Maharsha therefore reads *olam* as a long
period that nonetheless ends, which is why the pairing in Eruvin 54a is not
redundant. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg ties *olam* to *al*, "above": all of
space and time is the field on which each creature strives to rise above where it
stands, and Yovel is called *le'olam* because in it the slave rises to his
master's free status. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim reads *ne'elam*, "hidden," from the
same *al*: what is above one's range of perception is hidden from him.

### netzach (נצח)
Root נ-צ-ח. Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer: *netzach* is the **continuous**,
unchanging permanence, a state that never alters. Mecklenburg derives it from
*tzach*, "pristine, pure," the final state of being the soul reaches for.

### selah (סלה)
Root ס-ל-ה. Two readings, and they are not reconcilable on the surface. (1)
Eruvin 54a, followed by Rashi, Meiri and other classical *meforshim* to Tehillim
3:3: *selah* means forever, denoting what continues without end. The earliest
translations of Tanach render it so consistently: Targum Yonasan in Aramaic,
Aquilas in Greek, and the Peshitta in Syriac. Wertheimer sharpens it
to the **continual**, the ever-repeating, as against *netzach*'s continuous. (2)
Radak (to Tehillim 3:3 and in *Sefer HaShorashim*) and Malbim (Tehillim 3:3,
Chavakuk 3:3): *selah* is an interjection closing an idea, and for Radak a
musical direction to lift the voice at the close; Malbim adds that it can mark
off a parenthetical remark from the main line. Rabbi Chaim Friedlander: it
signals that an important idea has just ended and one should pause and take it
in. Rabbi Aviad Sar-Shalom Basilea faulted Radak for seeming to set aside the
Gemara's reading; Rabbi Yaakov Emden defended him, that Radak gives the *pshat*
and the tradition gives the deeper sense. Ibn Ezra: *selah* affirms that what was
just said is true, roughly "so it is." Mecklenburg himself ties *selah* to *suleh*
(Iyov 28:16), which denotes something of higher value, and elsewhere cites one of
the earlier commentators who apparently wrote that *Selah* is one of the Names of
Hashem.

### va'ed / ad (ועד, עד)
Root ע-ד. Wertheimer: *ad* literally means "until," and left hanging with no
object it is an ellipsis, "until... (a time that cannot be named)," so it denotes
infinite duration. Mecklenburg links *va'ed* to *adi'im*, "adornments," the
eternal ornaments of the soul in the World to Come.

Whether the three words carry "forever" everywhere or only beside *olam* is
disputed: Rabbi Avraham ben Ezriel of Vienna limits the Gemara's ruling to
verses where they stand next to *olam*; Maharsha takes it as a general lexical
finding. Rabbi Menachem ibn Saruk (*Machaberes Menachem*) and Rabbi Yosef Kimchi
(*Sefer HaGilui*) treat them as flat synonyms; Kimchi leaves *selah* off the
list. Rashi's own account of Eruvin 54a is the simple one: they mean the same
because they appear together.

## Rendering guidance

- *Le'olam* in a *din*: render by the actual span ("for as long as he lives,"
  "until the Yovel") or write "forever" and gloss it, since Rashi's cap is the
  standard reading. Never let it stand as bare "forever" where the halachah
  turns on the limit.
- The triple in the berachah said just before Shema in the morning, נצח סלה ועד
  ("Your mercy and Your kindness shall not forsake us"): English "forever"
  will swallow all three. Wertheimer's grid gives the way out: "unceasingly,
  continually, for all eternity." That the words differ is precisely the dispute:
  Rabbi Avraham ben Natan HaYarchi and Abudraham held the phrase redundant and
  would say only *va'ed*; Rabbi Yitzchak Abuhab kept all three, the piling-up
  being the point.
- *Selah* in Tehillim: leave it as *selah*. Choosing "forever" (the Gemara) or a
  pause marker (Radak, Malbim) hides a real *machlokes*, and no English word
  holds both.
- Trap: "eternal" absorbs *olam* and *netzach* alike. Reserve it for *netzach*
  and give *olam* a phrase with time in it ("for all time," "for generations").
