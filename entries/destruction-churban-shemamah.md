# Destruction: churban / shemamah

## Bottom line

*Churban* is **destruction** in the sense of a place cut off from what kept it
alive; the buildings may be standing. *Shemamah* is **desolation**, ruin
visible to anyone who looks, and the stronger of the two. Vayikra 26:31 puts
both in one pasuk, so a translation must not use one English word twice:
"I will make your cities a ruin (*charbah*) and lay your sanctuaries waste
(*vahashimosi*)."

## Distinctions

### churban (חורבן), charbah (חרבה)
Root ח-ר-ב. Malbim: *churban* is the word used for the destruction of a built-up
urban area, and it is the milder of the pair. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (1740 to
1814) reads it differently and more sharply: *churban* names a thing that is not
damaged in itself but has been severed from something outside it that it needed
in order to thrive. A ghost town wholly intact but abandoned is a *churban*; it
is destroyed with nothing about it looking destroyed. On the same logic an arid
land is a *charbah*, its barrenness owing not to a defect in the soil but to its
being cut off from rain.

### shemamah (שממה)
Root ש-מ-ם. Malbim: the general term for the utter desolation of a land, and
more severe than *churban*. Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg (1785 to 1865):
*shemamah* is specifically destruction that shows, so a land or building
physically wrecked is *shamem*, because whoever sees it registers the ruin at
once. Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*) notes that the root carries two senses,
destruction and astonishment, the latter in *mishtomem*, a man taken aback.
Mecklenburg joins them: one who comes upon utter ruin stands dumbfounded at the
sight. *Shemamah* is destruction in the eye of the beholder.

### shahm (שם)
Ibn Ezra: *shahm* is not always the ordinary "there"; it can belong to ש-מ-ם
and mean desolation. Mecklenburg applies this to *al naharos Bavel sham
yashavnu gam bachinu* (Tehillim 137:1), where "there" is otherwise redundant,
since "by the rivers of Bavel we sat" already says where. On his reading *sham*
names their condition, not their location: they sat in desolation and wept.

## Rendering guidance

- Default renderings: *churban* "destruction," *charbah* "ruin" or "waste
  place," *shemamah* "desolation," *shamem* "laid waste" or "desolate."
- Trap: English "destruction" absorbs both words, and "devastation" absorbs both
  as well. Where they stand together (Vayikra 26:31), keep one for ח-ר-ב and one
  for ש-מ-ם, with the *shemamah* word the heavier of the two.
- *Churban HaBayis* is a fixed term. Leave it transliterated and gloss it once;
  "the Destruction" with a capital D is acceptable in running English where the
  reference is unmistakable.
- Where a sefer builds on Pappenheim's sense of *churban* as severance, do not
  render it "demolition." The point of the word there is precisely that nothing
  visible was demolished. Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin (*Nefesh HaChaim* 1:4) holds
  that the sinners of those generations were worse than Nevuchadnetzar and
  Titus, since their sins defiled the Heavenly Mikdash and severed the earthly
  building from its Heavenly counterpart, which is what let the armies come and
  destroy it. Read with Pappenheim, *Churban HaBayis* names that severance and
  *shemamah* names the rubble.
- *Mishtomem* in a mussar context is "stands astounded," not "is desolate";
  Radak's two senses share a root but not a rendering.
