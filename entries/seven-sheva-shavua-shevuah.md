# Seven: sheva / sheviis / shavua / shabbos / shevuah

## Bottom line

*Sheviis*, "the seventh," is the Sabbatical year, and it is the Mishnah's word
for it. *Shavua* is a **week**, but in Daniel and in several mishnayos it is a
**seven-year cycle**, so context decides. *Shabbos* is the day, and by
synecdoche the **week** it closes and the Sabbatical **year** itself (Ramban).
*Shevuah* is an **oath**, tied to *sheva* by every authority here, though they
part on why. Do not translate *sheva* as "seven" where the sources read it as
"many" (Rasag to Bereishis 4:24 and Mishlei 24:16).

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| שבע | sheva | seven, many | non-numeric in places: "much," "more and more" (Rasag) |
| שביעית | sheviis | the seventh year | the ordinal of *sheva*; already Biblical for the Sabbatical year |
| שבוע | shavua | week, seven-year cycle | any seven-day period, not the calendar week (Mecklenburg); seven years in Daniel |
| שבת | shabbos | Shabbos, week, Sabbatical year | part standing for whole (Ramban); for Mecklenburg the sense is cessation, never "week" |
| שבועה | shevuah | oath | binds the swearer under Hashem (Ibn Ezra, Rav Hirsch); strengthened by sevenfold repetition (*Sefer HaChinuch* 330) |
| שבויה | shevuyah | captive | the picture behind both the cycle and the oath: no freedom to move |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Does shabbos mean "week"?** In "you shall count seven complete *shabbasos*"
(Vayikra 23:15-16), Ibn Ezra and Ramban hold that the word carries two senses
within a few pesukim: the first two occurrences name a holy day, the first day of
Pesach, and the last two mean weeks. Targum Onkelos reflects exactly this,
rendering the first pair *yoma tava* and the second *shavua*. Ramban adds that
*shabbos* for "week" is common in rabbinic speech (*Taanis* 4:7, *Megillah* 3:4,
*Kesubos* 1:1, 5:6-7, *Eduyos* 4:10, *Nedarim* 8:1, *Bava Metzia* 9:11, *Bava
Basra* 5:10, *Sheviis* 2:6, *Niddah* 4:5), and calls the word a synecdoche: a day
within the week and the week entire, and likewise the Sabbatical year (Vayikra
25:2, 25:4, 25:6, 26:34-35; Divrei HaYamim II 36:21) and the seven-year cycle
that contains it (Vayikra 25:8). Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg objects that
nowhere else in Tanach does *shabbos* mean week, and reads it there in its plain
sense of ceasing: between the Omer on the second day of Pesach and the Two Loaves
on Shavuos no new grain is used in the Mikdash, so those weeks are themselves a
cessation. The Bottom line follows Ibn Ezra, Ramban, and Onkelos, since the
Targum is decisive for how the pasuk was read.

**Shabbos or shavua, when both mean week.** Mecklenburg: *shabbos* is
specifically the calendar week that runs from Sunday to Shabbos, which lends the
day's name to the whole; *shavua*, from *sheva*, is any seven-day stretch and
need not begin on Sunday. Rabbi Alter HaLevi Hilowitz takes *shavua* too as the
calendar week, and draws a consequence: if the *shavuos* counted to the festival
are calendar weeks, the count would always begin on a Sunday, which is the
Tzedoki position the Gemara rejects; on his account that is why Chazal preferred
to call the festival *Atzeres* rather than Shavuos. The Bottom line follows
Mecklenburg, on whose reading the difficulty does not arise.

**Why an oath is a shevuah.** Five accounts, none of them exclusive:

1. **Sevenfold strengthening.** *Sefer HaChinuch* (mitzvah 330): repeating a
   thing seven times strengthens it and marks it as permanent. Oaths of treaty
   were made with seven animals (Bereishis 21:23-33) or a multiple of seven
   (Divrei HaYamim II 15:11-15). Onkelos renders the oath verb at Bereishis 24:9
   with a cognate of *kiyum*, establishing. Compare Rava's ruling that saying
   "no, no" or "yes, yes" is an oath (*Shevuos* 36a), which Rashi explains as
   strengthening by repetition.
2. **Hashem is the Seventh.** Ibn Ezra, building on *Sefer Yetzirah* 4:2 that
   the first six numbers are the six directions and the seventh is Hashem at the
   center: to swear is to tie one's claim to His existence, as though saying,
   just as Hashem is true, so is this. Ibn Ezra (to Bereishis 21:31) and Ramban
   (to Bamidbar 30:3) both note the link briefly without explaining it.
3. **Delivering oneself into His hand.** Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 21:23): since
   the world was made in seven days, seven is the number of the Creator, and the
   one who swears places himself under His direct control, to be dealt with
   justly if he is lying.
4. **Captivity.** Mecklenburg, Pappenheim, and Rav Hirsch all connect *shevuah*
   to *shevuyah*, a captive: the sworn man is bound and cannot move freely.
5. **Satiation.** Rabbi Moshe Elyakim Briah Hopstein: *shevuah* is akin to
   *seviah*, being satisfied, since swearing in Hashem's name is a way of
   clinging to Him, and the righteous are not satisfied until they cling to Him
   entirely. Rabbi Yitzchok Avigdor Lubchansky adds that the mitzvah to swear in
   Hashem's name (Devarim 6:13, 10:20) is the seventh positive commandment in
   Rambam's reckoning.

**Is sheva always the number?** Rasag's *Tafsir* renders Lemech's "sevenfold has
Kayin been avenged, and Lemech seventy-seven" (Bereishis 4:24) as "much... more
and more," and *sheva yipol tzaddik vakam* (Mishlei 24:16) as "many"; Ibn Ezra
and Ralbag read Mishlei the same way. Mecklenburg likewise takes the sevenfold
punishments of Vayikra 26:18, 26:24, 26:28 as recurring punishment rather than a
count.

## Distinctions

### sheva (שבע) and sheviis (שביעית)
*Sheviis* is simply the ordinal, the seventh year of a seven-year cycle, and it
is Biblical for the Sabbatical year (Shemos 23:11; Vayikra 25:4, 25:20; Devarim
15:12; Nechemiah 10:32) as well as the Mishnah's standing term and the name of
the masechta.

### shavua (שבוע)
In Tanach usually a week of seven days (Bereishis 29:27-28; Vayikra 12:5;
Devarim 16:9), and so in the Mishnah (*Bava Metzia* 9:11, *Erachin* 2:1, and
throughout *Negaim*). But in Daniel it is always a period of seven years (4:24,
9:25-27, 10:2-3), and so in several mishnayos (*Sheviis* 4:7-9, *Bava Metzia*
9:10, *Sanhedrin* 5:1, *Makkos* 1:10, *Parah* 8:9), as Rashi repeatedly points
out (*Pesachim* 12a, 86a; *Moed Katan* 12a; *Sotah* 44a; *Bava Metzia* 110b;
*Sanhedrin* 97a).

### The root
Mecklenburg traces both ש-ב-ת and ש-ב-ע to the two-letter ש-ב, whose sense is
returning; the ayin is not one of Pappenheim's radical letters, but the Aramaic
*shav*, seven, is *sheva* without it, which leaves room for the derivation.
Hence a *shevuyah*, a captive, caught in a closed system that keeps returning to
its start, and hence *shabbos* and *shavua* as cycles of time that restart on
reaching seven, whether of days, weeks, years, or, as the medieval mekubalim
have it, millennia. Pappenheim arrives at the same place from the other side: one
who has returned home no longer moves toward anything, so *shabbos* as cessation
grows out of returning, and a captive is forced into that same stillness.

Mecklenburg holds that seven's cyclical importance is embedded in creation, since
the week answers to no astronomical cycle as day, month, and year do; he points
to the seven musical notes, and Rabbi Moshe Zuriel to the seven colors of the
rainbow, to which the article adds the seven visible planets, the seven letters
that take a *dagesh*, and the seven openings of the head (*Sefer Yetzirah* 4:3),
while granting that notes and colors could be divided otherwise. The Shelah
closes the theme: Hashem made the world in six days and rested on the seventh to
teach a man to use his seventy years (Tehillim 90:10) and waste none of them, and
the mitzvos built on seven, the seven days of *chametz*, the seven days of *sheva
berachos*, carry the same reminder.

## Rendering guidance

- *Sheviis* and *Shemittah* both name the Sabbatical year; keep whichever the
  source uses rather than standardizing, since the two words say different things
  about it (see the *shemittah* entry for the other half of this pair).
- Trap: English "week" absorbs *shavua* and *shabbos* both, and in Vayikra 23
  the same English word would then render *shabbos* in two different senses
  within four pesukim. Follow Onkelos: "the festival day" for the first pair,
  "weeks" for the second.
- Second trap: *shavua* in Daniel and in *Sanhedrin* 5:1 is seven years, not
  seven days. Check what is being counted before writing "week."
- *Shevuah* is "oath," not "vow"; *neder* is the vow (see the *neder*/*shevuah*
  entry). Where a derashah turns on *shevuah* and *sheva* together, keep the
  Hebrew, since no English pair carries it. Where *sheva* or *shivasayim* means
  "many," say "many"; writing "seven" imports an arithmetic the sources deny.
