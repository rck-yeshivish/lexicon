# Enemy: oyev / soneh / tzar

## Bottom line

*Oyev* is the enemy who acts, or at least plans to act: **enemy**, **foe**.
*Soneh* is hatred held inside, shown by what a man refrains from doing:
**hater**. *Tzar* (*tzorer*) is open, self-costing hostility: **adversary**,
**persecutor**. Bamidbar 10:35 pairs them, and on Malbim's reading Moshe davens
there that both kinds of Hashem's enemies be vanquished.

## Distinctions

### oyev (אויב)
An enemy who actively tries to harm the object of his hatred, or has at least
entertained doing so. Malbim: even where the *oyev* makes no attempt at damage,
he will not hold back his gladness should misfortune strike his victim, because
he has already pictured himself causing it. Malbim on the *oyev* of Hashem: one
who actively denies His existence and opposes His Godliness. As for a *soneh* of
Hashem, the column adds that it would seem he does so only in his heart and not
in practice.

The *meforshim* add that the *oyev*, for all his activity, still tries to hide
his hatred beneath a façade of empathy. Like the *soneh*, he is a clandestine
enemy, not an overt one.

### soneh (שונא)
From *sinah*, hatred. The *soneh* is a hidden enemy: his hatred stays internal
and is not expressed outwardly, remaining in the realm of the theoretical. This
is the word of "*lo sisna
es achicha bilvavecha*" (Vayikra 19:17), where mere hatred of a fellow Jew is
forbidden whether or not it ever turns into action. His disposition is read
from what he does **not** do: the Mishnah (Sanhedrin 3:5), disqualifying a
*soneh* from testifying, defines him as one who has not spoken to his fellow
out of enmity for three days.

The Vilna Gaon draws the line differently, by target rather than by expression:
the *oyev* wants to injure his victim's physical existence (his body, his
money), the *soneh* his spiritual existence, to pull him off the path of
Hashem. On that reading the *soneh* is the more dangerous of the two, counter to
what one would assume.

### tzar / tzorer (צר / צורר)
The overt enemy. Where the *oyev* conceals his enmity, the *tzar* hates with
such heat that he attacks openly and will spend his own reputation and his own
resources to do it. Haman is called *tzorer haYehudim* (Esther 9:10).

## Rendering guidance

- Bamidbar 10:35, *kumah Hashem veyafutzu oyvecha veyanusu mesanecha mipanecha*:
  render as two distinct classes, "let Your enemies be scattered and let those
  who hate You flee," not "enemies... enemies." On Malbim's reading Moshe is
  davening that both kinds of Hashem's enemies be vanquished, the one who fights
  Him in deed and the one who, it would seem, only despises Him in his heart, so
  that His glory should keep spreading unimpeded.
- Vayikra 19:17: *soneh* is a verb here, "hate," and the whole force of the
  *lav* is that no act is required. Never soften it to "be hostile to."
- Trap: English "enemy" absorbs *oyev*, *soneh* and *tzar*. Keep "enemy" for
  *oyev*, "hater" (or "one who hates him") for *soneh*, and "adversary" or
  "persecutor" for *tzar*/*tzorer*.
- Where a passage follows the Vilna Gaon, the gloss has to shift: *oyev* is then
  "an enemy of his body and property," *soneh* "an enemy of his soul." Do not
  mix the Gaon's division with Malbim's inside one rendering.
