# Enemy: oyev / soneh / tzar

## Bottom line

An *oyev* is an **enemy** or **foe** who actively tries to harm his victim, or
at least contemplates doing so. A *soneh* is a **hater** whose hatred stays
inside, shown by what he refrains from doing. A *tzar* (*tzorer*) is an
**adversary** or **persecutor**, who attacks openly and will spend his own
reputation and resources to do it. Rabbi Yaakov Emden reverses the first two:
the *soneh* hates openly, in speech, and the *oyev* buries his enmity in his
heart and holds it forever, like Esav. The authorities differ, too, over which
is the graver: for the Vilna Gaon it is the *soneh*, who aims at the soul, and
for Shadal the *oyev*. Bamidbar 10:35 pairs *oyev* and *soneh*, and Rabbi Klein
concludes that Moshe davens there that both kinds of Hashem's enemies be
vanquished.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Which of the two hides his hatred.**

- The *oyev* acts, or has at least pictured himself causing harm (Malbim),
  while the *soneh* holds his hatred inside, read from what he refrains from
  doing.
- Rabbi Yaakov Emden (*Eim LaBinah* to Shemos 23:22): the reverse. The *soneh*
  hates openly, in speech (see Bereishis 37:4); the *oyev* keeps his enmity
  buried in his heart and holds the grudge forever, like Esav (see Yechezkel
  25:15).

**Which is the graver.**

- The Vilna Gaon: the *soneh*, who aims at his victim's spiritual existence,
  where the *oyev* injures body and money.
- Shadal (to Shemos 23:5): the *oyev*, the stronger hatred; the Torah obliges one
  to return a *soneh*'s lost object but not an *oyev*'s, since facing him one may
  fear for one's life.

## Distinctions

### oyev (אויב)
An enemy who actively tries to harm the object of his hatred, or has at least
entertained doing so. Malbim: even where the *oyev* makes no attempt at damage,
he will not hold back his gladness should misfortune strike his victim, because
he has already pictured himself causing it. Malbim on the *oyev* of Hashem: one
who actively denies His existence and opposes His Godliness. As for a *soneh* of
Hashem, it would seem to Rabbi Klein that he does so only in his heart and not
in practice.

The *meforshim* add that the *oyev*, for all his activity, still tries to hide
his hatred beneath a façade of empathy. Like the *soneh*, he is a clandestine
enemy, not an overt one.

Shadal (to Shemos 23:5): *oyev* is a stronger hatred than *soneh*. The Torah
obliges one to return a *soneh*'s lost object but exempts him from returning an
*oyev*'s, since facing an *oyev* one may justly fear for one's life.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 27:41-42): *sitmah*, from
ש-ט-ם, is hatred related to *satam*, sealed: hatred shut up in the heart while
on the surface one pretends to be appeased, as Esav did with Yaakov.

### soneh (שונא)
From *sinah*, hatred. The *soneh* is a hidden enemy: his hatred stays internal
and is not expressed outwardly, remaining in the realm of the theoretical. This
is the word of "*lo sisna
es achicha bilvavecha*" (Vayikra 19:17), where mere hatred of a fellow Jew is
forbidden whether or not it ever turns into action. His disposition is read
from what he does **not** do: the Mishnah (Sanhedrin 3:5), disqualifying a
*soneh* from testifying, defines him as one who has not spoken to his fellow
out of enmity for three days.

The Vilna Gaon draws the line slightly differently, by target rather than by expression:
the *oyev* wants to injure his victim's physical existence (his body, his
money), the *soneh* his spiritual existence, to pull him off the path of
Hashem. On that reading the *soneh* is the more dangerous of the two, counter to
what one would assume.

Rabbi Yaakov Emden (*Eim LaBinah* to Shemos 23:22): the *soneh* hates openly, in
speech (see Bereishis 37:4), while the *oyev* keeps his enmity buried in his
heart and holds the grudge forever, like Esav (see Yechezkel 25:15).

### tzar / tzorer (צר / צורר)
The overt enemy. Where the *oyev* conceals his enmity, the *tzar* hates with
such heat that he attacks openly and will spend his own reputation and his own
resources to do it. Haman is called *tzorer haYehudim* (Esther 9:10).

## Rendering guidance

- Bamidbar 10:35, *kumah Hashem veyafutzu oyvecha veyanusu mesanecha mipanecha*:
  render as two distinct classes, "let Your enemies be scattered and let those
  who hate You flee," not "enemies... enemies." On Rabbi Klein's reading Moshe is
  davening that both kinds of Hashem's enemies be vanquished, the one who fights
  Him in deed and the one who, it would seem, only despises Him in his heart, so
  that His glory should keep spreading unimpeded.
- Vayikra 19:17: *soneh* is a verb here, "hate," and the whole force of the
  *lav* is that no act is required. Never soften it to "be hostile to."
- Trap: English "enemy" absorbs *oyev*, *soneh* and *tzar*. Keep "enemy" for
  *oyev*, "hater" (or "one who hates him") for *soneh*, and "adversary" or
  "persecutor" for *tzar*/*tzorer*.
- Where a passage follows the Vilna Gaon, the gloss has to shift: *oyev* is then
  "an enemy of his body and property," *soneh* "an enemy of his soul." Do not
  mix the Gaon's division with Malbim's inside one rendering.
