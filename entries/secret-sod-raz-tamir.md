# Secret: sod / raz / tamir (kamus, dvar seter)

## Bottom line

*Sod* in Tanach is **counsel** or **council** (the circle of advisors and the
advice they give: Radak, who disagrees with Ibn Janach's split into two distinct
senses and holds the two are really one); only in Chazal does
it become **secret**, the confidence shared with a few. The Biblical word for a
secret proper is *dvar seter*, **a hidden matter** (Pappenheim). *Kamus* is
**stored away, sealed up** (Devarim 32:34, once only); for Rav Hirsch, classified information.
*Raz* (Aramaic, Daniel) is **mystery**, an idea closed off from the many
(Rashi: *seter*, *satum*). *Tamir* is Aramaic **hidden**, Targum's word for
many Hebrew hiding-words. In a pasuk, סוד is
usually "counsel" or "assembly," not "secret"; in a Gemara
it is "secret."

## Distinctions

### sod (סוד)
More than twenty times in Tanach, and in none of them quite the "secret" of
modern usage. Root ס-ו-ד (Ibn Janach, Radak) or the biliteral SAMECH-DALET (Ibn
Saruk, Pappenheim).
- Ibn Janach (*Sefer HaShorashim*): two distinct senses, a gathering of people
  (Amos 3:7, Hashem does nothing without revealing His *sod* to His servants the
  prophets) and advice (Mishlei 15:22, plans are cancelled without *sod*).
- Radak (*Shorashim*) disagrees with that formulation and holds the two are
  really one: the *sod* is the council one takes counsel from, an understanding
  analogous to the English "counsel/council." Hence the piyut rubric *misod
  chachamim u'nevonim* means "from the counsel of the earlier sages" who
  recommended adding the poetic compositions, not from any untold secret. The
  Rabbinic sense follows: only one's closest advisors are privy to one's
  innermost plans, so *sod* expanded to mean any restricted or confidential
  matter (Eruvin 65a and Sanhedrin 38a, "when the wine enters, the *sod* comes
  out"; Pesachim 49b, one of six things said of an ignoramus is that one should
  not reveal one's *sod* to him; Chagigah 14a, Hashem will reveal the *sod* in
  the World to Come to Torah scholars who deprive themselves of sleep in this
  one; Yevamos 63b, reveal your *sod* to one in a thousand), and *sod ha'ibbur*
  (Rosh HaShanah 20b, Kesubos 112a) is calendar lore taught in *remazim* (Rashi
  to Rosh HaShanah 20b).
- Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 49:6): SAMECH/ZAYIN/TZADI-VAV-DALET, linked by the
  interchange of samech, zayin and tzadi, all mean a plan fomenting in the
  depths of thought: *nazid* the dish left to cook (Bereishis 25:29, Melachim II
  4:38), the longer on the flame the more developed; *meizid* the premeditated
  act cooked up in the mind beforehand; *tzad* the trap carried out; *sod* the
  plan shared with others for mutual consultation.

Pappenheim: SAMECH-DALET is "foundation," most obviously *yesod* and *mossad*;
in the abstract a *yesod* is an axiomatic given of intellectual discourse, and
the less-apparent conclusions drawn from those *yesodos* are *sodos*. He adds
that *sod* may also name whatever tools one uses to draw those conclusions,
whether wise advisors or one's own reasoning, and since such conclusions are
usually confined to one's own mind or at most to a small group of counselors
they come to be thought of as secretive. Under the same root he puts the
Mishnaic *sadan*, the trunk of a tree, the main branch from which it grows
(Kilayim 1:8, 6:4; Shabbos 12:1; Bava Basra 4:9; Sanhedrin 7:3); *sadin*,
bedding or linen, four times in Tanach (Shoftim 14:12-13, Yeshayahu 3:23,
Mishlei 31:24), the sheet spread on a bed as the foundation for body, pillow
and blanket; *sadeh*, a field, the most fundamental unit of topographical and
agricultural space, as against anomalous mountains and hills and outlying
barren land; and from *sadeh* the word *sid*, plaster, made by burning stones
found in the field.

The Biblical *sad* is literally the ropes and chains that tie down a prisoner
(Iyov 13:27, 33:11).

- Pappenheim, following Rashi to Iyov 13:27, takes it as the tree trunks
  prisoners were tied to, and relates *sadan* to it.
- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg goes the other way and makes the tying sense the
  core of SAMECH-DALET: a *sod* as a secret is information tied down so it cannot
  escape its purview, and a *sod* as a council is a body of men tied together in
  a common advisory role.

### dvar seter (דבר סתר) and kamus (כמוס)
Pappenheim claims that although Biblical *sod* does not quite mean secret, the
true Biblical word for a secret is *dvar seter*, Ehud's "hidden matter" for
Eglon (Shoftim 3:19), where Ehud implied he had a secret to reveal and in fact
had a dagger ready; the second word goes to SAMECH-TAV-REISH, hiding. *Kamus*,
KAF-MEM-SAMECH, is a hapax: "For is it not *kamus* with Me, sealed in My
storehouses" (Devarim 32:34), said of the deeds for which Israel will be
punished while the nations fail to see why, so that Hashem alone is privy to
what they will really have done and really deserve. A possible cognate appears
in the Mishnah (Peah 5:8), where sheaves bundled into *kumsaos*, stack bases,
are exempted from the rule that bundles forgotten in the field are left for the
poor.
- Rav Hirsch (to Shemos 35:22, Vayikra 2:2, Devarim 32:34), implicitly invoking
  the interchange of kaf and kuf and of samech and tzadi, joins *kamus* to
  *kamatz*, gathered and minimized, both of them something stored away and
  protected: in the case of *kamus*, protected classified information kept from
  those who do not need to know.
- Rabbi Avi Kobernick similarly reads it against its metathesis SAMECH-MEM-KAF,
  next to or adjacent, arguing that closely guarded secrets are kept close and
  not revealed indiscriminately.
- Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein once theorized, as his own view, that the mem of
  KAF-MEM-SAMECH is not integral and the core etymon is the biliteral
  KAF-SAMECH, cover, since what is covered becomes hidden.

The compound *sod kamus*, a secretive secret, was coined by Rabbi Yitzchak Ibn
Ghiyyat in one of his poems, according to the Academy of the Hebrew Language,
and has only grown in popularity since.

### raz (רז, רזא)
Aramaic (*raz*, Daniel 4:6; *raza*, Daniel 2:18-47), possibly once in Hebrew
(Yeshayahu 24:16).
Rashi (to Daniel 2:19, 2:47; Sanhedrin 94a) glosses *seter*; to Daniel 4:6,
*satum*: either way an idea hidden and closed up so as to be inaccessible to the
masses. *Raz* is a very common word in the Dead Sea
Scrolls, used there for various mystical and esoteric allusions, and
linguists derive the Aramaic word from the Old Persian *raza*, itself related to
the Sanskrit *rahasya*, both said to go back to Proto-Indo-Iranian *rajh*.

- Pappenheim and Malbim (to Iyov 15:12) trace the Biblical *rezem*, hinting, to
  the biliteral REISH-ZAYIN, "hidden." As Pappenheim explains it, a wink of the
  eyes or a gesture tells those in the know that one is privy to a secret others
  are not. Pappenheim says this only of *rezem*, which is Biblical; Rabbi Reuven
  Chaim Klein finds it not much of a stretch to extend it to the Mishnaic *remez*
  (Gittin 5:7) as well.
- Pappenheim and Malbim also derive *roznim*, noblemen and ministers (Shoftim
  5:3; Yeshayahu 40:23; Tehillim 2:2; Mishlei 8:15, 14:28, 31:4), from the same
  root, since such aristocrats were often privy to information the common people
  were not (see also Malbim to Mishlei 14:28 and *Shoresh Yesha*).
- Rav Hirsch (to Tehillim 2:2) instead connects *roznim* to *resen*, the
  mouthpiece put into a horse's or donkey's mouth to control it, through the
  interchange of samech and zayin: the *roznim* lead the government just as the
  *resen* leads an animal.

Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein suggests, as his own proposal, reading *raz* as a
metathesis of *zar*, strange or foreign, since the information is withheld from
others and so estranged from them. Rabbi Dov Kook of Tiberias mentions that the
gematria of *raz*/*zar* equals that of *rahav*, the angelic representative of
Egypt, and of *bahar*, "at the mountain," the term for the mitzvos given at Har
Sinai; Rabbi Klein says plainly that he has not merited to understand the secret
behind those connections.

Rabbi Eliyahu HaBachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) writes that the angel Raziel (named in
many Kabbalistic sources; *Raziel HaMalach*, a book ascribed to Adam HaRishon,
discusses secrets Adam learned from him) takes his name from the Aramaic *raz*, alluding to his role as
Hashem's secretary of sorts. (The English "secretary" itself derives from
"secret.") The Chassidei Ashkenaz read *zer*, a
diadem, and the second syllable of *mashzar*, interwoven, in the account of the
Mishkan as allusions to the Kabbalistic *razin* contained there (*Tosafos
HaShalem* to Shemos 25:10 §9, 25:11 §6 and §16, 25:25 §6, 39:2 §1).

### tamir (טמיר)
Aramaic. Targum uses it for *tamun* (Bereishis 35:4; Shemos 2:12; Yirmiyahu
13:5), *kisui* (Iyov 36:32), *seter* (Tehillim 10:11, 27:5, 32:7, 139:15), and
*mischabeh* (Shmuel I 10:22); Targum Yonasan (to Devarim 32:34) renders *kamus*
as *tumrah*.

- Rabbi Ernest Klein proposes that the Hebrew root ט-מ-ן, hidden or buried,
  actually derives from the Aramaic ט-מ-ר, with nun and reish interchanging as
  they often do.
- Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein adds that it is equally possible the opposite is true
  and the Aramaic derives from the Hebrew.
- Rabbi Ernest Klein also relates the English *mattamore*, a subterranean
  dwelling or storehouse, to this Hebrew and Aramaic term, by way of French and
  Arabic.

The Maggid who spoke to Rabbi Yosef Karo consistently called the Provençal
posek Rabbeinu Yerucham "Yerucham Tamiri," Yerucham the Hidden One (*Maggid
Meisharim* to Vayakhel and Tzav). The Chida writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham's
published works are riddled with typographical errors and other mistakes, and
relates that he heard from the elder sages of Yerushalayim that Kabbalistically
those works belong to "the secret of the Hidden World," such that whoever writes
a commentary to elucidate them will have it destroyed before publication or will
die in his prime. Rabbi Elazar Lipa Gartenhaus points out that both came true
for Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein's own great-great-great-grandfather and namesake,
Rabbi Reuven Chaim Klein of Davidkoff, who published *Shenos Chaim*, a commentary on the first part of
Rabbeinu Yerucham's *Toldos Adam v'Chava*: he lost the rest of what he had
written before he could publish it, and he died at forty-seven.

## Rendering guidance

- סוד in Tanach: "counsel," "council," "assembly" (סוד ה' ליראיו, "the counsel
  of Hashem is for those who fear Him"). Check Radak before writing "secret."
- סוד in Chazal and mussar: "secret"; סודות התורה "the secrets of the Torah";
  בסוד "in confidence." A whispered-among-a-few nuance is in the word (Ralash
  in the hidden entry: *sod* is spoken quietly, *seter* known to one alone).
- דבר סתר: "a secret matter," "a private word." סתר alone: "secrecy," "hiding."
- כמוס: "stored away," "sealed up," "kept secret"; סוד כמוס "a closely guarded
  secret."
- רז: "mystery" (of Daniel, of Kabbalah); רזין דאורייתא "the mysteries of the
  Torah." Keep "mystery" for רז and "secret" for סוד so the two stay distinct.
- רמז: "hint," "allusion"; never "secret," though the root is the same.
- טמיר: "hidden," "concealed"; טמיר ונעלם "hidden and unknowable."
- Trap: "secret" absorbs סוד, סתר, כמוס, and רז. In a list or a doubled pair
  (סוד ורז, נסתר וכמוס) give each its own English: counsel/secret, hidden,
  sealed up, mystery.
