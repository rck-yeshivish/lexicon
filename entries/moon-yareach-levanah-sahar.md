# Moon: yareach / levanah / sahar

## Bottom line

Biblical Hebrew has three words for the moon, and Rabbi Aharon Marcus explains
that each names it by a different aspect. *Yareach* is the **moon** as the
traveler on its path; *levanah* is the **moon** as the white one; *sahar* is
the **crescent**, the moon named by its shape, though others note it is an
Aramaic loanword sometimes used for the moon. Rabbi Marcus adds that the words
may also represent different stages in the history of man's relationship with
the moon. The three words for the sun, *shemesh*, *chamah* and *cheres*,
somewhat parallel these three, each naming the sun by a different aspect in the
same way.

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether *sahar* is Hebrew.**

- Rabbi Aharon Marcus counts it as the third Hebrew word for the moon, the one
  that names it by its shape.
- Others hold *sahar* is not really Hebrew but an Aramaic loanword sometimes
  used for the moon.

On Rabbi Marcus's view the word carries the crescent shape; on the loanword view
it is simply a word sometimes used for the moon.

**The root of *yareach*.**

- The classical lexicographers Menachem Ibn Saruk, Yonah Ibn Janach and Radak
  trace *yareach* to the triliteral root י-ר-ח, and *reiach*, smell, to a
  different root, ר-י-ח.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim sees both as derivatives of the biliteral ר-ח, air,
  whose commonest form is *ruach*, wind: the moon is called *yareach* because
  its movements somehow control the *ruach*, the tides and winds.
- Rabbi Aharon Marcus relates *yareach* to *orach*, a path, for the moon's
  orbital path in the heavens.

**Whether the staged history is admissible.** Rabbi Aharon Marcus holds that the
words may also represent different stages in the history of man's relationship
with the sun and moon. Rabbi Klein notes that some might reject this historical
aspect of the explanation, because it would seem to contradict the Divine origin
of the Hebrew language, and answers that the basic argument still holds: the
three words for the sun and the three for the moon somewhat parallel each other
in their focus on different aspects of the sun and the moon.

## Distinctions

### The moon is unnamed at first
In the account of creation (Bereishis 1) *yareach* does not appear. The Torah
calls the moon only the small luminary, as if to say that its whole purpose is
to give light and that it carries no importance of its own. On Rabbi Aharon
Marcus's reading, that is the point: at first the moon had no name at all.

### yareach (ירח)
The first word coined, and coined against idolatry. When, as the generations
progressed, people began to worship the moon under the name Asherah, early
monotheists who fought idolatry coined a name that identifies it yet downplays
its importance: *yareach*, related to *orach*, a path, for the moon's orbital
path in the heavens. In time *yareach* itself came to be used as the name of
the idolatrous moon-god, and archeologists have identified the worship of
Yarikh in ancient Canaan and Mesopotamia. This is what necessitated the coining
of the next words. A place-name reflecting the old practice is still in use:
Yericho, related to *yareach*, was apparently an ancient center of the moon cult
before the Jewish People entered Eretz Yisrael.

### levanah (לבנה) and sahar (סהר)
The second layer: *levanah* and *sahar* for the moon's perceived color and
shape. In short, *yareach* focuses on the moon's orbital path, *levanah* on its
color, *sahar* on its shape. In time these words too, unsurprisingly, came to be
associated with celestial idolatry, as worshippers of the moon used ritual
objects in their cults: Yeshayahu (3:18) criticizes the people for wearing
*saharonim*, talisman necklaces shaped like the moon.

Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim* s.v. לבן): the moon is called *levanah* because it
looks as though it has a whiteness of its own.

Rabbi Doniel Baron: *levanah* is from *lavan*, white. An object seen as a
particular color absorbs every wavelength of visible light but that one, which
it reflects; what reflects every wavelength is seen as *lavan*, so *lavan* is
total reflection. The *levanah* reflects the light of the sun, and even before
it shrank its purpose was solely to reflect.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 38:17): *levanah* is the
source of Latin *luna*, the v and u sounds interchanging in Latin.

Rabbi Eliyahu Benamozegh (*Eim LaMikra* to Bereishis 18:1 and 38:17): *sahara*
has Indo-European parallels in Persian *sohre* and Sanskrit *souhra*, meaning
moon.

### The parallel with the sun
Rabbi Aharon Marcus reads the three words for the
[sun](sun-shemesh-chamah-cheres.html) the same way, and the two sets somewhat
parallel each other. The sun too is called only the great luminary at creation.
Its first name, *shemesh*, the servant, was coined in the same fight against
idolatry as *yareach*, and like *yareach* it was later taken up as the name of a
god, Shammash. Its later names, *chamah* and *cheres*, name it by its heat and
by pottery, as *levanah* and *sahar* name the moon by its color and its shape,
and they too came to be tied to idolatry, in the *chaman* of Vayikra 26:30.

## Rendering guidance

- Default: "the moon" for all three. The aspects are etymological, and forcing
  them into every rendering makes the English strange.
- Trap: English has one word for the moon, so a pasuk that uses two of the
  Hebrew words, or sets one of them against a word for the sun, will collapse
  into "the moon" or "the sun and the moon"; where the choice of word matters,
  name the aspect.
- *Sahar*: "crescent" or "crescent moon" on Rabbi Marcus's reading, where shape
  is what the word contributes; on the view that it is an Aramaic loanword for
  the moon, plain "moon" is defensible.
- *Yerach*, the month, is the same root as *yareach* and is treated in
  [month](month-chodesh-yerach.html); for *lavan*, white, see
  [white](white-lavan-chivar-tzochar.html), and for the place-name,
  [Jericho](jericho-yereicho-yericho.html).
