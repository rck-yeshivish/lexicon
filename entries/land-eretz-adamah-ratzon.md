# Land: eretz / adamah (and ratzon)

## Bottom line

*Eretz* is **land** and *adamah* is **ground**, and this article grounds the
difference in the two roots: *eretz* from REISH-TZADI, running, and *adamah* from
DALET-MEM, likeness and so inertness. *Eretz* is therefore the active land, and
often Eretz Yisrael in particular; *adamah* the passive ground, and often the
lands outside it. *Ratzon* belongs to the same root as *eretz*: it is a person's
**will** at its deepest, as against *chefetz*, the immediate goal in front of
him. The trap is "earth," which will take both Hebrew words at once.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| ארץ | eretz | land | root REISH-TZADI, running; the land that runs to do Hashem's will (Bereishis Rabbah 5:8) |
| אדמה | adamah | ground | root DALET-MEM, whence *domem*, inert; passive while its plants are active (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| רצון | ratzon | will | the ultimate will, not the immediate *chefetz* (Rav Tzvi Yehuda Kook) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**What the "running" in *eretz* is.** Five readings, all working from the same
root.

1. The land's own eagerness. Bereishis Rabbah 5:8: at creation the land *wanted*
   (*ratzah*) to do Hashem's *ratzon*, and so *ran* to cover as much surface as
   it could until He told it to stop. Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2, ch. 43)
   reads *eretz* from *ritzah* the same way, with the mashal of a king's relative
   who comes running at the summons and is granted a fiefdom.
2. Astronomical motion. Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): the planet never stops
   moving.
3. Running toward its source. The Netziv: *eretz* is the physical side of
   creation as against the spiritual *shamayim*; what draws its power from
   elsewhere runs continually toward what powers it.
4. Man running to it. Rabbi Toviah ben Eliezer, *Midrash Lekach Tov* (to
   Bereishis 1:1): everyone is running to the *eretz*, since all are buried in
   it in the end.
5. Man running on it. *Midrash Mishlei* ch. 8: the land is called *eretz*
   because people run about on it, the board on which a man plays out what he
   really wants.

Nothing in translation turns on the choice; the readings matter for a derashah.

**Whether *eretz* and *adamah* split by place or by depth.** Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi
Mecklenburg: in many places in the Torah an unqualified *eretz* is the Holy Land
and *adamah* is the Diaspora, and the roots explain why. Building on Rabbi Shlomo
Pappenheim, he reads *adamah* as the passive word, and applies it to lands where
one is sedentary as regards the agricultural mitzvos, which bind only in Eretz
Yisrael; *eretz*, the word of persistence and motion, fits the Land where one
must be constantly active to keep them. Against this stands the depth reading
recorded in the column's earlier piece on these two words: *adamah* is the top
layer of the crust and *eretz* reaches deeper. Rav Tzvi Yehuda Kook's account
sides with depth: *eretz*, like *ratzon*, takes in what lies beneath the surface,
while *adamah*, from DALET-MEM, likeness, is the word of the surface, the
neighbour of *dimyon*, imagination, what merely resembles the real thing.

## Distinctions

### eretz (ארץ)
Where a triliteral root opens with an ALEPH, the ALEPH is often outside the true
biliteral root, and that is the move every account here makes: *eretz* rests on
REISH-TZADI, the root of *ratz*, *ratzah*, and *ratzon*. Rabbi Shaul Mortera of
Amsterdam extends the family to *shoretz*, spreading out, and so to *sheretz*,
whether by the *shiphel* form or on the principle that any letter may join a
two-letter root.

### adamah (אדמה)
Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2): the land is called *adamah* because Adam was
created from it. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim works from the biliteral DALET-MEM,
likeness (*domeh*), which by its nature implies incongruity, since things merely
alike are not the same; from there *domem*, still or inactive, where inner
activity continues but outward activity has stopped. *Adamah* belongs to that
branch: the setting in which plants grow and are active while the ground itself
stays passive.

### ratzon (רצון) and chefetz (חפץ)
Rav Tzvi Yehuda Kook: *ratzon* is a person's final goal, his real will
underneath; *chefetz* is the immediate, tangible one, which is why Mishnaic
Hebrew calls physical objects *chafetzim* (see Rashi to Koheles 3:1). The pair
belongs in this entry because *ratzon* shares the root of *eretz*, and the same
contrast of depth against surface runs through both pairs.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "earth" absorbs *eretz* and *adamah* together, and "land" will
  take *adamah* if you let it. Hold "land" for *eretz* and "ground" or "soil" for
  *adamah*.
- Where an unqualified *eretz* stands in a Torah passage about mitzvos of the
  Land, "the Land" with a capital is usually right, and *adamah* in the same
  passage is "the ground," not a second name for the same place.
- A derashah on *eretz* and *ratz* cannot survive in English. Transliterate and
  gloss: "*eretz*, land, from *ratz*, running."
- *Ratzon* against *chefetz*: "will" and "desire" will not carry the distinction
  on their own; where a passage turns on it, say "ultimate will" for *ratzon* and
  keep "desire" or "object" for *chefetz*.
