# Land: eretz / adamah (and ratzon)

## Bottom line

*Eretz* is **land** and *adamah* is **ground**, and the difference lies in the
two roots: *eretz* from REISH-TZADI, running, and *adamah* from
DALET-MEM, likeness and so inertness. *Eretz* is therefore the active land, and
often Eretz Yisrael in particular; *adamah* the passive ground, and often the
lands outside it. *Ratzon* belongs to the same root as *eretz*: it is a person's
**will** at its deepest, as against *chefetz*, the immediate goal in front of
him.

Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 1, ch. 37) teaches that as there are seven heavens
so there are seven words for the earth: *eretz*, *adamah*, *arka*, *charavah*,
*yabashah*, *taivel* and *cheled*. Of these, this entry treats *eretz* and
*adamah*.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |

## Where the authorities disagree

**What the "running" in *eretz* is.** Five readings, all working from the same
root.

1. The land's own eagerness. Bereishis Rabbah 5:8: at creation the land *wanted*
   (*ratzah*) to do Hashem's *ratzon*, and so *ran* to cover as much surface as
   it could until He told it to stop. Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2, ch. 43)
   reads *eretz* from *ritzah* the same way, taking the connection to highlight
   the land's role as Hashem's loyal servant, with the mashal of a king's
   relative who comes running at the summons and is granted a fiefdom.
2. Astronomical motion. Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): the planet never stops
   moving.
3. Running toward its source. The Netziv: *eretz* is the physical side of
   creation as against the spiritual *shamayim*; what draws its power from
   elsewhere runs continually toward what powers it.
4. Man running to it. Rabbi Toviah ben Eliezer, *Midrash Lekach Tov* (to
   Bereishis 1:1): everyone is running to the *eretz*, since all are buried in
   it in the end.
5. Man running on it. *Midrash Mishlei* ch. 8: the land is called *eretz*
   because people run about on it; Rabbi Klein takes this to mean that the land
   is the board on which a man plays out what he really wants.

**Whether *eretz* and *adamah* split by place or by depth.** In many places in
the Torah an unqualified *eretz* is the Holy Land and *adamah* the Diaspora.

- Rabbi Yaakov Tzvi Mecklenburg sheds light on why the roots should fall that
  way. Building on Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim, he reads *adamah* as the passive
  word, and applies it to lands outside the Holy Land, where one stays sedentary
  and inactive as regards the agricultural mitzvos, which bind only there;
  *eretz* he derives from REISH-TZADI in the sense of fluency or persistency, an
  allusion to the earth's persistent orbit around the sun and its rotation on
  its axis, and so the word for the Land where one must be constantly active and
  vigilant to keep those mitzvos, the opposite of the passivity *adamah*
  implies.
- Alongside this stands Rabbi Klein's own depth reading, from an earlier piece
  of his on these two words: *adamah* is restricted to the topmost strata of the
  crust while *eretz* can reach deeper. The two are given one after the other,
  not set against each other. Rav Tzvi Yehuda Kook distinguishes *ratzon* from
  *chefetz*, and Rabbi Klein then draws the parallel himself: *eretz* likewise
  takes in what lies beneath the surface, while *adamah*, from DALET-MEM,
  likeness, he associates with superficiality, the neighbour of *dimyon*,
  imagination or delusion, which may resemble reality without being it.

## Distinctions

### eretz (ארץ)
Where a triliteral root opens with an ALEPH, the ALEPH is often outside the true
biliteral root, and that is the move every account here makes: *eretz* rests on
REISH-TZADI, the root of *ratz*, *ratzah*, and *ratzon*. Rabbi Shaul Mortera of
Amsterdam extends the family to *shoretz*, spreading out, and so to *sheretz*,
insect; Rabbi Klein adds that this is possibly by the *shiphel* inflection, or
possibly on the approach that any letter may join a two-letter root.

### adamah (אדמה)

- Avos d'Rabbi Nasan (version 2): the land is called *adamah* because Adam was
  created from it.
- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim works from the biliteral DALET-MEM, likeness
  (*domeh*), which by its nature implies incongruity, since things merely alike
  are not the same; from there *domem*, still or inactive, where inner activity
  continues but outward activity has stopped. *Adamah* belongs to that branch:
  the setting in which plants grow and are active while the ground itself stays
  passive.

### ratzon (רצון) and chefetz (חפץ)
Rav Tzvi Yehuda Kook: *ratzon* is a person's final goal, his real will
underneath; *chefetz* is the immediate, tangible one, which is why Mishnaic
Hebrew calls physical objects *chafetzim* (see Rashi to Koheles 3:1). The pair
belongs in this entry because *ratzon* shares the root of *eretz*, and Rabbi Klein
sees the same contrast of depth against surface running through both pairs.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "earth" absorbs *eretz* and *adamah* together, and "land" will
  take *adamah* if you let it. Hold "land" for *eretz* and "ground" or "soil" for
  *adamah*.
- Where an unqualified *eretz* stands in a Torah passage about mitzvos of the
  Land, "the Land" with a capital is usually right, and *adamah* in the same
  passage is "the ground," not a second name for the same place.
- A derashah on *eretz* and *ratz* cannot survive in English. Transliterate and
  gloss: "*eretz*, land, from *ratz*, running."
- *Ratzon* against *chefetz*: "will" and "desire" will not carry the distinction
  on their own; where a passage turns on it, say "ultimate will" for *ratzon* and
  keep "desire" or "object" for *chefetz*.
