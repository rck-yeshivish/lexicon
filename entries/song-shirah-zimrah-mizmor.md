# Song: shirah / zimrah / mizmor

## Bottom line

*Shir* / *shirah* is **song**: the sung, worded song, and the general term for
song sacred or secular (Malbim). *Zemer* / *zimrah* is **music**, or
**melody**: instrumental music (Ibn Ezra, Radak, Sforno, the Vilna Gaon, Rav
Hirsch, Wertheimer), or the tune as against the words (Mecklenburg), and in
Malbim's second view the specifically religious song of Hashem's praise; when
the two stand together *shirah* comes first, *zimrah* second, because *zimrah*
is the higher (Malbim). *Mizmor* is **psalm** as a title, and in the
superscriptions *mizmor shir* / *shir mizmor* the *mizmor* is the tune and the
*shir* the words (Pappenheim, Pincus; Ibn Ezra sees no difference). The thing
not to get wrong: שירו וזמרו is "sing and play," two acts, not "sing and sing."

## Distinctions

### shir / shirah (שיר, שירה)
Root ש-י-ר. The majority view: verbal song, the sung words. Malbim: the
general term, religious or secular; Rabbi Yehuda Leib Edel: a plain
expression of joy without necessarily naming its Source. Mecklenburg: the
lyrics of the poem without the tune. Rabbi Yitzchak Hutner: praise of Hashem
from rapture and attachment, His immanence. Maharal: *shirah* is a thing
whose beginning is joined to its end, as a *sher* is a bracelet (Yeshayahu
3:19) or an animal's collar (Shabbos 5:1) or a ring of coins (Bava Metzia
25a); song comes at the completion of a task, when one looks back and thanks.
Rabbi Moshe Shapiro: only in hindsight, when end meets beginning, is song
fitting, and the whole Torah is a *shirah* (Devarim 31:19) because Haazinu is
history whole; he also joins *shirah* to *yashar* through Avodah Zarah 24b on
the cows that "sang" (*vayisharnah*, Shmuel I 6:12). Pappenheim: from
*yashar*, straight, since a song holds one theme without deviating, so its
end links to its beginning. Rabbi Shimon Dov Ber Analak: from *shur*, wall, a
structure of lines each load-bearing, as a ring must be whole. Rabbi Immanuel
Frances: *shir* is the singer's power to hold his hearers (*sharir*, *shur*,
*sherarah*), and Biblical song speaks of the singer in the third person
(Bereishis 49:2; Bamidbar 24:3; Shoftim 5:12) because true song stands
outside oneself. The Chasam Sofer (via Rabbi Yaakov Haber): *shirah* is
*shirayim*, the leftovers of the soul in a material world.

### zemer / zimrah (זמר, זמרה)
Root ז-מ-ר, which also means "cut, prune" (*zomer*, Vayikra 25:3; one of the
39 melachos). The majority: instrumental music, hence *klei zemer* and
Yiddish *klezmer*. Malbim: the higher, more intense form, always second in a
pair; or, the specifically religious song of Hashem's praises. Edel: song that
acknowledges Hashem as the source of the joy and thanks Him. Mecklenburg: the
tune, sung or played. Rabbi Hutner: praise from awareness of Hashem's
transcendence, in which a man "cuts off" his own existence before His
infinite greatness. Noam Elimelech (to Bereishis 47:28, Shemos 19:1) and the
Chasam Sofer: *zimrah* cuts away the outer distractions, and Pesukei DeZimrah
cut down the *klipos* before prayer. Pappenheim: *zimrah* is the choicest
segment of a song, the rest pruned away; Analak: one part cut off from the
whole edifice; Frances: the artist prunes what is unneeded, as in *zimras
ha'aretz* (Bereishis 43:11), the choicest produce people sing over (Rashi);
Rabbeinu Efrayim ben Shimshon reads that *zimras* as wine, which gladdens and
makes one sing; Rabbi Nachman of Breslov reads it as the *niggun* of Eretz
Yisrael.

### mizmor (מזמור)
Root ז-מ-ר. Ibn Ezra (to Tehillim 48:1): no difference between *mizmor shir*
and *shir mizmor*. Pappenheim: side by side, *shir* is the words and *mizmor*
the melody; *shir mizmor* opens with words and the tune follows, *mizmor shir*
the reverse. Rabbi Shimshon Pincus (*Shabbos Malkesa*): *shir* is the story a
song tells, *mizmor* the wordless tune when elation outruns words; a psalm
titled *shir mizmor* begins in words and rises past them, *mizmor shir* begins
at the peak and settles into words.

## Rendering guidance

- שירו לה' זמרו: "sing to Hashem, play [music]" or "sing and make music."
  Two verbs in English, in this order.
- שיר / שירה alone: "song"; שירת הים "the Song of the Sea"; the Torah as
  שירה, "a song."
- זמרה alone: "music," "melody," or "song of praise" by context (Malbim's
  second view); זמירות "hymns," "songs" (Shabbos *zemiros* stay transliterated
  in most house styles).
- מזמור as a superscription: "a psalm"; מזמור שיר "a psalm, a song"; keep the
  Hebrew order and let the reader see two nouns.
- פסוקי דזמרה: "the Verses of Song" (or keep the Hebrew); the article's gloss
  is "Verses of Hymns."
- Trap: English "song" absorbs all three, and "sing" absorbs both verbs. Where
  the passage pairs them, or a derashah turns on the order (Malbim's rule),
  give שיר "song/sing" and זמר "music/play," and never invert.
- Trap: "psalm" is fine for מזמור as a title but not for מזמור in Pincus's
  sense, the wordless melody; there write "melody."
- Where the drush is on ז-מ-ר as pruning (Hutner, Noam Elimelech, Pappenheim),
  the English cannot carry the pun; gloss it once ("*zimrah*, which also means
  to prune") rather than translating the root into the running line.
