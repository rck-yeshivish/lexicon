# Citron: hadar / esrog / tapuach

## Bottom line

*Hadar* means **beautiful** or **splendid**; nothing in the word says citron.
The Torah's *pri eitz hadar* (Vayikra 23:40) is the esrog by Mesorah, and the
Gemara's derivations are asmachta (Ibn Ezra, Rambam). *Esrog* is the
**citron**, the Mishnaic and Targumic name. *Tapuach* is an **apple**, and only
in Shir HaShirim 2:3 does Rabbeinu Tam read it as a citron. Do not render *hadar*
"citron" in the pasuk; translate the word and let the halachah supply the rest.

## Where the authorities disagree

**How hadar comes to mean the citron.** Chazal (Sukkah 35a, Yerushalmi Sukkah
3:5, Sifra to Vayikra 23:40) give four routes: *hadar* modifies fruit and tree
alike, so the fruit must taste like its wood, as the citron's does; *hadar*
from *dir*, a corral, since a corral holds animals of every size and the citron
tree carries blossoms and full-grown fruit at once; *ha-dar*, "the one that
dwells," the citron staying on its tree from year to year; and *hadar* from the
Greek for water, the citron needing much watering. Against taking any of these
as the source of the law, Ibn Ezra (to Vayikra 23:40) and the Rambam
(introduction to his Commentary on the Mishnah): the identification is an
undisputed Mosaic tradition, and the derashos are exegetical allusions that do
not operate on the plain level; both add that the citron is in fact the most beautiful of fruits
(Rambam, *Moreh Nevuchim* 3:43), so the title suits it. The Bottom line follows them.

**Whether hadar and esrog are the same word in two languages.** The Ramban:
yes. The Ramban applies this to *esrog*, a cognate of *ragig*, making it "the
desirable fruit"; since *hadar* and *nechmad* come to the same thing, *pri eitz
hadar* must be the esrog, and *hadar* is the Hebrew of what Aramaic calls
*esrog*. Parts of this are taken up by the Ra'ah, Ritva, Meiri, Rabbeinu
Bachya, the Tashbetz, and Rabbi Moshe Mintz.

**The gematria route.** The Baal HaTurim (to Vayikra 23:40): *pri eitz hadar*
and *esrogim* match in gematria (the article prints 659 and 660, so the match
needs the kollel); Rabbi Meir Mazuz: *trung* likewise comes to 659.

## Distinctions

### hadar (הדר)
Root ה-ד-ר. In Tanach the word is glory or beauty, so *pri eitz hadar* on its
face is either a beautiful fruit or the fruit of a beautiful tree, and tells you
nothing about which fruit. In Modern Hebrew *hadar* was reassigned to the whole
citrus genus; the Chazon Ish discusses whether those count as one species.

### esrog (אתרוג) and its Aramaic forms
*Esrog* runs through the Mishnah (Maasros 1:4, Bikkurim 2:6, Sukkah 3:4-7,
Meilah 6:4), and Targum Onkelos (to Vayikra 23:40) renders *pri eitz hadar* with
it; other sources have *trunga* (Yerushalmi Sukkah 3:10) or *trugin* (Targum
Yonasan there). Kiddushin 70a records the register question: Rav Nachman
offered Rav Yehudah an *etronga*, and Rav Yehudah cited Shmuel that whoever
says *etronga* is suspected of haughtiness; one says *esrog* as the chachamim
do or *esroga* as ordinary people do.

### tapuach (תפוח)
Shir HaShirim 2:3 likens Klal Yisrael among the nations to a lone *tapuach* in
a forest, and Rabbeinu Tam reads *tapuach* there as the citron (Tosafos to
Shabbos 88a and Taanis 29b). Whether he means every *tapuach* in Tanach or only
that pasuk is not clear. *Tapuach* is a fruit elsewhere in Shir HaShirim and in
Yoel and Mishlei, and is also a Canaanite city and a man's name.

## Rendering guidance

- הדר in Vayikra 23:40: "a beautiful fruit of a tree," or "the fruit of a
  beautiful tree" where the ambiguity matters. Do not write "citron" in the
  pasuk itself; that is the halachic identification, and it erases the gap
  Chazal, Ibn Ezra, and the Ramban work on.
- אתרוג: "esrog" in a frum register, "citron" where a general reader needs the
  botanical word; Ashkenazi *esrog* and Sephardi *etrog* are both correct.
- אתרונגא: keep the form and gloss it, since Kiddushin 70a is about the word
  itself; "citron" for all three of them destroys the story.
- תפוח: "apple." Only in Shir HaShirim 2:3 is "citron" a live option, and there
  a note is better than a silent substitution.
- Trap: English "citron" will absorb *hadar*, *esrog*, *trunga*, and Rabbeinu
  Tam's *tapuach* alike. Where two of them stand in one passage (a derashah
  linking *hadar* to *esrog*, or the Ramban's claim that they are one word in
  two languages), keep them distinct or the argument vanishes.
