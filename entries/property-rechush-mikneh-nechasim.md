# Property: rechush / mikneh / nechasim

## Bottom line

*Rechush* is **property** or **possessions**, the movable goods a man has
gathered. *Mikneh* is not property at large but **livestock**, the herds that
were the ancient world's measure of wealth. *Nechasim*, always plural, is
**assets**, the word the Mishnah uses where Tanach would say *rechush* or
*mikneh*. The trap: *mikneh* shares the root of *kinyan* and looks like a general
word for what one has acquired, but unless context forces otherwise it is herds.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| רכוש | rechush | property, possessions | movables, gold and silver; a hypernym for all assets (Radak), or inanimate movables only (Rav Hirsch) |
| מקנה | mikneh | livestock | specifically herds, a man's chief acquisition (Ramban, Radak) |
| נכסים | nechasim | assets | plural always; the Mishnah's standard word; from *kisui*, hidden (Bamidbar Rabbah 22:8) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**How *rechush* and *mikneh* relate.** Three views:

1. **Hypernym and hyponym.** Radak (to Bereishis 12:5, 14:11): *rechush*
   includes gold, silver, other movables, and *mikneh* too, so livestock are one
   kind of *rechush*. Ibn Ezra (to Bereishis 12:5) reads *rechush* as meaning the
   same as *mikneh*, at least sometimes.
2. **Mutually exclusive.** Rav Hirsch (to Bereishis 12:5, 13:2, 31:18):
   *rechush* is inanimate movable belongings and *mikneh* is livestock, neither
   including the other. His reason: an animal recognizes the owner who acquired
   it (Yeshayahu 1:3) and follows him of its own accord, whereas inanimate
   *rechush* has to be carried.
3. **Animals against people.** Rabbi Menachem ben Shlomo, *Midrash Sechel Tov*
   (to Bereishis 31:18, 36:6-7): *mikneh* is animal property, *rechush* human
   property, servants and maidservants.

The Bottom line follows Radak, the safe default where a verse has only one of the
two; where both stand together, Rav Hirsch's contrast is what makes the pair say
something.

**Where *nechasim* comes from.** Bamidbar Rabbah 22:8 and Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
take it from KAF-SAMECH, covering. Rabbi Aharon Marcus derives *neches* from
*reches*, "binding," REISH for NUN, making it a cousin of *rechush*; he adds that
Yehoshua, an Ephraimite who said SIN for SHIN (Shoftim 12:6), would have said
*rechus*, and Yehoshua is the sefer where *nechasim* first appears.

## Distinctions

### rechush (רכוש)
Thirty-three times in Tanach, only in Bereishis, Bamidbar, Daniel, Ezra, and
Divrei HaYamim; the verb *rachash*, to accrue property, is in Bereishis alone.
Seven instances fall in Lech Lecha (Bereishis 12:5, 13:6, 14:11, 14:12, 14:16,
14:21, 15:14), the highest concentration in any parashah. Rav Hirsch (and Solomon
Rabinowitz) connect *rechush*, with a final SHIN, to *reches* with a SIN, "tying"
(see Rashi to Shemos 28:28): the pursuit of wealth binds asset to asset into one
net worth. *Ohalei Yehudah* reaches *reches* by way of *rechesh*, a horse.

### mikneh (מקנה)
Over seventy-five times in Tanach, the commonest of the three. Root
KUF-NUN(-HEI), which also yields *miknah* and *kinyan*, acquisition. Ramban (to
Bereishis 14:18, 34:23) and Radak (*Sefer HaShorashim*): the word names livestock
precisely because livestock were the chief acquisition. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim
gives KUF-NUN the core sense of a minimal dwelling, whence *ken*, a bird's nest,
and *kinyan*, which shifts an item from one domain into another; *mikneh* is then
either the commercially central asset (a beast serves for wool, milk, hide,
offspring, meat, and field work, where produce is only eaten) or the animal kept
in a barn rather than a home.

### nechasim (נכסים)
Seven times in Tanach, always plural: Yehoshua 22:8; Koheles 5:18, 6:2; Divrei
HaYamim II 1:11-12; and in the Aramaic of Ezra 6:8, 7:26. In rabbinic Hebrew it
is the standard term for total assets: *rechush* and *mikneh* never appear in the
Mishnah, and *nechasim* runs through it (Peah 3:6-8, Kesubos 8:1-2, Bava Basra
8:3-7, Avos 2:7, and dozens more). Bamidbar Rabbah 22:8 reads
it from *kisui*, covered: assets are *nechasim* "because they are hidden from
this one and revealed to that one," held only for a time rather than becoming
part of the man the way his strength or his wisdom does.
Rabbi Shmuel de Ucedah (*Midrash Shmuel* to Avos 2:8) sharpens it: a man accrues
all his life, dies, and another enjoys the fruit; many, he says, gather money for
their wives' future husbands. Rabbi Shimon Yehuda Leib Goldblit adds that a man's
worth is hidden from those who meet him, and that wealth covers his faults
(already in *Shoresh Yesha*). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim's KAF-SAMECH family:
*kisui*, *kesus* (clothing), *kos*, *kisei*, *kis* (pocket), *meches* (tax, what
falls into the government's *kis*), and *michsah* (the pooled korban Pesach fund,
Shemos 12:4). *Nechasim* is then what one pockets, or covers to protect, or what
covers one's needs so that he need not lean on others.

## Rendering guidance

- Trap: English "property" absorbs all three at once. Keep "livestock" for
  *mikneh* wherever the passage lets you; a verse with both *rechush* and
  *mikneh* is drawing a distinction, and "property and property" is no
  translation. Bereishis 13:6, the herdsmen's quarrel, has *rechush* in the wide
  sense: render "possessions."
- *Nechasim* in a Mishnah or Gemara is "assets" or "estate," not "property"; its
  halachic contexts (*nechasei melog*, a debtor's assets) want the financial word.
- Where a derashah turns on *nechasim* and *kisui*, transliterate and gloss.
