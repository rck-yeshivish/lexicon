# Drawing water: dalah / shoev

## Bottom line

Both verbs mean **draw water**, and the difference is effort. *Dalah* is
drawing from a deep well: lower the pail, haul it up, so render it **draw up**
or **draw out** (Malbim). *Shoev* is drawing where the water is already at
hand, from a spring or surface pool, so plain **draw** is right. If a passage
turns on the labor involved, do not let one English "draw" cover both.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| דלה | dalah | draw up, draw out | deep well; requires lowering and lifting (Malbim); one root with "lift up" (Radak) |
| שאב | shoev | draw | spring or surface water; effortless (Malbim); ש-ב, water brought to rest (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| דלי | dli | pail, bucket | the tool that does the lifting (Radak) |
| דליות | daliyot | topmost branches | as if lifted to the top of the tree (Radak); they draw water upward (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |
| דל | dal | poor | emptied of fortune as a well is emptied of water (Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**How many roots.** The triliteralists (Ibn Chayyuj, Ibn Janach, Radak) trace
*dalah* to ד-ל-ה and treat "draw water" and "lift up" as one meaning, since
drawing water simply is lifting it; *dal* they assign to a separate root,
ד-ל-ל. Menachem Ibn Saruk works from the biliteral ד-ל and files three separate
derivatives under it: *dalah* (draw water), *dalah* (lift up), and *dal* (poor).
He leaves *daliyot* unaccounted for. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim keeps the
biliteral ד-ל but makes "draw water from a well" the primary sense and
everything else borrowed from it, and he does bring *daliyot* and the Rabbinic
*dildul* (dangling) under it.

**Whether dal belongs here at all.** Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi Ashkenazi (*Hoil
Moshe*) derives *dal* from *zol* (cheap) by the interchange of zayin and dalet,
against Menachem.

The Bottom line follows Malbim on the practical question, which no one
disputes; the root debates change the etymology, not the rendering.

## Distinctions

### dalah (דלה)
Root ד-ל-ה, or biliteral ד-ל. Malbim: *dalah* is drawing from a deep well, an
act that costs effort, lowering the pail and raising it laden. The same verb
serves for lifting generally: *"aromimcha Hashem ki dilisani,"* "I will exalt
You, Hashem, for You have lifted me up" (Tehillim 30:2). Hence *d'li*, the pail
(Bamidbar 24:7, Yeshayahu 40:15), and *daliyot*, the branches at a tree's top
(Yirmiyahu 11:16; Yechezkel 17:6-7, 17:23, 19:11, 31:7, 31:9, 31:12). Rav
Hirsch (to Bereishis 6:10, 30:32) brings *delet*, a door, into the same family:
the door gathers everyone in the house into one place, as the pail gathers
water into one vessel.

### shoev (שאב)
Root ש-א-ב, on which Menachem, Ibn Janach and Radak agree; it appears nineteen
times in Tanach. Malbim: *shoev* is drawing without effort, water taken into a
bucket where it already lies at the surface. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim treats the
aleph as non-radical and reaches the biliteral ש-ב, "return," the root of
*hashavah* (returning a lost object), *lehashiv* (to answer), *yeshivah*
(dwelling), and *Shabbos* (returning to rest). On his reading, *shoev* is
bringing moving water to a halt in a vessel, which fits a spring, whose waters
were flowing until then, better than a well, whose waters were already still.

## Rendering guidance

- Yisro's daughters and Moshe at the well (Shemos 2:16-19) use *dalah*: "drew
  up" water, with the labor in view. That is the wording the Shemini Atzeres
  request for rain quotes back, *dalah dalah lanu*.
- Yeshayahu 12:3, *"u'shavtem mayim b'sasson mimaayanei hayeshuah,"* uses
  *shoev* precisely because the source is a spring (*maayan*). Render simply
  "draw."
- Mishlei 20:5: counsel in a man's heart is deep water and the man of
  understanding *yidlenah*, "draws it out," the effortful verb, matching the
  depth.
- Rivkah at the well (Bereishis 24:11-45) is *shoev* throughout even though it
  is a well, because the Midrash (*Bereishis Rabbah* 60:5) says the water rose
  to meet her. The easy verb records the miracle.
- Trap: English "draw" absorbs both, and also *moshech* (pull) and *mashui*
  (drawn out, as in Moshe's name), which belong to a separate cluster. Where a
  passage sets the two against each other, mark *dalah* with "draw up" or
  "haul up" and keep bare "draw" for *shoev*.
