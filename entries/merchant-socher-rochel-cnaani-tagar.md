# Merchant: socher / rochel / cnaani (and tagar)

## Bottom line

Where the three appear as a graded set, follow Malbim (to Yechezkel 17:4, 27:3):
*socher* is the **wholesaler**, *cnaani* the **trader** in the middle, *rochel*
the **peddler** who buys from the *socher* and goes door to door. Standing alone,
*socher* is simply **merchant**, and *Tagar*, the Aramaic word, also renders
**merchant** (Rashi, Eruvin 55a, makes him the local seller against the
travelling *socher*). The trap is *cnaani*: in a dozen pesukim it means "trader,"
not "Canaanite."

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| סוחר | socher | merchant, wholesaler | the general word; buys in bulk, supplies the rochel (Malbim); travels (Rashi) |
| רוכל | rochel | peddler | small-scale, door to door (Malbim); often a perfume seller (Rashi) |
| כנעני | cnaani | trader | "Canaanite" as an occupation (Pesachim 50a); the middleman (Malbim) |
| תגר | tagar | merchant | Aramaic; the seller who stays in his own town (Rashi to Eruvin 55a) |

## Where the authorities disagree

**Whether *socher* and *rochel* differ at all.** Rashi (to Erchin 23b) defines
*rochel* by *socher*, treating them as one. Malbim (to Yechezkel 17:4, 27:3)
builds a three-tier trade: importers who sail abroad, the *socher* who buys from
them in bulk, the *rochel* who buys from the *socher* and goes town to town, with
the *cnaani* between the last two; Rabbi Moshe Tedeschi-Ashkenazi agrees that the
*rochel* does not travel a *socher*'s distances. Follow Malbim where the words
appear together, Rashi where only one does.

**What a *rochel* sells.** Rashi, consistently (to Vayikra 19:16; Shabbos 90a;
Kiddushin 82a; Sanhedrin 100b), makes him a seller of perfumes for women, while
pseudo-Rashi to Nazir 21a uses it of any goods; render narrowly where Rashi is quoted.

**Where *cnaani* as "merchant" comes from.** Rabbi Yehuda Leib Shapira-Frankfurter
(*HaRechasim LaVikah* to Vayikra 16:21): the nation's name widened, once the
Canaanites were famous as traders, to anyone in the trade. Rabbi Shimon Yehuda
Leib Goldblit (*Leshon Chachamim*) and Rabbi Yosef Nechemias (to Mishlei 31:24):
from כ-נ-ע, "submission." Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche: from נ-ע, "movement."

## Distinctions

### socher (סוחר)
Root ס-ח-ר, about sixteen times in Tanach, with verbs for trading and nouns for
merchandise; the Midianite *socharim* who drew Yosef from the pit (Bereishis
37:28). The root also carries "around": *secharchar*, and Targum's *s'chor* for
*saviv*. Ibn Saruk and Ibn Janach keep the senses apart; Radak joins them.

### rochel (רוכל)
Seventeen times, mostly in Yechezkel, with a feminine *rocheles*. The same root
gives *rechilus*, talebearing (Vayikra 19:16; Mishlei 11:13), because travelling
merchants carried news and rumor from town to town. Rashi (to Vayikra 19:16) reads
the *kaf* as interchangeable with *gimmel*, linking *rochel* to *regel*, "foot,"
and *meragel*, "spy"; Rabbi Avraham Bedersi (*Chosam Tochnis*) explains that a
merchant must scout like a spy. Rashbam (to Bava Basra 29b): he circuits the
towns and comes home rarely.

### cnaani (כנעני)
Pesachim 50a rules that Yehudah's father-in-law cannot have been a literal
Canaanite, given how careful Avraham and Yitzchak were that their sons not marry
Canaanite women (Bereishis 24:3, 27:46), so *ish cnaani* (38:2) means a merchant;
Ramban and Radak add he lived in Canaan too. Rabbi David Chaim Chelouche objects
that the daughter is called a *cnaanis* (Divrei HaYamim I 2:3), but Targum Rav
Yosef renders that *pragmatita*. Yeshayahu 23:8 calls Tyre the city "whose merchants (*socher*) are princes, whose
*cnaanim* are the honored of the earth," which Rashi and Radak read as merchants
and *Metzudas Zion* calls a poetic pairing. The *eishes chayil* "gives a sash to
the *cnaani*" (Mishlei 31:24), glossed by Rashi with *tagar*, by Ibn Ezra and
Ralbag with *socher*. Zechariah 14:21 foretells no *cnaani* in the Beis HaMikdash:
merchants, says Rashi, who offers also *ein kan ani*, "no poor man here."

### tagar (תגר)
The word Chazal use when they gloss *cnaani*: Targum Onkelos and Targum Yonasan
to Bereishis 38:2, *Bereishis Rabbah* 85:4, Pesachim 50a, and Rashi there; Targum
also uses it for *socher* and *rochel* (Melachim I 10:28, 10:15).
Rabbi Eliyahu Bachur (*Sefer HaTishbi*) gives it a separate entry from *teigar*,
"quarrel." Since Eruvin 55a says Torah is not found among *socharim* or
*tagarim*, Rashi (there) reads a real difference: the *socher* circuits the
towns, the *tagar* sells at home.

## Rendering guidance

- One word alone: סוחר "merchant," רוכל "peddler," תגר "merchant." Where two or
  three stand together, grade them: "wholesaler," "trader," "peddler."
- Trap: English "merchant" absorbs all four. Yeshayahu 23:8 is the test, since
  *socher* and *cnaani* stand in one sentence; render "merchants" and "traders,"
  not "merchants" twice.
- כנעני in Bereishis 38:2, Mishlei 31:24, Yeshayahu 23:8, Yechezkel 16:29, Hoshea
  12:8, and Iyov 41:30 is "merchant." Writing "Canaanite" at Bereishis 38:2
  contradicts Pesachim 50a.
- רכילות stays "talebearing," not "peddling." Where a source pairs *socher* with
  *tagar* (Eruvin 55a), keep "travelling merchants" against "local dealers."
