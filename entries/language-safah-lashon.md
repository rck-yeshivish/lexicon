# Language: safah / lashon

## Bottom line

Both mean **language**, and both are body parts taken over for speech: *safah*
is the lip, *lashon* the tongue. Rav Dessler: the lip is outside the body and the
tongue inside, so a *lashon* is a language whose speakers have taken its inner
content into themselves, while a *safah* is one they hold only externally, its
mechanics without its values. That is why Hebrew is *Lashon HaKodesh*, and why
the generation of the Haflagah, who spoke the same words with none of their
content, had one *safah* (Bereishis 11:1).

## Distinctions

### safah (שפה)
Literally the lip, the outer organ. Rav Dessler: a tongue is called a *safah*
when its speakers carry only its technical, outward features and not its core
values. Bereishis 11:1 says the pre-Haflagah world was of one *safah*: they spoke
Lashon HaKodesh but applied it only on the surface, without its essence. In
modern Hebrew *safah* is the ordinary word for language, and Hebrew is called
*HaSafah HaIvris*; the older usage does not work that way.

### lashon (לשון)
Literally the tongue, the inner organ. Rav Dessler: a language earns the name
*lashon* when its speakers internalize what it means. Hence *Lashon HaKodesh*,
since we strive to take in the moral and esteemed values the language
signifies. Hebrew is traditionally called *Leshon Ivri* as well.

### Yosef and Lashon HaKodesh
Bereishis 42:23: the brothers spoke freely before the Egyptian viceroy, Reuven
telling them their troubles were a punishment for what they had done to Yosef,
because they did not know he understood them, a translator standing between
them. Rashi (there): Yosef deliberately used a translator throughout, since he
pretended to know only Egyptian and they knew only Lashon HaKodesh, so the
interpreter had to know both, which implied to them that the viceroy could not
follow Lashon HaKodesh on his own. Many commentators, Rashi among them, hold
that when Yosef revealed himself he spoke to them in Lashon HaKodesh, without an
interpreter, as proof of who he was. The Ramban (to Bereishis 45:12) objects:
that proves nothing, since the Canaanites next door spoke Lashon HaKodesh
(Yeshayahu 19:18 calls it the language of Canaan)
and a king is commonly familiar with the languages of nearby countries, as
Nevuchadnetzar spoke Hebrew though his advisers answered in Aramaic (Daniel
2:2-4). The Chasam Sofer answers with a novel
interpretation, and it turns on the nature of Hebrew rather than on the two
words. He begins from a historical observation: the Jews of Poland, Lithuania
and Russia spoke Yiddish, a form of Judeo-German, though the local populations
knew no German at all, because most of them descended from the Jews of Western Europe
ousted in the First Crusade (whence *Ashkenazim*, literally Germans), and every
family raised its children in that dialect for hundreds of years. Yet after a
mere seventy-year exile to Bavel, Hebrew was forgotten so fast that only half
the returnees could still speak it (Nechemiah 13:24). Hebrew is unlike other
languages: it is preserved only by one who maintains a certain degree of
kedushah, and a lapse in kedushah can easily lose it, while German can pass down for
generations. Yosef speaking Lashon HaKodesh therefore proved that after all his
years in Mitzrayim he had kept the purity and holiness the language requires,
and had not defiled himself with the promiscuity Egyptian culture stood for. In
this way, Rabbi Klein concludes, Yosef typified Hebrew held as a *lashon*, taken
inside, rather than as a *safah*.

## Rendering guidance

- Either word standing alone: "language." In a translated pasuk, "one language"
  for Bereishis 11:1 is right; add "one lip" only in a note if the derashah is
  being quoted.
- *Lashon HaKodesh*: leave it transliterated, or "the Holy Tongue." Not "Hebrew"
  where the sanctity of the language is the subject.
- Where *safah* and *lashon* stand together or are contrasted, English "language"
  will absorb both. Render one "tongue" and the other "language," or keep the
  Hebrew and gloss, since Rav Dessler's whole distinction is which organ the word
  names.
- Trap: *lashon* has other uses in rabbinic Hebrew (a wording, a manner of
  speech, *lashon hara*), and "tongue" will not carry them. Translate by what the
  passage is doing, not by the body part.
