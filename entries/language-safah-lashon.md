# Language: safah / lashon

## Bottom line

Both mean **language**, and both are body parts taken over for speech: *safah*
is the lip, *lashon* the tongue. Rav Dessler: the lip is outside the body and the
tongue inside, so a *lashon* is a language whose speakers have taken its inner
content into themselves, while a *safah* is one they hold only externally, its
mechanics without its values. That is why Hebrew is *Lashon HaKodesh*, and why
the generation of the Haflagah, who spoke the same words with none of their
content, had one *safah* (Bereishis 11:1). When translating, keep the body image
where a derashah leans on it; elsewhere "language" serves for both.

## Distinctions

### safah (שפה)
Literally the lip, the outer organ. Rav Dessler: a tongue is called a *safah*
when its speakers carry only its technical, outward features and not its core
values. Bereishis 11:1 says the pre-Haflagah world was of one *safah*: they spoke
Lashon HaKodesh but applied it only on the surface, without its essence. In
modern Hebrew *safah* is the ordinary word for language, and Hebrew is called
*HaSafah HaIvris*; the older usage does not work that way.

### lashon (לשון)
Literally the tongue, the inner organ. Rav Dessler: a language earns the name
*lashon* when its speakers internalize what it means. Hence *Lashon HaKodesh*
and *Leshon Ivri*, since we aim to take in the values the language carries.
Yeshayahu 19:18 calls Lashon HaKodesh the language of Canaan.

### Yosef and Lashon HaKodesh
Bereishis 42:23: the brothers spoke freely before the Egyptian viceroy because a
*meilitz* stood between them. Rashi (there): Yosef deliberately used a translator
throughout, since he pretended to know only Egyptian and they knew only Lashon
HaKodesh, so the interpreter had to know both. Rashi and others hold that when
Yosef revealed himself he spoke to them in Lashon HaKodesh as proof of who he
was. The Ramban (to Bereishis 45:12) objects: that proves nothing, since the
Canaanites next door spoke Lashon HaKodesh and a ruler is expected to know his
neighbors' languages, as Nevuchadnetzar spoke Hebrew though his advisers answered
in Aramaic (Daniel 2:2-4). The Chasam Sofer answers from the two words. Jews
driven east in the First Crusade kept German in their homes for centuries among
peoples who did not speak it, yet after seventy years in Bavel only half the
returnees could still speak Hebrew (Nechemiah 13:24). Hebrew is unlike other
languages: it is held only by one who holds a measure of kedushah, and a lapse in
kedushah loses it. Yosef speaking Lashon HaKodesh proved that after all his years
in Mitzrayim he had kept the purity the language requires. He held Hebrew as a
*lashon*, taken inside, not as a *safah*.

## Rendering guidance

- Either word standing alone: "language." In a translated pasuk, "one language"
  for Bereishis 11:1 is right; add "one lip" only in a note if the derashah is
  being quoted.
- *Lashon HaKodesh*: leave it transliterated, or "the Holy Tongue." Not "Hebrew"
  where the sanctity of the language is the subject.
- Where *safah* and *lashon* stand together or are contrasted, English "language"
  will absorb both. Render one "tongue" and the other "language," or keep the
  Hebrew and gloss, since Rav Dessler's whole distinction is which organ the word
  names.
- Trap: *lashon* has other uses in rabbinic Hebrew (a wording, a manner of
  speech, *lashon hara*), and "tongue" will not carry them. Translate by what the
  passage is doing, not by the body part.
