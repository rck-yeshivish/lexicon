# Tree: eitz / ilan

## Bottom line

*Eitz* is the Hebrew word and *ilan* the Aramaic one, but in *lashon Chazal*
they divide the field: *ilan* is the **living tree**, *eitz* is the **wood**.
In Tanach *eitz* does both jobs, so a Chumash rendering picks "tree" or "wood"
by context alone. The one thing not to get wrong: the berachah says *borei pri
ha'eitz* over the fruit of an *ilan*, and that is not a slip.

## At a glance

| Hebrew | translit | English | what sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| עץ | eitz | tree, wood | Hebrew; in Tanach both senses at once; root sense is hardness, with atzum and etzem (Pappenheim) |
| אילן | ilan | living tree | Aramaic, only in Daniel 4 in Tanach; adopted by Chazal for the standing tree (Hirschensohn) |

## Distinctions

### eitz (עץ)
Everywhere in Tanach: *eitz hadaas*, *eitz hachaim*, *atzei shittim*, *atzei
levanah*. Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim connects it to *atzum*, strong, and *etzem*,
bone: what marks a tree off from grass and the other flimsy growing things is
that it is hard and lasting. The word therefore names the substance as readily
as the plant, which is why it serves for timber as well.

### ilan (אילן) and ilana (אילנא)
*Ilan* appears once in Tanach, at Daniel 4:7, and *ilana* several times in the
same perek (4:8, 4:11, 4:17, 4:20, 4:23), all of it in Aramaic. It is then
everywhere in the Mishnah and the rest of rabbinic literature. Rabbi Chaim
Hirschensohn accounts for the switch: Biblical Hebrew used *eitz* for both the
living tree and cut wood, and *Chazal* wanted the two apart. They took the
Aramaic *ilana* from Daniel, Hebraized it to *ilan* for the living tree, and
narrowed *eitz* to wood. One word changed, and the ambiguity was gone.

### The berachah
Rabbi Betzalel Stern (*Be'tzel HaChochmah*) was asked whether the fixed wording
of berachos and tefillos follows Biblical or Rabbinic Hebrew. His proof is the
Mishnah in *Berachos* 6:1: over the fruits of an *ilan* one says *borei pri
ha'eitz*. Though the living word in their own speech was *ilan*, *Chazal* put
*eitz* into the berachah, which shows that they preferred Biblical Hebrew when
fixing a *nusach*.

## Rendering guidance

- In Tanach, *eitz* takes "tree" when it is growing and "wood" when it is cut;
  the Hebrew gives no help, only the context does. *Atzei shittim* is "shittim
  wood", *eitz hachaim* is "the Tree of Life".
- In a Mishnaic or halachic passage, keep the pair distinct: *ilan* is "tree",
  *eitz* is "wood". Trap: English "tree" will happily cover both words, and
  then a halachah that turns on standing timber against cut timber reads as a
  tautology.
- *Borei pri ha'eitz* stays transliterated in a frum text; if it must be
  rendered, "Who creates the fruit of the tree", with a note that the Mishnah
  says *ilan* in the same line.
