# Tree: eitz / ilan

## Bottom line

*Eitz* seems to be the Hebrew word and *ilan(a)* the Aramaic one (Rabbi Klein's
inference from Daniel 4). For Rabbi Chaim Hirschensohn, *Chazal* then divided
the field: *ilan* is the **living tree**, *eitz* only the **wood**. In Tanach
*eitz* does both jobs. The berachah says *borei pri ha'eitz*
over the fruit of an *ilan*, and that is not a slip; Rabbi Betzalel Stern takes
it as proof that *Chazal* preferred Biblical Hebrew when fixing the wording of
berachos.

## Distinctions

### eitz (עץ)
Countless times in Tanach: *eitz hadaas*, *eitz hachaim*, *atzei shittim*,
*atzei levanah*.

- Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim relates it to *atzum*, strong, and *etzem*, bone, as
  it denotes the strength and durability of a tree, against other, flimsy forms
  of flora such as grass.
- Rabbi Moshe Shapiro finds a common theme in all words containing ע-ץ: an
  *eitz* contains within itself everything that will be created from it, as
  *etzem*, self, contains the sum total of one's potential.

### ilan (אילן) and ilana (אילנא)
*Ilan* appears once in Tanach, at Daniel 4:7, and *ilana* several times in the
same perek (4:8, 4:11, 4:17, 4:20, 4:23), a chapter written, like most of
Daniel, in Aramaic; which, Rabbi Klein notes, would suggest that *eitz* is Hebrew
and *ilan(a)* Aramaic. He works from the premise that no two Hebrew
words mean exactly the same; so why two words for tree? And since *ilan* appears
constantly in the Mishnah and other rabbinic writings, why did the Sages prefer
the Aramaic word to the Hebrew one? Rabbi Chaim Hirschensohn answers both:
Biblical Hebrew used *eitz* for both the living tree and cut wood, and *Chazal*
wanted the two apart. They took the Aramaic *ilana* from Daniel, Hebraized it to
*ilan* for the living tree, and narrowed *eitz* to wood. One word changed, and
the ambiguity was gone.

### The berachah
Rabbi Betzalel Stern (*Be'tzel HaChochmah*) was asked whether the fixed wording
of berachos and tefillos follows Biblical or Rabbinic Hebrew. His proof is the
Mishnah in *Berachos* 6:1: over the fruits of an *ilan* one says *borei pri
ha'eitz*. Though the word for tree in the spoken vernacular was the
Aramaic-based *ilan*, the Mishnah nonetheless put *eitz* into the berachah,
which to him clearly shows that the Rabbis preferred Biblical Hebrew to their
own form of Hebrew when fixing the wording of berachos.

## Rendering guidance

- In Tanach, *eitz* takes "tree" when it is growing and "wood" when it is cut;
  the Hebrew gives no help, only the context does. *Atzei shittim* is "shittim
  wood", *eitz hachaim* is "the Tree of Life".
- In a Mishnaic or halachic passage, keep the pair distinct, following
  Hirschensohn: *ilan* is "tree", *eitz* is "wood". Trap: English "tree" will happily cover both words, and
  then a halachah that turns on standing timber against cut timber reads as a
  tautology.
- *Borei pri ha'eitz* stays transliterated; if it must be
  rendered, "Who creates the fruit of the tree", with a note that the Mishnah
  says *ilan* in the same line.
