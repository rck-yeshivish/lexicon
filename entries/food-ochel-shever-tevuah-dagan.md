# Food: ochel / mazon / yevul / tevuah / tenuvah / dagan / bar / shever

## Bottom line

*Ochel* is **food**, anything eaten, the widest word. *Mazon* is **sustenance**,
food that actually nourishes. Of the produce words: *tevuah* is **crop**, what
comes forth (ripe or not); *tenuvah* is **ripe produce**; *yevul* is
**produce** seen as perishable; *dagan* is threshed **grain**; *bar* is grain
**cleaned of its chaff**; *shever* is **provisions**, grain bought in a famine.
Onkelos renders *ochel*, *bar*, *shever* and *dagan* all by *avur*.

## Distinctions

### ochel (אוכל), maachal (מאכל)
Root א-כ-ל, and so anything eaten. Many grammarians note that the root covers
consuming and destroying generally, by fire (Shemos 3:2, 24:17) or by sword
(Devarim 32:42; Yirmiyahu 46:10). Rabbi Shlomo Pappenheim (1740 to 1814) traces
it to the biliteral כ-ל, utter destruction, *kilayon*. Rabbi Yehuda HaChassid
notes that *dagan* and *ochel* share a gematria of 57.

### mazon (מזון)
Bereishis 45:23. Narrower than *ochel*: food that gives the body what it needs.
Menachem Ibn Saruk and Pappenheim take the root as the biliteral ז-ן; Ibn
Chayyuj, Ibn Janach and Radak as ז-ו-ן. The sense is not in dispute.

### yevul (יבול)
- Ibn Saruk and Pappenheim: root ב-ל.
- Ibn Janach and Radak: י-ב-ל.

Pappenheim
reads ב-ל as decomposition and gives two applications: in *Yerios Shlomo*, all
produce is *yevul* because the seed decomposes to let what grows from it come
up; in *Cheshek Shlomo*, the especially perishable produce is *yevul* because it
can easily rot if it is not looked after.

### tevuah (תבואה)
- Rabbi Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (1866 to 1935): root ב-א, coming or bringing,
  so whatever comes forth from a seed or a tree; hence its extension to the
  fruits of any conduct (Mishlei 3:14, 18:20, and 10:16). Wertheimer stresses
  that produce is *tevuah* even before full ripeness and even while still
  attached to the ground, once it has reached a third of its growth (Yerushalmi
  *Kilayim* 7:5).
- Ibn Janach: produce is *tevuah* because it comes in at set times of the year.
- Radak (to Yehoshua 5:11 and *Sefer HaShorashim*) sets *tevuah* against *avur*:
  *avur* goes with *avar*, past, and is grain of previous years, while *tevuah*
  goes with *ba* and is this year's.
- Rav Hirsch: *tevuah* is the choicest produce, the kind a farmer wants to bring
  into his house.

### tenuvah (תנובה)
Pappenheim: root נ-ו-ב, fully developed products, agricultural and verbal
alike, so *tenuvah* is specifically ripe produce (Devarim 32:13), and *niv
sefasayim* (Yeshayahu 57:19), the product of the lips, is well-formed speech.

### dagan (דגן)
Wertheimer: the five grains (simplistically speaking, wheat, barley, oats, spelt
and rye), and only once
they have been ripened, reaped, and heaped into a smooth pile (see Rashi to
*Berachos* 47b).

*Dagan* is the fruit of the threshing floor: in the ear the grain is called
*tevuah*, and once threshed and winnowed, *dagan* (*Seder Berachos*, at the
beginning).

- Pappenheim traces *dagan* to the biliteral ד-ג, fecundity and quantitative
  increase, the root of *dag*, fish: grains are an especially fertile plant,
  since one seed can beget grains with many more seeds, just as one fish can
  father many more little fish.
- Menachem Ibn Saruk, Ibn Janach and Radak all see *dagan* as deriving from its
  own triliteral root, ד-ג-ן.

### bar (בר)
- Pappenheim: the biliteral ב-ר is separating and excluding, which gives *bar*
  as son (separated from the mother at birth), *bor* and *boris* (cleansers that
  separate off filth), and *bari* and *barah* (a clarity reached by excluding
  every other possibility). As food, *bar* is grain already separated from its
  chaff.
- Rabbi Yitzchok of Volozhin (1780 to 1849) says the same, that *bar* means
  chosen or refined, grain without chaff (see also Ramban to Bereishis 41:49),
  and explains that Yosef had the Egyptians store the grain that way (Bereishis
  41:35) because otherwise there would not have been room for it all.

The King James Version renders *bar*
"corn," which did not then mean the yellow stuff on the cob. "Corn" originally
meant grain with the seed still inside, and was used of whatever staple grain a
given place grew; only after the discovery of the Americas did it attach almost
exclusively to what is now called corn, or *maize* in the Native American
languages the word comes from.

### shever (שבר)
The word seems to derive from the triliteral ש-ב-ר, breaking.

- Radak (to Amos 8:5, Bereishis 41:56, and *Sefer HaShorashim*) and the Meiri
  (to Mishlei 10:3): produce is *shever* because it breaks the famine. Most of
  its occurrences in Tanach are in the Yosef narrative.
- Rabbi Yechezkel Weinfeld of Ramat Eshkol compares *shever*, which breaks a
  famine, to the English *breakfast*, the morning meal that breaks the night's
  fast.
- *Peirush HaRokeach* connects *shever* to *sever*, hope, since men look to
  Hashem trustingly and hopefully while He gives them their food (Tehillim
  145:15).
- Ibn Saruk, *Peirush HaRokeach* and Rabbeinu Bachaya all take *shever* as food
  regarded as a purchased commodity; Rabbeinu Bachaya says the word implies a
  sale, which is seen in rabbinic Hebrew, where *shover* is a receipt, attesting
  to payment and so breaking a litigant's ability to claim a debt. (In Modern
  Hebrew *shover* is a voucher and *kabbalah* is the receipt.)
- Rabbi Avraham Rivlin points out that *shever* thus carries two diametrically
  opposed senses at once, breaking or destroying and building or sustaining.
- Rabbeinu Bachaya adds that Yaakov asked his sons for *shever* rather than
  *ochel* or *bar* because *shever* implies the edible grain together with its
  chaff, alluding to them that in time they would endure the bad, the exile in
  which they would be broken as a people, along with the good.

### avur (עבור)
The Aramaic leveller. Onkelos renders *ochel*, *bar*, *shever* and *dagan* all
as *avur* (Bereishis 41:35, 42:1; Devarim 2:28, 7:13), and Targum Yonasan does
the same to both words in the single pasuk of Amos 8:5. The Hebrew form is at
Yehoshua 5:11, where Radak reads it as last year's grain.

## Rendering guidance

- Default renderings: *ochel* "food"; *mazon* "sustenance" or "nourishment";
  *yevul* "produce"; *tevuah* "crop" or "yield"; *tenuvah* "ripe produce";
  *dagan* "grain"; *bar* "cleaned grain" or "kernels"; *shever* "provisions" or
  "rations."
- Trap: English "grain" will absorb *dagan*, *bar* and *shever* together, and
  "produce" will absorb *yevul*, *tevuah* and *tenuvah*. Where two stand in one
  pasuk (Amos 8:5 has *shever* and *bar*), translate the state of the grain as well as the substance: "provisions" against "cleaned grain."
- Do not read Onkelos back into the Hebrew. His single *avur* is a feature of
  Aramaic, not evidence that the Hebrew words are synonyms; Radak's *avur* is
  itself a distinct thing, old grain.
- *Mezonos* in a halachic context stays transliterated. So does *tevuah* where
  it is the technical produce of the year for maaser or shemittah.
- The King James Version renders *bar* as "corn," which then meant any staple
  grain with the seed still in, not maize. "Corn" is a false friend; use
  "grain."
- *Shever* in the Yosef narrative is a purchase, not a gift. "To buy provisions"
  keeps Rabbeinu Bachaya's sense of a sale; "to get food" loses it.
