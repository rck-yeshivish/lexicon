# Lost or Mistaken

<!-- Sefer Bereishis / Vayeshev -->

Parshah = תעה בשדה by yosef's brothers

Rabbi Yehoshua Alt suggests that טועה [mistake] and נטע [plant, sapling] are from the same root because a persons' mistake has the potential to make him greater.

Every day in the *Uva LeTzion* prayer, we thank G-d for giving us the opportunity to worship Him and for making us different from other people: “Blessed is our G-d who created us for His honor and separated us from those who are lost (*toim*)”. The root of *toim* is TAV-AYIN-HEY and words that sprout from this root appear in the Bible some fifty times. In this article, we will explore the possible differences between the verb *toeh* with a TAV and the word *toeh* with a TET. Despite their slightly different spelling, both words are essentially pronounced the same. But do they mean the same thing?

The generally assumption amongst the commentators is that *toeh* (TAV) means “lost” and *toeh* (TET) means “made a mistake”. Indeed, throughout rabbinic literature, *taut* always refers to making a mistake. This assumption is illustrated by the following discussion: R. Avarham Aharon Broide (circa. early 19th century) was originally from Vilna and later became the Rabbi of the town of Strabin near Slutzk. In his commentary *Bayit HaGadol* to *Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer* (ch. 30), R. Broide poses the following question: When the Torah talks about Abraham expelling Hagar and Ishmael, it says about Hagar, “and she left and she strayed (*toah* with a TAV) in the desert of Beersheba” (Gen. 21:14). Targum Onkelos (there) translates the word *toah* (with a TAV) into Aramaic as *toah* (with a TET). Why does Targum change the meaning of this passage by switching “strayed” into “errored”?

R. Broide relates that this question was asked at a gathering of sages, but nobody provided an answer, until he spoke up. He answered that Targum did this as an allusion to the exegetical rabbinic tradition (found in *Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer* there and in other sources) that when Hagar left Abraham’s house, she returned to the idolatry of her youth. In general, the Targumim translate idolatrous deities as *tavata* (TET-AYIN-VAV-TAV-ALEPH), which is based on the notion that idolatry started off as a *mistake* on the part of mankind (see Maimonides’ Laws of Idolatry ch. 1). So following that rubric, Onkelos sought to imply that Hagar did not just lose her way in her travels, but she also *erred* by returning to idolatry.

R. Broide then cites something he found in R. Eliyahu HaBachur’s glosses to Radak's *Sefer HaShorashim*. HaBachur asserts that there is a fundamental difference between the meaning of *toeh* (TAV) and *toeh* (TET): The word *toeh* with a TET refers to a mistake in heart or intellect, while the word *toeh* with a TAV refers to a person who strayed off the path and ended up lost. However, R. Broide rejects this distinction and argues that even *toeh* with a TAV means one who makes a mistake, and he cites Biblical passages such as Ps. 98:10 and Hos. 4:12 to that effect. He bolsters this position by noting that Radak himself in *Sefer HaShorashim* (when discussing the word *taf*) explicitly writes that *taah* with a TAV and with a TET are the same word! [The interchangeability of these two spellings was already noted by Radak’s father R. Yosef Kimchi (1105–1170) in *Sefer Zikaron*, and by R. Shlomo Ephraim Lenchitz (1550–1619) in his work *Kli Yakar* (to Num. 5:12).]

So are the two words interchangeable, or is there a difference between them? R. Yaakov Zev Lev, author of *Me’at Tzari*, reconciles this difference of opinions by explaining that in Hebrew there is a diference between *toeh* with a TET and with a TAV, but in Aramaic there is only one word—spelled with a TET—that has both meanings. The truth is that HaBachur himself already acknowledged this in his work *Meturgaman* in which he correctly asserts that everytime cognates of *toeh* with a TAV appears in the Bible, the Targumim replace the TAV with a TET.

In fact, there is only place in the Bible where a cognate of *toeh* is spelled with a TET—Ezek. 13:10. In that passage, Ezekiel lambasts false prophets, but the meaning of the word *taah* in that context is ambiguous and could fit both meanings: “…because they led My people astray…” or “…because they led My people into a mistake…”. Radak (there) yet again writes that *toeh* with a TAV and with a TET both mean the same thing, but that the former is the preferred orthography in Scripture and the latter, in Rabbinic sources.

R. Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (1866–1935) also disagrees with HaBachur’s characterization of the difference between *toeh* (TAV) and *toeh* (TET). He argues that both words can refer to somebody lost on the road, but the wanderer can be lost in different ways. *Toeh* with a TAV denotes a wayfarer who has lost his way and is completely at a loss as to the proper direction in which he should travel. *Toeh* with a TET, on the other hand, denotes a traveller who has not only lost his way, but he thinks he knows where he is going, and has decided the the improper path is the proper path!

As a side note, this comment of Rabbi Wertheimer’s does not appear in his lexicon of Hebrew synonyms, *Biurei Shemot HaNirdafim*. Rather, I found this comment in a footnote to the work *Batei Midrashot* published by R. Avraham Yosef Wertheimer (d. 2002) in the name of his grandfather, R. Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer. That footnote claims that this comment appears in the senior R. Wertheimer’s *Biurei Shemot HaNirdafim*, when, in fact, it does not. I investigated this matter and, through R. Dr. Eliezer Brodt, came into contact with R. Avraham Yosef Wertheimer’s son, R. Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer of Ramat Shlomo (head of *Machon Ktav Yad VaSefer*). After looking into it, R. S. A. Wertheimer Jr. informed me that his great-grandfather’s comment was originally printed in the second volume of the multi-volume first edition of *Biurei Shemot HaNirdafim*, but that the single-volume later editions of that work for some reason omitted this comment. Mysterious, ain’t it? Maybe it got lost. Maybe it was a mistake.[^58]
